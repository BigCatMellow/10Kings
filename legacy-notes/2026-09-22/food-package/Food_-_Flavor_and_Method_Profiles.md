# Food — Flavor & Method Profiles

A reference grammar for each region's cuisine: the building blocks that determine what survives and what gets substituted when a dish crosses into someone else's kitchen.

Each profile has four parts:
- **Philosophy** — the one rule that tends to survive even when nothing else does
- **Palate** — the dominant taste sensations
- **Method** — how it's actually cooked
- **Pantry** — the load-bearing ingredients/techniques, tagged by the *job* they do (fat, acid, umami, sweetness, starch, aromatic, preservation)

The Pantry tags are the useful part. When a dish travels, take its source-region Pantry item, note its job, and check the host region's row in the cross-reference table below for the local equivalent. That's your first-draft substitution.

## Ironcrest

**Philosophy:** Food is fuel. No apology for plainness — if it didn't take hours over embers, it isn't done.

**Palate:** Smoke and salt dominate everything. What brightness exists comes from a sharp black vinegar cutting through the richness. Sweetness is rare enough to register as a small luxury.

**Method:** Iron cauldrons and coal-pits, fed by forge heat — long, unattended simmers and slow-roasts, because the fire's already burning for other reasons.

**Pantry:**
- Rendered animal fat / lard (richness)
- Black vinegar (acid/brightness)
- Dried mushrooms + smoke (umami/depth)
- Dense rye or wheat bread, root vegetables (starch)
- Smoked paprika / forge-char (signature aromatic)
- Smoking + salting (preservation)

## Northwind

**Philosophy:** Preserve first, season second. Let what's already there — the brine, the fat of the fish — do the talking.

**Palate:** Briny and mild. Sourness comes from pickled or fermented berries rather than vinegar. Almost no heat or layered spice — dill is about as far as it goes.

**Method:** Wood-fired hearth ovens and clay dishes, low and slow, built around fish and root vegetables that can cook gently while everyone's out on the water.

**Pantry:**
- Fish fat / flaxseed oil (richness)
- Pickled tart berries (acid/brightness)
- Salted or fermented fish (umami/depth)
- Oat crumbs, root vegetables (starch)
- Dill / sea kelp (signature aromatic)
- Salting + smoking + fermenting (preservation)

## Greenvale

**Philosophy:** Share it, and make sure there's bread. Abundance is the point — a meal isn't a meal unless there's enough for whoever shows up.

**Palate:** Comforting and mildly sweet. Orchard fruit and dairy carry most of the weight; cinnamon and nutmeg are about the closest thing to a spice rack.

**Method:** Communal ovens and long-simmered stews, built around whatever the season's abundance happens to be.

**Pantry:**
- Butter / cream (richness)
- Cider or wine vinegar (acid/brightness)
- Aged cheese (umami/depth)
- Orchard fruit + honey (sweetness)
- Whole-grain bread, oats (starch)
- Cinnamon / nutmeg (signature aromatic)
- Pickling + drying (preservation)

## Highridge

**Philosophy:** More layers, always. A dish should give you something new to notice three bites in — that's what makes it worth arguing about over dinner.

**Palate:** Warm and complex. Cumin, coriander, turmeric, and chili stack together, balanced by sour dairy and the sweetness of dried fruit.

**Method:** One-pot stews and curries in clay pots, multi-tier steaming for dumplings, stone-oven flatbread — everything built to travel and to be shared at a crowded table.

**Pantry:**
- Yak butter (richness)
- Sour yogurt / dairy (acid/brightness)
- Lentils (umami/depth)
- Dried apricots / raisins (sweetness)
- Flatbread, grains (starch)
- Cumin-coriander-turmeric-chili blend (signature aromatic)
- Drying, for caravan portability (preservation)

## Deepwood

**Philosophy:** Let the ingredient speak. The cook's job is to get out of the way, not to announce themselves.

**Palate:** Earthy and quiet. Wild mushroom umami is the backbone. Sweetness comes from forest honey, sourness from mild fruit ferments — and the one "aromatic" that matters is a ceremonial bark that's almost impossible to describe to outsiders.

**Method:** Clay or ceramic pots over low embers, leaf-wrapping, gentle roasting — slow, minimal-intervention cooking that treats heat as something borrowed, not commanded.

**Pantry:**
- Rendered game fat, used sparingly (richness)
- Fermented forest fruit (acid/brightness)
- Wild mushrooms (umami/depth)
- Forest honey (sweetness)
- Chestnut / acorn flour (starch)
- Ceremonial aromatic bark (signature aromatic — *no real equivalent exists elsewhere; this is intentional*)
- Mild fermentation + honey-preservation (preservation)

## Sunplains

**Philosophy:** Balance richness with brightness, and make it beautiful. A dish that doesn't look plated on purpose isn't finished.

**Palate:** Olive oil and citrus do most of the work. Honey supplies sweetness; cinnamon, saffron, and mint are the recognizable "warm" notes. Everything aims for balance over intensity.

**Method:** Clay tagines for slow braises, heated-stone flatbread, and a strong fresh-prep tradition fed by irrigation-channel produce.

**Pantry:**
- Olive oil (richness)
- Citrus / lemon (acid/brightness)
- Chickpeas / legumes (umami/depth)
- Honey (sweetness)
- Couscous / flatbread (starch)
- Cinnamon-saffron-mint (signature aromatic)
- Oil-curing + drying (preservation)

---

## Port Lumière

**Philosophy:** Borrow freely, but make it look intentional. Nothing here is "really" Port's — that's the point.

**Palate:** A Mediterranean base (olive oil, citrus, basil/oregano/rosemary/thyme) with whatever just arrived by ship layered on top. The only region where the signature aromatic genuinely shifts with the trade winds.

**Method:** Whatever works — grilling, stovetop reductions, communal ovens, fast street-food prep — chosen for the dish and the crowd, not for regional consistency.

**Pantry:**
- Olive oil (richness)
- Citrus / wine vinegar (acid/brightness)
- Seafood (umami/depth)
- Honey / fruit (sweetness)
- Mixed breads, rice (starch)
- Mediterranean herbs + current trade spice (signature aromatic)
- Drying + fermenting + pickling (preservation)

## How to Use This

For any pairing: take the traveling dish's load-bearing Pantry items, note what *job* each one is doing, then check the host region's row for that job. That's the substitution. Then check the host region's **Philosophy** line — that's usually what survives regardless of what happens to the ingredients.

Worked example, retroactively: Guardian Stew's signature aromatic is Deepwood's ceremonial bark. In Greenvale, the signature-aromatic slot is cinnamon/nutmeg — not a *good* substitute, but it's the nearest "this region's version of a flavor that signals something special." That's roughly the gap a third-generation refugee kitchen might eventually paper over once the original workaround stops feeling sufficient — and it's also why, when it doesn't get papered over, that absence is doing real work.

A second kind of gap is worth flagging too: sometimes what doesn't travel isn't an *ingredient* but an *environment*. Ironcrest's smoke isn't really a pantry item — it's a byproduct of cooking next to a forge that's burning anyway. That's why Forgefire Lamb in Sunplains could only ever be a brief pass over olive-wood embers: there's no equivalent row for "a fire that's already lit for other reasons," so the dish gets *lighter* rather than substituted ingredient-for-ingredient. Worth keeping an eye out for which gaps are pantry gaps and which are environment gaps — they fail differently.
