## Trade Dependencies

The Dependency Web explains why regions cannot simply walk away from each other—creating resentment that festers for generations, necessity that forces civility despite hatred, and leverage that shapes political dynamics.

### The Dependency Matrix

| Region | Needs | Provides | Depends On |
|--------|-------|----------|------------|
| Ironcrest | Food, timber, labor | Metal, tools, weapons | Greenvale, Deepwood, Northwind shipping |
| Northwind | Metal, grain, medicine | Fish, salt, furs, shipping | Ironcrest, Greenvale, Deepwood herbs |
| Greenvale | Tools, salt (preservation), shipping | Grain, produce, textiles | Ironcrest, Northwind |
| Highridge | Everything physical | Trade routes, knowledge, banking | Everyone passing through |
| Deepwood | Metal tools, grain | Timber, herbs, medicine, dyes | Ironcrest, Greenvale |
| Sunplain | Timber, furs, iron | Wine, oil, luxury goods, art | Deepwood, Northwind, Ironcrest |
| Port | Food, medicine, everything | Shipping access, neutral ground, festivals | Everyone |

---

### Ironcrest ↔ Greenvale: The Grudging Marriage

They need each other most and resent it most.

**Ironcrest's forges require:**
- Grain to feed workers (Greenvale)
- Timber for construction and fuel (Deepwood, but also Greenvale orchards for charcoal)
- Labor migration during peak production (landless Greenvale farmhands seeking wages)

**Greenvale's farms require:**
- Iron tools—plows, scythes, mill parts (Ironcrest)
- Salt for preservation (Northwind, but shipped through Ironcrest routes)
- Defense—Greenvale has weak military tradition, relies on Ironcrest steel

**This creates:**
- Border towns where Ironcrest forges process Greenvale timber, and the air smells of both smoke and hay
- Seasonal labor migrations—Greenvale workers flooding into Ironcrest foundries after harvest, resented by local workers, essential to production
- Price disputes that dominate Council mediation—Ironcrest raises tool prices, Greenvale raises grain prices, cycle repeats
- Intermarriage at borders, creating families with split loyalties
- Cultural contempt: Ironcrest sees Greenvale as soft, pastoral, naive. Greenvale sees Ironcrest as brutal, dirty, soulless.

---

### Northwind's Chokehold

Northwind controls north-south shipping. This means:
- Ironcrest metal heading to Sunplain goes through Northwind vessels or overland through Highridge (slower, more expensive)
- Greenvale grain heading anywhere coastal needs Northwind
- Deepwood timber floats downriver but needs Northwind ships for long hauls

This gives Northwind leverage despite having the smallest population and harshest land. They're not rich in resources—they're rich in *access*.

**Historical implication:** Northwind once controlled Port directly. They gave it up (or lost it) during the Convergence. This still stings. Some Northwind clans believe Port should be theirs again.

---

### Deepwood's Quiet Power

Everyone underestimates Deepwood because they're isolationist and spiritual. But they control:
- Medicinal herbs that no other region can grow
- Rare dyes that Sunplain's luxury trade depends on
- Quality timber that Ironcrest and Northwind shipwrights need

When Deepwood restricts exports—which they do for spiritual reasons, not economic ones—other regions suffer. The Rootkin don't care about gold. They care about forest preservation. This makes them unpredictable negotiating partners.

---

### Highridge: The Middleman

Highridge produces almost nothing physical. Their entire economy is:
- Caravan tolls
- Banking and lending
- Knowledge (archives, scholars, trained scribes)
- Neutral arbitration

This makes them essential but resented. Every region pays Highridge fees. Every region grumbles about it. But without Highridge's passes and banking, overland trade collapses.

---

### Sunplain: The Luxury Market

Sunplain is where wealth concentrates. They buy:
- Ironcrest metalwork (decorative, not industrial)
- Deepwood dyes and exotic woods
- Northwind furs and whale oil
- Greenvale wine grapes and specialty produce

They sell:
- Art, philosophy, trained professionals
- Olive oil, wine, luxury textiles
- Access to eastern sea routes

Sunplain's city-states compete with each other more than with other regions. Their internal rivalry is intense—which festival is grandest, which patron family most generous, which harbor most efficient.

---

### Port: The Starving Hub

**Port has:**
- The only protected deep-water bay
- Neutrality (enforced by everyone's mutual interest)
- The Three Moon Festival and other events that bring money *through* but not *to* the city
- A population of displaced people with nowhere else to go

**Port lacks:**
- Farmland
- Forests
- Mines
- Military
- Sovereignty

Every trade ship docks at Port. Every merchant profits. Port collects tariffs but can't grow food, so they spend those tariffs buying grain from Greenvale at prices Greenvale sets. The cycle keeps Port alive but never prosperous.

---

### Seasonal Rhythms

| Season | Ironcrest | Northwind | Greenvale | Port |
|--------|-----------|-----------|-----------|------|
| Spring | Production surge, need labor | Fishing season opens, ships launch | Planting, labor stays home | Trade picks up, ships arrive |
| Summer | Peak output, heat exhaustion | Peak fishing, salting/smoking | Growing season, minimal trade | Festival season, maximum traffic |
| Autumn | Slower forges, tool shipments | Storms begin, dangerous sailing | Harvest, labor surplus | Three Moon Festival, year's biggest event |
| Winter | Maintenance, stockpiling | Ports ice over, ships shelter | Surplus stored or sold | Lean months, ships rare, hunger |

Port's worst season is winter. Ships don't come. Stockpiles run low. This is when Port's people feel their dependence most keenly.

## Historical Grudges (Pre-Convergence Wars)

The Convergence didn't happen because everyone got along. It happened because everyone was exhausted.

---

### The Ember Wars (Year -80 to -60)

Ironcrest expanded aggressively into what's now the Greenvale-Ironcrest border. They wanted timber, farmland, and labor. Greenvale's decentralized farming communities couldn't mount unified resistance. Ironcrest forges consumed entire forests. Villages burned.

**What stopped it:** Ironcrest overextended. Supply lines stretched thin. A brutal winter killed more Ironcrest soldiers than Greenvale resistance ever did. Ironcrest pulled back, but kept the border territories.

**What's remembered:**
- **Greenvale:** "The Burning Years." Songs about villages torched, orchards cut down, families scattered. Grandparents still tell children "the forge-men came."
- **Ironcrest:** "The Expansion." A proud chapter—they took what they needed. The retreat is blamed on weather, not failure.
- **Border towns:** Both narratives exist in the same families. Some identify with Ironcrest, some with Greenvale, some refuse to pick.

---

### The Salted Coast Disputes (Year -120 to -90)

Northwind and Sunplain competed for control of southern fishing waters and trade routes. Northwind raiders hit Sunplain coastal towns. Sunplain hired mercenaries to burn Northwind fishing villages.

**What stopped it:** Neither could deliver a killing blow. Northwind's ships ruled open water; Sunplain's wealth outlasted raids. Eventually both sides accepted spheres of influence—Northwind controls northern shipping, Sunplain controls southern.

**What's remembered:**
- **Northwind:** "The Sunplain dogs burned our grandfathers' boats." Specific villages are named in drinking songs.
- **Sunplain:** "Northern pirates" is still a slur. Port cities have old watchtowers built against Northwind raids.
- **Port:** Was neutral ground even then—both sides needed somewhere to negotiate. This established Port's role.

---

### The Timber Tithes (Year -150 to -100)

Deepwood wasn't always isolationist. Once, they traded freely. Then outside demand grew. Logging crews pushed deeper. Sacred groves fell.

**What stopped it:** The "Ages of Silence"—the cultural trauma. Deepwood closed its borders, violently if necessary. They killed logging crews. They ambushed caravans.

**What's remembered:**
- **Deepwood:** This is their founding trauma. They will never again trust outsiders with their forests.
- **Ironcrest/Greenvale:** "The forest people went mad." Some version of events blames Deepwood for murdering honest workers.
- **Current tension:** Every logging proposal, every request for timber rights, reopens this wound.

---

### The Port Seizure (Year -70 to -50)

Northwind controlled Port for roughly two decades. They used it as a southern naval base, extracting fees from every ship that docked.

**What stopped it:** Coalition pressure. Ironcrest, Greenvale, and Sunplain all suffered under Northwind's chokehold. They threatened combined action. Northwind, facing war on multiple fronts, negotiated withdrawal. Port became "neutral"—which meant belonging to no one.

**What's remembered:**
- **Northwind:** "We built that harbor. We dredged those channels. It was stolen from us through threats."
- **Port:** Older families remember Northwind rule as "the occupation." Some remember it as "when the ships actually came."
- **Everyone else:** Port's neutrality is sacred because the alternative is someone controlling it—and that means war.

---

### What This Means for Present Day

The Convergence (Year 0) papered over these wounds. It didn't heal them. Now:

- Greenvale farmers selling grain to Ironcrest forges feel the ghost of the Ember Wars in every transaction
- Northwind sailors passing Sunplain ports remember the Salted Coast
- Deepwood's refusal to negotiate isn't stubbornness—it's survival memory
- Port's people know they exist because larger powers couldn't agree on who should own them

When tensions rise, these old narratives resurface. "They burned our villages once; they'll do it again." "The forest people can't be trusted." "Northwind wants Port back."

## Everyday Prejudice: Common Sayings

### About Ironcrest
- "Iron hands, iron hearts" — Greenvale proverb. Not a compliment.
- "He'd sell his mother for pig iron" — common across regions
- "Smoke in the lungs, soot in the soul" — Deepwood saying about forge workers
- "Ironcrest promise" — means a deal that benefits only one side

### About Northwind
- "Cold water, cold blood" — Sunplain expression
- "Trust a Northwind sailor to find your harbor and your wife" — Port dockworkers' joke
- "Fair as a Northwind trade" — sarcastic; means you'll be cheated
- "When the Northwind ships come in" — meaning "never" in some Sunplain dialects, "finally" in Port

### About Greenvale
- "Soft as Greenvale butter" — Ironcrest insult for weakness
- "He's got Greenvale hands" — meaning someone's never done real work
- "Feed a Greenvaler and he'll lecture you on farming" — Highridge merchant complaint
- "Greenvale generous" — genuine compliment in some contexts, mocking in others (implying naivety)

### About Highridge
- "Highridge math" — when the numbers somehow always favor the banker
- "Talks like a Highridge scholar" — uses too many words, says nothing
- "Every road leads to Highridge's pocket" — common grumble
- "He'd charge his mother toll to visit" — about Highridge greed

### About Deepwood
- "Gone to the trees" — means someone's lost their mind
- "Deepwood hospitality" — you'll be watched, judged, and possibly never leave
- "Quiet as a Deepwood answer" — they heard you, they won't respond
- "Ask a Rootkin for directions, end up in a mushroom circle" — Highridge caravan warning

### About Sunplain
- "Sunplain silk hides Sunplain steel" — beautiful but dangerous
- "Three Sunplain merchants, four opinions" — they argue among themselves
- "He's got a Sunplain palate" — too refined, impractical
- "Golden tongue, copper coin" — all talk, no substance

### About Port
- "Port-bred" — mixed blood, uncertain parentage, not quite trustworthy
- "Muddog luck" — surviving against odds through desperation, not skill
- "Worth a Port promise" — worthless, since Port has no power to enforce anything
- "Every ship lands at Port, none stay" — about being used and abandoned

## Social Dynamics

### Tavern Behavior

In a mixed-region tavern:
- **Northwind sailors** cluster near the door (quick exit, habit from port fights)
- **Ironcrest workers** take the seats nearest the fire, talk loudly
- **Greenvale merchants** sit in the middle, try to broker peace when tensions rise
- **Highridge travelers** order expensive drinks, get charged double by locals anyway
- **Deepwood visitors** don't come to taverns—when they do, people stare
- **Sunplain traders** take the best-lit table, spread papers, act like they own the place
- **Port locals** work the room—serving, cleaning, listening

**Who buys rounds:**
- **Greenvale custom:** the host or most prosperous pays
- **Northwind custom:** rounds rotate by age, eldest first
- **Ironcrest custom:** whoever lost the last bet
- **Sunplain custom:** elaborate negotiation disguised as generosity
- **Port custom:** whatever gets the drink in hand fastest

---

### Hiring Prejudices

What jobs go to whom (and why):

- **Ship crews:** Northwind preferred, Port locals acceptable for grunt work, never hire Ironcrest (they sink, joke goes)
- **Forge work:** Ironcrest only; outsiders can't be trusted with metal
- **Farm labor:** Greenvale locals first, then Ironcrest migrants, then Port desperate
- **Caravan guards:** Highridge trained, Ironcrest muscle, never Deepwood (too unpredictable)
- **Household servants in Sunplain:** Port-bred preferred—they're used to serving, they're grateful, they won't make trouble
- **Translators/scribes:** Highridge scholars or Port polyglots
- **Healers:** Deepwood trained commands premium; everyone else is second choice

---

### Casual Insults in Arguments

When a deal goes sour:
- "Go back to your forge and choke on smoke" — to an Ironcrest
- "Typical glacier brain" — to a Northwind
- "Why don't you go plant something" — to a Greenvale (implies uselessness)
- "Add that to your ledger, counter-kisser" — to a Highridge
- "Whisper that to your trees" — to a Deepwood
- "Too rich for this, aren't you?" — to a Sunplain
- "You'd sell your own dock" — to a Port local

---

### What People Don't Say Out Loud

- No one openly insults Deepwood's spiritual practices—too afraid of what might happen
- Northwind's naval dominance is resented but acknowledged; direct insults risk trade retaliation
- Ironcrest's military strength means jokes stay behind their backs
- Sunplain's wealth buys tolerance for their arrogance
- Port's people hear everything said about them and remember

## Wedding Customs

**Ironcrest-Greenvale marriage:** Families argue for months over whether the ceremony includes Greenvale's flower wreaths or Ironcrest's forge-blessing. Compromise: both, but ordered carefully so neither "comes first."

**Northwind-Sunplain marriage:** Rare. Families on both sides mutter. The couple usually settles in Port, where no one cares.

**Anyone marrying into Deepwood:** You move there. You don't come back often. Your family half-mourns you.

**Port marriages:** Brief, practical, witnessed by whoever's sober. No one expects it to last—people leave, ships sail, circumstances change.

## Major Festivals (Detailed)

### IRONCREST: Forge Reawakening (Early Spring)

After winter maintenance, the great foundries relight. The largest forge in Cindermarch—the Old Crucible—is ceremonially lit by the Forge-King or his representative. Smaller forges throughout the region light their fires from brands carried from Cindermarch.

**What happens:**
- Three days of controlled labor—rituals, not production
- Apprentices present their winter projects for judgment
- Widows of forge workers receive alms from guild coffers
- Public readings of the "Foundry Litany"—names of those who died in the Great Foundry Fires

**What it reveals:**
- Ironcrest sees itself as keepers of sacred labor
- Death in service to the forge is honored, not mourned
- The guild structure is reinforced publicly
- Outsiders are tolerated but not invited to participate

**What outsiders see:**
- Greenvale visitors find it grim, death-obsessed
- Northwind visitors respect the discipline
- Sunplain visitors find it aesthetically crude

---

### IRONCREST: Ember Remembrance (Late Autumn)

Commemorates the Great Foundry Fires. Solemn, not celebratory.

**What happens:**
- Forges bank their fires for one night
- Families visit memorial walls inscribed with ancestors' names
- A single ceremonial flame burns in the Old Forge Quarter
- No work, no commerce—the only such day in Ironcrest

**What it reveals:**
- The trauma is real and maintained deliberately
- "Never again" isn't rhetoric—it's religion
- Even the Council knows not to schedule demands on this day

---

### NORTHWIND: Ice Breaking (Early Spring)

When the coastal ice retreats enough to launch ships. The first vessel out is blessed, its crew honored.

**What happens:**
- Clan elders inspect hulls wintered in dry dock
- The High Chieftain or Clan Speaker throws a ceremonial net
- Racing between clan longboats—the winner earns first fishing rights to a contested ground
- Feasting on the last of winter stores, anticipating fresh catch

**What it reveals:**
- Competition between clans is constant but ritualized
- The sea is respected, almost worshipped
- Survival through winter is an achievement worth marking

**What outsiders see:**
- Port traders arrive hoping for early contracts—tolerated but not welcomed at ceremonies
- Sunplain visitors find the food appalling, the drinking impressive

---

### NORTHWIND: Aurora Nights (Late Summer)

When the northern lights are strongest. Spiritual, quiet, unlike other Northwind events.

**What happens:**
- Families gather on hillsides or shorelines
- Elders tell stories of ancestors whose spirits dance in the lights
- Children born during Aurora Nights are considered blessed
- Young couples announce betrothals

**What it reveals:**
- Beneath the rough exterior, Northwind has genuine spirituality
- The lights connect living and dead
- This is when Northwind shows its softer face

---

### GREENVALE: Harvest Home (Early Autumn)

The biggest event in Greenvale. Communities that competed all year now share.

**What happens:**
- Each village brings its best produce to regional gathering points
- Communal meals that last for days
- Dancing, music, courtship
- Surplus is allocated—communities that struggled receive from those who prospered
- Seed exchanges for next year's planting

**What it reveals:**
- Greenvale's communal identity is reinforced through redistribution
- Competition exists (whose crops are finest) but sharing trumps hoarding
- The Seven-year Blight is why—no village survives alone

**What outsiders see:**
- Ironcrest traders arrive to buy grain futures—tolerated but watched
- Northwind ships wait at river ports for cargo
- Genuine warmth, but outsiders aren't quite family

---

### GREENVALE: Root Cellar Night (Midwinter)

The deepest point of winter. Families retreat to root cellars and share stored food.

**What happens:**
- Small gatherings, immediate family only
- Stories of the Seven-year Blight told to children
- A ritual meal of preserved foods—pickles, dried fruit, salted meat
- Prayers for spring's return

**What it reveals:**
- Greenvale's communalism has limits—midwinter is intimate, not public
- The fear of scarcity runs deep
- Gratitude for what was saved

---

### HIGHRIDGE: Midsummer Debates (Midsummer)

Public intellectual contests. Scholars, merchants, and philosophers compete.

**What happens:**
- Topics announced one day prior—no preparation allowed
- Debaters argue before crowds who vote by acclamation
- Winners earn prestige, sometimes political appointments
- Foreign scholars are permitted to compete—winning earns respect

**What it reveals:**
- Highridge believes reason should govern
- Words are weapons; rhetoric is a martial art
- Even governance emerges from debate (Trial of Winds)

**What outsiders see:**
- Ironcrest visitors find it pointless—"just talking"
- Sunplain visitors take it seriously and often compete
- Deepwood visitors never attend

---

### HIGHRIDGE: Ledger Closing (Late Autumn)

The fiscal year ends. Debts are settled or renegotiated.

**What happens:**
- Banking houses open extended hours
- Merchants reconcile accounts
- Public announcement of major defaults (shaming mechanism)
- Feasting once the books balance

**What it reveals:**
- Highridge's real religion is commerce
- Reputation is everything—public default is social death
- The brief celebration after "Closing" is genuine relief

---

### DEEPWOOD: Canopy Vigil (Midsummer)

When the forest canopy is fullest. A time of listening.

**What happens:**
- Communities fall silent for one full day
- No fires, no cooking, no tools
- Individuals sit alone in the forest, listening
- At dusk, people return and share what they heard—or didn't

**What it reveals:**
- Deepwood's relationship with the forest is active, not passive
- Silence is valued above speech
- What you "hear" marks your spiritual state

**What outsiders see:**
- Nothing. Outsiders aren't permitted. Those who stumble in are guided out quietly.

---

### DEEPWOOD: Deep Silence (Midwinter)

Commemorates the Ages of Silence—when the forest spirits withdrew.

**What happens:**
- Week-long observance
- No one speaks above a whisper
- Community gathers in central halls, sharing warmth
- Stories told through shadow puppets, hand signs, written notes

**What it reveals:**
- The trauma of losing connection to the forest is kept alive
- Silence is penance
- Community sustains you when the spirits don't

---

### SUNPLAIN: Festival of Patrons (Midsummer)

Each city-state honors its wealthiest sponsor families.

**What happens:**
- Competitive displays—art commissions, public works unveiled, performances
- Patron families host open feasts
- Artists and scholars receive grants, announced publicly
- Inter-city rivalries peak

**What it reveals:**
- Wealth in Sunplain is meant to be displayed, not hoarded
- Patronage is political power
- The competition keeps culture vibrant—and vicious

**What outsiders see:**
- Excess. Ironcrest visitors are offended. Greenvale visitors are overwhelmed. Northwind visitors get drunk and start fights.

---

### SUNPLAIN: Solstice Lanterns (Midwinter)

The darkest night lit with thousands of lanterns.

**What happens:**
- Every household hangs lanterns—more is better
- Processions through streets, singing
- Alms given to the poor (very publicly)
- Lanterns floated on water at dawn

**What it reveals:**
- Sunplain fears darkness—the Radiant Path influence
- Generosity is performance, but still real
- Beauty matters even in hardship

---

### PORT: Three Moon Festival (Early Autumn)

The year's main event. When all three moons align (or close enough). Port's one moment of significance.

**What happens:**
- Traders from every region converge
- Markets run day and night for a week
- Performers, musicians, fortune-tellers flood the streets
- Negotiations happen—trade deals, treaties, marriages
- The Festival Council (Port's closest thing to government) announces major decisions

**What it reveals:**
- Port only matters when outsiders need it to
- The city comes alive when others arrive, dies when they leave
- Everything Port earns this week must last until next year

**What outsiders see:**
- A useful venue
- Cheap entertainment
- Servants who smile too much

**What Port's people feel:**
- Pride and humiliation mixed
- "They come for the festival, not for us"
- Frantic labor—everyone works the festival, from children to elders

---

### PORT: Lean Vigil (Midwinter)

Not a celebration. An endurance ritual.

**What happens:**
- Communal meals stretched thin—everyone eats, no one eats well
- Stories shared of previous winters survived
- The harbor is watched for any ship brave enough to sail
- If a ship arrives, the whole city turns out—the crew is treated as heroes

**What it reveals:**
- Port knows its dependence intimately
- Solidarity is survival
- Hope is a ship on the horizon

---

### Cross-Festival Dynamics

- Ironcrest traders arrive at Greenvale's Harvest Home to buy grain. They're fed but not trusted.
- Northwind ships time departures to arrive at Port's Three Moon Festival with holds full of salt fish.
- Highridge bankers use Ledger Closing to pressure debtors before Three Moon Festival negotiations.
- Sunplain patrons sometimes commission Ironcrest metalwork unveiled at Festival of Patrons—acknowledgment of skill, not friendship.
- No one visits Deepwood during their festivals. The invitation is never extended.

## Port: Internal Dynamics

*Based on the Free City of Danzig parallel*

### Population Structure
- Mixed population of displaced peoples from all regions
- Large minority of refugees, the stateless, the unwanted
- Criminals seeking reform (Port's neutrality = can't be extradited for outside crimes)
- "Sailors' droppings" — children of brief shore encounters, claimed by no one
- "Mud dogs" — orphans, the absolute lowest class
- More than 80% consider themselves "from Port" first rather than identifying with their ancestors' regions

### The Economic Trap
- Agricultural territory so small that food production is insufficient for the population—Port relies entirely on imported food
- Prices higher than in other regions due to importing necessities
- Working class economic situation is chronically unsatisfactory
- Unemployment greater than in most other regions
- Port revenues depend on trade volumes, which are volatile
- Port receives only a fraction of customs duties—most goes to surrounding kingdoms who "protect" them
- Population engaged extensively in speculative and illegal activities

### Port's Industries
- Shipbuilding and repair (the largest employer)
- Timber processing
- Food processing, brewing, confectionery
- Service industries supporting the harbor
- After demilitarization of the harbor, lean years and job cuts led to strike action by disillusioned workers

### The Smuggling Economy
- Port's strategic position facilitates contraband networks
- Residents export to markets that restrict imports
- Rising smuggling operations and labor unrest strain public order
- The smuggling economy is a survival mechanism, not a choice

### Identity Problem
- Citizens have no real sovereignty
- Governed themselves internally but external affairs controlled by treaty
- Neither fully part of any kingdom nor truly independent
- Identified as "from here" rather than belonging to anyone
- Watched helplessly as external powers decide their fate
- Experienced cycles of hope and despair as economy swings wildly
- Resent everyone—the kingdoms who use them, the Council that doesn't protect them, and each other

### Internal Political Factions
- **Harbor Masters' Alliance:** Old families who've controlled dock operations for generations. Conservative, want stability.
- **Workers' Collectives:** Dockworkers, ship-hands, seasonal laborers. Want better conditions but fear losing what little they have.
- **Merchant Factors:** Middlemen who facilitate trade. Pragmatic, wealthy by Port standards, often seen as collaborators.
- **The Displaced:** Recent arrivals with nowhere else to go. No political voice. Suspicious of everyone.
- **Festival Council:** Port's nominal government. Announces decisions at Three Moon Festival. Limited real power.

### Port's Response to Change
- Mixed, few outspoken
- Most fear losing what little they have
- Not a hungry revolution waiting to happen—survival pragmatism, learned helplessness
- "Whatever brings the ships to Port"
- Attitude is not enthusiasm—it's resignation

### The Working Class Trap
- The shipyards that once employed thousands now build smaller vessels
- The docks still function—but for others' benefit, not Port's
- Population that couldn't feed itself and knew it
- Worked jobs that existed only because outsiders needed the harbor
- Had higher prices and lower wages than surrounding regions
- Developed a smuggling economy as a survival mechanism

## Appendix: What This All Creates

The world exists because of these dynamics—not despite them. They explain:

1. **Why the Council is distracted:** Constantly mediating price disputes, border tensions, guild conflicts
2. **Why Port is overlooked:** No one thinks of them as a nation, just a useful harbor
3. **Why the system is already stressed:** Regional friction exists regardless of any villain
4. **Why dominos cascade naturally:** The world is already fragile
5. **Why historical grudges matter:** Every transaction carries the ghost of old wars
6. **Why neutrality is sacred:** The alternative is someone controlling Port—and that means war

This is *weather*, not plot. Background noise that makes the world breathe.
