# Ecology, Flora and Fauna

## Status

**Provisional.** This page owns what grows and lives in the world, and how it all fits together. It was asked for by bt 7274 on 2026-11-13 ([D51](Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)), after the [Realism Check](Reference/Realism-Check-2026-11.md#is-it-sustainable) found the wiki had no owner for it.

It is built from two sources:
- the grounded flora and fauna in the [2026-09-24 legacy pass](../legacy-notes/2026-09-24/Worldbuilding-Conversation-Complete.md), §15–§24, with that pass's own biology corrections kept;
- the climate on [Sky, Seasons and Sea](Sky-Seasons-and-Sea.md), and the land on the [map draft](../atlas/map/README.md).

Plant names are the legacy pass's working names. Where the lore already uses a name, it is marked; everything else is a lean.

[Sky, Seasons and Sea](Sky-Seasons-and-Sea.md) owns the weather and the sea, [Peoples and Land](Peoples-and-Land.md) owns who lives where, and the [Recipes](Culture/Recipes/README.md) own what's cooked.

## The rule

> A fictional plant may have a fictional common name, but it should behave like a believable organism in a believable biome. *(the legacy pass, kept)*

- **Every named species is a believable real-world kind under a local name.** Nothing grows because of magic, forge smoke or a god; its range follows climate, soil, grazing and people ([World Rules](World-Rules.md#2-geography-must-cause-culture-without-dictating-it)).
- **Fungi, kelp and lichen aren't plants.** Say so when it matters.
- **Unrelated animals don't interbreed.** Herds can share a pasture without crossbreeding: no yak × caribou. Oxen are castrated draft cattle; milk comes from cows.
- **Edibility is never assumed.** A "lily" or a "moss" isn't food until a safe analogue is named. Raw resin isn't food.
- **Beliefs about animals and plants are beliefs** ([Magic](Magic.md)). Aurora berries and dream fungi can matter to people without doing anything.

## The world's biomes

The world runs from about 56° north to 44° ([Sky, Seasons and Sea](Sky-Seasons-and-Sea.md#the-year-and-the-light)). Northwind is subarctic at a latitude that would be mild on Earth, because the cold White Current chills it. Labrador, at the latitude of Scotland, is subarctic for the same reason.

| Region | Biome | Earthly likeness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Northwind** | cold coast, fjords and kelp beds; peat moor and tundra on the fells (the Barrens); dwarf birch and willow; spruce only in sheltered valleys below the treeline | Labrador, Newfoundland, the Faroes, Norway's coast |
| **Highridge** | high grassland and sedge, cushion plants, juniper; terraces and irrigated valleys; a dry interior with salt flats | the Anatolian and Armenian highlands, the Tibetan margins |
| **Greenvale** | mixed farmland with hedgerows, hay meadows, orchards, riverside woods and fens (the Sedge Fens) | lowland England, Normandy, the Po valley |
| **Ironcrest** | upland grass and heath, conifer woods on the slopes, coppiced oak in the valleys (the Charcoal Woods), scree | the Harz, the Ore Mountains, Wales |
| **Deepwood** | broadleaf forest on wet lowland: oak, beech, chestnut and hazel kinds; alder carr along the slow rivers (the Alderfens); bog and marsh (Blackwater Marsh) | Białowieża, the Pripet Marshes, old Gaulish forest |
| **Sunplains** | Mediterranean scrub and open woodland; orchards, vines and olives; irrigated basins; the reed beds of the Thousand Channels | Provence, Valencia, the Ebro and Po deltas |

The edges blend, as the peoples do ([Peoples and Land](Peoples-and-Land.md)).

## By region

### Northwind

- **Sea.** Plankton feeds the kelp beds, the kelp beds shelter shellfish and young fish, and those feed cod- and haddock-like fish on the Grey Banks. Herring run in great shoals, and the salted catch goes inland on [the Herring Way](Places/Gazetteer.md#northwind). Seabirds nest on the cliffs (Gannet Sound), and seals and whales come in close (Sealfjord, Whalebone).
- **Land.** The fells are lichen, sedge, moss, crowberry and dwarf shrubs, grazed by reindeer-like herds. Cloudberry, lingonberry and bilberry kinds ripen in late summer (the "aurora berries" of the [recipes](Culture/Recipes/Northwind.md)). Arctic char swim in the cold lakes.
- **Kept animals.** Sheep on the moors, cattle where there's hay, goats and ponies.
- **Named working species.** Frost-spruce, a hardy conifer: its young tips make tea; raw resin is not food. Tundra kelp, an alga, used for broth and wrapping. Boreal berries. Snowflower lichen, emergency food only if a safe analogue is named.
- **Limits.** Seal and whale hunting belong to particular enclaves, under clan rules, seasonal taboos and rotating grounds, not modern quotas. The fish are "declining or shifting" ([Current Events](Story/Current-Events.md#fish-stocks-and-access)).

### Highridge

- **Plants.** Alpine grass and sedge, cushion plants, juniper, and willow along the water.
- **Crops.** Barley, buckwheat and hardy peas on the high terraces. Wheat only lower down; the legacy pass's *windrise wheat* is a short, wind-hardy kind for the middle terraces.
- **Kept animals.** Yak-like cattle, goats and sheep move between summer and winter pastures, and need hay for the winter. Mules and ponies carry the caravans.
- **Fuel** is scarce: dung, juniper and bought charcoal.
- **Named working species, with the pass's own fixes.**
  - *Spice thistle*, an aromatic thistle of the dry slopes.
  - *Alpine hibiscus*, recast as a mallow- or rosehip-like plant of the warm lower valleys, since true hibiscus can't take the cold.
  - *Caravan aloe*, recast as a houseleek-like alpine succulent, for the same reason.
- **No kelp grows up here.** It arrives dried, by trade.

### Greenvale

- **Crops.** Breezewheat (a wheat or spelt kind) and redtop millet (drought-hardy). Two cereals don't make a rotation: **the rotation turns on peas, beans, clover, pasture, fallow and manure** (the pass's correction). Apple, pear and plum kinds grow in the orchards, including the [Grand Orchard](Places/Places-of-Interest.md#the-grand-orchard).
- **Hedgerows and meadows.** Honeybloom, a flowering hedge creeper and the main honey source. Meadow herbs (thyme, sage and chamomile kinds) and hay grasses. Wildflower strips keep the pollinators.
- **Kept animals.** Cattle (cows for milk, oxen for the plough), pigs, sheep, goats, poultry and bees.
- **Wild animals.** Deer, boar, hare and foxes at the edges, and rooks in the fields. Fish, eels and wading birds in the fens.

### Ironcrest

- **Plants.** Upland grass, heath and sedge; conifers on the slopes; and coppiced oak in the valleys, cut in turns for charcoal ([the Charcoal Woods](Places/Gazetteer.md#ironcrest)). Berry shrubs fill the sheltered hollows.
- **Named working species.**
  - *Stonebloom*, a low, deep-rooted shrub that holds scree in place.
  - *Rock thyme* (used in the [recipes](Culture/Recipes/Ironcrest.md)), a creeping herb of sunny rock cracks.
  - *Basalt mushrooms*, a fungus of damp rock and old workings.
  - *Ashen briar*, a thorny berry shrub whose berries are good for jam.
- **Kept and wild animals.** Goats, sheep and mountain cattle. Boar and hare in the valleys.
- **What the mines do to it.** Erosion where the woods are cut, smoke, and fouled streams (Cinder Brook). **Nothing thrives *because of* the forges.**

### Deepwood

- **Trees.** Oak, beech and chestnut kinds give mast, the nut crop that feeds boar, deer, birds and people. Hazel grows in the understory, and alder in the wet. Deadwood and the fungal web under it keep the forest fed.
  - The mast comes in boom and bust years. A big mast year means more boar and more mice the next year, and more hungry foxes and owls the year after.
- **Undergrowth.** Ferns, nettles, wild garlic, berries and sedges.
- **Named working species.**
  - *Ghostbark*, a pale-barked hardwood, birch- or aspen-like.
  - *Evertwine*, a woody vine used for rope. Fermentable sap only if it is a grape-like kind.
  - *Cloudcap mushrooms*, a staple food fungus.
  - *Moss petals*, a streamside moss that holds water. Not tea until a safe kind is named.
- **Animals.** Deer and boar; wolf-like and bear-like predators that need large, connected forest; and river fish, beaver-like dam builders and otters. Rooks give Rookhallow its name.
- **Rare by design.** Sorrowcap and other "dream fungi" are rare and specific. The forest's reputation outruns its biology ([Magic](Magic.md)).

### Sunplains

- **Crops.** Olive, grape, fig, pomegranate, almond and pistachio kinds. Citrus only on the warmest southern shores. Chickpeas, lentils, durum wheat, and the herbs rosemary, thyme and mint.
  - *Crimson figs* need their fig wasp, so a new orchard has to bring one.
  - *Honey sage*, a silvery herb, is a bee plant.
- **Named working species, with the pass's own fixes.**
  - *Spicebloom* is split into two plants: a bark-spice shrub and a seed-pod spice.
  - *Ocean lily* is a dune-holding ornamental, not food. Its kitchen role goes to a samphire-like shore herb.
- **Wild country.** Scrub, open oak and pine woodland, and the reed beds of the Thousand Channels, full of wading birds and eels.
- **Kept animals.** Goats and sheep, poultry, bees, donkeys and mules.
- **Strains.** Irrigation, salt build-up, orchards of one kind, pollinators, pests and water rights ([Realism Check](Reference/Realism-Check-2026-11.md#is-it-sustainable)).

### Port and the Underpass

- **Port** grows little of its own. Its ecology is its harbor: gulls, rats and ships' cats. Its hazard is every region's seeds, pests and fevers arriving on the same quays. *Lean:* the harbor masters can hold a ship off a quay. Whether that has become a quarantine practice is open.
- **The Underpass** has cave fungi, bats and blind cave fish in the flooded sections. Mushroom beds in the old workings feed the enclaves ([The Underpass](Places/The-Underpass.md)).

## What links the regions

These are the legacy pass's ecological corridors, kept:

| Corridor | Transition | What crosses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ironcrest–Greenvale | rocky foothills → terraces → fields | grain and fruit uphill, tools downhill; pollinators forage across |
| Northwind–Highridge | cold coast → upland valleys → the pass | reindeer-like herds reach the foothills and yak-like herds hold the plateau. They share the ground but never interbreed; kelp only travels inland dried |
| Greenvale–Deepwood | field → hedge → managed wood → deep forest | mushrooms, herbs, honey; boar raiding the crops |
| Deepwood–Sunplains | wet forest → mixed woodland → wood pasture → orchard | boar and deer at the orchard edges; birds carrying seed |
| Highridge–Sunplains | dry foothills → orchards → cereal terraces → high grass | the caravan roads carry seed, weeds and pests by accident |

**How things spread.** Fruit-eating birds, mammals, livestock dung, wind, water, people, and seed caught in caravan baggage. Not hawks, and not bees: bees carry pollen between neighbouring plants of the same kind, and never cross unrelated ones.

## Still open

- The common names for animals: none are fixed beyond the plain words the stories use (goats, mules, ponies, sheep, herring, rooks).
- Whether the wolf-like and bear-like predators survive outside Deepwood and the high north.
- What the One Tree is biologically: one clonal root system, like a real aspen colony, is the lean ([The One Tree](Places/Places-of-Interest.md#the-one-tree)).
- Disease and quarantine at Port.
