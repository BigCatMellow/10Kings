# Religions

The world currently contains nine major trans-regional traditions. They do not map neatly onto kingdoms.

Each faith should have local schools, reform movements, institutions, charities, political interests, and internal disputes.

The compressed summaries below are **owners, not the total surviving religious material**.

A deliberate review of the large legacy religion corpus is preserved in [Religion Legacy Reconciliation](../Reference/Religion-Legacy-Reconciliation.md). Named deities, saints, sages, texts, sects, relics and rituals from that source remain available as provisional in-world theology rather than being discarded.

A faith may contain branches that:
- interpret a named figure as a literal deity;
- treat the same figure as a personified principle;
- regard the figure as a saint, sage or legendary teacher;
- reject personification entirely.

The setting does not need to confirm which interpretation is metaphysically correct. This is especially important under the world's scarce / uncertain magic rule.

Religious artifacts are governed by [Religious Artifacts Framework](../Reference/Religious-Artifacts-Framework.md).

## 1. The Cyclic Order

Core: existence moves through cycles of birth, death, renewal, time, balance, and moral consequence.

Institutional interests:
- calendars;
- rites of passage;
- mediation;
- burial;
- seasonal observance.

Current scheme:
A reform movement argues that post-Convergence stability has become stagnation. Some members want deliberate social disruption to "complete" overdue cycles; traditional elders oppose them.


### Retained depth

Legacy material worth keeping includes:
- **Symmetric Resonance** as a proposed ideal of alignment with larger cycles;
- **Moral Ripples** and **Life's Design** as theological vocabulary for consequence and responsibility;
- contemplative, monastic, wandering and reformist branches;
- calendrical / astronomical study;
- mediation and long-horizon ethical reasoning.

Preserved provisional names include **Chronospect, Omnia, Equilibria, Aevum, Mortalis**, the **Order of Stillness**, **Travelers of Aevum**, **Circle of Renewal**, and texts such as the **Spiral Codex**.

These names are theological vocabulary, not confirmation that the named beings objectively exist.

## 2. Path of the Great Weaver

Core: beings, places, ancestors, animals, and actions are threads in a living weave.

Institutional interests:
- stewardship;
- oral history;
- ancestry;
- ecological obligations;
- community memory.

Current scheme:
Networks across several regions are documenting land and water damage, creating a trans-regional moral case against large extraction/infrastructure projects. Some Council families see this as a threat to economic planning.


### Retained depth

The later legacy version strongly supports:
- the **Weave** itself as the central sacred reality;
- natural presences and spirits rather than a mandatory anthropomorphic pantheon;
- ancestors remaining part of community memory;
- oral tradition;
- stewardship obligations created by taking from land or water;
- craft as a form of devotion.

Older names such as **Arborealus, Montara, Flumenara, Zephyria, Viridia** remain available as local or historical personifications. Later terms such as **Voice of the Winds**, **Soul of the Mountain**, **Heart of the Forest**, and **Flowing Waters** show that different communities can name the same relational idea differently.

## 3. The Radiant Path

Core: moral life is struggle toward courage, justice, truth, and wisdom against cruelty, lies, and discord.

Institutional interests:
- charity;
- public ethics;
- justice;
- moral testimony.

Current scheme:
Different branches disagree over whether hidden Council rule is a necessary compromise or fundamentally a form of darkness through deception.


### Retained depth

Legacy material adds:
- charity and care institutions;
- schools and literacy;
- public moral testimony;
- legal / ethical education;
- protective lay or martial orders;
- saints / exemplars;
- disputes over scripture and moral action.

**Justitia, Valorus, Luxphera, and Concordia** can survive as personified aspects of the **Eternal Radiance**, while other branches explicitly interpret light as a moral metaphor rather than a personal divinity.

Preserved provisional material includes the **First Lantern**, **Luminous Codex**, **Lantern Schools**, **Guardians of Valor**, **Seekers of Luxphera**, and **Concordant Flame**.

## 4. The Eternal Forge

Core: creation and destruction are linked; character and society are shaped through disciplined transformation.

Institutional interests:
- craft;
- apprenticeship;
- ethical labor;
- rebuilding.

Current scheme:
Its guild-adjacent institutions are becoming active in labor and quality disputes, potentially turning a spiritual tradition into a cross-regional political force.


### Retained depth

The later deity-free legacy version is strongly compatible with the current faith.

Retain:
- the **Forge as a cosmic principle**;
- craft as moral practice;
- material honesty;
- repair and reuse;
- rebuilding after disaster;
- apprenticeship as ethical formation;
- the idea that no craft or person reaches absolute perfection;
- conflict between disciplined productivity and exploitative labor.

**The First Hammerer**, **Architect of the Ages**, and **Weaver of Sparks** remain useful legendary exemplars. Older divine names such as **Vulcanestra, Montara, Fortificus, Artesius** remain provisional theological figures rather than confirmed gods.

## 5. The Illuminated Circle

Core: knowledge, reason, inquiry, and intellectual freedom are sacred values.

Institutional interests:
- libraries;
- schools;
- debate;
- archives;
- public reasoning.

Current scheme:
Scholars are collecting contradictory trade and government records. Some may be close to proving systematic manipulation without yet understanding the Council.


### Retained depth

The legacy no-gods revision preserves four named ideals:
- **Omnia** — universal / interconnected knowledge;
- **Liberion** — intellectual freedom;
- **Epistemia** — inquiry and systematic learning;
- **Logos** — reason and order.

Strong institutions include:
- symposia;
- libraries as sacred spaces;
- schools and lifelong education;
- mentorship;
- manuscript preservation;
- public debate;
- ethical disputes over censorship and dangerous knowledge.

Preserved provisional schools / texts include the **Seekers of Omnia**, **Flamebearers of Liberion**, **Geometers of Logos**, **Luminary Codex**, **Records of Reason**, and **Chronicles of Seekers**.

## 6. The Silent Whisper

Core: silence, uncertainty, hidden truths, patience, and introspection.

Institutional interests:
- preservation;
- contemplation;
- secret archives;
- lost knowledge.

Current scheme:
Some custodians believe dangerous truths should be withheld until society can survive them. Others argue that this has become complicity with elite secrecy.


### Retained depth

The later legacy version centers:
- **The Whisper** — subtle intuition / insight;
- **The Shadow** — the unknown;
- **The Stillness** — restraint and patient perception;
- **Unseen Guides** — sages or teachers rather than necessary deities.

Strong practices / institutions include:
- silence fasts;
- contemplative listening;
- confidential mediation;
- diplomacy;
- protection of endangered knowledge;
- arguments over when secrecy is stewardship and when it becomes complicity.

Preserved provisional material includes the **Seekers of Shadows**, **Echoes of Silentus**, **Codex of Shadows**, **Whisperers' Parables**, and **Glyph Scrolls**.

Do not reduce this to "the spy religion."

## 7. The Harmonious Path

Core: social responsibility, service, restraint, conflict resolution, and balance.

Institutional interests:
- mediation;
- community welfare;
- nonviolent conflict settlement.

Current scheme:
Local Harmonious communities are building parallel mutual-aid and arbitration systems where official institutions have failed, unintentionally creating political power.


### Retained depth

The deity-free legacy core emphasizes:
- **Unity**;
- **Balance**;
- **Self-Control**;
- service and role fulfillment;
- nonviolence;
- reconciliation;
- community responsibility.

Strong institutional material includes:
- reconciliation circles;
- mediation courts;
- community charters;
- education in conflict resolution;
- mutual service;
- nonviolent reform.

Preserved provisional names include **Concordant Councils**, **Harmony Courts**, **Balancekeepers**, **Educators of Compassion**, **Circle of Lanterns**, **Path of Still Waters**, **Harmonizers**, and **Guardians of Temperance**.

The old "No Gods" draft contains copied / inconsistent ritual material, so its exact festival package is not authoritative.

## 8. The Dual Flame

Core: moral integrity comes from understanding and balancing constructive and destructive impulses.

Institutional interests:
- counseling;
- confession;
- mentorship;
- personal discipline.

Current scheme:
Its counselors are seeing similar fear, anger, and radicalization patterns across regions and may recognize that separate crises share a source before political leaders do.


### Retained depth

The strongest surviving version is psychological / moral rather than a literal cosmic war.

Useful legacy material includes:
- **Mirror of Self** reflection;
- dawn / dusk moral review;
- **Circles of Truth**;
- confession and counseling;
- mentorship;
- accountability without pretending destructive impulses do not exist.

Older figures **Thalos, Sereia, Valoron, Nefari, Lysivere** remain available as allegorical or branch-specific figures.

The cleaner later concepts are:
- **Light Flame**;
- **Shadow Flame**;
- **Golden Flame**.

Preserved provisional schools / texts include **Flamekeepers**, **Shadow Walkers**, **Lightbound**, **Flames Eternal**, **Valoron Chronicles**, and **Shadowed Lessons**.

The legacy "Dual Flame (No Gods)" block becomes corrupted by pasted Infinite Compass material after its opening concepts and must not be treated as Dual Flame canon.

## 9. The Infinite Compass

Core: life is journey, direction, exploration, and self-discovery.

Institutional interests:
- pilgrimage;
- maps;
- hospitality;
- travelers;
- road shrines.

Current scheme:
Its pilgrim networks move information across borders faster than many governments expect. The Council, the Villain, spies, and criminals all have reasons to influence these networks.


### Retained depth

The later no-gods version centers:
- **The Compass** — guidance / direction;
- **The Horizon** — possibility / unknown;
- **The Path** — the lived journey itself.

Retain:
- pilgrimage;
- hospitality;
- departure / return customs;
- route knowledge;
- life maps and travel journals;
- map preservation;
- traveler networks carrying news and culture;
- inward as well as physical journeys.

Preserved provisional names include **Navigos, Labyrintus, Infinis, Inventura**, **Wayfarers' Guilds**, **Pathfinders**, **Wanderer's Council**, **Keepers of the Compass / Maps**, **Horizon Walkers**, the **Compass Codex**, **Scroll of Endless Paths**, and **Wayfarer's Chronicles**.

The old event name **Grand Convergence** should not be promoted because The Convergence already has a specific historical meaning.

## Faiths as practical service networks

The legacy religion material repeatedly contains a stronger mechanism than its old supernatural packaging:

> **faiths do ordinary institutional work.**

Depending on tradition, branch, place, wealth, and local history, religious networks may support:

- alms / food distribution;
- lodging for pilgrims or stranded travelers;
- care for sick or injured people;
- burial and mourning assistance;
- mediation;
- record-keeping;
- teaching / literacy;
- protection of travelers;
- rehabilitation / reconciliation;
- disaster relief;
- preservation of local memory.

This should not make every temple a universal welfare office.

Different branches can disagree over whether material service is:

- central to faith;
- secondary to contemplation;
- a dangerous political distraction;
- an obligation best handled by lay members rather than clergy.

That disagreement is useful.

### Lay institutions matter

Legacy notes repeatedly distinguish:

- ordained specialists;
- monastic / contemplative specialists;
- itinerant teachers;
- reform / service movements;
- ordinary lay members.

Preserve that structural diversity.

A charity, mediation circle, traveler hostel, burial society, or neighborhood relief kitchen can be **religiously affiliated without being controlled by the highest clergy**.

This allows faith networks to react quickly to crises even when formal religious leadership is divided.

### Current-story consequence

During the current crisis, faith-linked service networks can notice material changes before governments do because they see:

- more people asking for food;
- more stranded travelers;
- rising burial / injury costs;
- displaced workers;
- families unable to pay lodging;
- sudden demand for mediation.

Those observations become another independent information stream in Port and across the continent.

See [Current Events](../Story/Current-Events.md).

## Faiths are federations more often than command chains

Legacy material repeatedly works better when a faith is treated as a **network of schools, houses, shrines, teachers, charities, lay associations, and local communities** rather than one church with one voice.

A major tradition may contain:

- ordained specialists;
- lay teachers;
- charitable houses;
- pilgrimage networks;
- local confraternities;
- scholarly schools;
- reform movements;
- rural shrines;
- wealthy urban institutions.

These do not need one perfect hierarchy.

A central council, synod, school, or respected lineage may exist without controlling every local branch.

That creates useful disagreement over:

- doctrine;
- money;
- political involvement;
- who may teach;
- who may administer charity;
- what counts as legitimate reform;
- whether a local custom is acceptable adaptation or corruption.

A local branch can therefore oppose the political position associated with its own wider tradition.

This matters especially during current crises.

One branch may call for:

- restraint;

while another calls for:

- intervention;

and a third insists the institution should stay out of politics entirely.

## Pilgrimage and gathering create secondary institutions

Legacy religion notes often pair pilgrimage with:

- markets;
- lodging;
- marriage / kin contact;
- information exchange;
- seasonal work;
- charitable distribution.

That mechanism survives even when old named holy sites do not.

A pilgrimage route can therefore support:

- inns;
- hostels;
- food distribution;
- guides;
- scribes;
- local fairs;
- cross-regional news.

The religious purpose remains primary for participants, but the institution produces economic and information effects around it.

This helps explain why faith networks can move information quickly without functioning as spy organizations.

## Religion and politics

Faiths should not behave like nine political parties.

A member's faith may influence:
- moral language;
- community obligation;
- charitable networks;
- interpretation of events.

But class, region, family, guild, and personal experience also matter.

## Religion and current events

Because these traditions span borders, they are ideal for showing that a crisis in one region can be understood completely differently elsewhere.

Religious networks may also provide the first genuinely trans-regional response to the unfolding political crisis.


## Religion and other forms of belonging

See [Social Fabric](../Social-Fabric.md).

Religious identity is one layer among several. A person can belong to a faith, guild, kingdom, neighborhood, family network and voluntary social movement simultaneously.

The provisional [Audience Movements](../Culture/Audience-Movements.md) model is deliberately **not** treated as a tenth major faith by default. Some participants may understand such a movement religiously while remaining members of one of the traditions above; other authorities may classify the same movement as a fraternity, cult, civic society or nuisance.

This overlap should create local disagreement rather than one universal theological verdict.
