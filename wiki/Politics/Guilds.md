# Guilds

## Status

**Working canon for the pattern; the named guilds are provisional.** What guilds are and want is working canon; the named roster and the armed and clandestine guilds' working core came in as provisional by bt 7274's ruling ([D24](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)). *(Labelled in the [2026-11 review](../Reference/World-Review-2026-11.md#status-labels).)*

## Core role

Guilds are part economic institution, part professional community, part political lobby, part welfare system, and sometimes part coercive cartel.

They matter because pre-modern governments cannot directly administer every profession and service.

### Armed and clandestine organizations

Earlier notes named a Warrior's Guild, an Assassin's Guild and a Spy Guild. They were to be developed as plausible institutions, not game classes, and D24 settled what comes in:

**Ruled 2026-11-04 ([D24](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)), provisional.** The legacy notes ([Volume 08, Part 3](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/08_Power_The_Council_Guilds_Underworld.md#src-guilds-class)) give each a fuller form. What comes in is the working core; the game-class flavor and the costume names stay in the legacy notes.

- **The fighters' guild** (the legacy's Ironclad Warriors' Guild, a working name): trains professional fighters, whose rank comes from real service such as "guarding caravans, dealing with local threats"; rulers and towns hire it to train militias. A sworn oath and yearly tournaments are possible customs.
- **The spy guilds:** "officially independent, thrive on selling information"; "even Council members hire them." Not one body but a loose set of city networks; the legacy's Veiled Eye Society, a confederation of city "Eyes" that also covers theft, is one working name.
- **The Assassins' Guild:** in the legacy notes, kept out of the stories and not developed further.

## What guilds want

No guild wants only "more power."

Typical institutional goals:
- preserve monopoly or standards;
- keep member income stable;
- control apprenticeship;
- prevent outsiders undercutting rates;
- gain legal privileges;
- protect trade secrets;
- influence taxation;
- secure routes;
- obtain contracts;
- maintain member welfare;
- stop rival guild expansion.

## Current schemes

### Metalworking guild factions

Some want controlled production and traditional apprenticeship.

Others want broader output and new methods.

Council-linked investors may fund one faction to restrain another.

### Agricultural guilds/cooperatives

Fighting over:
- seed control;
- storage;
- land consolidation;
- pricing;
- new crop strains.

### Navigator and shipping guilds

Debating:
- convoy systems;
- piracy response;
- Port influence;
- route exclusivity.

### Highridge caravan organizations

Competing over:
- safe-route certification;
- tolls;
- militia protection;
- maps.

### Builders and infrastructure guilds

Seeking long contracts and legal control over standards.

Their delays can become political weapons even when caused by ordinary disputes.

## The named guilds

**Provisional ([D24](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions), 2026-11-04).** bt 7274 ruled that the legacy roster ([Volume 08, Part 3](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/08_Power_The_Council_Guilds_Underworld.md#src-guilds-economics)) comes in as provisional, keeping the functions, ranks and everyday customs that fit the [World Rules](../World-Rules.md). The names are working names. Each guild still answers the [rule](#rule) at the end of this page: leadership, ordinary members, branches, reformers, funding, benefits and abuses. Left out: the Collegium Arcanum (ruled out by [Magic](../Magic.md)), patron gods for guilds (the world has [nine faiths](Religions.md) instead), and game-style ranks.

### The Ironheart Founders' Guild

Metal: smelting, smithing, tools, arms and structural metal, strongest in [Ironcrest](../Regions/Ironcrest.md). It is the guild the [metalworking guild factions](#metalworking-guild-factions) are arguing inside. Apprentice, journeyman and master smith, each reached by a trial piece; masters choose a guildmaster every five years on a masterpiece. Customs: every apprentice's name is cut into a steel pillar in the guild hall, hammers ring three times together at dawn, and the guild's quality seal is strict. The legacy also spells it Ironhart.

### The Seven Seas Mercantile Guild

Trade, shipping, warehousing and merchant credit, strongest in [Port](../Places/Port.md) and the Sunplains ports. Members hold voting shares, and a quorum of the largest elects a High Factor every three years. Customs: a yearly public reading of the guild's accounts, blacklisting for a broken contract or an unexcused default, and a small blessing before a caravan or fleet leaves. It is one side of the [navigator and shipping guilds](#navigator-and-shipping-guilds)' debate.

### The Masoncraft Builders' Guild

Building: roads, bridges, walls, dams and granaries. A Grand Mason chosen by a trial project; regional overseers who keep the standards. Customs: a cornerstone ceremony with keepsakes buried under the first stone, apprentices who must work in three regions before joining, and a small carved sign on every project naming who oversaw it. It is the core of the [builders and infrastructure guilds](#builders-and-infrastructure-guilds).

### The Harvestkeepers Collective

Farming and food supply, seated in [Greenvale](../Regions/Greenvale.md) with local circles wherever land is farmed. A council of elder farmers elects a High Harvestelder. Customs: seasonal seed exchanges, a blessing of shared tools at planting, and a shared almanac of planting practice that is revised as conditions change. It is the largest of the [agricultural guilds and cooperatives](#agricultural-guildscooperatives).

### The Apothic Circle

Medicine: apothecaries, healers, hospitals and travelling clinics, with no magical healing ([Medicine and Healing](../Medicine-and-Healing.md)). Apprentice, journeyman healer, master and circle elder, each by a test that includes an ethical judgment. Its list of approved remedies is one reason a healer can be "unable to obtain supplies".

### The Artisan Fellowship

Fine crafts and arts: tapestry, mosaic, jewelry and decorative work, run from guild houses by a council of masters. Its exports earn real money, and its endorsement is soft power.

### The Bardic Circle

Minstrels and performers, paid by commission for ballads and performances. It overlaps, and sometimes competes, with the ballad keepers of the [Ridge-Roamers](../Culture/Nomads.md).

### The Guards and Sentinels

Watch and guard work: town watches, escorts and guards for hire. Not warfare, which is the fighters' guild's.

### The Ranger and Hunters' Guild

Trackers, wilderness guides and hunters who keep roads and woods safe. The legacy's monster hunting reads here as dangerous or out-of-place animals ([Magic](../Magic.md#everything-else-that-has-been-claimed)).

### The Adventurers' Guild

Hiring halls with a job board and a network of inns for travelling hands: escorts, lost goods, old ruins, local trouble. Ranks are by reputation, not metals. Whether [Wurdren](../Story/Wurdren.md) belongs is part of his past, which stays open.

### Market and records guilds

A merchants' consortium that runs local markets, weights, measures and fair prices, alongside the Seven Seas guild's long-distance trade; and scribe and notary guilds that keep records, translations and legal documents ([Records, Literacy and Authentication](../Records-Literacy-and-Authentication.md)).

Their oldest books are in [the Ledger Vault](../Places/Places-of-Interest.md#the-ledger-vault) (provisional, [D36](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)).

## Guild interaction with the Council

The Council does not "own the guilds."

It influences them through:
- loans;
- contracts;
- access;
- leadership patronage;
- legal favors;
- information;
- insurance;
- selective enforcement.

Guilds sometimes knowingly manipulate Council families in return.

## Guild interaction with the Villain

The Villain exploits legitimate guild grievances.

He may:
- fund reformers;
- expose corruption;
- arrange shortages that make one faction look incompetent;
- create demand for guild security;
- encourage strikes at strategically chosen moments;
- leak real documents mixed with misleading conclusions.

## Guild interaction with Wurdren

Wurdren encounters guilds as people:
- an apprentice denied work;
- a caravan team abandoned after a contract dispute;
- a healer unable to obtain supplies;
- workers threatened by owners;
- owners threatened by a strike.

He sees why the institution exists and why it can become harmful.

## Rule

Every major guild should have:
- leadership;
- ordinary members;
- regional branches;
- internal reformers;
- a funding model;
- benefits members would lose if the guild disappeared;
- abuses that outsiders resent.
