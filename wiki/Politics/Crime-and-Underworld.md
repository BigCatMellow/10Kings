# Crime and the Underworld

## Principle

Crime exists because law, markets, borders, poverty, opportunity, corruption, and demand exist.

Criminal organizations should provide real benefits to some members — protection, work, credit, belonging, dispute resolution — while imposing serious costs on victims and communities.

The named legacy-faction review is preserved in [Underworld Legacy Reconciliation](../Reference/Underworld-Legacy-Reconciliation.md). Names such as **Black Tapestry, Painted Moon, Tallow Runners, Ebony Doves, Broken Sigil, Indigo Vow** and others remain available as provisional seeds rather than disappearing with the old regional model.

## Scale

The world should contain multiple levels:

### Street and neighborhood groups

Control:
- a few blocks;
- a dock;
- a market;
- a mining camp;
- a neighborhood.

Activities:
- theft;
- protection;
- gambling;
- informal lending;
- fencing.

### Regional gangs

Operate across:
- roads;
- river systems;
- forest routes;
- mining districts;
- coastal areas.

### Trade-based syndicates

Specialize in:
- smuggling;
- forged documents;
- contraband;
- stolen goods;
- illegal labor recruitment;
- bribery.

### Family-style criminal networks

Long-lived kin/business organizations similar in structural logic to mafias and other patronage-based syndicates.

They may offer:
- loans;
- protection;
- arbitration;
- jobs;
- political access.

In exchange they demand loyalty, money, silence, and sometimes violence.

### Cross-regional criminal alliances

Several independent groups can cooperate around shared interests without becoming one super-organization.

Examples:
- a Port syndicate finances;
- an Underpass-linked group moves goods;
- a High Roads gang supplies documents;
- a North Coast crew moves cargo by sea.

## Criminal organizations need an economy

A durable criminal organization needs identifiable:
- customers;
- revenue;
- suppliers;
- storage;
- transport;
- bribery / corruption costs;
- enforcement;
- information;
- replacement members.

Smuggling is a logistics problem before it is a costume or faction identity.

Useful mechanisms include:
- false manifests;
- forged seals;
- hidden cargo;
- legitimate mixed loads;
- corrupt inspectors;
- transshipment;
- warehouse substitution;
- repeated small loads;
- cross-border handoffs.

Ordinary businesses can provide fronts because they already have reasons to travel, store goods, enter properties or handle paperwork.

## Cell networks

Cross-regional crime does not require one central syndicate.

A useful structure is:

```text
shared name / contacts
+ semi-independent cells
+ recurring brokers / fences
→ network with incomplete central control
```

Cells may defect, disagree, imitate one another or use a famous name without permission.

## Information crime

Because the wider economy depends on records, certification and contracts, valuable criminal goods can include:
- forged credentials;
- copied seals;
- false manifests;
- blackmail;
- stolen technical knowledge;
- route intelligence;
- compromised records.

The legacy **Painted Moon** concept is preserved because pigments, inks, marks and art forgery create a plausible specialist route into document crime.

## Opportunity patterns, not regional personalities

### Stone Hills

Common opportunities:
- ore theft;
- illegal claims;
- tool/weapon diversion;
- labor intimidation;
- debt.

### North Coast

- piracy;
- smuggling;
- stolen cargo;
- illegal fishing;
- harbor protection.

### Low Rivers

- grain theft;
- land fraud;
- illegal seed markets;
- livestock theft;
- debt enforcement.

### High Roads

- caravan robbery;
- route extortion;
- document fraud;
- information brokerage.

### Longwood

- illegal timber;
- rare plant trade;
- poaching;
- hidden-route smuggling.

### Old Cities

- canal corruption;
- smuggling;
- merchant fraud;
- political bribery;
- protection around ports and markets.

### Port

All of the above can converge.

These lists describe **opportunities created by local economies**, not innate criminal personalities. A mining district outside Stone Hills can produce ore theft; a fishing harbor outside North Coast can produce illegal fishing; a forest outside Longwood can produce timber crime.

## Preserved legacy mechanisms

Several older named concepts are worth retaining as provisional seeds:

- **Black Tapestry** — loose smuggling / information cells, forged records and cross-route contacts;
- **Painted Moon** — pigments, inks, art forgery, copied marks and coded images;
- **Tallow Runners** — a mundane mobile trade used as a contraband front;
- **Ebony Doves** — information brokers / mediators whose income benefits from disputes continuing;
- **Broken Sigil / Obsidian League** — relic forgery and trafficking if the later artifact / magic baseline supports it;
- **Indigo Vow** — ideologically motivated sabotage whose classification as crime, vigilantism, cult or political movement may vary.

Exact names, leaders, symbols and regional branches remain provisional.

## Underworld and the main conflict

The Council sometimes tolerates criminal networks because they provide deniable services or keep unofficial markets functioning.

The Villain can exploit those same networks but cannot simply command them. Criminal leaders require profit, protection, revenge, or leverage.

Wurdren is likely to encounter criminals at the human level, where a "gang" may simultaneously be:
- extorting shopkeepers;
- feeding members' families;
- protecting a neighborhood from worse outsiders;
- trafficking stolen goods;
- punishing people who refuse them.

That ambiguity should never erase victims.
