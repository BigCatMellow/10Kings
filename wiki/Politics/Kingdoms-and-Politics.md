This page owns **the rule layer**: who governs, region by region ([World Structure](../Reference/Reviews/World-Structure-2026-11.md#1-region-does-three-jobs-and-no-page-does-the-job-of-rule), D57). [Who rules, region by region](#who-rules-region-by-region) gathers what the wiki already says about each ruler, seat and force. It was gathered from the wiki ([D59](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)), then filled from the legacy notes, provisionally, where they fit ([D61](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)). Nothing in it is invented; what neither source settles is marked open.

## Principle

The six regions should not necessarily share identical political systems.

"Region" is a cultural-geographic term. A region can contain a kingdom, confederation, city-states, autonomous districts, chartered towns, religious lands, and old privileges. How peoples and rule sit on the ground, and how land is held across the lines: [Peoples and Land](../Peoples-and-Land.md).

## Who rules, region by region

**The regions have no "kings" to match the title** (bt 7274, [D60](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)): "if it doesn't fit the world that's been built, maybe we don't need kings and we just call it the 6 kings referring to the council". The title's kings are the six houses of the [Council](Economic-Council.md#the-six-kings). The regions keep the rule the world already gives them, which the legacy notes fill in ([D61](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions), below). This replaces D58's twelve.

**How this section was built** ([D59](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)): bt 7274 asked to "make sure of the existing world and not invent things". Every line below is gathered from a page that already says it, and cited. Where the wiki says nothing, the entry says **open**. No ruler, title or seat has been named that wasn't named before.

**What the lore adds** ([D61](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)): bt 7274 asked to "let the established lore answer" these questions, and to bring him only what it can't. The D59 pass read only the wiki, and **it missed the rulers the legacy notes already name** ([Volume 02](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/02_Regions_Kingdoms.md), the "World" section of each region). There, five regions have a single ruler, each chosen a different way, and Sunplains has a rotating chair. They come in below as **provisional**, marked *from the lore*, where they fit what the wiki says. Where the lore disagrees with the wiki or with itself, the entry stays open and the question goes to bt 7274. Off-tech or undeniable-magic details in the same profiles stay out ([World Rules](../World-Rules.md), [Magic](../Magic.md)). The legacy capitals stay declined ([World Review](../Reference/World-Review-2026-11.md)), so the seats are still the meeting places the wiki has.

**Rulers, not kings.** Before D61 only Ironcrest's page had a crown ("crown/state institutions"). The lore crowns four more regions, each in its own way (by the clans, by blood confirmed by farmers, by blood alone, by blood tested by trial), and gives Sunplains a chair. None of them is a "king" in the title's sense (D60). Each entry gives the region's **ruler** in the form the region has.

### Northwind

- **Form.** "Strong clan, harbor, and local rights even under larger regional leadership"; "maritime law may be partially separate from inland law" ([tendencies](#northwind-1)). Before the Convergence: "coastal clans; fishing confederacies; harbor towns; island rulers; raiding bands; inland chiefs" ([Northwind](../Regions/Northwind.md#historical-identity)).
- **Where power meets.**
  - **Mootstead**, "the old clan seat in the sheltered inland valleys, where the lineages meet in moot" ([Gazetteer](../Places/Gazetteer.md#northwind)).
  - **The Moot Isle**, where "clans meet there to settle fishing banks and winter anchorages", and "whoever calls the moot has to bring the food for everyone" ([Places of Interest](../Places/Places-of-Interest.md#the-moot-isle)).
- **The ruler, from the lore** (provisional, D61). **Queen Ingrid Stormvale.** Northwind "eschews strict bloodline inheritance": leading families and clan chiefs choose the ruler in a "Rite of the Gales", and a candidate "must secure the favor of at least two-thirds of the clan leaders" ([Volume 02](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/02_Regions_Kingdoms.md#src-world-northwind)). That is the wiki's "larger regional leadership" over strong clan rights. She is clan-born, a navigator from "a remote fishing island". *Still open:* whether one crown covers both halves of Northwind's country across the North Gut, which the map draft places by bt 7274's layout (D40). The lore has one Northwind and one ruler.
- **Force.** Marines and coastal fighters ([Weapons](../Culture/Weapons-and-Elite-Troops.md#northwind)). Northwind's politics turn on "convoy protection; privateering/piracy; clan autonomy" ([Northwind](../Regions/Northwind.md#politics)).
- **Who decides war: open.** *Lean:* not the queen without the clan leaders whose favour made her.

### Ironcrest

- **Form.** "Likely relatively consolidated compared with its pre-Convergence past, but power is divided among: crown/state institutions; mine owners; guilds; town governments; labor organizations; old landed families" ([tendencies](#ironcrest-1)). Before the Convergence, "smith-chiefs, mine holders, fortified towns, valley rulers, merchant families, and warbands" ([Ironcrest](../Regions/Ironcrest.md#historical-identity)).
- **Where power meets.** No seat is named. Hammerford is "a guild city" ([Gazetteer](../Places/Gazetteer.md#ironcrest)), not a capital. **Open.**
- **The ruler.** "A ruler can command soldiers but cannot simply order the economy to function" ([Ironcrest](../Regions/Ironcrest.md#politics)). **From the lore** (provisional, D61): **King Gareth Blackbarrow.** The crown passes "through the Blackbarrow line, a dynasty famed for centuries for controlling the iron mines and forging techniques", and a new monarch takes a "symbolic 'brand'" of their sigil in "the Great Forge" ([Volume 02](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/02_Regions_Kingdoms.md#src-world-ironcrest)). He grew up "forging alongside common smiths", and he "resents any attempt to limit Ironcrest's growth", which is the pressure the Council watches. The same profile's "advanced engineering" and the section's steam foundries stay out (World Rules).
- **Force.** "Disciplined heavy infantry or engineering troops" ([Weapons](../Culture/Weapons-and-Elite-Troops.md#ironcrest)). Among Ironcrest's current pressures: "Council efforts to prevent Ironcrest from converting economic strength into unchecked military power" ([Ironcrest](../Regions/Ironcrest.md#current-pressures)).
- **Who decides war.** The ruler commands soldiers; the money is shared with mine owners and guilds (above).
- **Across the lines.** "The ironcrest people would have some farm land" ([D43](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)).

### Greenvale

- **Form.** "Could combine: regional monarchy or central authority; estate power; village councils; irrigation associations; cooperatives; market towns" ([tendencies](#greenvale-1)). Its politics turn on "estate versus cooperative power" ([Greenvale](../Regions/Greenvale.md#politics)). Before the Convergence: "estate rulers … irrigation leagues; seed-sharing alliances"; its rulers "raided neighbors, annexed healthier land, fought internally, and sometimes invited foreign military support" ([Greenvale](../Regions/Greenvale.md#historical-identity)).
- **Where power meets.** No seat is named. **Open.**
- **The ruler, from the lore** (provisional, D61). **King Roland Fairweather.** Greenvale is crowned. The line "is typically passed from parent to child, but a Farmers' Assembly (representing major agrarian guilds and local leaders) must confirm the next monarch", and the coronation is "a symbolic planting of heirloom seeds" ([Volume 02](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/02_Regions_Kingdoms.md#src-world-greenvale)). That is the wiki's "regional monarchy" beside its cooperatives. He works "to improve irrigation, protect smaller farms from exploitation", which is the wiki's estate-versus-cooperative politics.
- **Force.** "Mobile local defense, scouts, or disciplined levy cadres whose strength is logistics and local terrain" ([Weapons](../Culture/Weapons-and-Elite-Troops.md#greenvale)).
- **Who decides war: open.** *Lean:* the king, with the Farmers' Assembly that confirms him.

### Highridge Plateau

- **Form.** "Could be a federation/confederation of: cities; route districts; caravan houses; herding territories; old pass communities. Its political legitimacy may come from negotiated compacts rather than a single dynasty" ([tendencies](#highridge)).
  - Power is "distributed among route authorities; old families; merchant houses; caravan guilds; urban councils; religious institutions; herding communities" ([Highridge](../Regions/Highridge-Plateau.md#politics)).
  - Before the Convergence, "a ruler could control a pass but not the valley below it" ([Highridge](../Regions/Highridge-Plateau.md#historical-identity)).
- **Where power meets.**
  - **Lowmeet's Congress Hall**, "said to be where the Convergence was negotiated", though that is one town's claim ([Places of Interest](../Places/Places-of-Interest.md#the-congress-hall)).
  - **Kingshead**, the largest market city (lean, D45), named for [the Head in the Square](../Places/Places-of-Interest.md#the-head-in-the-square), the missing Sitting King's head.
  - Highridge "is the strongest candidate for the principal congress" of the Convergence ([The Convergence](../History/The-Convergence.md#negotiation)).
- **The ruler, from the lore** (provisional, D61, with a strain). **Queen Nalia Skylance**, holding the "Crown of the Passes". Rule "is semi-hereditary but tested by a 'Trial of the Winds,' a series of diplomatic, intellectual, and physical challenges", and a "Council of Sages" of "scholars, merchants, and travelers" advises her ([Volume 02](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/02_Regions_Kingdoms.md#src-world-highridge-plateau)). She was once "a caravan translator". *The strain:* the wiki gives Highridge legitimacy "from negotiated compacts rather than a single dynasty". A crown that has to pass a trial isn't a single dynasty, but it isn't a compact either. It comes in provisionally, and the question goes to bt 7274.
- **Force.** "Route guards and high-altitude scouts" ([Weapons](../Culture/Weapons-and-Elite-Troops.md#highridge)). [Seven Hill](../Places/Seven-Hill.md) is a fortress "emptied when Highridge's toll warfare gave way to negotiated" passage.
- **Who decides war: open.** It is an "arbitration environment" ([Law 20](../Law-and-Jurisdiction.md#20-highridge-plateau-is-an-arbitration-environment)).

### Deepwood

- **Form.** "Likely the least centralized. Possible layers: local councils; forest wardens; regional assemblies; sacred jurisdictions; market towns; hereditary authorities in some districts" ([tendencies](#deepwood-1)). Before the Convergence: "village alliances; clan territories; ranger/warden networks … forest-edge petty rulers"; its groups "sometimes united against these incursions, sometimes fought each other" ([Deepwood](../Regions/Deepwood.md#historical-identity)).
- **Where power meets.** **Rookhallow**, "Deepwood's old meeting town" ([Gazetteer](../Places/Gazetteer.md#deepwood)).
- **The ruler: open, because the lore disagrees with itself** (D61). Volume 02 has two Deepwoods ([Volume 02](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/02_Regions_Kingdoms.md#src-world-deepwood)):
  - **King Elden Mossglade**, whose line "must be blessed by leading shamans and an age-old living tree in a revered grove";
  - an **Elder Assembly** of "the heads of the most influential guilds", under **Elder Rowan Leafwhisper**, seated at Glenshade.

  The wiki's "least centralized" region fits the Assembly better. But the Assembly's guilds include a "Mystic Guild" whose magic isn't deniable ([Magic](../Magic.md)), and Glenshade would be a legacy capital. *Lean:* an assembly of guild and district heads, meeting at Rookhallow, and no king. *Note:* Mossglade is already the surname of [Naruin Mossglade](../Story/Villains-Dominoes.md#naruin-mossglade--deepwood), a senior warden and domino figure. If the king stands, that shared name needs a ruling.
- **Force.** "Rangers/wardens who know terrain, tracking, concealment, river crossings, and coordinated small-unit fighting" ([Weapons](../Culture/Weapons-and-Elite-Troops.md#deepwood)).
- **Who decides war: open.**

### Sunplains

- **Form.** "Most explicitly plural: city-states; leagues; estates; ports; rural territories. The Convergence may recognize the region externally even though internally it contains several sovereign or semi-sovereign governments" ([tendencies](#sunplains-1)).
  - "A permanent Sunplains union would be powerful enough to worry both neighbors and the Council" ([Sunplains](../Regions/Sunplains.md#politics)).
  - Before the Convergence: "city-states; estate regions; port towns; irrigation leagues; noble houses; merchant dynasties; rural client territories" ([Sunplains](../Regions/Sunplains.md#historical-identity)).
- **Where power meets.** The city-states the wiki names are:
  - **Vasselin** ([The Ladder Rules](../Sunday-Morning/Stories/The-Ladder-Rules.md));
  - **Accord**, home of the Argument House;
  - **Corvane**, "an old city-state of the coast";
  - and **Three Spouts**, "the meeting point of three city-states" ([Gazetteer](../Places/Gazetteer.md#sunplains), [the Boundary Fountain](../Places/Places-of-Interest.md#the-boundary-fountain)).

  "How many independent Sunplains city-states remain?" is **open** ([Open Questions](../Open-Questions.md#political-structure)).
- **The ruler, from the lore** (provisional, D61). **No monarch, but a chair.** The Sunplain Confederation's leadership "rotates among key city-state heads". Its presiding head, the "First Chair", is "basically the de facto spokesperson for the confederation", and the post "changes every few years". An "Annual Forum" of city-state envoys deliberates "policy, trade deals, maritime defense" ([Volume 02](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/02_Regions_Kingdoms.md#src-world-sunplains)). The First Chair is who answers for the region "externally". The present one is **High Councilor Aurelia Mirabelle**, who defends "city-state sovereignty". Her city, Coralbay, and the legacy's Oliveport and Bayrose aren't on the Gazetteer; whether they join Vasselin, Accord and Corvane is open.
- **Force.** "City-state guards, cavalry where terrain and horse culture support it, professional household troops, or disciplined spear formations" ([Weapons](../Culture/Weapons-and-Elite-Troops.md#sunplains)).
- **Who decides war.** Each city-state, by the plural model. The Annual Forum deliberates "maritime defense" (lore); how far the cities combine is **open**.
- **Across the lines.** "The sunplains people would have some mining lands they might hire people or lease out to" ([D43](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)).

### Port, which has no ruler of its own

- **Form.** Not a region and not a kingdom. "A citizen of Port, which is to be a citizen of nowhere" ([D32](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)).
  - Its charter "should therefore involve: local citizens; merchant interests; guild influence; treaty guarantees; foreign enclaves; limits on kingdom troops inside the city; a locally accountable watch or guard" ([Port](../Places/Port.md#neutrality)).
  - "Foreign militaries may protect outer sea lanes while being restricted inside Port itself" ([Port](../Places/Port.md#governance-and-protection)).
- **Who stands behind it, from the lore** (provisional, D61): all of the kingdoms. Port is "protected and maintained by all the kingdoms"; each sends representatives to its Coalition of Stewards and contributes to the Wardens of Port and a Joint Fleet ([Volume 02](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/02_Regions_Kingdoms.md#src-world-port), [Port](../Places/Port.md#governance-from-the-lore-d61)). These are the Villain's "guarantor nations" ([Villain](../Story/Villain.md#relationship-to-the-council)). The Council's Routes house, Veynor, is Port's ([Economic Council](Economic-Council.md#the-six-houses)).
- **Force.** "Harbor watch; customs officers; fire brigades; marine patrols; riot/public-order units" ([Weapons](../Culture/Weapons-and-Elite-Troops.md#port)).
- **What the Port-born have** (lore, D61): a Municipal Council of "influential locals" for the day-to-day, and no steward of their own ([Port](../Places/Port.md#what-the-port-born-have-d61)).

### Armies, in general

*Lean*, from the [2026-11 review](../Reference/World-Review-2026-11.md#insights) ([Weapons](../Culture/Weapons-and-Elite-Troops.md#elite-troop-rule)): "polities keep small household and garrison forces", and the [fighters' guild](Guilds.md#armed-and-clandestine-organizations), which "rulers and towns hire … to train militias" (D24), supplies most specialists for hire. The regional fighting traditions are "traditions of skill, not armies".

### The frame they all sit in

The [Convergence](../History/The-Convergence.md) is the frame for all six:
- It "recognized, regularized, and froze many compromises".
- Its rulers "wanted recognition of claims they could actually hold".
- It fixed borders "by rivers; ridges; roads; settlements; watersheds; named stones; old forts", which "creates later ambiguity". The [Treaty Line](../Places/Places-of-Interest.md#the-treaty-stones) is where it was marked.
- It formalized Port's neutrality, and it set agreed arbitration, though its "exact institutions remain open" ([Law](../Law-and-Jurisdiction.md#treaty--convergence-authority)).
- It was signed about fifty years ago, in Year 0 of the record's count ([When](../History/The-Convergence.md#when-d61), D61, provisional). Its signatories are **open**.

### What this shows

- **Five regions have a single ruler in the lore, and Sunplains has a chair** (D61). Each is chosen differently: by the clans (Northwind), by blood confirmed by farmers (Greenvale), by blood (Ironcrest), by blood tested by trial (Highridge). Deepwood's is open.
- **Every ruler but Ironcrest's has a body beside them:** the clan leaders, the Farmers' Assembly, the Council of Sages, the Annual Forum. *Reading (lean):* none of those rulers can take a region to war alone. Ironcrest's king can command soldiers, though not the money.
- **No region has a capital on the wiki.** The legacy capitals stay declined, so the seats are still meeting places, not palaces: Northwind's moot, Deepwood's meeting town, Highridge's congress claim.
- **This bears on D60.** D60's reason was "if it doesn't fit the world that's been built". The built world has five monarchs, close to bt 7274's own memory of "the 5 kings of the regions". D60 stands until he says otherwise ([below](#open-for-bt-7274)).

## Political behavior

Every government responds to:
- domestic legitimacy;
- old borders;
- guild pressure;
- food;
- credit;
- military readiness;
- religion;
- public rumor;
- elite family interests.

This is why the Villain can push states toward conflict even when the Council wants peace: rulers cannot always follow Council preferences without appearing weak, corrupt, or disloyal to their own populations.

## How war can begin against Council wishes

A plausible escalation chain:

1. a real grievance exists;
2. evidence appears to confirm the rival caused it;
3. local politics punish leaders who seem passive;
4. mobilization begins as deterrence;
5. mobilization is interpreted as preparation for attack;
6. border forces act without full central control;
7. deaths create public demand for retaliation;
8. Council de-escalation efforts appear suspicious;
9. leaders fear losing legitimacy if they compromise;
10. limited conflict becomes self-sustaining.

The Villain's genius is making each step locally rational.
