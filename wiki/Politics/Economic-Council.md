# The Economic Council

## Core concept

The Council is a secret or semi-secret association of **wealthy families whose power crosses regional borders**.

They do not represent Western Uplands, River Country, North Country, or any other kingdom.

They control economic systems on which *all* kingdoms depend.

The exact number of seats can still change. The current model uses six domains because each represents a distinct kind of leverage.

## 1. Food and Biological Supply

Working title: **Harvest / Provision Seat**

Controls influence over:
- grain storage;
- seed networks;
- orchard stock;
- livestock movement;
- emergency reserves;
- large agricultural contracts;
- food transport coordination.

Power does not mean owning every farm.

The family gains leverage by controlling bottlenecks:
- storage warehouses;
- seed credit;
- bulk contracts;
- mills;
- emergency reserves;
- financing for large estates/co-ops.

## 2. Materials and Production

Working title: **Materials / Metal Seat**

Influence over:
- mines;
- ore contracts;
- timber/fuel supply;
- large foundries;
- strategic materials;
- weapon-grade metal;
- construction metal.

Its power spans Western Uplands mines, Forest Country timber/fuel disputes, High Country demand, North Country ship fittings, and South Country construction.

## 3. Routes and Transport

Working title: **Routes / Navigation Seat**

Influence over:
- maritime shipping;
- caravan networks;
- pass access;
- major roads;
- warehouses;
- Tunnels connections;
- shipping schedules;
- convoy contracts.

It can change an economy by making a route expensive rather than formally banning trade.

## 4. Infrastructure

Working title: **Works / Infrastructure Seat**

Influence over:
- roads;
- bridges;
- canals;
- irrigation;
- ports;
- fortifications;
- large public works;
- repair contracts.

Infrastructure decisions have long tails. A bridge not repaired can redirect trade for decades.

## 5. Finance and Exchange

Working title: **Treasury / Commerce Seat**

Influence over:
- credit;
- debt;
- banking houses;
- currency exchange;
- large loans;
- merchant insurance-like arrangements;
- investment syndicates;
- emergency financing.

This may be the Council's most quietly powerful seat because every other domain sometimes needs capital.

## 6. Knowledge and Information

Working title: **Lore / Intelligence Seat**

Influence over:
- commercial records;
- maps;
- guild secrets;
- technical knowledge;
- scholars and scribes;
- market reports;
- intelligence networks;
- blackmail;
- communication.

It should not literally control all knowledge.

Its strength is knowing who knows what, buying access, delaying information, and deciding which innovations receive funding or distribution.

## How the Council governs without governing

The Council rarely says: "Do this because we command you."

Instead it creates choice architecture:

A kingdom may technically be free to refuse a treaty, but then:
- credit becomes scarce;
- grain contracts disappear;
- insurers raise rates;
- caravans choose another route;
- bridge repairs are delayed;
- a rival gains better terms.

No single action proves conspiracy.

## Why rulers tolerate it

Many rulers do not know the whole structure.

Those who do may still tolerate it because the Council:
- resolves shortages;
- provides emergency loans;
- coordinates trade;
- prevents financial panics;
- quietly restrains rivals;
- helped preserve the post-Convergence order.

## Internal conflict

The Council is not unified.

Potential fault lines:
- finance wants repayment while food wants famine relief;
- routes wants open trade while materials wants strategic embargo;
- infrastructure wants long investment while short-term political pressure demands cuts;
- knowledge may know a scheme is failing before others admit it.

Each family also has heirs, factions, clients, and private ambitions.

## The Council's moral problem

The Council may be correct that its coordination has prevented wars.

The story's question is whether **preventing catastrophe grants a private group the right to decide everyone else's future**.

## Relationship to the Villain

The Villain attacks dependencies and perceptions.

He wants kingdoms to make moves the Council cannot openly stop without exposing how much power it actually holds.

His goal is not merely to defeat six families. It is to make their system **unable to maintain equilibrium**.

## Relationship to Wurdren

Wurdren repeatedly fixes consequences the Council treats as acceptable losses.

This can accidentally help the Council in the short term while morally undermining its worldview in the long term.
