# The Economic Council

## Status

**Working canon.** The Council, its six seats and six houses are working canon (bt 7274, 2026-09-29); named members and domain titles are provisional, and so are the readings and leans marked on this page. *(Labelled in the [2026-11 review](../Reference/World-Review-2026-11.md#status-labels).)*

## Core concept

The Council is a secret or semi-secret association of **wealthy families whose power crosses regional borders**.

They do not represent Ironcrest, Greenvale, Northwind, or any other kingdom.

They control economic systems on which *all* kingdoms depend.

**Six seats (Working canon).** The author settled on six seats on 2026-09-29, each held by one house. The domain titles below are still working titles.

## The six kings

**The title's kings are the Council's six houses** (bt 7274, 2026-11-26, [D60](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)): "we just call it the 6 kings referring to the council". They rule more than any crown does, and nobody crowned them.

*Lean:* the phrase stays outside the world. It's the title, never what people in the world call the Council. D21 (the Council never acts as a named body on the page) and D28 (the Villain doesn't know it exists) both need nobody in the world to have a name for it. A reader who wonders who the six kings are is doing what the stories ask: putting it together alone (D2).

## The six houses

**Working canon** (the author, 2026-09-29): the house names and home regions come from the project's earlier notes, with House Drathain moved from the old Labor domain to the Materials seat. Each house comes *from* a region, but that is origin, not representation: the Council still speaks for no kingdom, and every house's power crosses borders. The named members are earlier-note figures, **Provisional**.

**Why Drathain holds Materials.** In the earlier notes House Drathain held Labor: guild alliances and the organizing of large workforces, rooted in Sunplains' city-states. The current model has no Labor seat, and Materials already reaches "Sunplains construction". Drathain's old leverage over crews and building became leverage over what they build with: mines, timber, fuel and construction metal. It also means the house that controls Ironcrest's ore is not an Ironcrest house, which fits a Council that represents no kingdom.

Still open: how seats are inherited, selected, bought or contested; how old the Council is relative to the Convergence; which parts of its existence are rumor and which are known to rulers; the domains' final titles.

**What the table implies** (provisional readings, [2026-11 review](../Reference/World-Review-2026-11.md#insights)):

- **Northwind has no house.** The maritime Routes seat is held by a Port house, though Northwind has the ships, sailors and harbors, and is sensitive to outsiders buying control of its harbors. One remembered tradition has Northwind controlling Port until coalition pressure forced it out ([Contested Memory](../History/Contested-Memory.md#the-port-seizure)). Lean: deliberate, and a live grievance the Villain can draw on, without making Northwind his homeland.
- **There is no Labor seat, and labor is the Villain's best lever.** No house reads strikes as its own domain ([Guilds](Guilds.md#guild-interaction-with-the-villain): he "encourages strikes at strategically chosen moments"), so strikes are where the Council misreads first.
- **The carrier is split from the content.** Veynor (Routes) holds the ships and caravans that carry mail; Rhaelen (Lore) holds communication, scribes and market reports. Message houses sit where the two overlap, a built-in friction.
- **Treasury sits away from both finance hubs.** Luthain is an Ironcrest house, while finance concentrates at Port and Highridge. Lean: its power is lending backed by metal and Ironcrest's skill with contracts and weights, which sets up a Luthain–Drathain fault line if coin is metal.
- **Greenvale distrusts exactly what its own house does.** The Seven-year Blight left "suspicion of concentrated control over seed and grain", and House Marielle controls seed networks and reserves. If Marielle's role surfaced in Greenvale it would read as betrayal from inside: a strong late-season revelation.

## 1. Food and Biological Supply

Held by **House Marielle** (see [the six houses](#the-six-houses)).

Working title: **Harvest / Provision Seat**

Controls influence over:
- grain storage;
- seed networks;
- orchard stock;
- livestock movement;
- emergency reserves;
- large agricultural contracts;
- food transport coordination.

Power does not mean owning every farm.

The family gains leverage by controlling bottlenecks:
- storage warehouses;
- seed credit;
- bulk contracts;
- mills;
- emergency reserves;
- financing for large estates/co-ops.

## 2. Materials and Production

Held by **House Drathain** (see [the six houses](#the-six-houses)).

Working title: **Materials / Metal Seat**

Influence over:
- mines;
- ore contracts;
- timber/fuel supply;
- large foundries;
- strategic materials;
- weapon-grade metal;
- construction metal.

Its power spans Ironcrest mines, Deepwood timber/fuel disputes, Highridge demand, Northwind ship fittings, and Sunplains construction.

## 3. Routes and Transport

Held by **House Veynor** (see [the six houses](#the-six-houses)).

Working title: **Routes / Navigation Seat**

Influence over:
- maritime shipping;
- caravan networks;
- pass access;
- major roads;
- warehouses;
- Underpass connections;
- shipping schedules;
- convoy contracts.

It can change an economy by making a route expensive rather than formally banning trade.

## 4. Infrastructure

Held by **House Torradon** (see [the six houses](#the-six-houses)).

Working title: **Works / Infrastructure Seat**

Influence over:
- roads;
- bridges;
- canals;
- irrigation;
- ports;
- fortifications;
- large public works;
- repair contracts.

Infrastructure decisions have long tails. A bridge not repaired can redirect trade for decades.

## 5. Finance and Exchange

Held by **House Luthain** (see [the six houses](#the-six-houses)).

Working title: **Treasury / Commerce Seat**

Influence over:
- credit;
- debt;
- banking houses;
- currency exchange;
- large loans;
- merchant insurance-like arrangements;
- investment syndicates;
- emergency financing.

This may be the Council's most quietly powerful seat because every other domain sometimes needs capital.

## 6. Knowledge and Information

Held by **House Rhaelen** (see [the six houses](#the-six-houses)).

Working title: **Lore / Intelligence Seat**

Influence over:
- commercial records;
- maps;
- guild secrets;
- technical knowledge;
- scholars and scribes;
- market reports;
- intelligence networks;
- blackmail;
- communication.

It should not literally control all knowledge.

Its strength is knowing who knows what, buying access, delaying information, and deciding which innovations receive funding or distribution.

## How the Council governs without governing

The Council rarely says: "Do this because we command you."

Instead it creates choice architecture:

A kingdom may technically be free to refuse a treaty, but then:
- credit becomes scarce;
- grain contracts disappear;
- insurers raise rates;
- caravans choose another route;
- bridge repairs are delayed;
- a rival gains better terms.

No single action proves conspiracy.

## What the ground sees (D61)

The stories show only the ground ([D21](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)), so this is what the Council leaves there. *Provisional*, from [Volume 08](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/08_Power_The_Council_Guilds_Underworld.md#src-the-council-stress-test), brought in by [D61](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions) (item 3 on the [legacy backlog](../Reference/README.md#legacy-reconciliation-backlog)):

- **Company names on contracts.** "Official documents rarely reference them by name; they rely on codewords or shell organizations to sign contracts." Each house keeps several fronts. The lore's examples are "Embercore Metals" and "Ironshore Trading Co." for metal, and "Silver Compass Logistics" and "Seven Seas Carriers" for routes. "These shell outfits keep the true Council involvement concealed from the average noble or merchant."
- **People who don't stay.** "The Council rarely uses the same agents in the same region for too long", so no one can pick out a face that keeps coming back. Foremen and master smiths placed in the war-forges report what they see.
- **Hints, not orders.** The Council "rarely issues direct orders. Instead, they 'suggest' or 'hint,' letting local powers believe it's their own initiative".
- **Delays with ordinary explanations.** Its moves stay "at a level that can be explained away by normal trade disruptions, local bandits, or resource depletion": goods "lost in transit", "mysterious shipping delays", a steel shipment quietly cut.
- **Help that comes fast, with strings.** In a crisis, "aid packages" arrive with "minimal conditions attached—only to find those conditions come with long-term strings".
- **Meetings in Port,** "behind innocuous 'trade consortium' facades", on rotating schedules.

**What a reader can use** (reviser's reading, a lean, for the [plan check](../Reference/Reviews/Story-Plan-Logic-2026-11.md#questions-for-bt-7274)'s question on the second hand): the lore rotates the people so that no face repeats, so the reader's signature for the steadying hand can't be a person. It can be **one front's name on contracts in two regions**, or **help that arrives before anyone asked, with a condition**. Which, and the name, are for bt 7274.

Left out: "magical seals" and "ephemeral illusions", "magical R&D", secret weapon designs, and the source's Dominion names (World Rules, [Magic](../Magic.md), the six houses of 2026-09-29).

## Why rulers tolerate it

Many rulers do not know the whole structure.

Those who do may still tolerate it because the Council:
- resolves shortages;
- provides emergency loans;
- coordinates trade;
- prevents financial panics;
- quietly restrains rivals;
- helped preserve the post-Convergence order.

## Internal conflict

The Council is not unified.

Potential fault lines:
- finance wants repayment while food wants famine relief;
- routes wants open trade while materials wants strategic embargo;
- infrastructure wants long investment while short-term political pressure demands cuts;
- knowledge may know a scheme is failing before others admit it.

Each family also has heirs, factions, clients, and private ambitions.

**An openness faction** (lean, provisional; from the legacy notes, [Volume 08](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/08_Power_The_Council_Guilds_Underworld.md#src-the-council)): the Council was founded before the Convergence, out of idealism and more openly, and the guilds' and kings' suspicion drove it into the shadows. Some members now want to return to partial openness. If this stands, the [ending](../Story/Main-Conflict.md#possible-resolution-model), where the Council loses its secrecy, is partly a return rather than only a defeat.

**How rulers know.** The legacy proposes that ruling families pass down private records of their dealings with the Council, so what an heir knows depends on what a grandparent wrote down. Provisional.

## The Council's moral problem

The Council may be correct that its coordination has prevented wars.

The story's question is whether **preventing catastrophe grants a private group the right to decide everyone else's future**.

## Relationship to the Villain

The Villain attacks dependencies and perceptions.

He wants kingdoms to make moves the Council cannot openly stop without exposing how much power it actually holds. He doesn't know the Council exists ([D28](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)); he's aiming at the nations that took Port, and the strain lands on the Council anyway.

His goal is not merely to defeat six families. It is to make their system **unable to maintain equilibrium**.

## Relationship to Wurdren

Wurdren repeatedly fixes consequences the Council treats as acceptable losses.

This can accidentally help the Council in the short term while morally undermining its worldview in the long term.
