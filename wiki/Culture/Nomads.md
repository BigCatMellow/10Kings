# Nomads and Itinerant Peoples

## Status

**Working area, not yet fixed canon.** One idea the project specifically wants to explore is a mobile people with significant **Appalachian cultural influence**, but grounded in the actual mobility needs of this world.

## Why mobile peoples exist here

Mobility should have an economic and ecological reason. Possibilities include:

- seasonal livestock movement between upland and lowland pasture;
- itinerant repair, metal, wood, basket, leather, or musical trades;
- caravan support and animal handling;
- seasonal agricultural labor;
- pilgrimage networks;
- moving between fairs;
- hunting/trapping circuits;
- river or boat communities;
- populations displaced by pre-Convergence border wars who made mobility permanent.

Different mobile peoples can exist; "the nomads" should not be one culture.

## Appalachian-influenced concept

The useful Appalachian inspiration is social rather than costume:

- dense extended-kin networks;
- strong local/family reputations;
- ballads and songs as archives of people, crimes, disasters, loves, wars, and places;
- extraordinary route knowledge in difficult upland terrain;
- portable practical crafts;
- repair and reuse culture;
- mutual aid outside formal institutions;
- suspicion of distant authorities, creditors, monopolies, and outsiders who extract wealth then leave;
- humor and storytelling as social weapons;
- strong attachment to places even within a mobile life.

Historical Appalachia was not generally a nomadic culture, so this should be combined with actual mobile traditions such as transhumant pastoralism, itinerant crafts, caravan peoples, seasonal work circuits, and traveling religious communities.

## A possible form

A large network of related traveling households could move on **circuits** rather than wander randomly.

Each circuit might connect:
- summer High Country pasture;
- Western Uplands repair work;
- River Country harvest labor;
- Forest Country edge markets;
- Port winter work or major fairs.

Families may own no kingdom-scale territory but possess traditional stopping rights, springs, pasture agreements, camps, shrines, and market privileges recognized by old custom.

## Political role

They are useful to everyone and fully trusted by almost no one.

Because they cross borders, they carry:
- songs;
- news;
- tools;
- marriages;
- rumors;
- contraband;
- religious ideas;
- prices;
- missing-person information.

The Council would want their information networks without allowing them to become independent infrastructure.

The Villain could use them as couriers, witnesses, or unwitting carriers of carefully selected rumors.

Wurdren could plausibly know them better than elite characters because adventurers share roads, camps, repairs, and dangerous crossings with them.

## Do not

- make them automatically criminal;
- make them mystical wanderers with no economy;
- turn Appalachian influence into caricatured spelling or "hillbilly" behavior;
- make all mobile groups ethnically identical;
- assume mobility means lack of attachment to place.
