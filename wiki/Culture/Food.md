# Food

## Principle

Cuisine follows ecology + trade + class + season + preservation + religion + history.

Each region has recognizable food families, but there is no single menu eaten by everyone.

## Recipe working set

Concrete recipe examples live in [Recipes](Recipes/README.md).

Those pages are **provisional culinary working material**, not a second food canon. This page remains the owner for broad regional food ecology; [Food Diaspora and Adaptation](Food-Diaspora-and-Adaptation.md) owns the rules for migration and hybridization.

## Ironcrest

Local base:
- barley/oats/other hardy grains;
- roots;
- legumes;
- dairy;
- mountain or valley livestock;
- mushrooms;
- orchard fruit in lower country;
- imported Greenvale grain.

Common techniques:
- stewing;
- baking;
- roasting;
- pickling;
- smoking;
- drying.

Breakfast examples:
- thick grain porridge with milk, cheese, or leftovers;
- flatbread with onions, curds, or preserves.

Midday:
- filled breads;
- bean/root stew;
- cheese, pickles, cold meat.

Dinner:
- grain + legumes + meat when affordable;
- roasted roots;
- soups thickened with bread.

Celebrations:
- large roast animals or meat pies;
- sweetened breads;
- imported fruit/spices as status goods.

Drinks:
- dark ales;
- small beer;
- cider in lower valleys;
- grain spirits in some districts.

## Northwind

Local base:
- fish and shellfish;
- sea greens;
- roots;
- hardy grains;
- dairy in pastoral zones;
- berries;
- preserved meat.

Techniques:
- drying;
- smoking;
- salting;
- fermentation;
- pickling;
- cold storage.

Breakfast:
- porridge with smoked fish;
- rye/barley cakes;
- sour milk.

Lunch:
- dried fish, broth, flatbread, pickles.

Dinner:
- fish soups;
- shellfish pots;
- root dishes;
- dairy and grain where available.

Celebrations:
- shared catches;
- rich stews;
- imported flour/sugar used for special pastries.

Alcohol:
- grain ales;
- berry ferments;
- honey drinks;
- imported spirits.

## Greenvale

Local base is extremely broad:
- wheat/barley/other grains;
- beans;
- peas;
- vegetables;
- dairy;
- eggs;
- poultry;
- pork/goat/sheep depending on district;
- orchard fruit;
- herbs.

Breakfast:
- bread, cheese, eggs;
- porridge;
- fruit preserves.

Lunch:
- soups;
- bean dishes;
- filled breads;
- cold farm meals.

Dinner:
- grain or dumplings;
- vegetables;
- meat where affordable;
- sauces using herbs, dairy, fruit, or vinegar.

Snacks:
- hand pies;
- toasted grains/nuts;
- fresh fruit;
- cheese.

Celebrations:
- enormous seasonal variety;
- communal baked goods;
- preserved foods opened from previous harvests;
- first-fruit/first-grain rites.

Alcohol:
- cider;
- ale;
- fruit wine;
- grain spirits.

## Highridge

Local base:
- hardy grains;
- legumes;
- dairy;
- goat/sheep/yak-like animals;
- dried meat;
- roots;
- trade goods.

Breakfast:
- flatbread and butter/curd;
- grain porridge;
- tea or herbal infusion.

Lunch:
- dumplings;
- noodles in market towns;
- meat or lentil stews;
- dried fruit and nuts.

Dinner:
- thick soups;
- roasted or boiled meats;
- market dishes incorporating foreign spices.

Street food:
- skewers;
- filled breads;
- dumplings;
- roasted nuts;
- tea stalls.

Celebrations:
- food intentionally assembled from multiple regions during caravan fairs.

Alcohol:
- grain beer;
- fermented dairy drinks in some communities;
- imported wines;
- herbal spirits.

## Deepwood

Local base:
- cultivated grains/tubers in clearings;
- nuts;
- mushrooms;
- greens;
- fish;
- game;
- domestic animals near settlements;
- berries;
- honey;
- trade grain and salt.

Breakfast:
- root cakes;
- broth;
- grain packets steamed in leaves;
- fruit/nut paste.

Lunch:
- smoked meat/fish;
- greens;
- breads from grain, nut, or tuber flour.

Dinner:
- mixed stews;
- roasted fish/game;
- mushrooms and vegetables;
- fermented condiments.

Celebrations:
- seasonal foraging and harvest combinations;
- highly valued rare forest ingredients.

Alcohol:
- fruit ferments;
- honey drinks;
- grain beer;
- herbal infusions.

## Sunplains

Local base:
- wheat/barley;
- pulses;
- vegetables;
- fruit;
- nuts;
- oils;
- dairy;
- lamb/goat/poultry;
- coastal fish;
- dried fruit;
- spices from trade.

Breakfast:
- flatbread, oil, cheese;
- fruit;
- yogurt/curds;
- tea.

Lunch:
- lentil/chickpea dishes;
- grain salads;
- stuffed breads;
- grilled vegetables.

Dinner:
- slow-cooked meat/vegetable pots;
- pilafs;
- fish in coastal areas;
- bread with many shared dishes.

Snacks:
- nuts;
- dates/figs or analogous fruits;
- fried/filled breads;
- honey pastries.

Celebrations:
- city-specific dishes used as civic identity.

Alcohol:
- wine in communities that permit it;
- beer;
- fruit ferments;
- distilled drinks in some areas;
- nonalcoholic mint/herb/fruit drinks.

## Port

Port cuisine is not merely "fusion."

It develops:
- dock-worker food;
- preserved ship food;
- wealthy merchant cuisine;
- immigrant neighborhood dishes;
- street foods designed for travelers;
- religiously specific kitchens.

## Border cuisine

Border food is often the clearest sign of cultural blending because ingredients and techniques combine faster than political identities.

See [Neighboring Border Fusion Recipes](Recipes/Border-Fusions.md) for provisional concrete examples.

## Food and story

Food shortages, preservation failures, grain prices, fish seasons, road closures, religious dietary rules, and harvest timing should all be usable as plot mechanics.
