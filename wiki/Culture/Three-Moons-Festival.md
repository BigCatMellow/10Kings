# Three Moons Festival

## Status

**Provisional.** Recovered from PR #4 as a historical proposal; the [Sunday Morning stories](../Sunday-Morning/README.md) have since used it, so its cadence and name follow them. Later author-settled decisions on magic, region names, Council membership and language outrank this source. See [Source Register](../Reference/Source-Register.md).

**Three Moon is held every seven years, when all three moons are visible from Port** (bt 7274, 2026-11-13, [D52](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions): "a party thrown every 7 years when all 3 moons are visible in Ports view"). That is the festival's original cadence from the recovered proposal. The yearly reading the [2026-11 review](../Reference/World-Review-2026-11.md) took from the stories is withdrawn. The spelling stays **Three Moon**; the file keeps its older name for its links.

**Its name is no mystery in Port.** The sky has three moons ([D52](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)), and the festival is named for the night they come round together. How that happens in the sky, and what it does to Port's tides, is on [Sky, Seasons and Sea](../Sky-Seasons-and-Sea.md#the-three-moons). Its season stays early autumn ([Festivals](Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#port)). Its historical origin, ritual sequence and duration remain open.

**One story line now disagrees.** [Three Pots at Three Moon](../Sunday-Morning/Stories/Three-Pots-at-Three-Moon.md) ends with the grandmother saying "Again next year." That is bt 7274's to change or keep; the story is untouched. Her "fifty years" at the stall still fits: about seven festivals.

Its social function is proposed as follows:

> **every seven years, Port becomes an unusually dense convergence point for people, institutions, goods, information, entertainment, contracts, and politics from across the known world.**

Its proximity to the current story is a historical proposal, not an adopted chronology.

Earlier opening-draft material developed this festival idea; the current story timeline has not adopted it.

Exact placement relative to the opening remains open:

- approaching;
- currently underway;
- recently concluded.

Do not lock that timing until the story chronology needs it.

---

# 1. Why it belongs in Port

Port is already the continent's strongest maritime / commercial information aggregator.

The festival amplifies qualities Port already possesses rather than inventing a temporary "world fair."

People already come to Port for:

- trade;
- shipping;
- arbitration;
- contracts;
- credit;
- guild business;
- diplomacy;
- migration;
- employment;
- news.

The Three Moons Festival gives many of them a reason to come **at the same time**.

That synchronized concentration is what makes the event extraordinary.

---

# 2. Scale

It is the largest festival Port hosts.

Every seven years, the city can expect an unusual influx of some combination of:

- merchants;
- guild delegates;
- craftspeople;
- sailors;
- performers;
- pilgrims / religious visitors;
- political representatives;
- scholars;
- itinerant workers;
- families returning to Port;
- tourists / ordinary visitors;
- criminals and opportunists;
- people seeking contracts or employment.

Do not assume every polity sends an official delegation or every guild formally attends.

The event is large because many independent reasons for travel overlap.

---

# 3. Guild role

The festival has become increasingly important to guild life because Port itself has become more important to trans-regional professional coordination.

Guild-related activity may include:

- branch meetings;
- credential recognition;
- contract negotiation;
- apprenticeship / employment recruitment;
- standards disputes;
- shared procurement;
- mutual-aid discussions;
- cross-guild problem solving;
- demonstrations of new techniques;
- legal / arbitration meetings;
- informal networking.

Some of this may occur under formal festival programming.

Much of it may simply happen because everyone is already in the same city.

This distinction matters.

The festival should not become one giant guild congress by decree.

---

# 4. Information effect

The festival creates an unusual information environment.

Normally, Port aggregates news arriving on different schedules.

During the Three Moons period, many information networks overlap in person:

- maritime;
- Highridge Plateau;
- guild;
- merchant;
- faith;
- diplomatic;
- migrant;
- criminal;
- scholarly.

That means people can compare:

- prices;
- route conditions;
- labor disputes;
- shortages;
- unusual purchasing;
- political rumors;
- contract changes;
- military / security precautions.

The festival therefore functions as an **information accelerator**.

It does not make information automatically true.

It increases:

- volume;
- cross-comparison;
- rumor;
- correction;
- leakage;
- accidental discovery.

---

# 5. The perfect-storm effect in the current story

The current story already contains:

- piracy;
- fishery tension;
- Greenvale abundance / debt stress;
- route unreliability;
- Deepwood access disputes;
- Ironcrest labor / strategic-material pressure;
- Sunplains drought anxiety;
- growing Port capacity strain;
- increasingly important guild networks.

The Three Moons Festival brings people carrying pieces of all those stories into the same city.

Most do not know the pieces are connected.

This gives the reader / Wurdren a plausible way to overhear or encounter apparently unrelated fragments.

It also gives the Villain an unusually rich observational window.

---

# 6. Relationship to the Villain

The Villain did **not** create the Three Moons Festival.

He did not arrange the current seven-year cycle for his plot.

That coincidence is part of the opportunity he exploits.

His earlier work encouraging guild presence / cross-guild communication in Port means the festival is even more information-rich than it might otherwise have been.

This is exactly the kind of circumstance the Villain's method favors ([Villain](../Story/Villain.md)):

> **a major independent event creates a temporary concentration of information and relationships; the Villain recognizes its value and changes his plans around it.**

He may use the festival to:

- compare reports from distant sectors;
- identify which pressure branches are actually moving;
- discover failed assumptions;
- find new intervention opportunities;
- connect people who have legitimate reasons to work together;
- observe who is buying, hiring, complaining, stockpiling, or seeking guarantees.

He does not need to personally attend every meeting.

Port's guild / commercial network already aggregates the information.

---

# 7. Relationship to secrecy

The festival helps solve an intelligence problem without creating a giant spy conspiracy.

Most information reaches the Villain because:

- people came to do business;
- guild representatives compared conditions;
- merchants discussed prices;
- workers complained;
- contracts were negotiated;
- travelers exchanged route information.

Only a small portion needs to be collected covertly.

That makes his intelligence advantage plausible while keeping the true conspiracy small.

---

# 8. Story-use rule

Do not use the festival as an exposition dump.

Prefer fragments:

- a guild delegate complaining that a particular metal grade is impossible to secure;
- a Greenvale merchant offering grain at an absurdly low price;
- a Northwind sailor describing new convoy procedures;
- a Highridge Plateau carrier warning that animals are being booked months ahead;
- an Sunplains buyer quietly seeking long-term food contracts;
- a Port builder complaining that repair material has been diverted.

Each conversation makes sense on its own.

Only later does their simultaneous presence become significant.

---

# 9. Material consequences

The festival itself adds temporary pressure to Port.

Likely categories:

- lodging;
- food;
- warehouse / stall space;
- harbor traffic;
- local transport;
- policing;
- fire / sanitation;
- temporary labor;
- prices;
- theft / fraud;
- public space;
- medical care.

These pressures should not all be blamed on the Villain.

They give Port genuine reason to feel both:

> **we are the center of the continent**

and:

> **we bear the cost of everyone coming here.**

That duality directly supports Port's larger political grievance.

---

# 10. Political disagreement around the festival

Three Moons should not be universally celebrated as proof of Port's importance.

## Supporters can see

- income;
- jobs;
- contracts;
- civic pride;
- continental prestige;
- easier guild / professional coordination.

## Critics can see

- rising rents / food prices;
- overcrowding;
- unequal gains;
- large guild houses gaining disproportionate access;
- temporary meetings becoming permanent Port institutions;
- Port accumulating continental influence under the language of neutrality.

Guild-centralization opponents can use the festival as an argument:

> **coordination that was supposed to be exceptional is becoming permanent.**

Other people can answer:

> **why deliberately make continental work harder simply to prevent Port from becoming important?**

Neither side needs to be insincere.

See [Current Events](../Story/Current-Events.md).
