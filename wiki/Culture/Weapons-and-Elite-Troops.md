# Weapons and Elite Troops

## Principle

Weapons should be reusable, maintainable, culturally plausible, and suitable for the environment.

Most people do not carry battlefield weapons all day.

Self-defense often uses tools already present in daily life.

## Ironcrest

Common:
- stout knives;
- hammers;
- short spears;
- axes;
- clubs;
- crossbows in some districts.

Why:
metal access makes durable fittings common, but good steel remains expensive.

Elite direction:
disciplined heavy infantry or engineering troops accustomed to fortifications, mines, and confined terrain.

Visual difference:
better helmets, standardized armor pieces, reliable tools, insignia tied to unit rather than flamboyant fantasy armor.

## Northwind

Common:
- utility knives;
- boat hooks;
- axes;
- short spears;
- bows;
- boarding weapons.

Elite direction:
marines/coastal fighters skilled in shipboard combat, rough weather, landing operations, and cold endurance.

Elite distinction:
waterproofed gear, layered clothing, rope skills, compact shields, superior boots.

## Greenvale

Common:
- staff;
- billhook;
- sickle;
- knife;
- hunting bow;
- spear.

Elite direction:
mobile local defense, scouts, or disciplined levy cadres whose strength is logistics and local terrain rather than exotic weaponry.

## Highridge

Common:
- staff;
- short spear;
- bow;
- sling;
- caravan knife.

Elite direction:
route guards and high-altitude scouts trained for ambush prevention, escort, signal systems, and fighting around narrow passes.

## Deepwood

Common:
- bows;
- spears;
- machete/billhook-like cutting tools;
- knives;
- slings.

Elite direction:
rangers/wardens who know terrain, tracking, concealment, river crossings, and coordinated small-unit fighting.

No magical "silent bows" are required.

## Sunplains

Common:
- knives;
- staffs;
- short spears;
- slings;
- bows;
- city watch weapons.

Elite direction:
city-state guards, cavalry where terrain and horse culture support it, professional household troops, or disciplined spear formations.

## Port

Port's best forces are likely specialized:
- harbor watch;
- customs officers;
- fire brigades;
- marine patrols;
- riot/public-order units.

Their advantage is training for dense urban environments rather than superior weapon technology.

## Underpass

Specialized troops use:
- short spears;
- axes/picks;
- compact shields;
- crossbows;
- helmets;
- ropes;
- lamps.

Long weapons are often impractical.

## Elite troop rule

Elite troops should stand out by:
- training;
- discipline;
- logistics;
- equipment quality;
- unit culture;
- communication;
- ability to operate in difficult terrain.

Not by enormous weapons, implausible armor, or supernatural competence.

## Cultural behavior

Each elite formation should have:
- recruitment tradition;
- social reputation;
- distinctive speech/register;
- initiation;
- practical uniform differences;
- rules of conduct;
- relationship with ordinary troops;
- political patrons.

A feared elite unit can be excellent at its specialty and still fail badly outside it.
