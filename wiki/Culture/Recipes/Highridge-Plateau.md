# Highridge Plateau Recipes

## Status

**Provisional examples consistent with [Food](../Food.md).**

Highridge is a trade crossroads, but local food still exists. Imported spice should vary by wealth, season and route access.

## Method profile

Common techniques:

- portable charcoal or dung-fueled braziers where wood is scarce;
- clay or stone ovens for flatbreads;
- steaming using stacked vessels;
- boiling and braising in heavy travel pots;
- drying meat, fruit and dairy for caravans;
- combining local staples with whatever trade goods are available.

### 1. Caravan Flatbread with Spiced Butter

**Typical ingredients**
- hardy plateau flour;
- water;
- salt;
- starter/yeast or unleavened versions;
- fermented yak-like butter;
- cumin/coriander-like seeds, dried herbs or pungent spice.

**In-world method**
1. Dough is mixed stiff enough to travel.
2. It rests while camp is made.
3. Thin rounds are slapped onto a hot stone, griddle or clay-oven wall.
4. Fermented butter is mixed with crushed spice.
5. Bread is brushed while hot.

---

### 2. Caravan Curry / Spiced Caravan Pot

**Cultural logic:** “Curry” is a useful real-world analogy; in-setting it is better understood as a family of thick, spiced caravan stews.

**Typical ingredients**
- goat, yak-like meat or legumes;
- onion/garlic-like aromatics;
- dried ginger-like root where traded;
- cumin/coriander-like seed;
- dried chile/pepper analogue depending on trade;
- cultured dairy, broth or nut paste;
- dried fruit in richer versions.

**Method**
1. Meat is browned in a heavy pot.
2. Aromatics and whole spices toast in the fat.
3. Liquid and legumes/meat are added.
4. The pot simmers beside the brazier while camp work continues.
5. Sour dairy or fruit is added at the end to brighten it.

---

### 3. Wind Dumplings

**Typical ingredients**
- wheat or barley dough;
- minced goat/yak-like meat or lentils;
- onion;
- herbs;
- fermented dairy;
- optional imported spice.

**Method**
1. Small rounds of dough are filled and pinched shut.
2. Dumplings sit in stacked steam trays over one boiling pot.
3. The lowest trays usually hold sturdier dumplings; upper trays hold vegetables or lighter fillings.
4. They are served communally with sour dairy, broth or chile-like condiment.

---

### 4. Plateau Noodle Soup

Hand-cut or pulled noodles cook in broth with dried meat, greens, onion and root vegetables. Market versions add trade spices and fresh herbs unavailable to remote villages.

---

### 5. Caravan Skewers

Small pieces of meat are marinated in sour dairy or vinegar, spice and salt, then quickly cooked over a brazier. The point is speed and portability, not giant feast portions.

---

### 6. Spice Rice / Grain Pilaf

Where imported rice or another long-grain cereal is affordable, cooks toast it in fat, simmer it in broth, and fold in dried fruit and nuts. Elsewhere the same technique uses local wheat berries, millet or barley.

---

### 7. Lentil Fritters

Soaked pulses are mashed with onion, herbs and spice, shaped small and shallow-fried in a brazier pan. Common market food because the batter can be mixed in advance.
