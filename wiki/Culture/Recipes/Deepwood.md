# Deepwood Recipes

## Status

**Provisional examples consistent with [Food](../Food.md).**

Deepwood is not purely foraged cuisine. Settlements cultivate grains, tubers and domestic foods in clearings and trade for salt and staples.

## Method profile

Common techniques:

- low-ember earthen-pot cooking;
- leaf wrapping;
- hot-stone and pit steaming;
- clay-sealed roasting;
- mushroom and nut broths;
- fermentation;
- drying and smoking in moderation;
- seasonal use of forest foods rather than constant mystical ingredients.

### 1. Mushroom-Simmered Broth

**Typical ingredients**
- mixed edible mushrooms;
- leek/onion-like allium;
- light vegetable or game stock;
- wild thyme;
- root trimmings;
- salt.

**In-world method**
1. A ceramic pot is set beside, not over, fierce flame.
2. Roots and alliums soften gently.
3. Mushrooms simmer only long enough to flavor the broth.
4. Strong herbs are used sparingly.
5. The broth may be served alone or over grain/root cakes.

---

### 2. Guardian Stew

**Cultural logic:** The name fits ceremonial or communal versions; ordinary households make simpler descendants.

**Typical ingredients**
- venison, boar or domestic meat;
- mushrooms;
- roots;
- onion;
- forest herbs;
- stock;
- a little honey or fermented fruit paste.

**Method**
1. Meat is lightly browned in a clay pot if fat is available.
2. Roots and stock are added.
3. The pot cooks for hours in low embers or a banked earth hearth.
4. Mushrooms enter later to preserve their character.
5. Fermented fruit or honey balances the broth.

---

### 3. Whispering Leaf Wraps

**Typical ingredients**
- cooked grain;
- mushrooms;
- chestnuts/hazelnuts;
- greens;
- fish, fowl or shredded game when available;
- broad edible/non-toxic wrapping leaves.

**In-world method**
1. Leaves are softened over steam or hot water.
2. Filling is cooked or partially cooked first.
3. Packets are tied with fiber.
4. They steam in a covered pit above hot stones, separated from ash by leaves and brush.
5. Packets are opened at the table.

---

### 4. Clay-Sealed Forest Roast

A bird, fish or small cut of meat is wrapped in leaves, coated in clean cooking clay and set among hot stones. The hardened clay is cracked away after cooking. The clay is a vessel, not an ingredient.

---

### 5. Night-Ember Root Soup

This replaces the earlier brainstorm name “Nightshade Ember Soup,” which implied unsafe ingredients.

**Typical ingredients**
- roasted parsnip/turnip/carrot-like roots;
- chestnut;
- mushroom stock;
- mild wild herbs;
- fermented berry paste.

**Method**
1. Roots roast in the edge of the hearth.
2. They are peeled and mashed into mushroom broth.
3. Crushed chestnut thickens the soup.
4. It simmers overnight in banked heat.
5. Berry ferment is added just before serving.

---

### 6. Acorn or Chestnut Cakes

Tannin-leached acorn meal or chestnut flour is mixed with grain flour, water and honey, then cooked on a stone or griddle. These are seasonal foods, not universal staples.

---

### 7. Fermented Forest Fruit Paste

Berries or plums are lightly salted or sweetened and allowed to sour in crocks. The paste functions as acid, condiment and preservation method.
