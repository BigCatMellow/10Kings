# Greenvale Recipes

## Status

**Provisional examples consistent with [Food](../Food.md).**

Greenvale should show class, season and district variation. Its abundance means diversity, not permanent plenty.

## Method profile

Common techniques:

- communal stone or brick ovens;
- farmhouse hearth stewing;
- fruitwood roasting;
- dairy culturing;
- pickling and preserving;
- cellar storage;
- grain fermentation;
- use of leftovers across several meals.

### 1. Fresh Morning Bread and Milk

**Cultural logic:** Bread ties the workday to the shared oven schedule.

**Typical ingredients**
- wheat-like flour, often blended with barley;
- starter or yeast;
- water or milk;
- salt;
- butter or nut oil.

**In-world method**
1. Dough is mixed before dawn or the night before.
2. Households carry shaped loaves to a shared oven.
3. Bread bakes after the fire is raked down.
4. It is eaten warm with milk, curds, butter or preserves.
5. Day-old bread becomes soup thickener or field food.

---

### 2. Braided Honey and Grain Loaf

**Cultural logic:** Braiding makes a familiar loaf ceremonial without requiring luxury ingredients.

**Typical ingredients**
- wheat and barley flour;
- warm milk;
- honey;
- starter/yeast;
- butter;
- salt.

**Method**
1. A soft enriched dough is kneaded and risen.
2. Three ropes are braided.
3. The loaf is brushed with milk or butter.
4. It bakes during a communal oven cycle.
5. Festival versions add nuts, dried fruit or seed.

---

### 3. Harvest Stew

**Cultural logic:** The recipe changes with whatever the village has in quantity.

**Typical ingredients**
- beans or barley;
- onion;
- carrots, squash, greens, roots;
- chicken, goat or pork-like meat when affordable;
- garden herbs;
- broth;
- vinegar, cider or cultured dairy for acidity.

**Method**
1. Meat, if used, is browned.
2. Beans/grain and sturdy vegetables simmer first.
3. Delicate vegetables and greens go in later.
4. The pot is finished with fresh herbs.
5. Communal versions accept contributions from multiple households.

---

### 4. Orchard-Fire Roasted Fowl

**Cultural logic:** A celebratory roast makes direct use of orchard pruning wood and cider.

**Typical ingredients**
- duck, goose, chicken or game bird;
- salt;
- sage/thyme/fennel-like herbs;
- cider;
- honey;
- apples, pears or plums.

**In-world method**
1. Orchard wood burns down to clean coals.
2. The bird is salted and tied to a spit or set in a roasting cradle.
3. It turns slowly over indirect heat.
4. Cider is used for basting; honey appears late so it does not burn.
5. Fruit roasts in a pan beneath and catches the drippings.

---

### 5. Farmer's Midday Mash

**Typical ingredients**
- cracked barley;
- milk or water;
- butter;
- hazelnuts or walnuts;
- thyme or sage;
- salt.

**Method**
1. Barley cooks slowly until soft.
2. Milk and butter enrich the final texture.
3. Nuts and herbs are stirred in.
4. It is carried to fields in covered crocks or eaten at midday tables.

---

### 6. Orchard Hand Pies

**Typical fillings**
- apple and soft cheese;
- pear and onion;
- beans and herbs;
- chicken and leek;
- plum and honey for sweet versions.

Thick pastry is filled, sealed and baked during the communal oven's falling heat.

---

### 7. Roasted Grain Clusters

Leftover cooked grain or toasted cereal is mixed with honey, nuts and seeds, pressed into clusters and dried in a cooling oven. A common field snack.

---

### 8. Herbed Farm Cheese

Fresh curds are drained, salted and mixed with meadow herbs. Some are eaten soft; others are pressed and aged for winter.
