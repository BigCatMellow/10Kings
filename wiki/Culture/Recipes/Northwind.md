# Northwind Recipes

## Status

**Provisional examples consistent with [Food](../Food.md).**

Northwind cooking is not only fish. Inland herding, dairy, grain, berries and preserved meat should remain visible.

## Method profile

Common techniques:

- salt curing and brining;
- cold and warm smoking;
- drying in wind or protected racks;
- fermentation;
- root-cellar and ice-cellar storage;
- all-day hearth pots;
- baking in clay or stone ovens heated for both food and household warmth.

### 1. Salt-Fish and Barley Porridge

**Cultural logic:** Preserved fish stretches a grain breakfast through winter.

**Typical ingredients**
- barley or oats;
- water or light fish broth;
- soaked salt fish;
- leek or onion;
- root vegetable;
- dill-like herb;
- rendered animal fat or butter.

**In-world method**
1. Salt fish is soaked and rinsed.
2. Grain simmers until nearly soft.
3. Chopped roots and alliums go in.
4. Fish is added near the end so it remains in flakes.
5. A little fat is stirred through before serving.

---

### 2. Hearthfire Fish and Root Bake

**Cultural logic:** The evening meal uses the same masonry hearth that warms the house.

**Typical ingredients**
- whole cod-like fish, haddock-like fish or char;
- thin-sliced turnip, parsnip or potato-like roots;
- coarse grain crumbs;
- sea salt;
- kelp;
- dill-like herbs;
- tart berries or imported sour fruit.

**In-world method**
1. The oven is fired earlier for bread and heat.
2. Roots go into a clay dish with fat and a little broth.
3. Fish rests on top, protected by crumbs and herbs.
4. The dish cooks in falling heat rather than fierce flame.
5. Tart berry mash is spooned over at the table.

---

### 3. Frostbound Fish Stew

**Cultural logic:** A communal pot turns small fish, kelp and roots into a substantial supper.

**Typical ingredients**
- fresh and/or smoked fish;
- fish stock;
- kelp;
- roots;
- onion;
- barley;
- seal fat, butter or other local cooking fat;
- juniper-like herb.

**In-world method**
1. Roots and barley begin first.
2. Kelp and smoked fish flavor the broth.
3. Fresh fish enters only near the end.
4. The pot stays hot at the central hearth and is replenished during the day.

**Correction from early brainstorming:** chilling cooked stew in snow can preserve it, but it does not magically improve or “gelatinize” fish broth. Thick versions come from barley, collagen-rich fish parts or reduction.

---

### 4. Frosted Mead Cakes

**Cultural logic:** A durable oat cake that turns scarce sweetness into travel food.

**Typical ingredients**
- coarse oats;
- honey;
- small beer or mead;
- butter or rendered fat;
- salt.

**In-world method**
1. Oats are mixed into a stiff batter.
2. Cakes are cooked on a griddle or baking stone.
3. Honey is brushed on while hot.
4. In winter they cool rapidly outdoors, setting the glaze.
5. They travel well with cheese, smoked fish or berry paste.

---

### 5. Snow-Dried Fish Cakes

**Typical ingredients**
- soaked salt fish;
- mashed roots;
- oat or barley meal;
- egg when available;
- onion;
- herbs;
- cooking fat.

**Method**
1. Fish is flaked into root mash.
2. Grain meal binds the mixture.
3. Patties are formed and chilled.
4. They are fried on a hearth griddle.
5. Coastal households serve them with fermented berry relish or pickled kelp.

---

### 6. Kelp Crisps

Clean kelp is rinsed, salted lightly, dried on racks and briefly toasted near the hearth. They are a snack, soup garnish and travel seasoning.

---

### 7. Pickled Winter Roots

Turnip, beet-like roots, carrot-like roots and onion are packed in salt brine and allowed to sour. Better-off households add berry vinegar, honey or imported spice.

---

### 8. Aurora Berry Preserve

Berries are simmered with honey or malt syrup until thick. In lean households the preserve remains sharply sour; festival jars use more sweetener and may include mead.
