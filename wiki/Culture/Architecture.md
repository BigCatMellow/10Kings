# Architecture

## Principle

Architecture is accumulated adaptation.

No region has one house type. Cities, towns, villages, elite compounds, worker housing, farmsteads, religious buildings, warehouses, and border settlements should differ.

The world remains low fantasy and pre-industrial: stone, earth, brick, timber, thatch, tile, plaster, reeds, hides, rope, simple glass where affordable, iron fittings, manual hoists, mills, water management, and animal labor.

## Ironcrest

Inspiration pools:
- Scottish/Welsh/Appalachian upland settlements;
- Ethiopian/Yemeni highland stone towns;
- Andean mining settlements;
- East Asian craft districts.

### Cities

Dense stone or mixed-material cores, workshops, courtyards, guild halls, warehouses, narrow older lanes, later planned streets, cisterns, retaining walls, and firebreaks.

Industrial buildings are large because of people, animals, bellows, furnaces, water wheels, and storage — not because of mechanized factories.

### Towns

Usually mixed-function: mining + farming, ford + market, quarry + craft.

### Homes

Stone or earth lower walls, timber where available, attached yards/workshops, storage, gardens, animal pens.

## Northwind

Inspiration pools:
- North Atlantic;
- Ainu/Japanese northern coastal;
- Aleut/Inuit adaptations;
- Scottish/Irish islands.

### Cities

Harbor districts, breakwaters, boatyards, warehouses, smokehouses, market halls, clan or civic meeting halls.

### Towns

Compact settlement around sheltered coves, work yards, communal storage, storm-resistant building orientation.

### Homes

Earth-insulated, timber, turf, stone, reed/grass insulation, steep or aerodynamic roofs depending on snow/wind.

## Greenvale

Inspiration pools:
- Andean agricultural settlements;
- South/Southeast Asian irrigation villages;
- Irish rural forms;
- courtyard compounds and commons traditions globally.

### Cities

Market and storage cities: mills, granaries, river quays, courtyards, crafts districts, dense housing, administrative buildings.

### Towns

Cluster around water, common storage, markets, estates, cooperative facilities, or road junctions.

### Homes

Mixed farm compounds with storage, animals, kitchens, garden plots, work yards; raised floors in wet districts; earthen walls in drier ones.

## Highridge

Inspiration pools:
- Tibetan/Himalayan settlements;
- Andean terraces;
- Central Asian and Persian caravanserai;
- Caucasus mountain towns.

### Cities

Terraced, retaining walls, central market/caravan courts, cisterns, inns, storage, arbitration halls, multi-language commercial districts.

### Towns

Pass towns, fortified waystations, herding-market villages, repair settlements.

### Homes

Stone/earth for thermal mass, compact plans, enclosed yards, rooftop work/storage where useful.

## Deepwood

Inspiration pools:
- Dayak/Bornean longhouse traditions;
- Amazonian forest settlement;
- Japanese woodland management;
- Indigenous North American forest adaptations;
- northern European woodland traditions.

### Cities

River- or road-oriented cities with dense market districts, raised walkways where needed, older tree stands incorporated into plans, warehouses, religious compounds, working-class districts.

### Towns

Longhouses, clustered compounds, raised floors in wet country, detached work shelters.

### Homes

Timber, bark, thatch, woven material, clay, stone; built to ventilation, rainfall, insects, and forest-floor conditions.

## Sunplains

Inspiration pools:
- Maghrebi/Levantine;
- Persian/Anatolian;
- Iberian;
- South Asian hot-climate towns;
- South American dry/coastal settlements.

### Cities

Courtyard houses, shaded lanes, thick walls, cisterns, fountains, market arcades, roof terraces, elite garden compounds, dense poor districts.

### Towns

Water-centered settlements: springs, canal gates, river crossings, market squares, road inns.

### Homes

Courtyards, shade screens, thick earthen or masonry walls, small exterior openings, roof terraces, water jars/cisterns.

## Port

Port should visibly contain architectural layers from all regions but become its own style through:
- high land value;
- fire regulation;
- warehouses;
- docks;
- mixed materials;
- vertical additions;
- foreign quarters;
- rebuilding after fires/storms.

## Border towns

Border architecture is adaptive, not decorative fusion.

Examples:
- Ironcrest stone foundations + Greenvale flood/yard planning;
- Northwind insulation + Highridge compact stone windbreaks;
- Deepwood raised floors + Sunplains courtyard shade where forest gives way to hot lowland.

## Rule

Every major settlement should answer:
1. Where does water come from?
2. Where does waste go?
3. Where is food stored?
4. How is fire managed?
5. How do people move goods?
6. What was here before the present city?
7. What does poverty look like?
8. What does wealth look like?
