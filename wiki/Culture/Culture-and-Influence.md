# Cultural Inspiration

## Status

**Working method, not canon content.** How real-world inspiration is used: draw globally, copy nowhere whole ([World Rules](../World-Rules.md#14-draw-globally-copy-nowhere-whole)). Each region's inspiration pool is owned by its region page. *(Labelled in the [2026-11 review](../Reference/World-Review-2026-11.md#status-labels).)*

## Guiding rule

The regions are **not fantasy versions of single real cultures**.

Real-world societies are used as research pools for solving comparable problems: cold-water fishing, high-altitude trade, irrigation, mining towns, forest settlement, communal farming, civic city-states, migration, clan organization, craft specialization, oral history, and so on.

A believable regional culture should emerge from:

1. environment;
2. historical population movements;
3. pre-Convergence states and wars;
4. trade;
5. language;
6. religion;
7. class;
8. technology;
9. institutions;
10. neighboring peoples.

## Current inspiration pools

### Ironcrest

Useful reference pools include:
- Scottish and Welsh mining/industrial communities;
- Appalachian mining and craft communities;
- Andean highland mining settlements;
- Ethiopian and Yemeni highland stone towns;
- Japanese and Chinese craft lineages;
- mining communities in Central Europe and the Balkans.

The aim is not "Celtic miners" or "Yemeni foundries." Ironcrest should feel like a place where generations of upland settlement, ore extraction, specialized crafts, dangerous labor, merchant capital, and repeated political consolidation produced a distinctive culture.

### Northwind

Useful reference pools include:
- North Atlantic fishing societies;
- Icelandic, Faroese, Scottish island, Irish coastal, and Scandinavian maritime communities;
- Ainu, Japanese, Korean, Aleut, Inuit, and other northern/coastal adaptations;
- maritime communities where seasonal migration, communal risk, preservation, and boat ownership shape social structure.

Avoid turning Northwind into generic Vikings.

### Greenvale

Useful reference pools include:
- Andean communal agriculture and terrace/cooperative traditions;
- South and Southeast Asian irrigation villages;
- Irish rural community traditions;
- Eastern European mixed agriculture;
- Indigenous North and South American seed stewardship;
- African and Asian compound/courtyard models where relevant.

Greenvale should feel like a heavily inhabited agricultural country, not a pastoral postcard.

### Highridge Plateau

Useful reference pools include:
- Tibetan and Himalayan plateau communities;
- Andean highland settlements;
- Central Asian caravan networks;
- Persian and Silk Road caravanserai culture;
- Caucasus mountain trade;
- upland market cultures in South and East Asia.

Highridge's defining history is its role as intermediary, which means cultural borrowing should be especially visible.

### Deepwood

Useful reference pools include:
- Amazonian and other tropical/forest societies;
- Dayak and other Bornean longhouse traditions;
- Japanese woodland management;
- Indigenous North American forest cultures;
- Baltic, Slavic, Celtic, and Finnic forest traditions;
- historical communities practicing coppicing, selective logging, hunting, resin gathering, charcoal burning, medicinal collection, and woodland agriculture.

Avoid the "mystical forest people" monoculture.

### Sunplains

Useful reference pools include:
- Maghrebi, Levantine, Anatolian, Persian, Iberian, Greek, and southern Italian dry-climate urban/agricultural traditions;
- South Asian water management and market culture;
- Andean and South American dry/coastal agriculture;
- historical city-state and merchant-republic structures from many regions.

Its civic competition should be as important as climate.

## Nomads and itinerant peoples

The Appalachian-influenced people are now the **Ridge-Roamers** ([D24](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)); their inspirations and do-nots are on [Nomads](Nomads.md), which owns them.

This should not imply that historical Appalachians were generally nomadic. It is an inspiration for social texture that would be combined with genuinely mobile pastoral, trading, pilgrim, and itinerant traditions from elsewhere.

## Cultural blending

Border populations should not look like "half of culture A plus half of culture B." Over generations, they develop their own institutions, accents, marriage patterns, foods, building types, jokes, prejudices, and identities.

Port should have neighborhoods whose cultures are several generations removed from their ancestral regions and no longer fit clean categories.

## Recovered comparative research

The [Cultural Research Library](../Reference/Cultural-Research-Library.md) indexes preserved legacy notes, partially recovered 2024–2026 cultural discussions, and independently checked case studies of real-world institutions (oral performance, recordkeeping, reciprocity, irrigation governance, ritual and diaspora). It is **non-canon reference**, not another owner or a list of approved regional analogues. All culture decisions remain governed by this page and [World Rules](../World-Rules.md).

The [Community Institution Casebook](../Reference/Community-Institution-Casebook.md) adds source-grounded, **non-canon** comparisons for local water rights, raised-field agriculture, seasonal grazing corridors, navigation teaching, and stonework apprenticeship. Each proposed fictional scene is a design test, not an approved cultural detail.

The [Obscure Cultural Systems Dossiers](../Reference/Obscure-Cultural-Systems-Dossiers.md) extend comparative research to 17 additional practices and trace their specific communities, knowledge transmission, institutional functions and limits. The [Original Culture Generation Method](../Reference/Original-Culture-Generation-Method.md) supplies a non-canon process and five test cases **derived from local geography, work, risk and conflict** rather than copying recognizable cultural surfaces. These reference files do not create fictional canon.

Further [obscure social-systems research](../Reference/Obscure-Social-Systems-Batch-2.md) and [eight original institutional prototypes](../Reference/Original-Cultural-Systems-Batch-2.md) are available as **non-canon** source studies and design tests. Neither real-world examples nor fictional prototypes represent approved region-wide cultural identity or lore.
