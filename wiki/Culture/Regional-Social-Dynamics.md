# Regional Social Dynamics

## Status

**Provisional scene texture.** This page records in-world attitudes, jokes, habits and prejudices that may help scenes feel inhabited.

None of the stereotypes below are objective descriptions of a region or people. They are things **particular people might say or expect**. Apply [World Rules](../World-Rules.md): regions are ecosystems, culture is contested, and institutions and communities contain internal disagreement.

## Why this exists

Cultural difference becomes visible in ordinary friction:

- who sits with whom in a tavern;
- which accent changes a shopkeeper's assumptions;
- who is trusted for a job;
- what joke lands badly at a mixed table;
- what a mixed marriage has to negotiate;
- which old grievance surfaces during a price dispute.

The point is not to turn regional shorthand into truth. The point is to show how people carry incomplete models of one another.

## Example sayings

Use sparingly and vary by class, age, town and speaker.

| Target | Example in-world shorthand | What the speaker is implying |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stone Hills | “Iron hands, iron hearts.” | toughness becoming cruelty |
| North Coast | “Cold water, cold blood.” | maritime hardness becoming callousness |
| Low Rivers | “Soft as Low Rivers butter.” | agricultural comfort becoming weakness |
| High Roads | “Every road leads to High Roads's pocket.” | intermediaries profit from everyone |
| Longwood | “Gone to the trees.” | outsiders treating seclusion as irrationality |
| Old Cities | “Golden tongue, copper coin.” | polished rhetoric hiding little substance |
| Port | “Every ship lands at Port, none stay.” | a city used by outsiders and abandoned |

These sayings can be affectionate, hostile, self-mocking or obsolete depending on context.

## Mixed taverns and markets

A mixed-region tavern should not have a fixed seating chart, but occupational habit can shape behavior.

Possible texture:

- sailors choose seats with sight of doors or docks;
- forge crews cluster around heat and talk over one another after loud work;
- caravan merchants spread maps, contracts or samples across a table;
- travelers from small or secluded communities watch before joining local customs;
- Port workers know who has money, who is lost and which argument is about to become expensive.

The useful detail is the **reason** behind the habit, not the regional label.

## Hiring prejudice

Employers may carry biased shortcuts even when reality contradicts them.

Examples:

- North Coast sailors are assumed to be safer hires for ship work;
- Stone Hills workers are stereotyped as better metalworkers;
- High Roads-trained clerks or Port polyglots may be preferred for translation and records;
- Longwood-trained healers can command prestige for some specialties;
- displaced Port workers may be offered the hardest work because employers assume they have fewer alternatives.

Show counterexamples. A stereotype should create tension, opportunity or injustice—not become a rule of the world.

## Cross-regional marriage

Mixed families are especially useful because they reveal culture as negotiated rather than pure.

Possible points of friction:

- which ritual occurs first;
- whose family name is used;
- where the couple settles;
- what language children learn;
- whether inheritance law follows birthplace, residence or family custom;
- whose food is “normal” at home;
- whether an old regional insult becomes impossible to use casually after marriage.

Port and border towns should contain generations of families for whom the supposedly “mixed” practice is simply local tradition.

## What people avoid saying openly

Power changes prejudice.

A person may privately resent a region's naval, military, financial or religious influence while avoiding the insult in front of someone who can retaliate economically or socially.

That gap between private joke and public speech can carry more worldbuilding than a paragraph of exposition.

## Scene-use checks

Before using a regional social detail, ask:

- Who believes this?
- What experience taught them?
- Who in the same region would disagree?
- Is the belief current, old-fashioned or local?
- Does the scene contain evidence that complicates it?
- Is this a character fact, or have I accidentally written it as world fact?
