# Contested Historical Memory

## Status

**Provisional historical texture.** The remembered conflicts and labels below are useful in-world material, but their exact dates, participants, territorial outcomes and official names are **not locked canon**.

[Pre-Convergence History](Pre-Convergence.md) remains the owner for the period and requires messy, overlapping politics rather than clean region-versus-region wars.

## Purpose

People do not inherit one neutral history.

The same old campaign may survive as:

- a patriotic victory in one region;
- an atrocity story in another;
- a family migration story at the border;
- a footnote in a Highridge archive;
- a legal precedent in a Port court;
- a song whose details no historian accepts literally.

These disagreements create useful “breath” without requiring a hidden conspiracy.

## Working memory labels

### “The Ember Wars” / “The Burning Years”

A recurring Greenvale memory concerns Ironcrest-linked expansion, logging, burned settlements and disputed border land.

Possible perspectives:

- **Greenvale folk memory:** orchards and villages destroyed; families displaced.
- **Ironcrest patriotic memory:** a period of expansion and hard-won security, with reversals blamed on logistics or weather.
- **Border memory:** several campaigns, changing alliances and mixed families that make both regional versions too simple.

Treat the label as a later cultural shorthand until the actual campaigns are mapped.

### “The Salted Coast Disputes”

Northwind and Sunplains traditions remember old violence over fishing waters, ports and maritime access.

Possible surviving memories include:

- Northwind songs about burned boats or coastal settlements;
- Sunplains stories about raids and “northern pirates”;
- Port traditions that present negotiation and neutral anchorage as older than the Convergence.

Do not assume this was one continuous war or that modern regional governments existed in their current form.

### “The Timber Tithes” / Deepwood closure traditions

Deepwood memory contains older stories about outside extraction pushing too far into forests and sacred or protected areas.

Outsider traditions may remember the same period as sudden Deepwood violence against workers, caravans or settlers.

This is a strong candidate for explaining later suspicion around logging rights, but the event should remain entangled with local factions, trade partners and ecological conditions.

### “The Port Seizure”

One uploaded tradition proposed a period when Northwind interests controlled Port before coalition pressure produced withdrawal and neutral status.

That is **not settled chronology**.

Useful surviving forms:

- some Northwind families claim they built or maintained facilities others later took from them;
- some Port families remember foreign control as occupation;
- some merchants use the story as a warning about allowing any one power to dominate the harbor.

[Port](../Places/Port.md) remains the owner for the city's current institutional history. Since [D28](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions), Port was the Sea People's city before the other nations took it after a war ([Port](../Places/Port.md#the-sea-peoples-city-d28)); this tradition may be one people's memory of that loss. Since [D30](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions), Port is all that's left of that empire, so Northwind's claim stays what it was: a contested memory, not the empire's.

### Other remembered labels

Region pages hold two more: Greenvale's [Seven-year Blight](../Regions/Greenvale.md), "whether exactly seven years or later mythologized" (the Cellar Song's seven verses may be what fixed the number: [The Eighth Verse](../Sunday-Morning/Stories/The-Eighth-Verse.md)), and Deepwood's [Ages of Silence](../Regions/Deepwood.md#historical-identity). [Pre-Convergence](Pre-Convergence.md) keeps a separate bank of war names; the likely pairs are the Great Coastal Skirmish with the Salted Coast Disputes and the Iron-Field Conquest with the Ember Wars. Each region probably keeps one "never again" memory; the legacy notes propose the other four (the Great Foundry Fires, the Long Freeze, the Great Caravan Unification and the City-State Renaissance), still unreconciled ([backlog](../Reference/README.md#legacy-reconciliation-backlog)).

## Writing rule

When using these memories:

1. identify **who** is telling the story;
2. separate the event from the later label;
3. allow local exceptions and contradictory evidence;
4. prefer claims such as “my grandfather called it…” over omniscient exposition;
5. let ruins, treaties, family records and place names disagree with popular stories.

A remembered history can be emotionally true to a community without being an objective account.
