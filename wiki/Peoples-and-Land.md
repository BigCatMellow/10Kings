# Peoples and Land

## Status

**Working canon** for the principle and bt 7274's own examples ([D43](Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions), 2026-11-13). **Working canon** too for how rule works on mixed ground, which bt 7274 agreed to (D44). **Provisional** for everything marked *lean*, which is the reviser's working-out and waits on bt 7274.

This page owns **how the peoples sit on the ground**: where each tradition is thick or thin, where they mix, how land is held across those lines, and how a map should show it. It doesn't own identity ([Social Fabric](Social-Fabric.md)), jurisdiction ([Law and Jurisdiction](Law-and-Jurisdiction.md)), political forms ([Kingdoms and Politics](Politics/Kingdoms-and-Politics.md)), speech ([Language Families and Contact](Language-Families-and-Contact.md)) or the regions' own descriptions (the six region pages). It links to them.

## The principle

In bt 7274's words:

> "I had always partially imagined the world as not like a cartoonish, here is the desert, here is the forest, but as a more real world where the different peoples are more blended. Like, there are obviously some areas that are more of a certain type, but like where there are dense forests would be deepwood people, or greenvale on the farming lands, ironcrest in the mining areas, etc. And then like Northwind is northern and costal, etc. And then again obviously it wouldnt be like a comically hard line, the ironcrest people would have some farm land and the sunplains people would have some mining lands they might hire people or lease out to."

So:

- **A people is tied to a kind of land, not to a shape on a map.** Where the land is dense old forest, most people are Deepwood people. Where it's farmland, most are Greenvale. In the mining hills, most are Ironcrest; on the cold northern coasts, Northwind.
- **The lines are soft.** One people thins out as another thickens, and the change takes a valley, a market, a generation of marriages.
- **Land is held across the lines.** Ironcrest people hold farmland. Sunplains people hold mining land, which they lease out or work with hired crews.

The wiki already said most of this. [World Rules](World-Rules.md) 1 to 3 say regions are ecosystems, geography causes culture without dictating it, and borders are gradients. [Social Fabric](Social-Fabric.md#regional-identity-is-not-residence) says regional identity is not residence. [Kingdoms and Politics](Politics/Kingdoms-and-Politics.md#principle) says "region" is a cultural-geographic term. [Law and Jurisdiction](Law-and-Jurisdiction.md#15-cultural-borders-and-political-borders-do-not-match) says cultural and political borders don't match. [Language](Language-Families-and-Contact.md#6-dialect-continua-should-exist) has dialect continua. The Atlas maps were the outlier: they drew each region as a solid block. This page puts the principle on the ground, so maps and stories can follow it.

## Three layers that don't line up

Every place has three answers, and they needn't agree:

1. **The land.** What the ground is and what it offers: forest, farmland, ore hills, plateau, coast, irrigated basin. This is what draws people and shapes their work ([World Rules 2](World-Rules.md#2-geography-must-cause-culture-without-dictating-it)).
2. **The people.** Which traditions live there, and how thick each is. A valley can be mostly Greenvale with an Ironcrest smithing quarter and two Deepwood families who came for the charcoal trade.
3. **The rule.** Who claims authority over the place, and over which matters: tax, land title, roads, crime, marriage. Often several claimants at once ([Law and Jurisdiction](Law-and-Jurisdiction.md#3-jurisdiction-is-layered)).

A region's name can mean any of the three, and the sentence usually tells you which. "Ironcrest country" is the land. "An Ironcrest family" is the people. "Ironcrest's tax-takers" is the rule. Keep them apart in writing, and a map draws them as separate layers.

## Where each people is thickest

The land each people is thickest on, where they're also found, and what they thin into. A people's land explains its famous specialty, not everything it does ([World Rules 1](World-Rules.md#1-regions-are-ecosystems-not-gimmicks)): every region also has farms, markets, herders, poor districts and old estates.

| People | Thickest where the land is | Also found | Thins into |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Northwind** | cold northern coasts, fjords, islands, sheltered inland valleys ([Northwind](Regions/Northwind.md)) | ship crews and port quarters on every coast; a Northwind family four generations in a Sunplains port ([Social Fabric](Social-Fabric.md#regional-identity-is-not-residence)) | the cold high country where the plateau begins, at Icestep |
| **Highridge** | plateau, passes, grazing uplands, caravan corridors ([Highridge](Regions/Highridge-Plateau.md)) | caravan towns, inns and waystations on every route; clerks and translators in Port | everyone's edges, wherever the roads run |
| **Ironcrest** | mineral hills, the valleys under them, charcoal country ([Ironcrest](Regions/Ironcrest.md)) | foundry and smithing quarters in market towns; **farmland it holds in the foothills and lowlands** (bt 7274) | Greenvale's farms through foothills (Stonefield Forge), Deepwood's forest margins (Darkroot Gulch) |
| **Deepwood** | old forest, wooded river valleys, wetlands, clearings ([Deepwood](Regions/Deepwood.md)) | forest communities far outside the core ([Social Fabric](Social-Fabric.md#regional-identity-is-not-residence)); healers in the cities | Ironcrest's charcoal country, Greenvale's woodland margins, the plateau routes (Twilighthollow) |
| **Greenvale** | temperate lowlands, river valleys, productive farmland ([Greenvale](Regions/Greenvale.md)) | milling families on distant rivers; tenant farmers on other peoples' land | foothills, forest edges, warmer drier country (Harveston Vale) |
| **Sunplains** | warm dry plains, irrigated basins, orchard belts, city-state coasts ([Sunplains](Regions/Sunplains.md)) | merchant houses and ships' companies everywhere; **mining land it holds, leased out or worked by hired crews** (bt 7274) | Greenvale's mixed farmland, where grain gives way to orchard and irrigation |
| **Port** | the harbor city itself ([Port](Places/Port.md)) | wherever Port's ships, clerks and debts reach | belongs to no kingdom: "to be a citizen of Port is to be a citizen of nowhere" |

## Heartlands, edges, and mixing places

How thick a people is, roughly, and what each kind of place feels like (*lean*, built from [Social Fabric](Social-Fabric.md#mixed-communities-create-local-culture) and [Language](Language-Families-and-Contact.md#15-border-towns-create-stable-mixed-speech)):

- **Heartland.** One tradition sets the custom, the speech and the holidays. Other peoples are there as minorities: a trading family, a smith, a healer, a crew in for the season. A heartland scene still has outsiders in it.
- **Edge country.** Two or three traditions share the valleys. Markets run in mixed speech, families marry across, and food and building methods blend. The locals often defend their mixed way against both source cultures. The border towns ([Border Towns](Places/Border-Towns.md)) are the thickest edge country.
- **Mixing places.** Towns where the routes meet pull every people in: Port above all, then caravan towns, river markets, mining towns and old garrison towns like [Seven Hill](Places/Seven-Hill.md).
- **People who move.** Seasonal crews, herders, menders, pilgrims and circuits like the Ridge-Roamers' carry each people through the others' country every year ([Nomads](Culture/Nomads.md), [Social Fabric](Social-Fabric.md#social-circulation-follows-physical-circulation)).

The edge between two peoples can be thinner where the land changes sharply, like a ridge or a forest wall. It is wider where it changes slowly, as foothills into lowland do.

## Holding land across the lines

**Working canon (bt 7274):** Ironcrest people hold some farmland. Sunplains people hold some mining land, which they lease out or work with hired crews.

How that works, as a *lean* to check against him. Nothing here names a house, a guild or a treaty.

- **Who holds.** A family, a merchant house, a guild, a temple or an old lineage. Holding usually comes from purchase, marriage, an old grant, a debt called in, or a claim that outlasted a war ([World Rules 4](World-Rules.md#4-history-is-messy): "one lineage still claims a field it has not controlled for 140 years").
- **Who works it.** Mostly the people who live there: tenants, hired hands, seasonal crews. Ironcrest-held fields are usually worked by Greenvale tenants. A Sunplains-held mine is worked by Ironcrest crews under a Sunplains steward, or leased to an Ironcrest mine-holder for a share.
- **Why.** Each people wants what its own land lacks. Ironcrest's mining towns need grain they can count on, so Ironcrest houses buy fields. Sunplains city-states have money and want metal, so they buy or lease mining rights.
- **What it makes.** Absentee owners and resident workers. Rents and shares flowing one way, resentment the other. Disputes over water, timber, tolls and mine boundaries ([Law 14](Law-and-Jurisdiction.md#14-borders-create-jurisdictional-ambiguity)). Hiring prejudice, like "Ironcrest crews are better miners" ([Regional Social Dynamics](Culture/Regional-Social-Dynamics.md#hiring-prejudice)). And families who are Greenvale by kin and Ironcrest by tenancy.

This is ordinary, small-scale texture, not a system. Most holdings are a few fields, a mill or one mine, and most are known only locally.

## Rule over mixed ground

**Working canon** (bt 7274 agreed, 2026-11-13, [D44](Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)): "That sounds good, and sounds like it could make things interesting / complicated." It applies [Law and Jurisdiction](Law-and-Jurisdiction.md) to the land.

- **A ruler's authority is strong at the heart and thin at the edges.** Claims run along what can be held: towns, roads, passes, fords, mines and markets, not lines. Highridge's page already says borders there "followed control of roads more often than neat territory".
- **The law over the land follows whoever holds it**: land title, rent, taxes on the harvest or the ore. A Sunplains-held mine pays its rent to Sunplains, and whatever tax the local ruler can collect on top.
- **Personal law follows the person**: marriage, inheritance, faith, guild discipline ([Law 6](Law-and-Jurisdiction.md#6-membership-matters)). A Greenvale tenant on Ironcrest-held land marries by Greenvale custom, pays rent to an Ironcrest house, and owes tax to whichever ruler holds the valley. These can disagree.
- **Lines on paper exist, but people live across them.** The Convergence made borders clearer ([World Rules 3](World-Rules.md#3-borders-are-gradients)), and treaties fixed some, like the Treaty Line. A fixed line settles where taxes are collected, not who lives there.

## What this means for "borders"

The relations on [Geography](Geography-and-Connections.md#strongly-established-regional-relationships) ("Ironcrest ↔ Greenvale", and the rest) mean **where two peoples live side by side, and where their rulers' claims touch**. They are contact zones, not fence lines. A border town is where a contact zone is thickest.

This undoes any argument that treats the regions as tiles on a map. The [lore map](../atlas/lore-map/README.md)'s "Ironcrest has no coast" (D42) came from tiles. With soft edges, four peoples can all meet in one shared stretch of country, and nothing forces Ironcrest inland. Port matters because of geography: the best harbor, a river mouth, where the sea routes and the Spine's roads meet. No neighbor's borders are needed to explain it.

## How a map should show it

*Lean*, for the redraw when bt 7274 asks:

1. **The land as the base.** Forest, farmland, mining hills, plateau, irrigated country, coast, drawn as land, not as regions.
2. **The peoples as soft colour.** Each people's colour is strongest in its heartland and fades through edge country. Colours overlap in mixing places, and Port shows every colour faintly. No filled shapes, no hard edges.
3. **Rule as light marks.** Seats of power, claimed towns, roads and passes, treaty lines where they exist, and contested stretches. Each of these can be shown or hidden.
4. **Holdings as small marks.** Where it matters to a story, show a held field, mine or estate in the holder's colour inside another people's country.

Both current [Atlas](../atlas/README.md) maps draw filled regions, so both predate this page.

## Writing it

A quick check for scenes, alongside [Social Fabric's scene test](Social-Fabric.md#scene-test):

- In a heartland scene, someone from elsewhere is still present.
- In an edge scene, show at least one mixed thing: food, speech, a building, a marriage, a law.
- When land is held across a line, know who holds it and who works it, even if the scene never says.
- Use a people's name for the people, and a ruler's name for the rule. "Greenvale" alone is the land and its people, not a state.
