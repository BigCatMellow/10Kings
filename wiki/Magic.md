# Magic and Relics

## Status

**Working canon.** James decided on 2026-09-29 that magic in Two Sons is **real but deniable**. The rules below follow from that choice. Named relics and their stories are **Provisional** examples unless another page establishes them.

This page owns magic, relics and supernatural claims. [World Rules §7](World-Rules.md#7-magic-is-scarce-uncertain-limited-or-costly) sets the principle; this page answers the questions it asks.

## The decision

A handful of old relics really do something: small, moral effects, like a lantern that dims when its holder lies or ink that smudges on a dishonest contract. But every effect could have an ordinary explanation, and **the story never confirms it**. Nobody casts spells, there is no magic trade, and nothing supernatural walks the world.

This keeps the older vision from the project's notes (magic that is "subtle, rare, and morally resonant", with relics as communal "moral barometers") while keeping the rest of the wiki's naturalism: Aurora Nights beliefs stay beliefs, Underpass creatures stay ecology, Deepwood speech doesn't imply supernatural wisdom.

## How the world-rule questions are answered

| World Rules asks | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Who can use it? | Nobody *uses* it. A relic responds, or seems to, to the people around it. There are no mages, no trained practitioners and no inherited gift. |
| How reliable is it? | Not at all on demand. Effects are occasional, small and never repeatable under test; the same relic may do nothing for a generation. |
| What does it cost? | Nothing measurable. The price is social: keeping a relic means keeping its people honest about it, and a relic that "fails" a respected person causes a crisis. |
| Why hasn't it transformed every industry? | Because nothing about it can be scaled, sold, taught or relied on. A relic can't be copied, and its effects can't be told apart from chance, suggestion or fraud. |
| How do ordinary people understand it? | Mostly through faith, custom and argument. Most people have never seen a relic do anything; many doubt any ever did; almost everyone knows a story. Frauds and fakes are common, and exposing them is a respected craft. |

## Hard limits

- **No spellcasting.** No incantations, rituals that reliably work, wizards, witches or magical schools.
- **Small and moral only.** A relic's effect is perceptual or symbolic at human scale: a light dims or warms, water tastes bitter, ink smudges, a blade feels heavier, a needle wavers. It never moves armies, heals the dying, kills, flies, or changes the weather.
- **Always deniable.** Every effect has a plausible ordinary explanation in reach: nerves, a draft, a guilty hand, bad ink, a clever keeper.
- **Never confirmed.** Narration stays agnostic. Characters may be certain; the story is not.
- **Never load-bearing.** No plot turn may depend on a relic's effect being real. If the relic did nothing, the scene still has to work.
- **Not industrial.** No relic powers a machine, a forge, a ship or a city.
- **Magic cannot erase logistics** ([World Rules §5](World-Rules.md#5-low-fantasy-means-material-limits-matter)).

## Relics

Relics are old objects kept by communities, not individuals: temples, shrines, guild halls, villages. They belong to the [faith traditions](Politics/Religions.md) and regions that made them, and their stories carry that culture's values. A relic's supposed effect is always a moral one: it answers sincerity, honesty, care or restraint.

Examples from the project's earlier notes, kept as **Provisional** texture:

- **The Quill of Saint Othriel.** A crow-feather quill said to keep written agreements honest: the ink smudges when a signer means to deceive. Its saint is remembered for negotiating fair grain distribution during a famine.
- **The Chalice of Mother Amalthea.** A worn clay chalice, warm to the touch, said to give a moment's calm when shared, and to turn water bitter for anyone withholding care from the sick.
- **A lantern that dims at lies, a blade that grows heavier with needless killing, a navigation charm that wavers without true purpose.** The old notes' three patterns for what a relic does.

## Everything else that has been claimed

The Open Questions page asked whether earlier "illusions", magical artifacts, special creatures and supernatural claims are real, misunderstood, religious interpretation, or should go. The answer, case by case:

| Claim | Disposition |
| --- | --- |
| Moral relics and artifacts | **Real but deniable**, as above. |
| "Illusions" (veils, dream-knives, refracted lights, sects that saw through lies) | **Legend and stagecraft.** Stories told about vanished sects and old relics; in the present, illusions are rumor, performance or fraud. |
| Special creatures (sea monsters, drakes, forest beasts) | **Animals, misidentified.** Real animals, rare or strange ones included, seen badly and retold well. Underpass "monsters" are ecological disruption ([The Underpass](Places/The-Underpass.md#current-events)). |
| Spirits, ancestors, aurora omens | **Belief.** Treated as faith and custom, never as proof ([Festivals](Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#northwind)). |
| Mystical armies, forest mages, living-root peoples from earlier notes | **Removed.** They conflict with the low-fantasy frame and aren't carried forward. |

## Writing rule

When magic appears in a scene:

1. let a character believe, doubt or exploit it;
2. put an ordinary explanation within reach, even if nobody takes it;
3. keep the effect small enough that a sceptic could shrug;
4. never let the narration settle it.

The interesting question is never "is it real?" but "what do these people do because they believe it is, or isn't?"
