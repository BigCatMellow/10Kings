# Applying the Framework to Two Sons

## Status

**Writing reference — not setting canon.**

This page explains how to use the [Sunday Morning Story Writing Framework](Framework.md) inside the Two Sons setting. It does not add facts about the world. Where it names places, festivals, dishes or language features, the linked owner pages remain authoritative, and their own status labels still apply.

## Why the setting supports this mode

Two Sons is built around a continental-scale engine: the [Economic Council](../Politics/Economic-Council.md), the [Villain's Dominoes](../Story/Villains-Dominoes.md) and the escalation described in [Main Conflict](../Story/Main-Conflict.md). Sunday Morning stories are that engine seen from the ground: the places where a domino lands on ordinary people who never learn it was one.

That works because the setting's *texture* is already human-scale:

- [Wurdren](../Story/Wurdren.md) exists to make large systems specific again: the widow, the apprentice, the farmer whose storehouse is empty.
- [World Rules](../World-Rules.md) rule 20 says the world exists when the protagonists are absent, and that a town may solve its own problem before Wurdren arrives.
- [Worldbuilding Breath](../Reference/Worldbuilding-Breath.md) asks what was happening yesterday that has nothing to do with the plot.
- [Border Towns](../Places/Border-Towns.md) are contained social ecosystems with their own food, accents, festivals and quarrels.

Sunday Morning stories live in that texture layer.

## The world-tie rule: connected, not driven

*Changed 2026-09-27 at James's direction. The earlier rule kept the main conflict as background only; Sunday Morning stories now belong to the domino web.*

Every Sunday Morning story is a **node in the [Villain's Dominoes](../Story/Villains-Dominoes.md) and [Current Events](../Story/Current-Events.md) web**, told from the ground where the people involved cannot see the web. This is the setting's own design: the plot moves through causal handoffs, and [Wurdren](../Story/Wurdren.md) (and ordinary people like him) change the larger game "without understanding it at first" ([Main Conflict](../Story/Main-Conflict.md#plot-movement)).

At story scale the domino is always a **nail**, the smallest kind: a letter, a rumor, a delayed cart, a tightened loan, an offer to buy. The legacy domino notes use this term for the tier of moves that go unnoticed because they look like ordinary local trouble; the [Source Register](../Reference/Source-Register.md) records that those notes are evidence, not canon.

### What every story must carry

| Element | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Current event | At least one [Current Events](../Story/Current-Events.md) item shapes the story's situation, not just its dialogue. |
| Nail | One small, concrete domino-scale pressure lands in the community. |
| Independent reasons | Every step has an ordinary reason a participant would give ([World Rules §10](../World-Rules.md#10-the-villains-schemes-must-survive-ordinary-scrutiny)). |
| Attribution | Who is behind the nail — Villain, Council, ordinary life, or `UNKNOWN` — recorded **out of story**, and provisional. Not everything is the Villain; Current Events says the distinction is essential. |
| Local outcome | The nail falls or doesn't because locals act for local reasons ([World Rules §20](../World-Rules.md#20-the-world-exists-when-the-protagonists-are-absent)). |
| Outward effect | The second- and third-order effect beyond the story, including how the Villain or Council could use it ([World Rules §12](../World-Rules.md#12-every-major-event-should-have-second--and-third-order-effects)). |

### What keeps it Sunday Morning

- **Stakes on the page stay local.** The domino web raises what the outcome *means*, not what the characters risk.
- **Nobody in the story sees the pattern.** Characters may repeat a rumor that explains things badly; no one exposes the Council or the Villain.
- **The Villain never appears on the page as himself**, and the Council never acts as a named body. Domino characters (Orin, Maris, Rosana, Samir, Naruin, Bahriyya) may appear or be mentioned as ordinary people.
- **No on-page harm from the scheme.** Hardship can exist as context (a displaced family, a lost contract), never as the story.
- **Readers of the saga get the irony; Sunday readers don't need it.** Deleting the thread should leave a working story; keeping it should make the story matter more.
- **Nothing here fixes the Villain's identity, grievance or plan.** Those stay [open](../Open-Questions.md#villain).

The cross-story view — calendar, ripple chain and per-story threads — is [World Threads](World-Threads.md). Each story page owns its own thread.

## Wurdren in Sunday Morning stories

Wurdren can headline Sunday Morning stories. They suit him, because his arc already moves toward small situations.

Keep the canonical constraints:

- He is not the smartest person in the room, the rightful anything, or chosen.
- His influence comes from relationships and credibility, not speeches or strategy.
- His [weaknesses](../Story/Wurdren.md#character-weakness) — regret over an unremarkable career, the desire to be needed, nostalgia for simpler heroism — work well as gentle comedy and quiet heart.
- His exact age, biography, family and starting point remain [open questions](../Open-Questions.md). A Sunday Morning story should not settle them in passing.

Most Sunday Morning stories should **not** star Wurdren. Rule 20 matters: the world is full of people with their own small problems.

## Festivals as deadlines

A festival gives a story a date, a reason for the whole cast to converge (§10 Act III), and a built-in soft landing (the festival itself).

Good pairings from the [seasonal calendar](../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md):

- **Forge Reawakening** — apprenticeship judgments, relighting, remembrance.
- **Pass Opening / Ice Breaking** — first caravans, first launches, inspections.
- **Canopy Vigil / Midsummer Debates** — enforced stillness beside enforced argument.
- **Harvest Home / Wine Crush** — public accounting, competition between houses.
- **Three Moon Festival** — Port proving incompatible people can share one space.
- **Last Sail / Ledger Closing** — end-of-season settling of boats and debts.

Remember the calendar's own rule: people should argue about how a festival **used to be** celebrated.

## Language habits as running jokes

The grammatical habits in [Language and Thought](../Culture/Language-and-Thought.md) make excellent recurring bits because they are character habits, not jokes about a people:

- Ironcrest speakers distinguishing *attempted*, *completed*, *inspected* and *guaranteed* — applied to swords and to breakfast alike.
- Northwind evidentials: *I saw it* / *someone I trust told me* / *it is said*.
- Northwind inclusive versus exclusive "we" — who is being counted in.
- Highridge exact quotation and conditional promises.
- Deepwood animacy categories — whether a tree, stream or path is a participant.
- Sunplains indirect refusal and face-saving public speech.

Guardrail: the habit belongs to the speaker, not the region. Show counterexamples, and never let the habit imply a people is smarter, colder or more spiritual.

## Stakes calibration

"Human-scale" in this setting usually means one of:

- a local institution's honor (a public scale, a guild judgment, a regatta bell);
- a festival going ahead, or going ahead properly;
- a family stall, permit, workshop or boat;
- a feud, lien or precedent that everyone has quietly outgrown;
- someone's place in a community (the outsider, the apprentice, the in-law).

Material limits from [World Rules](../World-Rules.md) (rule 5) are the best complication generator: mud, late carts, cold, short timber, fuel, a flooded cellar. They create inconvenience without catastrophe.

## Rules that still apply

The Sunday Morning mode relaxes stakes, not worldbuilding discipline.

- **Regions are ecosystems** (rule 1). A cove town has woodworkers who aren't fishers; a forge town has farmers.
- **Stereotypes are character evidence** ([Regional Social Dynamics](../Culture/Regional-Social-Dynamics.md)). "Soft as Greenvale butter" can be said to someone; the story should prove it wrong.
- **Institutions have constituencies** (rule 8). A guild in a Sunday Morning story still has a stickler, a reformer and someone who just wants lunch.
- **Magic stays scarce** (rule 7). Most of these stories need none.
- **History is messy** (rule 4). An old document, feud or festival split should have a plausible, human origin.

## Checklist addendum

Use the framework's [checklist](Framework.md#20-the-sunday-morning-story-checklist) first, then:

- [ ] Does the story carry a current event, a nail, an out-of-story attribution, a local outcome and an outward effect ([world-tie rule](#the-world-tie-rule-connected-not-driven))?
- [ ] Would the story still work with the thread deleted, and matter more with it kept?
- [ ] Does no character see the pattern, and does the Villain stay off the page?
- [ ] Is the setting one community, not a region?
- [ ] Is there a festival, season or material limit acting as the clock?
- [ ] Does at least one language or food habit recur as a running element?
- [ ] Does any stereotype appear only as a character's belief, and get complicated?
- [ ] Are new names, towns and customs marked **Provisional**?
- [ ] Does the story avoid settling an item in [Open Questions](../Open-Questions.md)?

## Promotion rule

Story pages in this folder are provisional working material. A detail invented for a story — a town, a dish, a custom, a character — does **not** become setting canon by appearing here.

If a detail should graduate, promote it into its owner page (for example [Border Towns](../Places/Border-Towns.md) or [Festivals and Seasonal Life](../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md)) with an explicit status, and link back.
