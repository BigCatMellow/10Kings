## How this was made

**THINK**, as Pilot Projects runs it ([THINK project](https://github.com/BigCatMellow/Pilot_Projects/blob/main/ai-creativity-and-ideation/THINK_PROJECT.md)):
- **The fixed four methods on one path** (decomposition, assumption mapping, first principles, counterexample search). This is the control the research kept, because a dynamic or wider search didn't earn its cost.
- **Reserve methods** only where a failure signal called for one: a frame challenge, inversion, and a perspective shift to the Villain's and the Council's desks.
- **For the Villain question**, the ideation pattern from its [research hub](https://github.com/BigCatMellow/Pilot_Projects/blob/main/ai-creativity-and-ideation/README.md): generate widely, keep the alternatives apart, judge each separately, then choose.

**PLAN**, as MAPS_L's project bootstrap does it ([PLAN baseline](https://github.com/BigCatMellow/Pilot_Projects/blob/main/ai-planning-and-orchestration/MAPSL_PLAN_BASELINE.md)):
- Inspect what exists, define DONE, plan backward from the end, then forward.
- Set the **planning resolution**:
  - the destination precise;
  - the current phase (Season 2) detailed;
  - the first wave (The Same Paper, then Season 2's batch) bounded;
  - later phases (Seasons 3 to 5) directional, refined as evidence comes in.
- At each season boundary, choose CONTINUE, CHANGE, CUT SCOPE, RESEARCH or STOP.
- Keep the frame, alternatives, assumptions, unknowns and reconsideration triggers, so a later planner doesn't flatten a live branch.

**The Sunday story method doesn't change** ([D22](Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)). Every story on the slates still runs the [pipeline](Pipeline.md) from Stage 0 on its own page, with a test story before each batch.

## The destination (DONE)

Main Conflict's [possible resolution model](../../Story/Main-Conflict.md#possible-resolution-model) is the end goal ([D18](Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)). Made precise for the stories:

- **The political end.** A limited war at a border has pushed the kingdoms to talks, and Port is the only ground they'll all accept. The Villain forces the Council into the open: it has to be seen, because the talks can't end the war without its grain, credit and routes. His last escalation makes the human cost undeniable, and his coalition breaks. The outcome is one neither side intended: public negotiations, some Council mechanisms exposed, institutional reform, and a concession that meets the legitimate part of his grievance. *(Free City lean: a new Port charter that gives the Port-born a real voice in their own city.)* Nobody wins outright.
- **Wurdren's end** ([D18](Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)). He matters to one person, then to more. He's at the talks, or near them, because of the people who trust him. He doesn't lead. What he contributes is that the elites can no longer treat ordinary people as pieces.
- **How it's told.** From the ground, like every other story. Once the Council is public news, people react to it the way they react to any news; they're hearing it, not working it out, so [D2](Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions) holds (the [Long Road lean](The-Long-Road.md#the-seasons-loosely)).
- **The last story** is "in the voice of the first story": back at the Vale of [One Square, Two Harvests](../Stories/One-Square-Two-Harvests.md), at Harvest Home, five years on, where the news from Port arrives by letter.
- **The reader at the end** can say what the stories never said: who was behind the offers, what he wanted, why the Council let him get so far, and why it mattered that a lot of small places held.

## THINK

### Frame

*Frame challenge: is this a novel told in pieces, or an anthology with a spine?*

Neither, exactly. It's a **linked cycle in which the reader is the main story's only detective**. Every story stands alone and nobody in it sees the pattern ([D21](Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)), so the main plot exists only in what the reader sets side by side. Wurdren carries the heart; the reader carries the plot. That one decision governs everything below. The plan has to control **what the reader can see, and when**, not what the characters do.

### Decomposition: seven strands

| Strand | What it is | Who carries it on the page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The Villain's campaign | the cause: nails, then horseshoes, then steeds | nobody; it shows only as its effects |
| 2. The Council's countermeasures | the second hand: credit, food, routes, guilds | clerks, cooks, borrowers; never the Council as a body |
| 3. The world's escalation | the weather, one rung a season ([escalation](../../Story/Main-Conflict.md#escalation)) | everyone, as prices, letters and sons called up |
| 4. What the reader can piece together | the plot, one or two inferences a season | the placement of stories side by side |
| 5. Wurdren | the heart and the signal ([D19](Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)) | Wurdren, from time to time |
| 6. The people | the earlier protagonists, who become his network | Returns, Threads, cameos kept small |
| 7. The ending | public, but from the ground | Season 5 |

The strands must stay **separable**: each story carries at most one or two, and strand 4 is made by the order, not by any one story.

### The Villain: alternatives, judged separately

The question: what homeland and grievance make a Villain whose campaign the stories can carry from the ground, who fits [Villain](../../Story/Villain.md) and the resolution model, and who uses what the wiki and the first two seasons already hold?

Six alternatives were kept apart and each judged on the same six tests:
1. **Grievance:** can readers understand it?
2. **Ending fit:** does it suit "concessions/autonomy" in the [resolution model](../../Story/Main-Conflict.md#possible-resolution-model)?
3. **Canon:** does it use canon that already exists, without new lore?
4. **Off the page:** can the stories carry it while he stays off the page?
5. **Planted:** what have Seasons 1 and 2 already planted for it?
6. **Risk:** what could go wrong with it?

**A. The Free City: he is Port-born.** *(Lean.)*

*The Danzig mapping:*

| Danzig | Port |
| --- | --- |
| A city made neutral because no power would let a rival hold it | "It survives because every major power fears what would happen if a rival controlled it" ([Port](../../Places/Port.md#neutrality)) |
| Under the protection of an outside body | Formalized by the [Convergence](../../History/The-Convergence.md#neutral-port), "treaty guarantees" |
| Demilitarized | "limits on kingdom troops inside the city" |
| A people who belonged to neither side | "people who identify as Port-born first" |
| Customs officers from a neighbor in its harbor | foreign enclaves, guild headquarters, envoys |
| A revanchist claim | Northwind families' memory of the [Port Seizure](../../History/Contested-Memory.md#the-port-seizure) |
| A landlocked state that depended on the port | every kingdom's trade depends on Port, and the inland ones most |

*The grievance:* a people with a city and no country. They're run by charter for everyone else's trade, and the dockside has the least say in the city it keeps working. Behind the guarantees he sees the [Council](../../Politics/Economic-Council.md), with the Routes seat held by House Veynor of Port, and he concludes that a city nobody owns is the city the Council runs best.

*His end:* Port answering to the Port-born. "Port-born first" is already a thing Port people say ([Port](../../Places/Port.md#culture)).

*His method,* from the Danzig alternate history: make the guarantors distrust each other until a neutral Port is the only ground they'll accept, then be the indispensable broker, with recognition as the price of peace. The sparks map onto the seasons:

| Danzig spark | Season | How it shows here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the doctored dispatch | 3 | a copied report |
| dock riots | 4 | offstage, the morning after |
| a staged border incident | 4 | — |
| a peace conference on neutral ground | 5 | — |

*How each test came out:*
1. **Grievance:** strong, and sympathetic at first ([Villain](../../Story/Villain.md#moral-trajectory)).
2. **Ending fit:** exact. "Autonomy" becomes a new charter.
3. **Canon:** almost all of it is already there.
4. **Off the page:** yes, because a sincere Port-born movement is the public face and he's inside it, steering its timing.
5. **Planted:** a great deal. Port paper bought through a Port stationer. A Port buyer for Rhona's bellows. Port insurance at step 2 of the [ripple chain](../../Story/Current-Events.md#example-ripple-chain). Three Pots' Port street. Port is everyone's stationer ([Records](../../Records-Literacy-and-Authentication.md#paper-and-provenance)), so the clue stays fair and deniable.
6. **Risk:** the Danzig history ends in fascist revanchism. None of that comes in. Port-born identity is civic and mixed, and that's the point: a mixed people asking for a say. The comparison is for mechanisms only, as the [Reference rules](../../Reference/Cultural-Research-Library.md) require.

**B. Northwind revanchism (the Port Seizure).** He's from the families who say they built Port and were pushed out.
- **For:** a strong grievance.
- **Against:** it pulls the plot toward a war for territory. It also makes Northwind the villain's people, which leans on the "northern pirates" stereotype that [World Rules](../../World-Rules.md) says may only be a character's belief.
- **Kept anyway:** as a grievance he plays, not his own.

**C. A frontier people the Convergence erased.** The Convergence "froze" borders, and "old mixed frontier identities were sometimes suppressed" ([The Convergence](../../History/The-Convergence.md#cultural-effect)).
- **For:** a good grievance that fits [Border Towns](../../Places/Border-Towns.md) and the "autonomy" ending.
- **Against:** a weaker link to the Port paper, and fewer stories planted for it.
- **Ranked second.** It would also combine well with A as a second grievance he courts.

**D. The Underpass born.** Families born underground, with no recognized sovereignty.
- **For:** it fits [D22](Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)'s push to use the Underpass.
- **Against:** the Convergence only "perhaps" covered the tunnels, so too much would have to be invented.
- **Ranked third.**

**E. A disinherited Council heir** (e.g. Appan Veynor). **Rejected.** It turns the saga into palace intrigue, and the Villain would know the Council from the inside, which empties the reader's detective work.

**F. A kingdom of his own** (the legacy's "broker peace to found his new kingdom"). **Rejected as written**, because one man's ambition isn't a grievance readers can share. A keeps its mechanism and gives it a people.

**Choice: A, as a lean for bt 7274.** C stays live as the alternative.

**Whether he knows the Council's structure at the start** ([open](../../Open-Questions.md#villain)). *Lean:* he knows the hidden order exists and that a Port house sits in it, but not all six seats. Seasons 3 and 4 then let him learn, the way the reader does.

### Inversion and counterexample search

*How would this saga fail?*

- **The reader works it out in Season 2.** The Port clues come too thick. Guard: one Port clue per season before Season 3, never two in one story, and Port is always also "everyone's stationer".
- **The reader never works it out.** The clues are too thin, or the order hides them. Guard: each season's expected reader state is checked by the cold read of a test story, and by the [shape check](Pipeline.md#collection-shape-check-for-a-set-of-stories).
- **The stories become a plot.** Each one starts serving the next, and stops standing alone. Guard: the delete-the-thread test, story by story ([Rules](Rules.md#what-keeps-it-sunday-morning)).
- **The tone darkens with the rungs.** Mobilization and war in Season 4 make readers brace. Guard: the war stays weather. Every Season 4 story is set where the war isn't: a market that won't repeat a story, a family feeding a stranger, a bell that rings. "No on-page harm from the scheme."
- **The ending is a speech.** Guard: the ending is told by a kitchen, a harbor clerk, a ferry and a village, not by a summit.
- **Wurdren becomes the protagonist.** Guard: two or three appearances a season, mostly in other people's stories ([Rules](Rules.md#wurdren-in-sunday-morning-stories)).
- **The cast reunion.** The network in Seasons 4 and 5 turns into a parade of cameos. Guard: [Collection](Collection.md#extension-to-fourteen-stories-2026-11-02)'s trigger, one or two earlier people per story.
- **Danzig imported whole.** Guard: mechanisms only. No corridor, no ethnic majority, no great-power dictator.

### At each season boundary

Before a season's batch is drafted, choose one, and record it on [Decisions](Decisions.md):
- **CONTINUE** with the slate.
- **CHANGE** stories (the garden grew elsewhere).
- **CUT SCOPE** to fewer stories.
- **RESEARCH** something the season needs (Season 3 needs the Villain's homeland; Season 4 needs near-war or limited war).
- **STOP.**

## For bt 7274

One question at a time, each with a lean. Season 2 doesn't need any of them; Season 3 needs the first.

1. **The Villain's homeland and grievance.** *Lean:* the Free City (A). He's Port-born, and wants Port to answer to the Port-born. The Danzig mechanisms are used, not the Danzig history. The alternative is the frontier people the Convergence erased (C).
2. **His end-state.** *Lean:* a Port with a real voice for its own people inside a renewed Convergence, which the ending can partly grant. The other choice is full sovereignty, the legacy alternate history's "Republic of Danzig", which the ending would refuse.
3. **What he knows at the start.** *Lean:* that the hidden order exists and a Port house sits in it, but not all six seats.
4. **Season 4.** *Lean:* a limited war at one border, felt from the towns.
5. **The Night Fare.** May the Villain be on the page once, at the very end, as an unnamed passenger in a waterman's boat? *Lean:* no, D21 holds. The passenger is someone else leaving Port that night, and the reader may wonder. If it's yes, it's the only time.
