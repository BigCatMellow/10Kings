# Rules

## Status

**Writing reference, not setting canon.** This page owns every must and must-not for a Sunday Morning story set in Two Sons: the tone guardrails, the world tie, canon discipline and the setting's own rules. It ends with the setting palette, a menu of places, festivals and habits to build from. It adds no facts about the world. Where it names places, festivals, dishes or language features, the linked owner pages stay authoritative, with their own status labels.

The [Framework](Sources/Framework.md) sets tone and stakes; this page applies it to Two Sons and records what the collection learned about keeping inside it. See the [order of authority](README.md#order-of-authority).

## Tone guardrails

The collection is Sunday Morning first ([Decision D9](Decisions.md)). Check every pass against the Framework's core promise, "You do not need to brace yourself", before checking it against the voice guide. The guide's "that was unexpectedly sad" is welcome only inside that promise.

- **One sad moment, said once.** Don't tell the same loss two or three ways. (The Goat File's estrangement was cut from three tellings to one.)
- **Then give the reader somewhere soft to land:** warmth, absurdity or food right after.
- **No public shaming.** When a character's mistake is revealed, lead with what they were doing right. (Brisa was holding the roof up before the swapped stone.)
- **Hardship is background, in one line.** No repeated "bad year" paragraphs from story to story. "Still fell elsewhere" gets one line, ideally carried by an object.
- **Stakes stay human-scale.** One tree, one stall, one boat. Characters don't list the region-wide damage. (The Tree's "every stand of forest" was cut.)
- **Every story has a meal, and a moment where nothing dramatic happens** (Framework §12 and §21).
- **Endings land warm.** A showman goes back to being a showman; a lonely morning ends with a dog to walk.

## The world-tie rule: connected, not driven

*Set 2026-09-27 at the author's direction ([D2](Decisions.md)). The earlier rule kept the main conflict as background only.*

Every Sunday Morning story is a **node in the [Villain's Dominoes](../../Story/Villains-Dominoes.md) and [Current Events](../../Story/Current-Events.md) web**, told from the ground, where the people involved can't see the web. This is the setting's own design: the plot moves through causal handoffs, and [Wurdren](../../Story/Wurdren.md) and ordinary people like him change the larger game "without understanding it at first" ([Main Conflict](../../Story/Main-Conflict.md#plot-movement)).

At story scale the domino is always a **nail**, the smallest kind: a letter, a rumor, a delayed cart, a tightened loan, an offer to buy. The legacy domino notes use the term for moves that go unnoticed because they look like ordinary local trouble. The [Source Register](../../Reference/Source-Register.md) records that those notes are evidence, not canon.

### What every story must carry

| Element | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Current event | At least one [Current Events](../../Story/Current-Events.md) item shapes the story's situation, not just its dialogue. |
| Nail | One small, concrete domino-scale pressure lands in the community. |
| Independent reasons | Every step has an ordinary reason a participant would give ([World Rules §10](../../World-Rules.md#10-the-villains-schemes-must-survive-ordinary-scrutiny)). |
| Attribution | Who is behind the nail (Villain, Council, ordinary life, or `UNKNOWN`), recorded **out of story** and provisional. Not everything is the Villain; Current Events says the distinction is essential. |
| Local outcome | The nail falls or doesn't because locals act for local reasons ([World Rules §20](../../World-Rules.md#20-the-world-exists-when-the-protagonists-are-absent)). |
| Outward effect | The second- and third-order effect beyond the story, including how the Villain or Council could use it ([World Rules §12](../../World-Rules.md#12-every-major-event-should-have-second--and-third-order-effects)). |

### What keeps it Sunday Morning

- **Stakes on the page stay local.** The domino web raises what the outcome *means*, not what the characters risk.
- **Nobody in the story sees the pattern.** Characters may repeat a rumor that explains things badly. No one exposes the Council or the Villain.
- **The Villain never appears on the page as himself,** and the Council never acts as a named body. Domino characters (Orin, Maris, Rosana, Samir, Naruin, Bahriyya) may appear or be mentioned as ordinary people.
- **No on-page harm from the scheme.** Hardship can be context (a displaced family, a lost contract), never the story.
- **Saga readers get the irony; Sunday readers don't need it.** Deleting the thread should leave a working story. Keeping it should make the story matter more.
- **Nothing here fixes the Villain's identity, grievance or plan.** Those stay [open](../../Open-Questions.md#villain).

Each story page owns its own Larger-world thread. The cross-story view (calendar, ripple chain, where the nail still fell) is on [Collection](Collection.md#the-web).

## Canon discipline

- Every story page is **Provisional**. Characters, new towns, businesses, customs and plot details invented for a story are not setting canon.
- Linked owner pages remain authoritative. The festivals, border-town details and recipes these stories use carry their own status labels there, and several are themselves provisional.
- Stories must not settle items in [Open Questions](../../Open-Questions.md), including Wurdren's biography.
- New small towns are fine. Name them with the logic in [Naming](../../Culture/Naming.md) and mark them **Provisional**.
- So far, no story detail has been promoted to canon.

### Promotion rule

A detail invented for a story (a town, a dish, a custom, a character) does **not** become setting canon by appearing in a story or a draft. If it should graduate, promote it into its owner page (for example [Border Towns](../../Places/Border-Towns.md) or [Festivals and Seasonal Life](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md)) with an explicit status, and link back.

## Wurdren in Sunday Morning stories

Wurdren can headline a Sunday Morning story; his arc already moves toward small situations. Keep the canonical constraints:

- He is not the smartest person in the room, the rightful anything, or chosen.
- His influence comes from relationships and credibility, not speeches or strategy.
- His [weaknesses](../../Story/Wurdren.md#character-weakness) (regret over an unremarkable career, the desire to be needed, nostalgia for simpler heroism) work well as gentle comedy and quiet heart.
- His exact age, biography, family and starting point remain [open questions](../../Open-Questions.md). A story should not settle them in passing. If a draft needs his past, stop and ask the author.

Most Sunday Morning stories should **not** star him. World Rules §20: the world exists when the protagonists are absent, and a town may solve its own problem before Wurdren arrives.

## Setting rules that still apply

The Sunday Morning mode relaxes stakes, not worldbuilding discipline ([World Rules](../../World-Rules.md)).

- **Regions are ecosystems** (rule 1). A cove town has woodworkers who aren't fishers; a forge town has farmers.
- **Stereotypes are character evidence** ([Regional Social Dynamics](../../Culture/Regional-Social-Dynamics.md)). "Soft as Greenvale butter" can be said to someone; the story should prove it wrong.
- **Institutions have constituencies** (rule 8). A guild still has a stickler, a reformer and someone who just wants lunch.
- **Magic stays scarce** (rule 7). Most of these stories need none.
- **History is messy** (rule 4). An old document, feud or festival split has a plausible, human origin.
- **Language habits belong to the speaker, not the region.** Show counterexamples. A habit never implies a people is smarter, colder or more spiritual.

## Checklist addendum

Run the Framework's [checklist](Sources/Framework.md#20-the-sunday-morning-story-checklist) first, then:

- [ ] Does the story carry a current event, a nail, an out-of-story attribution, a local outcome and an outward effect?
- [ ] Would the story still work with the thread deleted, and matter more with it kept?
- [ ] Does no character see the pattern, and does the Villain stay off the page?
- [ ] Is the setting one community, not a region?
- [ ] Is there a festival, season or material limit acting as the clock?
- [ ] Does at least one language or food habit recur as a running element?
- [ ] Does any stereotype appear only as a character's belief, and get complicated?
- [ ] Are new names, towns and customs marked **Provisional**, and registered in the [Registry](Registry.md#names)?
- [ ] Does the story avoid settling an item in [Open Questions](../../Open-Questions.md)?
- [ ] Would a reader ever need to brace? (See the tone guardrails.)

Sunday Morning stories show that engine from the ground, in the setting's texture layer: [Wurdren](../../Story/Wurdren.md) making large systems specific again, [World Rules](../../World-Rules.md) §20's world that exists without the protagonists, [Worldbuilding Breath](../../Reference/Worldbuilding-Breath.md)'s "what was happening yesterday", and the contained social ecosystems of the [Border Towns](../../Places/Border-Towns.md).

### Festivals as clocks

A festival gives a story a date, a reason for the cast to converge (Framework §10, Act III) and a built-in soft landing. Good pairings from the [seasonal calendar](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md):

- **Forge Reawakening:** apprenticeship judgments, relighting, remembrance.
- **Pass Opening / Ice Breaking:** first caravans, first launches, inspections.
- **Canopy Vigil / Midsummer Debates:** enforced stillness beside enforced argument.
- **Harvest Home / Wine Crush:** public accounting, competition between houses.
- **Three Moon Festival:** Port proving incompatible people can share one space.
- **Last Sail / Ledger Closing:** end-of-season settling of boats and debts.

The calendar's own rule: people argue about how a festival **used to be** celebrated.

### Language habits as running elements

From [Language and Thought](../../Culture/Language-and-Thought.md). They work because they are character habits, not jokes about a people.

- Ironcrest *attempted*, *completed*, *inspected* and *guaranteed*, applied to swords and breakfast alike.
- Northwind evidentials: *I saw it* / *someone I trust told me* / *it is said*.
- Northwind inclusive versus exclusive "we": who is being counted in.
- Highridge exact quotation and conditional promises.
- Deepwood animacy categories: whether a tree, stream or path is a participant.
- Sunplains indirect refusal and face-saving public speech.

All seven stories already use one of these as their running element; see the [Registry](Registry.md#devices-already-used) before reusing one.

### Stakes calibration

"Human-scale" here usually means one of:

- a local institution's honor (a public scale, a guild judgment, a regatta bell);
- a festival going ahead, or going ahead properly;
- a family stall, permit, workshop or boat;
- a feud, lien or precedent everyone has quietly outgrown;
- someone's place in a community (the outsider, the apprentice, the in-law).

Material limits ([World Rules](../../World-Rules.md) rule 5) are the best complication generator: mud, late carts, cold, short timber, fuel, a flooded cellar. They create inconvenience without catastrophe.
