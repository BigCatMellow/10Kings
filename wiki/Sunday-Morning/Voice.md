# Voice

## Status

**Writing reference — not setting canon.** This page owns how James's prose should sound. It has two sources:

1. **The author voice guide** (below, verbatim). James supplied it on 2026-09-27 as an in-depth analysis of his writing style. **It is the authority.** SHA-256 of the file as received: `a2542d19f98e1afcb1ed842fd85d157e823d847f229894d2ce129fdae85cd75d`.
2. **Observations from two sample chapters** of his (a Pathwell and Elizabeth story), shared the same day. The chapters are not stored here. These notes are secondary evidence and give way to the guide wherever the two disagree.

The [Framework](Framework.md) says *what* a Sunday Morning story does; this page says how the prose sounds while it does it. Where they pull apart, the voice wins on the sentence and the framework wins on the story's shape.

Provenance: [Source Register](../Reference/Source-Register.md) §11.

## How AI should work with this voice

The guide sets the division of labor ("AI Collaboration Guidance", below): lean on AI for continuity, structure, motivation, pacing, action clarity, repeated beats and thematic consistency; be **cautious** with AI for jokes, emotional language, philosophical statements, character-defining dialogue, metaphors, narrator commentary and final sentence rhythm. Every AI-drafted Sunday Morning story sits inside that caution: it is a scaffold for James to write over, not a substitute voice.

## Correction to the first voice notes

The first version of this page, taken from the sample chapters alone, overweighted fragments and one-line paragraphs. The guide is explicit:

- **Ordinary movement:** mostly clean, medium-length sentences.
- **Fragments:** for danger and comic timing, not the default.
- **Reflection and emotion:** longer sentences that carry accumulation and memory.
- **Avoid** a manuscript made entirely of rapid fragments, and avoid every scene ending on a joke.

The sample-chapter habits still hold where the guide agrees with them: starting in motion, dialogue carrying scenes, humor from character contrast and mundane worries, feeling through smell and taste, grief carried by objects, callbacks, and a turn on the last line.

## The author voice guide

### North Star

The goal is to write fiction that feels:

- slightly irreverent
- fun without becoming frivolous
- emotionally sincere without becoming sentimental
- thoughtful without becoming preachy
- philosophical without sounding like a philosophy lecture
- accessible without feeling simplistic
- strange without becoming inaccessible
- polished without losing spontaneity or personality

The ideal story should be enjoyable even if the reader never consciously notices the deeper themes.

The deeper material should be there for the reader who wants to look for it.

### Desired Reader Experience

The reader should regularly move between:

This is fun.

This is weird.

These people feel real.

That was unexpectedly sad.

Wait, that's actually an interesting question.

The story should be capable of making the reader laugh shortly before or after something emotionally serious without making either moment feel fake.

The goal is tonal range rather than tonal purity.

### Tone

The default tone is grounded, conversational and slightly irreverent.

Even when extraordinary things happen, the characters remain people with:

- petty concerns
- appetites
- habits
- annoyances
- unfinished errands
- bad timing
- misunderstandings
- personal baggage

The fantastic should coexist with the mundane rather than replace it.

A supernatural crisis can still happen while somebody worries about dry cleaning, bad coffee, food, work, rent, a damaged car, or whether they remembered their shoes.

That contrast is not merely comic relief.

It is part of the worldview of the story.

Extraordinary events do not stop ordinary life from mattering.

### Humor

Humor should come primarily from:

- character
- conflicting priorities
- understatement
- conversational timing
- absurd circumstances treated seriously
- serious circumstances treated with inappropriate practicality
- petty disagreements during enormous events
- people misunderstanding one another in believable ways

Characters should not feel like stand-up comedians.

Not every line should be quotable.

Not every scene needs a joke.

The funniest moments should often occur because a character is completely sincere about something ridiculous.

Whenever possible:

let the situation be funny before making the prose funny.

### Irreverence

The writing should be willing to puncture grandeur.

Magic can be majestic and inconvenient.

Ancient things can be terrifying and slightly stupid.

A profound supernatural experience may still end with:

- bad food
- paperwork
- a petty argument
- an awkward silence
- somebody asking an extremely ordinary question

The story should not worship its own mythology.

Characters are allowed to be unimpressed by things the world considers important.

But irreverence should not become cynicism.

Things still matter.

People still care.

Love, grief, memory, loyalty and hope should be allowed to exist without being mocked merely because sincerity can feel vulnerable.

### Emotional Tone

The goal is emotional sincerity without melodrama.

Emotion should often arrive indirectly.

Rather than announcing:

«This was the saddest she had felt since her grandmother died.»

prefer something ordinary that contains the feeling:

- a stained recipe card
- an old spoon
- an unfinished voicemail
- a pet's paw print
- a lost ticket
- a smell from childhood
- a routine that can no longer happen

Objects and habits should carry emotional history.

The strongest emotional scenes should often begin with something small.

Characters may remain functional through enormous events and then collapse over something apparently trivial.

That is not irrational.

Often the trivial thing was simply the last structure holding everything else up.

### Philosophy

The stories should have something to think about.

Recurring interests may include:

- identity
- memory
- agency
- grief
- control
- possibility
- regret
- meaning
- mortality
- ownership
- relationships
- what makes a life valuable
- what makes something irreplaceable
- whether protecting someone can become controlling them
- what we owe to our past selves and future selves

But the story should not pause so the author can explain the answer.

Prefer:

question → character choice → consequence

over:

question → speech explaining the theme

Different characters should be capable of embodying different answers.

The story does not always need to decide which one is correct.

The philosophical material should feel discovered rather than delivered.

### Depth

Depth should emerge gradually.

A scene may initially appear to be about:

- losing a laundry ticket
- eating dumplings
- buying a book
- crashing a car
- arguing about directions

and only later reveal that it was also about:

- denial
- grief
- agency
- control
- fear of moving forward
- the need to preserve something lost

Do not force every event to symbolize something.

But allow ordinary events to accumulate meaning.

### Characters

Characters should be recognizable before they are admirable.

They should be:

- inconsistent
- occasionally petty
- capable of being wrong
- funny without knowing it
- emotionally defensive
- capable of kindness without announcing themselves as kind
- capable of hurting people while believing they are helping

Character flaws should produce actual consequences.

Growth should appear in changed behavior before it appears in speeches.

If a character has learned something, the reader should often notice it through what they do differently.

### Dialogue

Dialogue should carry a large portion of the story.

Preferred dialogue is:

- quick
- character-specific
- imperfect
- slightly overlapping in intention
- often indirect

People do not always answer the question they were asked.

They may:

- deflect
- misunderstand
- change subjects
- answer the emotional question instead of the literal one
- answer the literal question to avoid the emotional one

Exposition should usually enter because someone wants something, resists something, misunderstands something, or needs to make a decision.

Avoid dialogue whose only function is explaining the world to the reader.

### Pathwell-Style Comic Character

A character like Pathwell should not simply be "the funny one."

His humor should emerge from worldview and defense mechanisms.

He may:

- focus on food during danger
- answer serious questions with practical concerns
- treat impossible situations as familiar annoyances
- dodge emotional questions
- behave as though chaos is administrative

The humor becomes more interesting when the reader eventually realizes what it is protecting.

Occasionally he should:

- stop joking
- give a completely sincere answer
- not know what to say
- make a joke that fails
- realize that humor will not solve the situation

Those moments should matter precisely because they are unusual.

### Prose

The prose should be primarily clear and invisible.

It should not constantly remind the reader that someone is writing it.

Default language should be:

- straightforward
- concrete
- sensory
- rhythmic
- readable

Then occasionally allow a sentence to become:

- beautiful
- strange
- funny
- emotionally sharp
- philosophically resonant

Those sentences should stand out because the surrounding prose gives them room.

Do not turn every observation into a clever observation.

Do not make every sentence compete for attention.

### Sentence Rhythm

Use rhythm deliberately.

**Ordinary movement**

Mostly clean, medium-length sentences.

**Comedy**

Quick exchanges and abrupt timing.

**Danger**

Shorter sentences and fragments where appropriate.

**Reflection and emotion**

Allow thoughts to stretch.

Longer sentences can carry accumulation, uncertainty and memory.

The prose should breathe.

Avoid a manuscript made entirely of either:

rapid fragments

or

long polished sentences.

Contrast creates emphasis.

### Sensory Writing

The desired prose is experiential rather than purely visual.

Use:

- smell
- taste
- temperature
- texture
- sound
- bodily sensation

especially when they connect to memory.

A magical experience should sometimes be understood through the body before it is understood intellectually.

Sensory details should be specific enough to trigger association.

### Magic and Wonder

Magic should feel wondrous without becoming overly ornate.

It should often be:

- beautiful
- unsettling
- physical
- inconvenient
- emotionally consequential

Magic is especially interesting when it interacts with ordinary human material:

- recipes
- diaries
- letters
- songs
- photographs
- notes
- mundane objects

The supernatural should often reveal that ordinary human experiences already contained something extraordinary.

### Mystery

The reader should sometimes lack information.

That is different from lacking clarity.

Mystery should make the reader ask:

«Why did that happen?»

not:

«What physically happened?»

Action should remain understandable even when its meaning is not.

### Pacing

The desired rhythm is:

movement → interruption → reaction → breathing room → emotional or thematic development → renewed movement

Do not sprint through the entire story.

Quiet scenes are not filler.

They are where:

- relationships form
- strange things become normal
- emotional material surfaces
- philosophical questions gain context
- the next disruption earns its impact

### Sincerity

The story should never become embarrassed by caring.

Humor may surround emotional material, but it should not automatically undermine it.

A character can genuinely love someone.

A loss can simply hurt.

A memory can be beautiful.

A moment can occasionally be allowed to remain quiet.

The story's irreverence should target pretension more often than sincerity.

### Imperfection

Do not polish the humanity out of the prose.

Some dialogue may be clumsy.

Some characters may explain themselves badly.

Some jokes may be stupid.

Some emotional moments may be awkward.

Some decisions may be irrational.

That is acceptable when it is true to the character and situation.

Polish should improve communication, not sterilize personality.

### What to Avoid

Do not drift toward:

- constant quipping
- Marvel-style emotional deflection
- self-consciously clever narration
- generic whimsical fantasy prose
- overly ornate magic descriptions
- endless metaphors
- philosophical monologues
- forced profundity
- manufactured sentimentality
- excessive exposition
- everyone sharing the same sense of humor
- every scene ending with a joke
- every emotional scene explaining what the reader should feel
- writing designed primarily to produce quotable lines

### AI Collaboration Guidance

AI should help the author become more himself, not produce a substitute literary voice.

Use AI heavily for:

- continuity
- structural analysis
- identifying weak motivations
- pacing diagnosis
- action clarity
- finding repeated beats
- challenging thematic inconsistencies
- brainstorming alternatives
- line-level cleanup when necessary

Use AI cautiously for:

- jokes
- emotional language
- philosophical statements
- character-defining dialogue
- metaphors
- narrator commentary
- final sentence rhythm

When improving prose:

clarify first, strengthen second, embellish last.

Do not assume more polished means better.

### Revision Test

For every substantial revision, ask:

Does this make the scene clearer?

If yes, probably useful.

Does this make the character more specific?

If yes, probably useful.

Does this strengthen the emotional or thematic consequence?

If yes, probably useful.

Is it merely a cleverer way to say the same thing?

Maybe not useful.

Does it sound like the story, or simply like good writing?

Prefer the story.

### Final Aim

The ideal work should feel like someone telling an entertaining strange story who occasionally, almost by accident, says something true.

It should be capable of being funny without becoming shallow.

Emotional without becoming manipulative.

Philosophical without becoming instructional.

Beautiful without constantly trying to be beautiful.

The reader should come for the characters and the strange things happening to them.

The deeper questions should follow them home.
