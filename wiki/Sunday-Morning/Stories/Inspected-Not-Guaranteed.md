# Inspected, Not Guaranteed

## Status

**Provisional story concept.** Written with the [Sunday Morning framework](../Framework.md). All characters, businesses and plot details are new and non-canon. Wurdren's biography is not settled by anything here.

**Development level: L2 Outlined** — developed through the [Story Pipeline](../Story-Pipeline.md). The L0 concept is preserved in git history (commit `3427bdd`).

## Premise

Wurdren brings the sword he has carried for decades to Stonefield Forge because its tang has cracked. The forges are cold for winter maintenance, so nobody can weld it until the fires are relit. While he waits, the guild drafts him as the one judge the apprenticeship rules require who has no family, guild or debt tie to any apprentice. In a border town where everyone is related to someone, the stranger is the only one who qualifies.

**Setting:** [Stonefield Forge](../../Places/Border-Towns.md#stonefield-forge--ironcrest--greenvale) (Ironcrest / Greenvale)
**Clock:** [Forge Reawakening](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#ironcrest), early spring — about a week
**Mode:** character / competition

## Protagonist

**Wurdren** — see [Wurdren](../../Story/Wurdren.md).

- **Good at:** listening to people who are not usually listened to; noticing who is about to be left out.
- **Small flaw:** wants badly to be needed, and tells the story of his sword more often than anyone wants to hear it.
- **Why he resists:** he came to get a sword mended, not to sit in judgment over apprentices' metalwork in a town he doesn't know.

## Place

A mixed farming-and-craft town where the foothills meet farmland. Smoke, water and timber are old arguments; half the town repairs what the other half grows with.

Recurring locations:

1. **Tamsin Rake's shop** — cold hearth, swept floor, every tool hung by its outline.
2. **The Quench** — a tavern where forge crews cluster near the fire and talk over one another.
3. **The guild hall** — judgment benches, and the memorial wall of names honored at Forge Reawakening.
4. **The charcoal road** — the muddy route the relighting fuel has to come down.
5. **Nell Haskett's farmyard and ditch** — where the valley's broken hinges collect, and where the forge's quench water ends up.

## Cast

- **Tamsin Rake** — an old smith who will not let anything leave her shop merely *attempted*. Dry, exact, secretly kind.
- **Col Barrowfield** — an apprentice with a Greenvale mother and an Ironcrest father. His masterwork is a pump and settling trough that returns a forge's quench water to its own cistern instead of the farm ditches.
- **Marta Dunnock** — the other apprentice presenting this year. Her masterwork is a traditional blade, and it is genuinely good.
- **Master Oswin Vey** — the district guildmaster. Overly serious, runs judgments by the book, and keeps a ledger of water and smoke complaints he is losing.
- **Nell Haskett** — farmer, town gossip, and unofficial census-taker of everything broken in the valley.
- **The Quench's keeper** — has heard every forge story ever told and believes none of them, including Wurdren's.

## Problem

Without a third, unbound judge, the judgments slip past Forge Reawakening and Col and Marta both wait another season. Col's piece may be refused anyway, as plumbing rather than smithing.

## Complications

1. Vey objects to an outsider, then decides an outsider is at least *neutral*, then regrets it.
2. Col's piece is dismissed as "plumbing," not craft.
3. The farmers, tired of fouled ditches, threaten to stand in the charcoal road on relighting day.
4. The charcoal is late anyway: the road is mud.
5. Tamsin inspects Wurdren's sword and finds ordinary steel that a smith long ago *guaranteed* as something better.

## Running elements

- Ironcrest's four-way distinction — *attempted*, *completed*, *inspected*, *guaranteed* — applied to everything, including the porridge. See [Language and Thought](../../Culture/Language-and-Thought.md#ironcrest-languages).
- Wurdren's sword story, which gets shorter every time he tells it.
- Nell's census of broken things, and who fixed them.
- Vey's complaint ledger, which gains a page every scene.

## Emotional core

Competence deserves respect even when nobody sings about it. Col's useful, unglamorous work mirrors the career Wurdren is quietly disappointed in, and the sword shows him that ordinary steel, kept well for thirty years, is its own kind of worth.

## Climax

The guild rules say a masterwork must be *inspected*. Wurdren asks the one question that matters: do the rules say where? They don't. Nell walks the judges down the lane to see Col's work in service — the ditch running clear below a forge that installed his trough, and the census of repaired hinges and gates along the way. Both apprentices pass; judging was never a contest between them. The water complaint is answered by the masterwork itself, and the farmers help drag the charcoal carts out of the mud.

## Soft landing

The fires are relit and the names on the memorial wall are read. Tamsin welds the tang, re-hilts the sword and stamps it *inspected*, not *guaranteed*: a fine, ordinary sword. Wurdren eats [Forged Harvest Stew](../../Culture/Recipes/Border-Fusions.md#blacksmiths--forged-harvest-stew) at The Quench and stays two extra days for no stated reason. Nell has a gate that sticks.

### Stage 1 — THINK

**Reasoning allocation:** structured single path. The story involves Wurdren, whose biography is an open question, and it turns on a guild procedure a reader could catch out.

**Decomposition.** Separable problems:

1. why Wurdren is stuck;
2. why he, of all people, judges;
3. what the apprentices present and how it is judged;
4. the town's water and smoke dispute;
5. Wurdren's own arc through the sword;
6. the clock (relighting).

Connection to keep: Col's arc and Wurdren's arc are the same story told twice, and the town dispute must be answered by Col's work, not by Wurdren.

**Assumption mapping**

| Assumption | Status | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The forges are cold before Forge Reawakening | `VERIFIED` (provisional festival) | [Festivals](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#ironcrest): foundries return from winter maintenance. |
| Re-hilting needs a forge fire | `ASSUMED` → **false** | A plain re-hilt can be done cold. The wait now comes from a cracked tang that needs welding. |
| A guild rule requires an unbound judge | `ASSUMED` | Invented, provisional. Fits Ironcrest's value on inspection by someone with no stake. |
| No local is unbound | `ASSUMED` → **grounded** | Border towns are full of cross-line marriages ([Border Towns](../../Places/Border-Towns.md#principle)), so everyone is kin to some apprentice. |
| Col's pump "settles the smoke dispute" | `ASSUMED` → **false** | A pump can't fix smoke. The dispute becomes quench-water runoff into farm ditches, which canon lists alongside smoke. |
| Wurdren's past can be referenced | `UNKNOWN` — [Open Questions](../../Open-Questions.md#wurdren) | Only the sword's age is used. No origin, age, or career events are stated. |

**First principles.** The story must let unglamorous competence win by evidence, give Wurdren a quiet self-recognition, and land at the relighting. Removed convention: a rival apprentice as antagonist. Marta is good, and both pass. The contest is against the guild's definition of craft, not against a person.

**Counterexample search**

| Attack | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Wurdren wins the judgment with a speech | **Contradicts** [World Rules §11](../../World-Rules.md#11-wurdren-does-not-win-by-becoming-the-biggest-power). He only asks where inspection may happen; Nell supplies the evidence. |
| The farmers' blockade makes them antagonists | **Narrows.** It stays a threat, and they end up pushing the carts. |
| The sword reveal humiliates Wurdren | **Narrows.** Tamsin calls the steel ordinary but the upkeep inspected — thirty years of care. |
| Would guild judges accept non-members' testimony? | **Survives.** The tools, not the farmers' opinions, are the evidence. |
