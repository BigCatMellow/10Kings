# The Heavy Scale at Icestep Summit

## Status

**Provisional story concept.** Written with the [Sunday Morning framework](../Framework.md). All characters, businesses and plot details are new and non-canon.

**Development level: L3 Drafted** — [read the draft](../Drafts/The-Heavy-Scale.md) (fourth pass, checked for fit and uniqueness across the collection; not yet reviewed). Outlined at L2; the pilot of the [Story Pipeline](../Story-Pipeline.md). The concept below reflects the THINK pass; the original L0 concept is preserved in git history (commit `3427bdd`).

## Premise

On his first posting, a painfully precise weights inspector runs the pre-season check on Icestep Summit's public scale and finds it reading heavy. He has three days before the first caravan of the year weighs in publicly to find out since when and why, and to work out who is owed what.

**Setting:** [Icestep Summit](../../Places/Border-Towns.md#icestep-summit--northwind--highridge) (Northwind / Highridge)
**Clock:** [Pass Opening and Ice Breaking](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#seasonal-calendar), early spring
**Mode:** mystery (fair-play)

## Protagonist

**Anselm Quill** — a Highridge-trained weigher on his first posting.

- **Good at:** measurement, records, and remembering exactly what people said.
- **Small flaw:** quotes people back to themselves, word for word, at the worst possible moment. He watches instruments closely and people hardly at all.
- **Why he resists:** he wants to report the fault and let someone senior handle it. There is no one senior until the pass opens.

## Place

A cold pass town where maritime supply routes meet plateau caravans. The pass has been closed all winter. The only traffic has been locals, winter stores, a few stranded travelers, and one salt-fish trader who never left.

Recurring locations:

1. **The public scale** — the town's honor, beam and pans under a slate roof.
2. **The customs house** — ledgers, stamps, one stove, and a window facing the Second Gate's patched roof.
3. **The Second Gate** — Brisa Zell's inn, where stranded travelers winter.
4. **The repair shop** — gruff smith, every tool the pass needs.
5. **An Infinite Compass road shrine** — with a pilgrim log going back decades.
6. **The drifts under the Second Gate's eaves** — melting.

## Cast

- **Brisa Zell** — Northwind-born innkeeper who feeds everyone snowed in, paying or not.
- **Tove Marrick, a salt-fish trader** — tags every claim by how she knows it: *I saw it*; *someone I'd lend a boat to told me*; *it is said.*
- **The customs chief** — wants no scandal whatsoever. With fewer caravans this year his post's fees are down and his appointment is under review; he isn't corrupt, just frightened.
- **The repair smith** — maintains the scale, rude, capable, and innocent.
- **The shrine keeper** — pilgrim host and quiet historian of who went where.
- **The caravan's cook** — a Deepwood man cooking for caravans since a blight took his village's mushroom harvest, grumbling about prices.
- **Samir Tareh** — a Highridge caravan negotiator who rides with the first caravan of the year. After a season of caravan attacks, merchants follow his advice on which passes are reliable, and he is famous for checking every scale from here to the lowlands. He is tired: this year nearly every report he receives is exaggerated, and an honest one has become rare.

## Problem

A public scale that reads wrong makes every toll and sale weighed on it wrong. At Pass Opening the first caravan weighs in publicly, with Samir Tareh deciding which passes to recommend this season. A crooked scale would strike Icestep off his list, and caravans would take another pass.

## Complications

1. The customs chief wants it "resolved quietly," meaning not at all.
2. The ledger shows readings jumped partway through the winter, but the entries are dated "after the blow," and everyone dates the big storm differently.
3. Quill's suspicion lands on the repair smith, who had access to the scale and is rude about it.
4. Word comes that the pass will open a day early.
5. Quill starts doubting his own reference weights and spends a night re-weighing them against each other.

## Running elements

- Tove's evidential tags ([Northwind languages](../../Culture/Language-and-Thought.md#northwind-languages)). The difference between *told* and *saw* becomes a clue.
- Quill quoting people exactly ([Highridge languages](../../Culture/Language-and-Thought.md#highridge-languages)).
- Brisa feeding Quill every time he interviews her, so every interview gets longer.
- "The night of the big blow": everyone's date marker, never the same date twice.

## Emotional core

Honesty and kindness can coexist. Quill learns that precision can be a form of care instead of armor, and that a small public institution holds a surprising amount of trust.

## Solution

Innocent. In the worst storm of winter, part of the Second Gate's roof covering began tearing loose. Brisa borrowed the scale's heavy counterweight to hold it down. The gust took the covering, and the counterweight went with it into the drift below the eaves.

Before morning she replaced it with a Northwind net-stone stamped "one stone," the same word Icestep uses for the counterweight. The two systems use the same word for different masses. The net-stone is lighter, so everything weighed against it has read heavy ever since. Nobody lied; one word meant two things. In this story both systems call their unit a "stone"; that is a provisional story detail, not canon measurement.

The thaw uncovers the original counterweight in the melting drift on the morning of Pass Opening.

## Climax

Quill corrects the scale publicly as part of the Pass Opening ceremony, with Samir Tareh watching. Brisa is mortified; she had no idea. Quill has the ledger worked back to the storm night, dated by the shrine log, so he can name what was overcharged: a winter's worth of small household weighings and Tove's salt fish. The town decides — by shouting — to repay it as a free first-caravan supper at the Second Gate. Tove takes her refund in coin and puts it straight back into the supper.

## Soft landing

Quill's report reads: *Error inspected and corrected. Cause: weather.* He apologizes to the smith, who is still rude about it. Samir writes one line in his route book, under an earlier entry that reads *Seven Wells — water and guides, one roof*: *Icestep — scale honest, corrected in public.* He mentions, without relish, a pass town further east whose scale was not honest and will not see his caravans this year. The chief takes the credit for the ceremony, and everyone lets him. The caravan's cook grumbles that the mushrooms cost more than the meat. The supper runs late: [Icefire Brined Fish](../../Culture/Recipes/Border-Fusions.md#icefire-brined-fish), [Frost and Spice Fish Cakes](../../Culture/Recipes/Border-Fusions.md#frost-and-spice-fish-cakes), and caravan flatbread. Tove tells the story that night, tagged *I saw it*.

### Stage 1 — THINK

**Reasoning allocation:** structured single path. The story rests on a physical mechanism and a fair-play clue trail a reader could catch out, so direct drafting would be risky. No parallel versions were generated.

**Decomposition.** Separable problems:

1. the physical fault;
2. why nobody noticed;
3. the clue trail across the recurring locations;
4. Quill's change;
5. the social settlement (who loses face, who is repaid);
6. the clock.

Connection to keep: the clue trail *is* Quill's arc. Each interview yields a clue and also teaches him to look at people.

**Assumption mapping.** Load-bearing premises of the L0 concept:

| Assumption | Status | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The scale was used heavily all winter | `ASSUMED` → **false** | Winter closes routes ([World Rules](../../World-Rules.md#5-low-fantasy-means-material-limits-matter)), and passes reopen in spring ([Trade and Dependencies](../../Economy/Trade-and-Dependencies.md#seasonal-stress)), so winter volume is low. The fault is found at the pre-season check, not through complaints. |
| A substitute weight makes readings heavy | `ASSUMED` → **conditional** | Only if the substitute is *lighter* than the original, for both balance and steelyard designs. The mechanism must supply a reason for that. |
| A caravan master was overcharged | `ASSUMED` → **false** | No caravans crossed during the winter. He becomes deadline pressure and a public audience instead. |
| Highridge pass towns keep public scales | `VERIFIED` | [Highridge Plateau](../../Regions/Highridge-Plateau.md) — pass towns list public scales and customs houses. |
| Unit names for Northwind and Highridge weights | `UNKNOWN` | The wiki defines no measurement systems. The shared word "stone" is kept as a provisional story detail. |
| Which authority employs Icestep's weigher | `UNKNOWN` | Left vague in the story ("the pass authority"). A question for James if a draft ever needs it. |

**First principles.** The story must deliver a real puzzle, fair clues, an innocent cause, a festival convergence, and a changed protagonist. Removed convention: a culprit. Sunday Morning tone does not need one, so the smith is only Quill's mistaken suspicion. Smallest mechanism that does the job: one physical fact (a lighter substitute), one hidden event (the storm on the roof), and one datable moment. That mechanism is a border-town problem: two systems of measure, one word.

**Counterexample search** against the L0 resolution:

| Attack | Result |
| --- | --- |
| "Brisa confesses" — would she? | **Contradicts.** She never knew the stone was different. The solution must come from evidence (the thaw), not a confession. |
| Would a trader accept a supper as a refund? | **Narrows.** Tove takes coin, then chooses to give it to the supper. That makes it a character moment rather than a forced settlement. |
| Could frost or cold explain a heavy reading instead? | **Survives as an alternative, rejected.** Uniform cold barely affects a balance, and it gives no human payoff. |
| Can a reader solve it too early? | **Risk.** If the ledger jump and the roof are both prominent in the first two scenes, the answer is visible by scene 3. Mitigation: date the jump late, via the shrine log. |
| Would Quill really choose a public correction over the chief's quiet fix? | **Survives.** It is the turn in his arc: precision in the service of the people who were overcharged. |

**PLAN handoff**

```text
selected strategy lighter substitute weight, from two measuring systems sharing one word; storm; thaw reveal
alternatives      A fraud with a sympathetic motive — set aside: needs a wrongdoer
                  B frost / mechanical cause — set aside: physically weak, no human payoff
                  D Quill's own reference set is wrong — kept as a mid-story doubt, not the solution
assumptions       winter volume is low (verified); substitute is lighter (required by mechanism)
unknowns          unit names (provisional); weigher's employing authority (left vague)
reconsider if     readers can solve it by scene 3; the "stone" collision needs heavy exposition;
                  Quill's precision reads as a copy of Tamsin's in Inspected, Not Guaranteed
```
