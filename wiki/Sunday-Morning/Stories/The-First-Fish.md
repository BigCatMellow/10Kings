# The First Fish at Snowbreaker Quay

## Status

**Provisional story concept.** Written with the [Sunday Morning framework](../Notes/Sources/Framework.md). All characters, places, customs and plot details are new and non-canon.

**Development level: L3 Drafted, first pass** (2026-11-03): [read the draft](../Drafts/The-First-Fish.md). ## Premise

At Snowbreaker Quay nobody sells a fish until the first fish of the spring run has been landed from the Quay's own water and smoked in the quay smokehouse. This spring the run has moved across the headland into the neighbors' water. The smokehouse keeper, who has never bargained in her life, is sent over the headland to fix it before the neighbors take a buyer's money and shut the water for the season.

**Setting:** Snowbreaker Quay, a [Northwind](../../Regions/Northwind.md#towns) fishing town that also farms inland. The name comes from the [name bank](../../Places/Border-Towns.md#other-provisional-names). Across the headland is Skerrimouth, a smaller clan town (provisional). Neither is Kettle Cove or Narrow Sound from [The Greenvale Man](The-Greenvale-Man.md).
**Clock:** [First Catch](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#seasonal-calendar), Year 2 late spring, over two days and a night.
**Mode:** a quiet two-hander.

## Protagonist

**Kirsa Lowe**, keeper of the quay smokehouse.

- **Good at:** smoke. She can hold a fire at one heat all night, she reads the weather from how smoke lies on the water, and she can make anything keep.
- **Small flaw:** she won't bargain. "Smokehouses don't haggle." She hears every question about her fires as an insult to them.
- **Why she resists:** the First Catch belongs to the elders and the custom, not to her. And she hasn't left the smokehouse in a run season since she took it over. Somebody has to mind the fires, and it's always been her.

## Place

A stone quay, a long smokehouse with its racks, drying frames above the shingle, boats waiting on the slip with nothing to fish for, and the headland path to Skerrimouth.

Recurring locations:

1. **The quay smokehouse**, cold for the first spring anyone can remember.
2. **The slip**, where the boats wait.
3. **The headland path**, a half-day's walk in wind.
4. **Skerrimouth's drying shed**, a family smokehouse too small for a real catch.
5. **The headland cairn**, where the two waters meet and you can see both.

## Cast

- **Edda Kjeld**, Skerrimouth's headwoman. She's dry, practical and poorer than Kirsa assumed. Her water is full of fish she can only sell fresh or salted, cheap.
- **The Quay elders**, who send Kirsa because a smokehouse matter can be discussed by the smokehouse without the elders losing face.
- **The Frostbay buyer's man**, never on the page. His offer arrives by letter: coin now for sole fishing of Skerrimouth's offshore water this season.
- **The Quay's boat crews**, idle on the slip and running up debts.

## Problem

The run has moved, and nobody knows why. The custom says nobody sells until the first fish is in from the Quay's own water, and the season's credit, advanced against the catch, falls due at the first sale. The Quay's water is empty. Skerrimouth's is full, and Skerrimouth has a buyer's offer of coin for the season. If Edda signs it, Skerrimouth's water is closed to everyone, the Quay can't sell, and two towns that need each other sail separately in convoy.

## Complications

1. Kirsa can't bargain, and she's the one who's been sent.
2. Edda is proud and assumes the Quay has come to take her fish.
3. The buyer's offer is better than anything the Quay can pay in coin.
4. The custom itself: even a deal leaves the first fish coming from someone else's water, and the elders won't hear of it.
5. Maris Bleakshore wants every cove's convoy list confirmed before the first summer convoy sails. The Quay's list, sent in the autumn, names Skerrimouth, as it always has. The Quay's crews are already saying that anyone who takes Frostbay money isn't one of the "coves whose waters were clean", and want Skerrimouth struck off.

## Running elements

- **Kirsa's smoke-sayings** (provisional folk weather sayings): *smoke lies down, boats stay up*; *smoke that climbs is smoke that lies*. She reads every fire and chimney she passes, aloud.
- **The cold smokehouse.** Every scene at the Quay comes back to the fires nobody lights.
- **Edda's shed**, too small for her own fish.

## Emotional core

A custom is for the people who keep it. Pride on both sides can be traded for something both towns need, if somebody stops guarding her own door long enough to look at the other one.

## Solution

A swap. The Quay's boats fish Skerrimouth's water this season under Skerrimouth's mark, and Skerrimouth takes a share of the catch. In return the quay smokehouse smokes Skerrimouth's own catch with no charge, so Skerrimouth sells smoked fish at smoked prices. That is worth more over a season than the buyer's coin is now. The custom bends at the edge: the first fish is landed from "our water", meaning the water the two towns share this year, and it's smoked at the Quay.

## Climax

The headland cairn at dusk. Kirsa, who doesn't haggle, can't offer coin, so she offers the one thing she has: her fires. Edda tests her: will the Quay smoke Skerrimouth's fish first, ahead of its own? Kirsa says yes before she can talk herself out of it.

## Soft landing

The first fish comes in from the shared water and goes into the smokehouse, lit at last, and the racks fill. Each town puts the other on its convoy list for Maris Bleakshore. The keeper eats the first fish by custom. Kirsa hands it to Edda instead. (The story's meal is earlier: the stiff supper Edda gives her in Skerrimouth, [Frostbound Fish Stew](../../Culture/Recipes/Northwind.md#3-frostbound-fish-stew) from a communal pot.)

### Stage 1 — THINK

**Reasoning allocation:** structured single path. The negotiation has to be small enough for two people and still carry two towns.

**Decomposition.**

1. Why nobody can sell.
2. Why it's Kirsa who goes.
3. What each town has that the other needs.
4. What the buyer's offer would cost.
5. How the custom bends without breaking.
6. The convoy lists.

Connection to keep: what Kirsa has to offer (3) is the thing she guards hardest, her fires. Her flaw and her solution are the same object.

**Assumption mapping.**

| Assumption | Status | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Northwind towns have a First Catch custom | `ASSUMED` | Only the festival's name exists. The landing-and-smoking rule is a provisional local custom, with a practical origin: it proves the fires and keeps anyone from undercutting before the market's set. |
| Fish runs move between neighboring waters | `VERIFIED` in kind | [Fish stocks and access](../../Story/Current-Events.md#fish-stocks-and-access). The cause is `UNKNOWN` and nobody explains it. |
| Towns own their water | `ASSUMED` → **narrowed** | Precise maritime law is open ([Law](../../Law-and-Jurisdiction.md)). "Our water" is local custom between clans, never a code. |
| Credit falls due at the first sale | `VERIFIED` in kind | Debts carry seasonal triggers ([Money #18](../../Money-Credit-and-Measures.md#18-debt-is-also-temporal)). The first-sale trigger is a provisional detail. |
| Maris's lists come up again this season | `ASSUMED` | [The Greenvale Man](The-Greenvale-Man.md): she asked every cove to name the coves it would sail with in convoy next season, and coves sent their lists in the autumn. A spring confirmation before the first convoy sails is a provisional detail; the independent check caught the earlier `VERIFIED`. |
| Who backs the buyer | `UNKNOWN` | Left open. |

**First principles.** The story needs two towns that each hold half of the answer, someone who can't bargain doing the bargaining, and an ending that changes a list in Maris Bleakshore's hands without anyone knowing why it matters. Removed convention: the clever negotiator. Kirsa wins by giving the first thing asked for. The smallest mechanism: fish without fires on one side, fires without fish on the other.

**Counterexample search.**

| Attack | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Why wouldn't Skerrimouth just build a bigger smokehouse? | **Survives.** Not this season, not with the run already in. The buyer's coin is now. |
| Is the swap better for Skerrimouth than the coin? | **Narrows.** Only over the season, and only if the Quay smokes Skerrimouth's fish first. That's the test at the cairn. |
| Is this The Greenvale Man again (a Northwind town, trust, convoy)? | **Risk.** There, a rumor and a regatta, a craftsman proving himself. Here, a trade between two towns, no rumor, and two women. The convoy lists are the link, not the engine. |
| Would elders really bend a custom? | **Survives.** They don't have to say they bent it. "Our water" covers it. |
| Does Maris see anything? | **Contradicts.** She gets two lists. That's all. |
