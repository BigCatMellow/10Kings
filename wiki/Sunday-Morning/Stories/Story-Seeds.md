## World seeds, 2026-11-04

*bt 7274, 2026-11-04: "maybe we look at the world and brainstorm … how we can use the various aspects of it in stories to make sure we are using the dominos as well as fleshing out the world. I guess not every story has to progress the storys dominos or main plot, some can just be part of the world at large." That last sentence is [D23](../Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions).*

### How these were made

The brainstorm used the THINK pattern from bt 7274's [Pilot Projects](https://github.com/BigCatMellow/Pilot_Projects/blob/main/ai-creativity-and-ideation/README.md) research: generate widely first, keep the different ideas apart, judge them separately, then plan.

1. **Split the world into families** from the [World coverage](../../../atlas/coverage/Coverage.md) report and his examples: the Spine above and below, Seven Hill, Port, moving peoples, faiths and relics, remembered history, guilds, how life works.
2. **Generate in each family** with a few different operations, so the seeds don't all come out the same shape: a point of view no story has used (perspective shift), the expected thing not happening (inversion), two parts of the world meeting (recombination), a season or scale moved (constraint shift).
3. **Keep the families apart.** Forty-odd ideas came out; thirty-one were kept, and eleven guild seeds, twelve faith seeds and thirteen Season 2 seeds were added the same day because each works a different way. Near-duplicates were dropped, not averaged.
4. **Judge separately** against the [Rules](../Notes/Rules.md) and the [Registry](../Notes/Registry.md#story-shapes) (the [table](#how-they-compare) below), then pick a few for different reasons rather than by one score ([picks](#picks)).

Nothing in a seed is canon. Where a seed needs something the wiki doesn't have, its **Needs** line says so, and nothing is assumed.

### Two kinds

- **World**: part of the world at large ([D23](../Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)). No nail, no domino. Current events can show as weather, or not at all. Every other rule holds.
- **Domino**: carries a nail and goes through the [world-tie check](../Notes/Pipeline.md#world-tie-check) as now.

A few seeds note where **Wurdren** could pass through, as the signal ([D19](../Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)). Those are Domino stories by definition.

### The main story, Season 2

*Added 2026-11-04 under [D24](../Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions), to give Season 2 its spine.*

Season 2's world is the second rung of the [escalation](../../Story/Main-Conflict.md#escalation): "recognizable patterns", the same kinds of trouble in more places ([The Long Road](../Notes/The-Long-Road.md)). These seeds are aimed at the eleven dominoes the [Domino Map](../../../atlas/domino-map/Domino-Map.md) shows as touched by a Season 1 story but never at the center of one, and at the threads Season 1 [planted](../Notes/The-Long-Road.md#planted-already). Each names its [kind](../Notes/The-Long-Road.md#kinds-of-story). Every rule still holds: nobody sees the pattern, the Villain never appears, the Council never acts as a named body, and domino figures appear only as ordinary people. What the reader can piece together by the end of the season: the trouble has a shape, and more than one hand is in it.

#### Harvest Home Again

`DOMINO` (a Return) · *the Vale, Greenvale · Harvest Home, Year 2 early autumn*

A year after [One Square, Two Harvests](One-Square-Two-Harvests.md), the Vale's harvest is good again and nobody is buying. Bettany Corlew chairs Harvest Home as usual, and this year the redistribution table, the part of the festival that was always a formality, is the part everyone needs.

Possible heart: a festival is a promise a town makes to itself, and this year it gets kept.

Possible thread: [farmers default](../../Story/Current-Events.md#example-ripple-chain) (ripple step 5), after the Salve house's credit was tightened, as the [desk view](../Notes/Collection.md#the-view-from-the-desks) expected. The warm ending of One Square isn't undone; it's what lets the Vale carry this.

#### The Penny Sale

`DOMINO` · *a Greenvale farm · late autumn*

A neighbor's farm is going to auction for debt. The whole district turns up, and nobody bids more than a penny for anything, and anyone who tries to bid more finds a large farmer standing very close to them. Everything goes for pennies, and everything goes back.

Possible heart: a neighborhood can decide what a thing is worth.

Possible thread: [land consolidators buy distressed farms](../../Story/Current-Events.md#example-ripple-chain) (step 7). The buyer's agent leaves with nothing and no grudge; the agent is not shamed, only outbid.

- **Needs:** the "penny auction" is a real-world custom, used here as a story-level one. Avoid [C7](../Notes/Collection.md#the-threads)'s phrases.

#### The Trial Rows

`DOMINO` (a Figure story) · *a Greenvale hill farm · spring to harvest*

Possible thread: [Rosana Meadowcroft](../../Story/Villains-Dominoes.md#rosana-meadowcroft--greenvale) is the steward, as an ordinary person; her seed is the "agricultural innovation" that will become "a political fight over control". Here it's two rows.

#### The Route Book

Possible thread: [Samir Tareh](../../Story/Villains-Dominoes.md#samir-tareh--highridge)'s route book from [The Heavy Scale](The-Heavy-Scale.md), and his domino: "'safe' trade concentration makes one route strategically vulnerable and impoverishes another." ### Faiths and relics

*bt 7274, 2026-11-04: "lets also do the religions, those are another large part of the world".*

Season 1 touches four of the nine [faiths](../../Politics/Religions.md): the Great Weaver ([The Tree](The-Tree-With-a-Debt.md), [The Blank Square](The-Blank-Square.md)), the Illuminated Circle (The Blank Square), the Silent Whisper ([The Goat File](The-Goat-File.md)) and the Infinite Compass (The Blank Square, [The Heavy Scale](The-Heavy-Scale.md), [The Road in Use](The-Road-in-Use.md)). Mostly in passing: a custom, a phrase, a way of keeping records. No story yet happens *inside* a faith's own life, at a rite, in a temple, between a priest and the people.

The wiki's Religions page gives each faith its core belief, its interests and a current scheme. The [legacy notes](../../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/06_Religion_Gods_Artifacts.md) give each a full working life: rites for naming, marriage and death, festivals, temples and clergy, sacred texts, sects, pilgrimages, and pantheons of named gods. They also tie each faith to one kingdom (the Eternal Forge to a "Kingdom of Blaze", a "Pilgrim's Compass" to a "Kingdom of Frost"), which the wiki rules against: faiths "span borders" and "should not behave like nine political parties". None of that is canon ([question 6](#questions-for-bt-7274)). The seeds below borrow a few legacy rites as story-level customs and use no god's name.

Two rules shape every faith story. Faith is belief, never proof: the [Magic](../../Magic.md#writing-rule) writing rule applies to prayers and omens as much as to relics. And the faiths' current schemes come close to seeing the pattern (the Illuminated Circle's scholars, the Dual Flame's counselors, the Radiant Path's argument about hidden rule), so a story keeps them at the scale of one congregation, or it breaks the [world-tie rule](../Notes/Rules.md#the-world-tie-rule-connected-not-driven).

#### The Lantern

`WORLD` · *a village shrine · any season*

The village's relic lantern dims when the most respected woman in the village speaks at her granddaughter's betrothal. Nobody knows whether it meant anything, and the village has a week to decide what it meant.

Possible heart: what a thing means is up to the people who keep it.

- **Uses:** [Magic and Relics](../../Magic.md): real but deniable; "a relic that 'fails' a respected person causes a crisis". The story never confirms it.
- **Needs:** whose faith keeps the lantern.

#### Under the Lights

`WORLD` · *a [Northwind](../../Regions/Northwind.md) coast · Aurora Nights, late summer*

Two families have planned a betrothal under the lights, and the lights don't come. The grandmothers disagree about whether it counts.

Possible heart: a promise doesn't need the sky's permission.

- **Uses:** [Aurora Nights](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#northwind) (beliefs stay beliefs), [Northwind cuisine](../../Culture/Food.md#northwind).
- **Needs:** nothing new. Aurora Nights is the only festival no story has used.

#### The Wrong Day

Possible heart: being right isn't always the same as being on time.

Possible thread: the Cyclic Order's reformers, who say stability has become stagnation, would love a drifted calendar. One of them visits; the keeper's reasons stay her own.

- **Uses:** [Calendar and Timekeeping](../../Calendar-and-Timekeeping.md).

#### The Kitchen Court

### Guilds

*bt 7274, 2026-11-04: "the guilds are a huge part of the world".*

Season 1 has two guild stories without saying so: [Inspected](Inspected-Not-Guaranteed.md) turns on a smiths' guild judging a journeyman's piece, and [The Ladder Rules](The-Ladder-Rules.md) on the lamplighters. Neither links the [Guilds](../../Politics/Guilds.md) page, so the [coverage](../../../atlas/coverage/Coverage.md) report doesn't count them. Everything else about guilds is unused.

The wiki's Guilds page holds the structure: what guilds are, what they want, five current schemes, and how the Council, the Villain and Wurdren meet them. The [legacy notes](../../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/08_Power_The_Council_Guilds_Underworld.md#src-guilds-economics) hold a named roster with leaders, ranks and customs: the Ironheart Founders' Guild (metal), the Seven Seas Mercantile Guild (trade and credit), the Masoncraft Builders' Guild, the Harvestkeepers Collective (farming), the Apothic Circle (medicine), the Artisan Fellowship, the Bardic Circle, the Guards and Sentinels, the Ranger and Hunters' Guild, the Adventurers' Guild and others. None of that is canon ([question 5](#questions-for-bt-7274)). The seeds below use the functions and a few of the customs, never the names, so they work whichever way he rules.

### Plan

How the stories are made doesn't change. Each picked seed runs the [pipeline](../Notes/Pipeline.md) from Stage 0, and a batch starts with a test story.

1. **bt 7274 picks** which seeds go forward, and answers the [questions](#questions-for-bt-7274) for any seed that needs them.
2. **Test story first:** lean, Mending Day, because it tests the new thing (a story with no thread) and needs no new lore. Its cold read checks one extra question: does it feel like part of the same book?
3. **Season 2's mix** ([D24](../Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)): about half World and half Domino. The Domino half comes mostly from [the main story, Season 2](#the-main-story-season-2), with Wurdren in two or three of them (lean: The Same Paper early, Small Jobs in the middle, Very Nearly last).
4. **After drafting,** rerun the [coverage](../../../atlas/coverage/Coverage.md) report to see what the season added.

### Questions for bt 7274

**Answered 2026-11-04.** bt 7274: "I trust you leans, lets just get everything done" ([D24](../Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)). Each lean below is now his ruling and has been applied to its owner page; only whether the faiths have gods stays open. The questions are kept as asked. The [legacy notes](../../../legacy-notes/README.md) were searched for answers on 2026-11-04; what they hold is now on each owner page.

1. **Seven Hill: where is it, and whose base was it?** The legacy notes have nothing on it ([Seven Hill](../../Places/Seven-Hill.md#what-the-legacy-notes-say)), so it's new with bt 7274. Lean: on a Spine pass, a fortress emptied when Highridge's toll wars gave way to negotiated passage, so it has been a town for generations and the base is the town's old bones. The other way would be a recent garrison stood down by a treaty, which makes it fresher and sorer.
2. **The Overpath: is it the legacy notes' Overpass?** The legacy has an *Overpass*: a dangerous surface way through the mountains for pilgrims, adventurers and caravans with goods too big for the tunnels, with bandits, tolls and two named landmarks ([The Spine](../../Places/The-Spine.md#what-the-legacy-notes-say)). That answers who walks it. Lean: the same thing, keep bt 7274's newer name, and let it be a trail along the range that the pass roads cross, so both descriptions hold.
3. **The circuit people's name.** The legacy calls the Appalachian-influenced people the **Ridge-Roamers**, travelling in clans named for a ridge or an ancestor, with seven more named bands besides ([Nomads](../../Culture/Nomads.md#the-ridge-roamers)). Lean: keep Ridge-Roamers as what outsiders call them, and let the stories use the clan names, the way they'd name themselves.
4. **The Warrior's, Assassin's and Spy Guilds.** The legacy gives each a fuller form, mostly game-class flavor ([Guilds](../../Politics/Guilds.md#armed-and-clandestine-organizations)). The usable core: a fighters' guild that guards caravans and trains militias, and spy guilds that sell information to anyone, the Council included. Lean: keep those two cores for The Retired Blade and The Tea Boy, leave the Assassin's Guild out of the stories, and drop the costume names.
5. **The guild roster.** Should the legacy notes' named guilds come into the wiki? Lean: yes, as provisional: keep the functions, ranks and everyday customs that fit the [World Rules](../../World-Rules.md) (apprenticeship, guild suppers, welfare, cornerstones, masons' travel); drop the Collegium Arcanum (magic, ruled out by [Magic](../../Magic.md)), the guild patron gods (the world has [nine faiths](../../Politics/Religions.md) instead) and game-style ranks; and let bt 7274 keep or rename the names.

6. **The faiths' working life.** Should the legacy notes' rites, festivals, clergy, texts and sects come into the wiki, faith by faith? Lean: yes, as provisional, so stories can stand inside a faith. Keep the practices; leave out the one-faith-per-kingdom mapping, which the wiki rules against; keep the named gods out of canon until bt 7274 says whether these faiths have gods at all, or a Weaver and a Compass that people speak of as one.
