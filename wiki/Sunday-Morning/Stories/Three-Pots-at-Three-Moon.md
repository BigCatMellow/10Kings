# Three Pots at Three Moon

## Status

**Provisional story concept.** Written with the [Sunday Morning framework](../Framework.md). All characters, businesses and plot details are new and non-canon. The stew lineage is an illustration of the [Food Diaspora](../../Culture/Food-Diaspora-and-Adaptation.md) model, not a canonical dish history.

## Premise

A retiring dockside cook will sign her Three Moon Festival stall permit over to whichever of her three grandchildren makes the "right" stew. She won't say what right means.

**Setting:** one Port neighborhood — see [Port](../../Places/Port.md)
**Clock:** [Three Moon Festival](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#port), early autumn
**Mode:** family / food

## Protagonist

**Jory** — the middle grandchild, a multilingual court clerk.

- **Good at:** forms, languages, and getting two relatives who share a language to actually understand each other.
- **Small flaw:** doesn't cook, and defends not cooking at unnecessary length.
- **Why he resists:** he assumes the stall should go to a cousin who cooks, and that his role is just to file the paperwork.

## Place

A working Port neighborhood with generations of mixed families, native Port speech, and festival street stalls that pass down like heirlooms.

Recurring locations:

1. **The stall on festival street** — a fixed spot for three generations.
2. **Tobiah's dockside kitchen** — smoke, salt, dock-worker hours.
3. **Ines's merchant-quarter kitchen** — tiled, bright, imported spice jars.
4. **Mother Seral's back room** — the original pot and nothing written down.
5. **The Market Regulation office** — where the stamp you need always belongs to another office.

## Cast

- **Mother Seral** — the grandmother. Her stew descends from a Deepwood [Guardian Stew](../../Culture/Recipes/Deepwood.md#2-guardian-stew) three generations back. Answers every question with a food question.
- **Tobiah** — from the docks. Makes the stew with preserved fish and a lot of pepper.
- **Ines** — from the merchant quarter. Makes it rich with imported spice and calls that respect.
- **The permit clerk** — knows Jory professionally; enjoys this far too much.
- **A Deepwood caravan cook** — passing through; tastes all three versions and declares none of them Guardian Stew.
- **A newly arrived family of displaced workers** — cooking at the next spot without a permit.

## Problem

A stall permit transfers to one named holder. If the family can't agree before the festival opens, the spot reverts to the ward and goes to the next name on the list.

## Complications

1. The permit transfer requires one name, three stamps and two offices.
2. Tobiah and Ines turn the stew into an argument about excess versus aspiration — see [Two common emotional directions](../../Culture/Food-Diaspora-and-Adaptation.md#two-common-emotional-directions).
3. The Deepwood cook's verdict shakes everyone.
4. The newcomers next door could get both stalls fined by a market inspector.
5. Mother Seral refuses to taste anyone's version until the festival itself.

## Running elements

- Stamps, and the office that holds the other stamp.
- Jory translating between cousins who speak exactly the same language.
- Mother Seral answering questions with food questions.
- The Deepwood cook's increasingly gentle critiques.

## Emotional core

A practice outlives an exact taste. The stew was never a list of ingredients; it was what the family does when it opens the pot.

## Climax

Each grandchild remembers a different fragment of how Mother Seral served the stew. Together they reconstruct the ritual the [Food Diaspora](../../Culture/Food-Diaspora-and-Adaptation.md#stage-3--distinct-cuisine) page predicts survives longest: the pot is opened at the table, and the first bowl goes to a stranger. The Deepwood cook recognizes it immediately — that part *is* Guardian Stew. Jory finds an old "family stall" form nobody uses that allows three names.

## Soft landing

The cousins run the stall together, each pot labeled by neighborhood. On the first night the first bowl goes to the newcomers next door, and Jory helps them with their own permit the next morning. Mother Seral finally tastes all three and says only, "Again next year."
