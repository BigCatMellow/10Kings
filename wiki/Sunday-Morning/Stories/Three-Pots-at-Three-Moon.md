# Three Pots at Three Moon

## Status

**Provisional story concept.** Written with the [Sunday Morning framework](../Notes/Sources/Framework.md). All characters, businesses and plot details are new and non-canon. The stew lineage illustrates the [Food Diaspora](../../Culture/Food-Diaspora-and-Adaptation.md) model; it is not a canonical dish history, and the serving ritual belongs to one family, not to Deepwood as a whole.

**Development level: L3 Drafted** — [read the draft](../Drafts/Three-Pots-at-Three-Moon.md) (fourth pass, checked for fit and uniqueness across the collection; independent L4 review done 2026-09-28; waiting for James's reading). Outlined at L2; developed through the [Story Pipeline](../Notes/Pipeline.md). The L0 concept is preserved in git history (commit `3427bdd`).

## Premise

A retiring dockside cook will sign her Three Moon Festival stall permit over to whichever of her three grandchildren makes "the right stew." She isn't testing them. She has cooked it for fifty years and genuinely cannot say what "right" means.

**Setting:** one Port neighborhood — see [Port](../../Places/Port.md)
**Clock:** [Three Moon Festival](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#port), early autumn — two festival nights
**Mode:** family / food

## Protagonist

**Jory** — the middle grandchild, a multilingual court clerk.

- **Good at:** forms, languages, and getting two relatives who share a language to actually understand each other.
- **Small flaw:** doesn't cook, and defends not cooking at unnecessary length.
- **Why he resists:** he assumes the stall should go to a cousin who cooks, and that his job is only the paperwork.

## Place

A working Port neighborhood with generations of mixed families, native Port speech, and festival-street stalls that pass down like heirlooms.

Recurring locations:

1. **The stall on festival street** — a fixed spot for three generations, and one dented pot.
2. **Tobiah's dockside kitchen** — smoke, salt, dock-worker hours.
3. **Ines's merchant-quarter kitchen** — tiled, bright, imported spice jars.
4. **Mother Seral's back room** — the original pot, mismatched bowls, nothing written down.
5. **The Market Regulation office** — where the stamp you need always belongs to another office.

## Cast

- **Mother Seral** — the grandmother. Her stew descends from a Deepwood [Guardian Stew](../../Culture/Recipes/Deepwood.md#2-guardian-stew) three generations back. Answers every question with a food question.
- **Tobiah** — from the docks. Makes it with preserved fish and a lot of pepper, since forest mushrooms cost a fortune this year; eats standing up.
- **Ines** — from the merchant quarter. Makes it rich with imported spice, "the way great-grandmother would have if she could have afforded it."
- **The permit clerk** — knows Jory professionally; enjoys this far too much.
- **The street's market inspector** — under orders to show numbers during the smuggling surge; fines unpermitted stalls because his superiors count fines, and is visibly relieved by anything properly filed.
- **A Deepwood caravan cook** — on the caravans since a fungal blight took his village's mushroom harvest; he came over Icestep on the first caravan of spring and has been working his way to Port since; tastes all three versions and declares none of them Guardian Stew.
- **The Arden family** — newly arrived from a Greenvale farm they had to sell to a land agent last year ("a very polite man," Mrs. Arden says, "he bought us lunch"), cooking at the next spot without a permit.

## Problem

A stall permit transfers to one named holder. If the family hasn't filed by the festival's second night, the spot reverts to the ward and goes to the next name on the list.

## Complications

1. The transfer needs one name, three stamps and two offices.
2. Tobiah and Ines turn the stew into an argument about excess versus aspiration — see [Two common emotional directions](../../Culture/Food-Diaspora-and-Adaptation.md#two-common-emotional-directions).
3. The Deepwood cook's verdict shakes everyone.
4. With smuggling up across Port, inspectors are fining unpermitted stalls, and a street rumor says the family next door are smugglers. A fine could land on both stalls.
5. Mother Seral refuses to taste anyone's version until the festival itself.

## Running elements

- Stamps, and the office that holds the other stamp.
- Jory translating between cousins who speak exactly the same language.
- Mother Seral answering questions with food questions.
- The one bowl in the set that is always kept back.

## Emotional core

A practice outlives an exact taste. The stew was never a list of ingredients; it is what the family does when it opens the pot.

## Climax

Each grandchild remembers a different fragment of how Mother Seral served the stew. Tobiah remembers the timing: the pot went on at dawn and was never hurried. Ines remembers the bowls: one was always kept back. Jory, the one who never cooked, remembers what he did as a boy: carry that first bowl to whoever was a stranger at the stall. Together the fragments make the ritual the [Food Diaspora](../../Culture/Food-Diaspora-and-Adaptation.md#stage-3--distinct-cuisine) page predicts survives longest: the pot opened at the table, the first bowl to a stranger. The Deepwood cook recognizes it at once — his own village did the same. Jory finds an old "family stall" form nobody uses that allows three names.

## Soft landing

The cousins run the stall together, three pots labeled by neighborhood. On the second night the kept-back bowl goes to the Ardens next door, and Jory helps them with their own permit the next morning. The inspector signs off on both stalls and eats standing up. On the next street over, the smuggling rumors are still running; this street has simply stopped listening. The Deepwood cook tries Ines's version, winces, and has a second bowl. Mother Seral finally tastes all three and says only, "Again next year."

### Stage 1 — THINK

**Reasoning allocation:** structured single path. The resolution depends on a ritual claim that touches Deepwood culture, and the grandmother's motive needs checking.

**Decomposition.** Separable problems:

1. the permit mechanics (the clock);
2. the three versions of the stew;
3. what "right" actually means;
4. Jory's role and skill;
5. the newcomers;
6. the outside verdict from the Deepwood cook.

Connection to keep: Jory's clerk skill and his forgotten childhood role must both be necessary; if only one matters, he is a bystander.

**Assumption mapping**

| Assumption | Status | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The grandmother withholds the answer as a test | `ASSUMED` → **false** | That reads as manipulative. She can't articulate a tacit practice, so "the right one" is all she can say. |
| Guardian Stew has a communal serving ritual | `VERIFIED` (illustrative) | [Food Diaspora](../../Culture/Food-Diaspora-and-Adaptation.md#guardian-stew): identity from slow cooking, forest aromatics and a communal serving ritual. |
| "First bowl to a stranger" is Deepwood custom | `ASSUMED` → **narrowed** | Stated as this family's form of the ritual. The Deepwood cook's village shares it; the region isn't claimed to. |
| Port stall permits pass to one named holder | `ASSUMED` | Invented, provisional; consistent with [Port](../../Places/Port.md#governance-and-protection) market regulation. |
| Jory only files paperwork | `ASSUMED` → **false** | He also holds a fragment of the ritual, and as the non-cook he was the one who carried bowls. |

**First principles.** The story must reconstruct a tacit practice that no single heir holds. That structure is recombination: the whole survives only as fragments spread across three people. It is the one reserve method from THINK's library that this story needs, and it is used because the problem is literally recombination, not by default.

**Counterexample search**

| Attack | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Why doesn't anyone just ask the grandmother? | **Survives.** They do; she answers with food questions, because the practice isn't in words. |
| The Deepwood cook's authority settles the question | **Narrows.** He recognizes the ritual; he doesn't rank the stews, and he quietly prefers Ines's. |
| Helping the newcomers turns the story into a charity lesson | **Narrows.** It stays a small act: one bowl, one form. |

**PLAN handoff**

```text
frame             three heirs each hold a fragment of a practice their grandmother can't put into words
selected strategy fragments recombined (timing, kept-back bowl, first bowl to a stranger); family-stall form
alternatives      A grandmother's deliberate test — set aside: manipulative
                  B Deepwood cook names the "correct" recipe — set aside: contradicts diaspora model
assumptions       permit rule and the ritual are provisional family/local details
unknowns          Port ward governance of stalls stays vague
reconsider if     the ritual reads as a Deepwood-wide custom; the newcomers become a lesson
```
