# The Ladder Rules

## Status

**Provisional story concept.** Written with the [Sunday Morning framework](../Notes/Sources/Framework.md). All characters, places, customs and plot details are new and non-canon. Promoted from [Story Seeds](Story-Seeds.md).

**Development level: L2 Planned** (2026-11-02). One of seven stories added inside the collection's year at bt 7274's request ([D16](../Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)). THINK and PLAN are recorded below; there is no draft yet.

## Premise

A new rule agreed between the Sunplains cities says every lamp ladder needs two people, one to climb and one to foot it. The city pays for one. Three days before the longest night of the year the lamplighters announce they'll stand down, and the street lamps stay dark unless somebody pays for the second person. The woman who has footed her father's ladder her whole life, and never once climbed it, is the one who thinks to count the footers the city already has.

**Setting:** Vasselin, a Sunplains city-state of courtyard houses and shaded streets around an old cistern square. The name is provisional ([Naming](../../Culture/Naming.md)).
**Clock:** [Solstice Lanterns](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#sunplains), Year 1 midwinter: the three days before the longest night, and the night itself. No date is fixed ([Calendar](../../Calendar-and-Timekeeping.md#4-seasonal-thresholds-can-matter-more-than-fixed-dates)).
**Mode:** a countdown caper.

## Protagonist

**Yusa Rennet**, a ladder-footer on the eastern lamp round.

- **Good at:** counting. She knows every lamp on the eastern round by its number and how long each one takes, and she can hear from the scrape of a ladder whether it's set right.
- **Small flaw:** she's never climbed, she's afraid of heights, and she's defensive about it. She says footing's the real job a little too often, as if someone's arguing.
- **Why she resists:** the quarrel is between the lighters' guild and the city's assembly. Footers aren't in the guild, nobody pays them, and nobody asks them anything.

## Place

A walled core of courtyard houses, a cistern square with the assembly steps on one side, and lamps on iron arms high up the walls. In a drought year the fountains run short and oil is being hoarded.

Recurring locations:

1. **The lamp yard**, behind the cistern: ladders racked by round, oil jars, the guild's chalk tally.
2. **The assembly steps**, where the city's lamp warden receives deputations politely and refuses them politely.
3. **The Rennets' roof terrace**, which looks over the eastern round.
4. **The eastern round**, its lanes and courtyards, every lamp numbered.
5. **The great lamp** over the assembly steps, the last lit and the highest.

## Cast

- **Dov Rennet**, Yusa's father, a lighter for longer than she's been alive. He has a knee that complains, and he has never asked her to climb.
- **Qadir Lemm**, master of the lighters' guild. Proud, fair, and boxed in by his own announcement.
- **Rafiq Daunt**, the assembly's lamp warden. He speaks in the formal register, refuses without ever saying no, and needs a way to agree that doesn't look like losing.
- **Zeyna**, an oil-seller's widow with a cellar of jars she's been keeping "for the drought".
- **The footers:** wives, sons, neighbors and apprentices who have footed ladders for years without a name in any ledger.

## Problem

After a lighter's fall in another city, the cities in the coordination talks agreed a joint lamp standard: two to a ladder. Vasselin's assembly adopted it at once. The guild says the city pays one wage per ladder, and with oil short and the standard in force it can't put up a ladder it isn't paid to staff. Unless the assembly pays for footers, the guild stands down for Solstice Lanterns, and the city's household lanterns walk the longest night under dark lamps.

## Complications

1. The warden and the guild master are both stuck in public positions, and the Sunplains formal register lets each refuse the other at length without saying no.
2. Oil is short. Households have been hoarding it against the drought, and the city's lamp store is thin, so even a deal leaves lamps unlit.
3. Yusa's count shows the guild's ledger doesn't have a single footer in it. Nor does the city's. On paper, the footers don't exist.
4. Zeyna won't open her cellar to anyone who calls her a hoarder, which is everyone.
5. Dov's knee won't take the great lamp's ladder this year, and the great lamp is his.

## Running elements

- **The lamp count.** Yusa counts every lamp aloud as it's lit, a number for each, all night.
- **"Light holds!"**, the lighters' call when a lamp takes. By a lighters' superstition nobody says the word *light* before the first lamp of the night is lit, so the city spends the afternoon talking around it ([Folk Belief #3](../../Culture/Folk-Belief-Taboo-and-Superstition.md#3-occupational-superstition)).
- **The formal register.** Every refusal on the assembly steps is a compliment.

## Emotional core

Work nobody names still holds the ladder. Being counted is a kind of respect, and the job you do at the bottom doesn't need defending once somebody's had to do it.

## Solution

Yusa counts the footers. She goes door to door on the eastern round and then across the city's other rounds with Qadir's lighters, and she gets a name for every lighter's footer on every round last year. Every lighter had one. The city has had two to a ladder all along; it has never paid the second. That lets everyone agree without anyone losing. The standard asks for no new people, only for the people already there to be counted. The footers are paid in a lamp-share: each footer's household lantern is filled from the city's store. Rafiq can give that without the assembly's vote, because a lantern filled at Solstice Lanterns is a festival gift from the city's store, not a wage. Zeyna, asked as a supplier and not accused as a hoarder, sells the store the oil it's short.

## Climax

The lighting. The rounds go up one after another, and "Light holds!" travels across the city. Dov's knee won't take the great lamp's ladder, and everyone turns to Yusa. She says no. She's never climbed and she isn't going to start in front of the whole city. Then Rafiq, to everyone's astonishment, takes off his good coat and says he'll climb it, because the warden who agreed the standard should be seen keeping it. He's never climbed a ladder in his life either. Yusa foots it. Halfway up he stops, and she talks him the rest of the way from the bottom, the way she's talked her father up every lamp on the eastern round, and he lights it.

## Soft landing

Not food this time. The meal comes earlier, on the roof terrace. The landing is the view: the lamps lit across the city and the household lanterns moving under them. By custom the lighter calls the lamp. Rafiq is too out of breath to call anything, so the call comes from the bottom of the ladder this year. The final line is Yusa's "Light holds!"

### Stage 1 — THINK

**Reasoning allocation:** structured single path. The mechanism is a small piece of labor arithmetic, and the risk is that the strike reads as the villain's tool, which the [Guilds](../../Politics/Guilds.md#guild-interaction-with-the-villain) page warns about.

**Decomposition.**

1. Why the standard exists.
2. Why it can't be met in time.
3. Why neither side can back down.
4. What Yusa knows that no ledger does.
5. How everyone agrees without losing face.
6. Who climbs the great lamp, and who holds it.

Connection to keep: her count (4) is also her flaw's opposite. She knows the ground better than anyone because she's always stayed on it.

**Assumption mapping.**

| Assumption | Status | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sunplains cities have street lamps and lighters | `ASSUMED` | Not stated anywhere. A modest, plausible civic service, given oil exports and walled cores. Marked provisional. |
| Oil is short in a drought year | `ASSUMED` → **narrowed** | Sunplains exports oils ([Trade](../../Economy/Trade-and-Dependencies.md#core-model)). The shortage is local hoarding, not a failed harvest, which fits drought *anxiety* rather than drought. |
| The cities' talks produce joint standards | `VERIFIED` in kind | City-state coordination is a current event. A lamp standard is a provisional example. |
| How many city-states there are | `UNKNOWN` | Never said. "The cities in the talks" is enough. |
| Solstice mechanics and the date | `UNKNOWN` | No calendar detail exists. "The longest night" is said loosely ([Calendar](../../Calendar-and-Timekeeping.md#2-what-is-actually-established)). |
| A relic lamp that dims at lies | `UNKNOWN` by rule | Kept out. Nothing magical is needed ([Magic](../../Magic.md#relics)). |

**First principles.** The story needs a deadline a few days off, a quarrel with no villain, and a fix that comes from below. Removed convention: the strike-breaker or the hero who talks everyone round. Yusa doesn't argue; she counts. The smallest mechanism is that the second person always existed; only the wage didn't.

**Counterexample search.**

| Attack | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Would a city adopt an unfunded standard? | **Survives.** Agreed abroad, adopted fast to look cooperative. That's the nail. |
| Is the stand-down harm on the page? | **Narrows.** No one is hurt, and the dark night never happens in Vasselin. The other city's dark night is offstage and secondhand. |
| Is a count just "an old document" again? | **Survives.** It's made this week, from doors and memory, and it's a list of people, not a record. |
| Does the warden footing the ladder shame him? | **Contradicts if forced; fixed.** He offers. It's his way to be seen keeping the standard, so it saves his face instead of costing it. |
| Should Yusa climb at the end? | **Changed after the independent check.** In the first plan she climbed with the warden footing: the same arc as The Eighth Verse next to it (a helper from the edge does the feared thing in public with an elder steadying her). Now she refuses, the warden climbs, and she foots. Her change is that she stops defending the job and does it, named, in front of everyone. |
