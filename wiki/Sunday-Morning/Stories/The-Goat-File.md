# The Goat File

## Status

**Provisional story concept.** Written with the [Sunday Morning framework](../Framework.md). Seven Wells, the two houses, all characters and plot details are new and non-canon.

## Premise

A reforming administrator rules that every deferred dispute must be closed at this year's Ledger Closing. The junior arbitration clerk is handed the oldest one as hazing: a forty-three-year feud between two respected houses over a goat.

**Setting:** Seven Wells, a [Highridge](../../Regions/Highridge-Plateau.md) pass town (provisional name)
**Clock:** [Ledger Closing](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#highridge), late autumn
**Mode:** comedy / mystery

## Protagonist

**Pim Aldash** — junior arbitration clerk.

- **Good at:** reading old records and noticing what they don't say.
- **Small flaw:** so eager to prove himself that he takes the hazing seriously.
- **Why he resists:** nobody has ever closed this file, and everyone is waiting for him to fail.

## Place

A terraced pass town built around cisterns, caravan yards and an arbitration hall, where the end of the trade cycle means debts settled, renegotiated or publicly disputed before a brief period of relief.

Recurring locations:

1. **The arbitration hall** — benches, ledgers, audience gallery.
2. **The tea stall inside the hall** — the real center of town information.
3. **The cisterns** — House Kesh's domain.
4. **The caravan yards** — House Tarrow's old world.
5. **The archive** — slow, silent, and kept by a custodian who takes silence hours seriously.
6. **The upland pasture** — home to an unreasonable number of the original goat's descendants.

## Cast

- **Ebbe Tarrow** — retired caravan master; dignified everywhere except on this subject.
- **Mardin Kesh** — cistern-keeper; equally dignified; equally not.
- **The senior arbiter** — assigned the file to Pim and is taking bets on the outcome.
- **The tea seller** — knows everything, charges by the cup.
- **A grandchild from each house** — quietly courting, and very much hoping nobody looks too closely at the file.
- **The archive custodian** — a [Silent Whisper](../../Politics/Religions.md#6-the-silent-whisper) observer who releases nothing during silence hours.

## Problem

Every Ledger Closing for forty-three years, both houses have filed a claim to "the Ledger Goat" and its offspring, and the hall has ruled *deferred*. It has become tradition. Now it has to end, and whatever Pim rules, one respected house loses face in public.

## Complications

1. Decades of exactly quoted testimony contradict each other.
2. Neither elder will give an inch.
3. Nobody can agree on what the herd count is supposed to prove — the descendants have been traded all over the plateau.
4. The senior arbiter's bets turn the gallery into an audience.
5. The archive custodian won't release the oldest records until after silence hours, which end the evening before the ruling.

## Running elements

- The tea seller's information, delivered one cup at a time.
- The goat census.
- Testimony quoted "as recorded in the seventeenth year of the dispute" ([Highridge languages](../../Culture/Language-and-Thought.md#highridge-languages)).
- The senior arbiter's odds, updated daily.

## Emotional core

Pride sometimes needs to be swallowed, and ordinary lives are worth the paperwork.

## Climax

In the archive Pim finds the original terms. The goat was never a loan. It was a friendly wager between the two elders' grandmothers, who were best friends: it goes "to whichever house hosts the next wedding between us." There hasn't been one since — until now, if the grandchildren are brave enough. They are.

## Soft landing

Pim closes the file: *Completed. Pending wedding.* The senior arbiter pays out. The tea seller already knew. Next spring's wedding requires a goat, and both houses insist on providing it.
