# The Goat File

## Status

**Provisional story concept.** Written with the [Sunday Morning framework](../Framework.md). Seven Wells, the two houses, all characters and plot details are new and non-canon.

**Development level: L3 Drafted** — [read the draft](../Drafts/The-Goat-File.md) (third pass, revised against James's full voice guide as the test story; not yet reviewed). Outlined at L2; developed through the [Story Pipeline](../Story-Pipeline.md). The L0 concept is preserved in git history (commit `3427bdd`).

## Premise

Lenders in Port will renew Seven Wells' credit this year only if its books are clean, so a reforming administrator rules that every deferred dispute must be closed at this year's Ledger Closing. The junior arbitration clerk is handed the oldest one as hazing: a forty-three-year feud between two respected houses over a goat.

**Setting:** Seven Wells, a [Highridge](../../Regions/Highridge-Plateau.md) pass town (provisional name)
**Clock:** [Ledger Closing](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#highridge), late autumn — about ten days
**Mode:** comedy / mystery

## Protagonist

**Wen Ostry** — junior arbitration clerk.

- **Good at:** reading old records and noticing what they don't say.
- **Small flaw:** so eager to prove himself that he takes the hazing seriously.
- **Why he resists:** nobody has ever closed this file, and everyone is waiting for him to fail.

## Place

A terraced pass town built around cisterns, caravan yards and an arbitration hall, where the end of the trade cycle means debts settled, renegotiated or publicly disputed before a brief period of relief. Attacks up the route have thinned this year's caravans, and credit has tightened with them.

Recurring locations:

1. **The arbitration hall** — benches, ledgers, an audience gallery.
2. **The tea stall inside the hall** — the real center of town information.
3. **The cisterns** — House Kesh's domain.
4. **The caravan yards** — House Tarrow's old world.
5. **The archive** — slow, silent, and kept by a custodian who takes silence hours seriously.
6. **The upland pasture** — home to an unreasonable number of the original goat's descendants.

## Cast

- **Ebbe Tarrow** — retired caravan master; dignified everywhere except on this subject. His house's caravan trade has shrunk with the attacks.
- **Mardin Kesh** — cistern-keeper; equally dignified; equally not. The renewed credit would pay for his cracked cisterns, so he needs the file closed and cannot be seen to lose it.
- **The senior arbiter** — assigned the file to Wen and is taking bets on the outcome. Thirty years ago he was handed the goat file as a junior, and failed.
- **The tea seller** — knows everything, charges by the cup. She has heard of two towns where forced closures this year ruined families.
- **Samir Tareh** — a caravan negotiator in town to settle accounts at Ledger Closing; in the gallery for the ruling.
- **Lio Tarrow and Nessa Kesh** — a grandchild from each house, quietly courting, who fear a public ruling will wreck their chances.
- **The archive custodian** — a [Silent Whisper](../../Politics/Religions.md#6-the-silent-whisper) observer who releases nothing during silence hours.

## Problem

Every Ledger Closing for forty-three years, both houses have filed a claim to "the Ledger Goat" and its offspring, and the hall has ruled *deferred*. It has become tradition. Now it has to end, and whatever Wen rules, one respected house loses face in public, and Lio and Nessa lose their chance.

## Complications

1. Decades of exactly quoted testimony contradict each other.
2. Neither elder will give an inch.
3. Nobody can agree what the herd count is supposed to prove; the descendants have been traded across the plateau.
4. The senior arbiter's betting book turns the gallery into an audience.
5. The archive custodian won't release anything until silence hours end, the evening before the ruling.

## Running elements

- The tea seller's information, delivered one cup at a time.
- The goat census.
- Testimony quoted "as recorded in the seventeenth year of the dispute" ([Highridge languages](../../Culture/Language-and-Thought.md#highridge-languages)).
- The senior arbiter's odds, updated daily.

## Emotional core

Pride sometimes needs to be swallowed, and ordinary lives are worth the paperwork.

## Climax

The tea seller mentions, several cups in, that the two elders' mothers used to walk to the archive together. The custodian, once silence hours end, releases a sealed private memorandum with one line: "You are the first to ask." It records a friendly wager between two best friends. The goat was never a loan: it goes "to whichever house hosts the next wedding between us." There hasn't been one since. At the Ledger Closing hearing Wen reads it aloud. Lio and Nessa stand up.

## Soft landing

Wen closes the file: *Completed. Pending wedding.* The ruling is lawful because the ownership is settled by a condition, and Highridge law is comfortable with conditions. The senior arbiter pays out. The tea seller already knew. Next spring's wedding requires a goat, and both houses insist on providing it. The administrator sends clean books to Port, and Mardin's cisterns will be mended. The senior arbiter tells Wen he failed this file thirty years ago, and buys him tea. And a caravan house joined to a cistern house can offer passing caravans water and guides under one roof. Samir Tareh, leaving the gallery, asks Wen one question about the water, and writes something down.

### Stage 1 — THINK

**Reasoning allocation:** structured single path. It is a fair-play mystery with a legal resolution a reader could catch out.

**Decomposition.** Separable problems:

1. the dispute's history;
2. where the true record lives and why it never surfaced;
3. how Wen finds it;
4. the ruling's legality;
5. the grandchildren;
6. the clock.

Connection to keep: the record's discovery must depend on people (the tea seller, the custodian), not only on paperwork, or Wen's skill becomes the whole story.

**Assumption mapping**

| Assumption | Status | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ledger Closing settles or disputes debts publicly | `VERIFIED` (provisional festival) | [Festivals](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#highridge). |
| The original terms were in the hall's file | `ASSUMED` → **false** | Then someone would have read them in 43 years. The terms now sit in a sealed private deposit in the archive. |
| Why nobody found the deposit | `ASSUMED` → **grounded** | Its seal opened long ago; nobody asked. Fits the Silent Whisper's preservation practice ([Religions](../../Politics/Religions.md#6-the-silent-whisper)). |
| A hall can close a file on a future condition | `ASSUMED` → **grounded** | Highridge registers mark conditions and obligations precisely ([Language](../../Culture/Language-and-Thought.md#highridge-languages)). |
| The grandchildren fear the file | `ASSUMED` → **explained** | A public win for either house would make their families' reconciliation impossible. |

**First principles.** The story must turn an absurd feud into something both houses can accept without either losing. A conditional ruling does that: nobody loses, and the condition is a happy one. Removed convention: a culprit who lied. The sons simply each remembered the goat as their mother's.

**Counterexample search**

| Attack | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Wen finds it alone by reading | **Narrows.** The tea seller's hint and the custodian's release are both required. |
| Which house "hosts" a wedding joining both? | **Survives as the joke.** Both claim to host; both provide the goat. |
| The senior arbiter's betting is cruel | **Narrows.** He pays out cheerfully and is the first to congratulate Wen. |

**PLAN handoff**

```text
frame             a clerk closes an absurd feud by finding the friendly wager that started it
selected strategy sealed private memorandum in the archive; tea seller's hint; conditional ruling
alternatives      A terms hidden in the hall's own file — set aside: implausible for 43 years
                  B one house proven right — set aside: someone loses face; tone
assumptions       archive deposit practice is a provisional story detail
unknowns          Seven Wells' governance and the administrator's office stay vague
reconsider if     readers guess the wedding clause by s5; the custodian reads as a gatekeeper villain
```
