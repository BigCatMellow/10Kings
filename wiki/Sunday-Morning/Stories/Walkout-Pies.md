# Walkout Pies

## Status

**Provisional story concept.** Written with the [Sunday Morning framework](../Notes/Sources/Framework.md). All characters, places, customs and plot details are new and non-canon.

**Development level: L3 Drafted, first pass** (2026-11-03): [read the draft](../Drafts/Walkout-Pies.md). One of seven stories added inside the collection's year at bt 7274's request ([D16](../Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)), drafted after he approved the test story, [The Eighth Verse](The-Eighth-Verse.md) ("go for it"). ## Premise

The pie-maker at an Ironcrest foundry gate keeps a chalkboard by her oven where the crews chalk their names for walkout pies, ordered ahead for the picket line. All spring the crews have been on short hours. Now, in Foundry Week, the list is longer every morning. The crews' leaders want to walk out at the great pour, when it would hurt most, because they've heard the owners are bringing in outside carters to replace them.

**Setting:** Coalglen Meadows, an [Ironcrest](../../Regions/Ironcrest.md#towns) foundry town in a valley that hasn't had a meadow in living memory. The name comes from the [name bank](../../Places/Border-Towns.md#other-provisional-names). It isn't Stonefield Forge.
**Clock:** [Foundry Week](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#seasonal-calendar), Year 2 midsummer, from the first day to the great pour.
**Mode:** a warm labor comedy.

## Protagonist

**Kettie Sowerby**, who bakes the shift pies at the foundry gate and keeps the crews' mutual-aid box.

- **Good at:** feeding people. She knows who's eating less, who's buying for two, and whose pie money has gone short before they've said a word. She keeps the box honest to the copper.
- **Small flaw:** she meddles. She tells crew and foremen alike what she thinks, loudly, and usually before they've asked.
- **Why she resists:** she isn't crew and she isn't guild. She rents her stall from the foundry, and the walkout's not hers to call. She'd like to stay a pie-seller.

## Place

A narrow valley of foundries and workers' rows, smoke and water wheels, slag heaps, and a gate where the shifts change.

Recurring locations:

1. **Kettie's stall at the foundry gate**, with its oven, its chalkboard and the box.
2. **The casting floor**, where the great pour will happen, glimpsed through the gate.
3. **The crews' tavern**, which is losing customers on short hours.
4. **The slag-heap road**, where the outside carters camp.
5. **The long table** outside the gate. By custom the pour crew eats there together before the great pour. Nobody pours hungry.

## Cast

- **Bosk Ferrin**, the crews' loudest leader. He's right about the short hours, and he has a family on half pay.
- **Old Nadia Pryor**, a pour-master near the end of her working life. She thinks a ruined pour hurts the town more than the owners, and says so.
- **Foreman Lusk**, who works for the owners and eats Kettie's pies like everyone else. He says the carters aren't there to replace anybody. Nobody believes the owners' man, and the truth is he doesn't know what they're for either; nobody told him.
- **The outside carters**, a dozen men and their teams, camped by the slag heap and hungry.
- **Kerra Dunnock**, a young bladesmith from Stonefield just past her masterwork, come to watch the great pour. A cameo; see [Inspected](Inspected-Not-Guaranteed.md).

## Problem

The great pour is the year's biggest casting, the order the town's next season rests on. The leaders want the walkout called at the long table. If the pour crew won't eat, the crew won't pour, and the casting is lost. They've heard that the owners have hired outside carters to take their places. The box holds what's left after a spring of paying out for short hours, and the leaders want it handed over for the walkout. Kettie keeps the box, and Kettie feeds the long table.

## Complications

1. The chalkboard: on the third day of the Week, the walkout list runs off the bottom of the board.
2. Bosk asks Kettie for the box in front of the gate.
3. Nadia won't support a walkout at the pour, and the crews split along age.
4. The carters arrive early and camp by the slag heap with nothing to eat. Nobody on either side will go near them.
5. Gossip from up the valley: Orin Slatehallow's workshop is hiring at full pay, some patron or other. Half the young crew want to go.

## Running elements

- **The chalkboard**, a list of names ordering walkout pies, the town's mood in chalk. (One list, not two columns: Hobb's slate in One Square has the two columns.)
- **The box.** Its weight is checked in every scene, by hand.
- **"Nobody pours hungry"**, the pour crew's custom (provisional): it's said at the start of every shift and is the reason the long table exists.

## Emotional core

Feeding people isn't taking a side, and it isn't staying out of it either. People who share a table can find out who they're actually angry with.

## Solution

A meal. On the night before the pour, Kettie has a walkout's worth of pies and nobody to eat them. She takes them to the slag heap and feeds the carters. At dawn she brings the carters to the long table, where the pour crew is sitting down. It isn't a friendly supper. Bosk goes straight at them across the table: whose places were they promised? The carters' headman goes straight back. They were hired to haul charcoal, because the charcoal carts from the forest border have been short all summer. They're on short hours in their own valley. And they've been called scabs all week by men who wouldn't come near enough to ask; the day they came in, somebody threw a stone at the lead cart, and missed. It's a row, loud and long, with pies on the table between them. Nobody hired the carters to break anything, and the rumor dies in the row. The crews don't walk out at the pour, and they don't stop being angry either. They call the walkout for the morning after the Week, with notice, so the owners have to answer before it.

## Climax

The long table at dawn, after the row. The pour crew and the carters are at the same table, and nobody's said sorry. Bosk can still call the walkout by refusing to eat, and everyone knows it. Kettie puts a pie in front of him and, for once, says nothing at all. He eats. The great pour goes.

## Soft landing

The casting comes out of the sand whole. Kettie gives Bosk the box, openly, in front of the gate: it's for the walkout, which is now the crews' to call properly. She wipes the list off the chalkboard. The final beat is the board with a single order on it: walkout pies, for the morning after the Week, by name.

### Stage 1 — THINK

**Reasoning allocation:** structured single path. The risk is taking the owners' side. Saving the pour must not read as breaking the strike, so the strike has to survive the ending.

**Decomposition.**

1. The short hours and the box.
2. The chalkboard.
3. The rumor about the carters.
4. Why the pour, and why the long table is the moment.
5. The carters' real reason.
6. What happens to the walkout.

Connection to keep: Kettie's flaw (meddling) is what puts the carters at the table, and her change is the one time she says nothing.

**Assumption mapping.**

| Assumption | Status | Finding |

| Foundry Week has a great pour | `ASSUMED` | Only the festival's name exists. A great pour with a crowd watching is a provisional custom, and it fits summer as peak production ([Trade](../../Economy/Trade-and-Dependencies.md#seasonal-stress)). |
| The pour crew eats together first | `ASSUMED` | Provisional occupational custom, a "ritual before dangerous work" ([Folk Belief #3](../../Culture/Folk-Belief-Taboo-and-Superstition.md#3-occupational-superstition)). |

| Charcoal is short | `ASSUMED` | Provisional. It's plausible from forest-border conflict over extraction ([Border Towns](../../Places/Border-Towns.md#darkroot-gulch--deepwood--ironcrest)). The story gives no cause beyond "the carts have been short". |
| Orin can be mentioned | `VERIFIED` with limits | Gossip, as in Inspected. His patron, his workshop's place and his outcome stay open. |

**First principles.** The story needs a real grievance, a moment of maximum damage, a rumor that makes the damage look necessary, and a way for the rumor to die that doesn't kill the grievance. Removed convention: the strike as the problem. The problem is the timing, and the story never says the crews are wrong. The smallest mechanism: the people the rumor is about are hungry and nearby.

**Counterexample search.**

| Attack | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Is Kettie breaking the strike? | **Narrows.** She saves the pour and then gives the box to the walkout. The walkout happens, with notice. |
| Wouldn't the carters lie about why they're there? | **Survives.** They've heard the rumor, as an insult shouted at them, and they're angry about it. They say it back across the table because somebody finally said it to their faces. |
| Is a meal resolution just One Square's again? | **Risk, raised by the independent check.** One Square also kills its rumor over food: the Vale eats the "unsafe" grain at long tables. Here the meal isn't a demonstration. It puts two angry groups close enough to have the row, and the row kills the rumor. |
| Is anyone hurt by a lost pour? | **Contradicts the tone; fixed.** The lost pour is in another valley, offstage, as money and blame, not injury. |
| Does Kerra's cameo add anything? | **Narrows.** She's someone from outside who admires the pour, the town's pride seen from outside. One scene, no lines that matter to the plot. |
