# The Same Paper

## Status

## Premise

Every week of the frost somebody has to climb inside the great bellows at the Upper Works and work warm dripping into the leathers, or they crack, and there'll be no pour in spring. For forty years that was Iolo Gwynne. Since the summer he's been in bed, and it's been his granddaughter Rhona, who makes bellows of her own design and has a letter behind the jug offering her a workshop of her own, anywhere she likes. The carrier comes back for her answer the morning after the shortest day. A stranger with a split boot stops till the carrier comes and stitches her bellows. He looks at her letter for longer than it takes to read.

**Setting:** Clinker Row, a row of crew cottages under the Upper Works, in the [Ironcrest](../../Regions/Ironcrest.md#towns) valley above Coalglen Meadows: the valley that lost its pour to a walkout the summer of [Walkout Pies](Walkout-Pies.md). All provisional.
**Clock:** the shortest day, and the carrier the morning after. Year 2, midwinter. The Works is cold for [winter maintenance](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#ironcrest), and nobody knows if it'll light in spring.
**Mode:** quiet, wry, winter.

## Protagonist

**Rhona Gwynne**, twenty, who makes bellows. Her own pattern blows on the push and the pull, so a small forge never loses its heat between strokes.

- **Good at:** wood, iron and air. She can hear a leak in a bellows from the next room.
- **Small flaw:** she's decided that nobody here wants anything from her, so leaving will cost nobody anything, and she won't ask in case she's right.
- **Why she resists staying:** there's no work in the valley, the saddler's boy who did her leathers has gone up to the Slatehallow workshop, and her grandfather tells everybody she's off.

## Place

1. **The Upper Works**, cold since summer: two great bellows the size of cart sheds, a water wheel iced to its trough, the casting floor still crusted with summer's sand, and a vent in the roof that lets the snow in.
2. **The side gate**, kept by Jupp, the owners' watchman.
3. **The Gwynnes' kitchen** on the Row, with the bench, the jug on the shelf and the door to Iolo's room open so he can join in.
4. **The doorstep**, where a pot of dripping turns up most mornings.
5. **The bridge** at the bottom of the Row, where the valley road goes down to Coalglen.

## Cast

- **Iolo Gwynne**, her grandfather, the Works' bellows-keeper for forty years, in bed since the summer with his chest, and mending: he'll be up by the thaw. Loud, hospitable, rude about everything, and shouts through the wall. Tells every passer-by from his window that she's off.
- **Wurdren**, passing down the valley with a boot split across the sole. Iolo saw him limping and had him in. He's mended his own kit a long time and can sew leather with two needles.
- **Jupp**, the owners' watchman, paid to keep everybody out of the Works. Opens the side gate for Rhona every week and says he never saw her.
- **Cadoc**, a ladle-man, one of the crews that walked out. Won't set foot in the owners' Works.
- **Verity**, next door.
- **Druce**, the valley's carrier, with a mule. He takes letters down and brings replies.

## Problem

The leathers have to be kept through the frost or there's no spring for anybody, crews or owners. Rhona is leaving on the carrier's day, and nobody else on the Row will go into the Works. She won't ask anybody to take it on, because asking would be asking whether anyone wants her to stay.

## Complications

1. Iolo has already told the stranger, and half the Row, that she's off.
2. Her own bellows has split along a side seam; her stitches pulled out, and the saddler's boy is up the valley at the Slatehallow workshop. She'd have left it; Wurdren stitches it for his bed.
3. Wurdren holds her letter to the lamp and turns it till a watermark shows that she'd never seen: a little ship's lantern. He's seen this paper before, and can't think where.
4. Up in the Works she asks him whether to take it. He starts to say yes, the way he does, and stops: "I don't know."
5. The last dressing before the shortest day is done. Iolo, who heard Coalglen's bell for its pour last summer and listened for the Upper Works' and heard none, asks her to give the Works its breath. It takes more weight than two people have.

## Running elements

- **The dripping:** the fat skimmed off every supper into the crock "for the Works", and the pot on the step most mornings, which Rhona thinks Iolo arranged.
- **"I never saw you."** Jupp at the side gate, every time.
- **Iolo through the wall,** joining in everything from bed.
- **Rhona's bellows,** split, then stitched, then lighting the fire.

## Emotional core

You can know a thing matters before you know why. Rhona keeps the leathers without being able to say what for; the Row keeps her in dripping without saying anything at all; Wurdren knows the paper matters and can't think why.

## Solution

**Gifts left in secret, seen all at once.** The morning after the breath, Rhona opens the door before light to wait for the carrier and finds the Row on her step: Cadoc, Jupp, Verity and two more, each with a pot of dripping, each caught, each pretending he just happened by. They heard the Works breathe, and they've heard from Iolo's window that she's going. The pots have been them all winter, crews and owners' man alike, every one on the quiet.

## Climax

The step, before light. Nobody says anything that matters, and nobody asks her to stay. She writes her answer at the kitchen table, "After the pour", and gives it to Druce.

## Soft landing

Wurdren carries the pots in and goes down the valley with Druce and the mule, and her reply in the bag. At the bridge he stops and looks back up the valley, like a man who's left something and can't think what, and then goes on. Rhona lights the fire with her bellows, which blows on the push and the pull, and tells Iolo who's been bringing the dripping. "Who did you think?"

**First principles.** The story has to let a reader feel a valley's affection given without words, and has to let the paper be noticed without being understood. The smallest mechanism for the first is a pot left on a step; for the second, a watermark that a stranger knows to look for and its owner never saw.

**Counterexample search.**

- *Does the thread drive it?* Replace the letter with an ordinary offer of work down the valley and every scene still works. The letter makes it matter more ([Rules](../Notes/Rules.md#what-keeps-it-sunday-morning)).
- *Does anyone see the pattern?* Rhona thinks it's a kind offer, as Col did. Iolo mocks the wording, not the sender. Wurdren can't place the paper, doesn't think of Col or Orin, and gives no warning. His "I don't know" is about his own advice.
- *Does it repeat Inspected?* Inspected was told from Wurdren, turned on a guild judgment, and had him tell Col "Take it". This is told from Rhona; there's no judgment; he starts to say it and stops. He holds the letter to the lamp without the moneychanger, and shows her the watermark she'd missed, so the reader sees the recognition as an act.
- *Does the step read as a crowd turning up* (Inspected's farmers, Walkout Pies' carters)? Five people with pots in the dark, one at a time, each embarrassed to find the others. No line, no march.
- *Shadow Charity* also turns on food left on doorsteps, so it waits a season ([Collection](../Notes/Collection.md#season-2-reading-order-plan)).
- *Is the sad moment said twice?* The lost pour is weather everywhere except Iolo's one line about the bell. Its payoff, Iolo hearing the breath, is three words.
- *Is anybody shamed on the step?* Nobody comments on anybody.
- *Is the arc "does the feared thing in public"?* Rhona doesn't do anything in public. She finds out something.

**PLAN handoff.**

```text
frame               a young bellows-maker leaving a cold valley finds out the valley wants her, without anybody asking
selected strategy   the leathers, three days to the shortest day, the breath, gifts seen all at once on the step;
                    the third letter as a detail Wurdren notices and can't place
alternatives        Rhona is Orin's sister (rejected: she'd know the paper, and the pattern would be on the page);
                    she refuses outright (rejected: too tidy; the hand should come back);
                    the Row asks her to stay (rejected: a speech, and the arc the collection already overuses);
                    Wurdren leaves a pot of his own on the step (rejected: too sweet; he carries them in instead)
assumptions         as above
unknowns            what Rhona's answer will be after the pour (for The Second Pour or later)
stop / reverse      if Wurdren seems to suspect the sender; if the step needs explaining; if Iolo's bell is said twice
```
