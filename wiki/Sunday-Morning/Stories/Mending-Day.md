# Mending Day

## Status

**Provisional story concept.** Written with the [Sunday Morning framework](../Notes/Sources/Framework.md). All characters, places, customs and plot details are new and non-canon, except where they use the wiki: the [Ridge-Roamers](../../Culture/Nomads.md#the-ridge-roamers) and their clans are provisional canon ([D24](../Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)).

**Development level: L3 Drafted, first pass** (2026-11-04): [read the draft](../Drafts/Mending-Day.md). The test story for Season 2 and for [world stories](../Notes/Rules.md#world-stories) ([D23](../Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions), [D24](../Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)). It was the first of the [world seeds](Story-Seeds.md#mending-day) picked, because it tests the new thing, a story with no domino, and needs no new lore. Its cold read asks one extra question: does it feel like part of the same book?

## Premise

Once a year, the week before Grain Ripening, a Ridge-Roamer clan of menders comes down the lane into Kellerby, and the village has its whole year's broken things laid out on trestles before the wagons are in sight. This year the old mender died in the spring, and his grandson has to do the lot. He's quick, and his repairs last. He doesn't talk, and he can't play, and on a trestle by itself is a fiddle that isn't broken.

**Setting:** Kellerby, a [Greenvale](../../Regions/Greenvale.md) village of farms and orchards (provisional), on the Wethercrag clan's summer [circuit](../../Culture/Nomads.md#a-possible-form).
**Clock:** three days, then the clan moves on to the Ironcrest repair season. [Grain Ripening](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#seasonal-calendar) is the week after. Season 2, Year 3, late summer.
**Mode:** warm and rueful.

## Protagonist

**Kit Wethercrag**, nineteen, the clan's best hand with a bow drill and rivets. He can put a smashed platter back together so it holds stew.

- **Good at:** the work. Pots, kettles, riveted crockery, chair joints, hinges. He's fast, and his repairs last.
- **Small flaw:** he thinks the job is the mending. In a clan where everybody plays, he can't, and in a trade that's half talk, he hasn't much.
- **Why he resists:** his grandfather Lewin's way, an hour of chat over every bowl and things left over for next year, looks to Kit like a slow way to do a fast job.

## Place

A village green with a shrine at one end and the common at the other, where the wagons pull in.

Recurring locations:

1. **The trestles** outside the shrine, laid out by household, getting emptier.
2. **The wagons** on the common, where the pay goes: eggs, beans, cheese, a ham.
3. **Effie Rusk's step**, at the last cottage, by the orchard.
4. **The camp fire** on the common at night.
5. **The road out**, toward Ironcrest.

## Cast

- **Isa Wethercrag**, Kit's aunt, who runs the clan's wagons and the circuit's dates. Practical, dry, never late.
- **Dace Wethercrag**, Kit's cousin, the clan's best fiddler, who would rather play for the children than sort pots.
- **Ursel Tanner**, who keeps Kellerby's trestles: she knows every broken thing in the village and whose it is, and lays them out the same way every year.
- **Effie Rusk**, old, sharp, who has brought the same fiddle to the trestles every year for as long as Kit can remember.
- **The village:** the households, who pay in kind and expect to be talked to.

## Problem

Kellerby has saved a year of broken things for the menders, and it expects Lewin: the hour of talk over each one, the stories about what was wrong with it, and a few things taken away for next year. It gets Kit, who mends everything properly, quickly and in silence. The village is pleased with the work and a little flat about the visit. And Effie's fiddle, which Lewin "looked at" every year, has nothing wrong with it but old strings.

## Complications

1. Everybody asks for Lewin, and Isa tells them, once.
2. Kit works through the trestles faster than Lewin ever did, and the villagers who stand about to chat drift off.
3. The fiddle is fine. Isa tells him what Lewin did with it: sat on Effie's step an hour every year and played it, and called that "finding the trouble". Kit can't play.
4. At the mending supper Effie asks after her fiddle, and Kit says he's still looking at it.
5. Working the last bowls by lamplight, Kit sees that the trestles will be empty by morning. Lewin always left something. Isa: he could have done the lot, and didn't.

## Running elements

- **The pay**, in kind, filling the wagons as the trestles empty: eggs, a cheese, beans in a sack, a ham, a pie. Money is short this year; nobody says why.
- **The bow drill and rivets**, the sound of Kit's work all day.
- **"Finding the trouble"**, Lewin's phrase, which Kit ends up using his own way.

## Emotional core

Being expected somewhere is a kind of home. The visit was the mending; the things left over were how Lewin promised to come back.

## Solution

A thing taken away to be brought back. On the last morning the trestles are empty except for the fiddle. Kit can't play it and can't do the hour of talk, so he tells Effie it needs more than he can do here: he'll take it round the circuit for the year, Dace will play it every night so the wood doesn't go dead, and he'll bring it back next Mending Day. She knows exactly what he's doing, and lets him.

## Climax

Effie's step, the last morning. Kit with the fiddle and the offer. Effie lets him finish before she answers, and pays him in plums.

## Soft landing

The wagons go up the lane with Kellerby's fiddle aboard, and the village waves them off as if it's sure of them. At the first camp on the Ironcrest road, Dace plays Effie's fiddle by the fire, and Kit listens.

### Stage 1 — THINK

**Reasoning allocation:** structured single path. The story depends on one mechanism, the fiddle that isn't broken, and on one canon assumption.

**Decomposition.** The mechanism (Lewin's yearly fiddle); the complication trail (the trestles emptying, the flat village, the supper, the lamplight); Kit's change (the job is the coming back); the social resolution (the village sure of next year); the clock (three days). The fiddle and Kit's change must stay one thread.

**Assumption mapping.**

| Assumption | Status | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ridge-Roamers do itinerant repair and string music, by clan | `VERIFIED` | [Nomads](../../Culture/Nomads.md#the-ridge-roamers), D24 |
| A circuit stops at the same village each year, by custom | `VERIFIED` | [Nomads](../../Culture/Nomads.md#a-possible-form): "traditional stopping rights … market privileges recognized by old custom" |
| Greenvale has a late-summer Grain Ripening | `VERIFIED` | [Festivals](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#seasonal-calendar) |
| The old mender's yearly "repair" could be a clock | **false** | [Calendar](../../Calendar-and-Timekeeping.md) says not to assume mechanical clocks. Load-bearing: changed to a fiddle, which also ties it to the clan's music |
| Riveting broken crockery with a bow drill is within the world's technology | `ASSUMED` | pre-industrial, real-world; nothing in the wiki contradicts it |
| Fiddles kept played "stay alive" | `ASSUMED` | a player's belief, said as a belief |

**First principles.** The story has to let a reader feel that a visit can be the thing mended, without anyone saying so. The smallest mechanism: one object that comes back every year unfixed, and an heir who can't do what fixed it.

**Counterexample search.**

- *Is the fiddle trick a deception that hurts Effie?* Only if she doesn't know. She knows, and the climax lets her show it without saying it.
- *Does it repeat the shared arc of the last seven* (a quiet craftsperson who breaks a credo by saying one plain thing in public)? Kit has no credo, and the climax is private, at a gate. He says more than usual, but what he does is take the fiddle.
- *Does Isa become a docent?* She gets one line about Lewin and the fiddle, and one about the trestles. Nothing else is explained.
- *Is the ending "Again next year"?* That's [Three Pots](Three-Pots-at-Three-Moon.md)' final line; this one mustn't use it or its shape.
- *Does "very nearly right" leak in from [Inspected](Inspected-Not-Guaranteed.md)?* Lewin's phrase is "finding the trouble" instead.

**PLAN handoff.**

```text
frame               a mender's heir learns the visit was the mending
selected strategy   one unbroken fiddle, three days, a thing taken away to come back
material alternatives  the old woman plays it herself (rejected: a performance, and Blank Square's quiet request);
                    Kit leaves random things unfinished (rejected: a trick, not a choice)
assumptions         as above
unknowns            what Effie's husband played (kept unsaid)
stop / reverse      if the climax needs Kit to explain what Lewin was doing
```

### Stage 4 — JUDGE

**Independent review** (2026-11-04), cold read first, by a reader who had just read Walkout Pies and The Road in Use. "Same book? Mostly yes": same spoken register, ensemble village, food and gentle comedy; but "the thinnest of the three on invented-world texture" ("chapel", "Mrs.", "lay-by" read as a real English village). It predicted the fiddle and the thing taken away, braced once (the trestles finished early; Isa's answer kept it gentle), and found the ending warm, "Effie's hand on the worn varnish, then 'Mind you're not late,'" the best moment, with the last line "a shade too neat".

**Must-fixes, all made:** a clock hour (the wiki assumes no clocks); "lay-by"; the climax measuring Kit's words, the shared arc's signature move ([Decisions](../Notes/Decisions.md#open-for-the-author), "the shared arc"); explaining after "He took his time."; hardship said twice in one paragraph; throat-clearing; a "same week next year" exchange in Three Pots' shape.

**Taken from the should-considers (leans, for bt 7274 to overrule):** "chapel" became "shrine" and "Mrs." became "Nan", for world texture; Effie's fingers keep time instead of planting that she'll play; Effie pays in plums, which delivers the ledger's "Effie's pay"; Old Daws plants in scene 2 that Lewin always took something away; the meeting moved from Effie's gate to her step, away from Inspected's ending; "Eat that." (Walkout Pies' Kettie) cut; the final exchange ("It was in it… I just let it out."), which shared The Blank Square's last-line shape and moved the ending off Kit, was cut, so the story ends on Kit listening, as the soft landing planned.

**Left for bt 7274:** "He hadn't known. He knew now."; Kit's "Yes. But it'll take a year.", which keeps Lewin's formula on purpose; Effie's goose story, close to the Registry's elder who answers sideways.

**Fresh check of the fixes** (2026-11-04): every fix in, nothing nearby broken. It caught six continuity slips the fixes made or exposed (going inside for orchard plums, a doubled "Mind you", who restrings the fiddle, Ursel calling to the wrong wagon, the blue cloth, the fiddle never returned to the trestle on the first night); all fixed.
