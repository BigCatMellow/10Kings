# The Road in Use

## Status

**Provisional story concept.** Written with the [Sunday Morning framework](../Notes/Sources/Framework.md). All characters, places, customs and plot details are new and non-canon.

**Development level: L3 Drafted, first pass** (2026-11-03): [read the draft](../Drafts/The-Road-in-Use.md). One of seven stories added inside the collection's year at bt 7274's request ([D16](../Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)), drafted after he approved the test story, [The Eighth Verse](The-Eighth-Verse.md) ("go for it"). Planned to L2 on 2026-11-02; THINK, PLAN and DO are recorded below.

## Premise

A spring rockfall has cut the road a Highridge pass town walks for its Caravan Blessing. The only way round passes an old toll tower the slip uncovered, which two families each say was their great-grandmother's. A letter from the route authority says the town's share of the season's tolls will be reckoned "on the road in use at the blessing". The mule-wrangler chosen by lot to lead the blessing string would rather talk to anything with four legs than either family.

**Setting:** Ninewell Saddle, a pass town on a lower saddle of [the Spine](../../Places/The-Spine.md), on the [Highridge](../../Regions/Highridge-Plateau.md#towns) side (provisional). It isn't Icestep Summit or Seven Wells.
**Clock:** [Caravan Blessing](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#seasonal-calendar), Year 2 late spring, over three days.
**Mode:** an ensemble road comedy.

## Protagonist

**Zan Coyle**, a mule-wrangler in the town's animal yards.

- **Good at:** animals. He knows every mule and pony in the yards by temper, and he can get a string over bad ground without raising his voice.
- **Small flaw:** he doesn't do people. He agrees with whoever's talking so they'll stop, and he hides in the yard.
- **Why he resists:** the lot picked him, and he didn't want it. Leading the string means walking in front of the whole town, between two families who aren't speaking.

## Place

A walled town astride a saddle: two gates, inns, animal yards, warehouses, a customs house, and a Compass shrine at the far end of the saddle where outbound caravans are blessed.

Recurring locations:

1. **The animal yards**, Zan's refuge.
2. **The blessing road**, from the west gate along the saddle to the shrine, cut halfway by the fall.
3. **The rockfall**, a slope of fresh scree across the road. Nobody was on the road when it came down.
4. **The bypass and the tower**: a goat track round the fall, past the stump of a stone tower with a name carved over its door, worn past reading.
5. **The Compass shrine**, where the blessing ends.
6. **The stair-hut by the east gate**, where the Underpass stair comes up. It's shut this spring.

## Cast

- **Vinegar**, the old lead pony who has headed the blessing string for years. She has opinions about edges.
- **The Jaskes and the Merrows**, two families, each sure the tower was theirs. Each reads the carved name as its own great-grandmother's. Neither is wrong to be sure, and neither can prove it.
- **Lio Tarrow**, from Seven Wells, hired to guide the first outbound caravan. He was married this spring, he doesn't care whose tower it is, and he's the only person Zan finds easy to talk to.
- **The shrine keeper**, who'll bless whatever road arrives.
- **The customs clerk**, who read the letter out and wishes he hadn't.

## Problem

The blessing has to walk a road, and the road it walks is the road of record. If the string goes by the bypass, the toll point moves to the tower, and whichever family can hold the tower holds the town's toll share. If it goes by the old road, the toll stays with the town gate. But the old road is under scree, and there are three days to the blessing. Both families want the bypass, each sure of the tower. The town wants the old road and has no time to clear it.

## Complications

1. Both families court Zan, since he leads the string: gifts at the yard, invitations, hints.
2. The carved name. It's worn nearly smooth. Each family reads its great-grandmother's name in what's left, and the shrine keeper, who has read every old stone on the saddle, says she can't tell either.
3. The clearing crew is short. Half the able-bodied are Jaskes or Merrows, and neither family will dig.
4. Word comes that the caravan is a day early.
5. The stair-hut's broker hasn't come up from below this spring. The deep road fell in the same shake. He's alive, word came up the stair, and working some other way round on the far side. But there are no cellar mushrooms in the market, and nobody knows when there will be.

## Running elements

- **Vinegar.** Every scene with her is a negotiation Zan wins by not talking.
- **The lot.** People keep reminding Zan it chose him, as if that meant something. Lio decides it does.
- **The carved name**, read three ways, settled never.

## Emotional core

You can't hide from people forever, and you don't have to win an argument to end it. Sometimes the kindest thing is to make a quarrel unnecessary.

## Solution

Vinegar refuses the bypass. At the tower the goat track runs along an open edge, and the old pony plants her feet and won't go on. Zan won't force her, and the string won't pass without her. So the blessing can't go by the bypass, and the town has one night to clear the old road. The families dig because the letter cuts both ways: no blessing means no road of record, and no road of record means no toll share this season for anyone, tower or gate. Once they're digging, neither will let the other say it dug less.

## Climax

The night of digging, by lantern. Zan has to say one thing in public, to both families at once: that the pony won't go, and he won't make her. It's the longest speech of his life, and it's four sentences. Then the Jaskes and the Merrows dig side by side because nobody will be the first to put a shovel down. Lio guides the work, and the caravan's mules help haul.

## Soft landing

Morning. The string walks the old road to the shrine with Vinegar in front, and the keeper blesses it. The tower claim stays open; the two families will take it to arbitration on the plateau, and they argue about who'll pay the clerk all the way back to town. The stair-hut stays shut. (The story's meal is earlier, at the shut stair-hut with Lio: [plateau](../../Culture/Recipes/Highridge-Plateau.md) caravan fare from the market.) The final beat is Zan leaving the yard on his own, unasked, and going to the inn where both families are arguing, to sit down with them.

### Stage 1 — THINK

**Reasoning allocation:** structured single path. The risk is that an animal resolution reads as luck. Vinegar's refusal has to be planted as character from the first scene.

**Decomposition.**

1. The fall, and that nobody was hurt.
2. The letter's rule.
3. The tower and the families.
4. Why Zan.
5. Vinegar's refusal.
6. The night's work.
7. The seed for the Underpass story.

Connection to keep: Vinegar's refusal (5) and Zan's flaw (4) work together. He doesn't have to argue, only refuse to force her. Then he has to say so in public.

**Assumption mapping.**

| Assumption | Status | Finding |

| The Spine has earthquakes | `VERIFIED` | [The Spine](../../Places/The-Spine.md#current-events), [Current Events](../../Story/Current-Events.md#earthquakeland-movement). The date isn't fixed anywhere; this shake falls between Pass Opening and the Blessing. |
| The quake is the one in The Heavy Scale | `ASSUMED` → **false** | The Heavy Scale has no quake. This one comes after it, on another stretch of the range. |

**First principles.** The story needs a deadline, a quarrel with no villain, a protagonist who'd rather not, and a resolution that isn't a ruling. Removed convention: deciding who owns the tower. The story makes the question unnecessary for the season. The smallest mechanism: the toll goes where the blessing walks, and the pony won't walk there.

**Counterexample search.**

| Attack | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Is the pony luck? | **Narrows.** Vinegar's dislike of edges is planted in s1 and tested in s3. Readers should expect it before Zan does. |
| Could Zan just lead with another animal? | **Survives.** By custom the blessing string follows its lead, and a new lead in front of the town would be a ruling for one family. |
| Could a rockfall be cleared in a night? | **Narrows.** Only a road-width through loose scree, with the whole town and a caravan's mules. The fall is small. The story says so. |
| Does anyone get hurt? | **Contradicts the tone; fixed.** Nobody was on the road when it fell, and the night's work is slow and careful. |
| Is the magic deniable? | **Survives.** Some say the tower is unlucky and that's why the pony stopped. Zan says it's the edge. Nobody settles it. |
