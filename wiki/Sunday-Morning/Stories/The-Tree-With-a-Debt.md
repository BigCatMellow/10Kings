# The Tree With a Debt

## Status

**Provisional story concept.** Written with the [Sunday Morning framework](../Framework.md). All characters, the tree, the lien and plot details are new and non-canon.

**Development level: L2 Outlined** — developed through the [Story Pipeline](../Story-Pipeline.md). The L0 concept is preserved in git history (commit `3427bdd`).

## Premise

A Deepwood forest warden and a Highridge route surveyor are ordered to route the new mule path to Twilighthollow's herb market around an ancient tree. Then they discover the town pledged the tree as collateral on a Highridge loan sixty years ago and never paid it back.

**Setting:** [Twilighthollow](../../Places/Border-Towns.md#twilighthollow--deepwood--highridge) (Deepwood / Highridge)
**Clock:** [Canopy Vigil and Midsummer Debates](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#seasonal-calendar), midsummer, with the herb fair right after
**Mode:** romance

## Protagonists

**Sessa Alderwater** — forest warden.

- **Good at:** reading ground, water and animal sign; knowing what a place is doing.
- **Small flaw:** treats every outsider's question as a threat until proven otherwise.
- **Why she resists:** a path near that tree is exactly the kind of thing wardens exist to stop.

**Ismet Carrow** — Highridge route surveyor.

- **Good at:** measurement, obligations, and precisely what a promise commits someone to.
- **Small flaw:** footnotes his own speech.
- **Why he resists:** he was promised a simple job, and he is allergic to anything with a moral dimension that isn't in writing.

## Place

A town where forest uplands meet exposed plateau routes. Mixed families of rangers, traders, herders and guides; the Deepwood half of town and the Highridge half keep different midsummer customs on the same days.

Recurring locations:

1. **The tree** — older than the town; carved with marks from several eras.
2. **The herb market** — the reason the path matters.
3. **The vigil grounds** — Deepwood side, deliberately quiet.
4. **The debate hall** — Highridge side, deliberately not.
5. **The mule yard** — home of Contract.
6. **The records office** — including a lien register nobody has opened in decades.

## Cast

- **Contract** — a mule who refuses the surveyed line every time.
- **The market master** — needs the path open before the herb fair.
- **Hollis Varne** — the lender's grandson, who arrives from the plateau expecting sixty years of interest.
- **A Weaver-path elder** — documenting everything for the community record, including things nobody asked to have recorded. See [Path of the Great Weaver](../../Politics/Religions.md#2-path-of-the-great-weaver).
- **Pip** — a child who climbs the tree daily and knows where every root surfaces.

## Problem

In Sessa's language the tree belongs to the grammatical class of living participants. In Ismet's records it is "Lot 14, encumbered," and Highridge practice forbids works on pledged collateral without the lender's consent. The path can't be built until they agree what the tree is, and the herb fair won't wait.

## Complications

1. Canopy Vigil halts all work: the two of them are obliged to sit together and simply observe the tree for a day, while the Highridge half of town debates at full volume across the square.
2. They disagree on what the tree *is*, then on what a path *is*, then on what "around" means.
3. Hollis arrives expecting payment with sixty years of interest; the town panics.
4. The herb fair deadline moves closer.
5. Contract still refuses the surveyed line.

## Running elements

- Contract.
- The debates audible over the vigil.
- Ismet's footnotes.
- Sessa's several words for "old," none of which translate.
- The Weaver elder writing things down at the worst moments.

## Emotional core

Two professional vocabularies turn out to describe the same care. The border speech the wiki describes — "environmental categories with contractual precision" — becomes a relationship. It also touches Deepwood's larger question, at the smallest possible scale: [who gets to define stewardship](../../Regions/Deepwood.md#story-function).

## Climax

Ismet reads the old pledge in the Highridge trade register it was written in, and Sessa hears what he doesn't: the tree was pledged "for as long as it stands." The debt only falls due if the tree falls. For sixty years the lender's family has held a legal interest in the tree *staying up*, the same interest the wardens have. The Highridge contract has been protecting the tree all along. The path follows Contract's line, which runs along the edge of the root zone Pip has been climbing over for years. The town pays the small original principal as a gift, and Hollis agrees to renew the pledge as a guardianship rather than collect.

## Soft landing

The tree ends up with a name in both languages and a clean ledger. The herb fair opens on time. Sessa and Ismet co-sign the survey in a hybrid register neither of their guilds would accept alone; it carries exactly one footnote, and it credits her. Hollis decides to stay and open a tea stall by the new path, which is the next small problem.

### Stage 1 — THINK

**Reasoning allocation:** structured single path. The resolution depends on a debt mechanism, and the romance must emerge from the plot rather than be announced.

**Decomposition.** Separable problems:

1. what the tree is (legal and grammatical);
2. the debt and what it demands;
3. the path's route;
4. the romance;
5. the deadline;
6. the town's two midsummer customs.

Connection to keep: the debt's resolution must be the moment the two vocabularies meet, so plot and romance resolve together.

**Assumption mapping**

| Assumption | Status | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canopy Vigil and Midsummer Debates share midsummer | `VERIFIED` (provisional festivals) | [Seasonal calendar](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#seasonal-calendar). |
| Highridge lends against collateral and keeps registers | `VERIFIED` at pattern level | [Trade and Dependencies](../../Economy/Trade-and-Dependencies.md#core-model): Highridge exports brokerage, credit and records. |
| Paying the debt resolves it | `ASSUMED` → **false** | Sixty years of interest would be absurdly large. The contract's own wording resolves it instead. |
| The lender wants money | `ASSUMED` → **narrowed** | Hollis expects money, but the pledge gives his family an interest in the tree standing. |
| A mule's route follows the roots | `ASSUMED` → **grounded** | Mules avoid uneven, root-broken ground; Pip's climbing knowledge confirms the line. |
| Both leads are adults | `VERIFIED` by design | Pip is a child and has no romantic role. |

**First principles.** The story must make two ways of caring about the tree turn out to be one. The smallest mechanism that does this is a single conditional clause read in both languages. Removed convention: a villainous creditor. Hollis is only mistaken about what he inherited.

**Counterexample search**

| Attack | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Would a real lender accept a conditional that never matures? | **Survives.** Sixty years ago the lender judged the tree would fall first; the joke is that he was wrong. |
| The romance feels forced | **Narrows.** No declaration; the co-signed survey and its one footnote carry it. |
| Deepwood law alone could protect the tree | **Survives as the point.** Both systems protect it; neither side knew the other did. |

**PLAN handoff**

```text
frame             two professions discover they have been protecting the same tree in different languages
selected strategy "for as long as it stands" conditional; mule line = root-zone edge; pledge renewed as guardianship
alternatives      A town pays debt plus interest — set aside: absurd sum, no meeting of vocabularies
                  B Deepwood law overrides the lien — set aside: one side wins, theme lost
assumptions       lien practice and wording are provisional story details
unknowns          who governs Twilighthollow's works permits stays vague
reconsider if     the clause twist needs a long explanation; the romance needs a declaration to land
```
