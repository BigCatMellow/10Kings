# The Eighth Verse

**Development level: L3 Drafted, first pass** (2026-11-03): [read the draft](../Drafts/The-Eighth-Verse.md). ## Premise

On Root Cellar Night a Greenvale village walks its round of cellars and sings one verse of the old Blight song at each door. This year the song-keeper has lost her voice, and the cellar warden, who can't hold a tune, has to lead. A young reformer has walked in with a notice asking every village to add an eighth verse about the buyers. The round ends at the door of the family who sold.

**Setting:** Reedveil Bend, a cooperative village on a river bend in [Greenvale](../../Regions/Greenvale.md). The name comes from the [name bank](../../Places/Border-Towns.md#other-provisional-names).
**Clock:** [Root Cellar Night](../../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#greenvale), Year 1 midwinter, from dusk to the small hours.
**Mode:** winter ensemble, one night.

## Protagonist

**Ulla Quarrie**, the cooperative's cellar warden.

- **Good at:** stores. She knows every cellar in the Bend by its smell, and she can tell you which apple is turning through a shut door.
- **Small flaw:** she can't sing, and she's touchy about it. She also does her job from the doorway: she checks a cellar and goes, and never stays for the cup.
- **Why she resists:** the Song is Vessa's, and has been as long as anyone's walked the round. Ulla's job is the cellars, not the singing.

## Place

A low village of earth-banked cellars, reeds frozen at the edges of the bend, and one meeting house over the big shared cellar dug after the [Seven-year Blight](../../Regions/Greenvale.md#historical-identity).

Recurring locations:

1. **The cooperative cellar**, under the meeting house: barrels, racks, the smell of apples and cold stone.
2. **The meeting house**, where the round gathers and where the notice is read.
3. **The lane along the reeds**, the round's route, iced and lantern-lit.
4. **The seven doors of the round**, each with its own smell and its own verse.
5. **The Kemble cellar**, the oldest in the Bend: a stone door set into the bank, always the seventh and last door.

## Cast

- **Vessa Ruel**, the song-keeper. A winter cough has taken her voice, and she gives orders in a whisper that carries further than most people's shouting.
- **Jessop and Oda Kemble.** In the autumn defaults they sold their fields to a buyer and stayed on as tenants. They have always walked the round. This year they haven't come out.
- **Yorick Fell**, a young copyist for a reform circle in the market town. He's sincere, half-frozen and has walked all day. He copied his notice twenty times by hand, and the last copies went wrong.
- **The round:** the Bend's households, children with lanterns, and a few people who'd like the eighth verse very much.

## Problem

The reform circle's notice asks every village to add an eighth verse this Root Cellar Night, "for the year the cellar rats came", meaning the buyers, and to put its name to the circles' joint statement that the buying-up of distressed farms is coordinated theft. Half the Bend wants to. The last door of the round is the Kembles'. Sung there, the eighth verse would shame neighbors in public, and Ulla is the one leading the singing.

## Complications

1. Vessa's voice is gone, so Ulla leads, and she can't sing.
2. Nobody wants to tell a cold, earnest young man no in front of the round, so the meeting half-agrees by not disagreeing.
3. Ulla's copy of the notice reads "cellar cats". The children love it, and the eighth verse gets sillier and more popular with every door.
4. At the fifth door Vessa whispers what the seventh verse is about: the last year of the Blight, when the whole Bend ate from one cellar, the Kembles'.
5. Word comes down the lane that the Kemble door is shut and the lamp over it isn't lit.

## Running elements

- **The Cellar Song** (provisional custom): seven verses, one for each year of the Blight, one sung at each door. The count of doors is the count of verses, which makes the eighth door the one that isn't there.
- **"Cellar cats"**: the copying error, comic at first and then a way out of the anger.
- **Ulla's nose.** She names what's turning in each cellar, unasked: comic at the first doors, tender at the last.

None of these is a language habit, which the collection's first seven stories all used ([Registry](../Notes/Registry.md#devices-already-used)).

## Emotional core

You can be angry about what happened to the village without making your neighbors pay for it. A song that remembers sharing can't be used to shut a door.

## Solution

Ulla doesn't sing the eighth verse. At the Kembles' shut door she sings the seventh, the verse about this cellar, on her own and badly. On the second line the Bend joins in.

## Climax

The Kemble door. Ulla forgets a line, and Vessa whispers it to her. The round picks up the song, children included, and nobody sings the eighth verse. Jessop opens the door. Oda has pressed cheese for the round all the same, in case anyone came. The story's one sad line belongs to Jessop, said once: they sold the fields, and they didn't know if they'd sold this too.

## Soft landing

Supper in the Kemble cellar: [Herbed Farm Cheese](../../Culture/Recipes/Greenvale.md#8-herbed-farm-cheese), [Roasted Grain Clusters](../../Culture/Recipes/Greenvale.md#7-roasted-grain-clusters) and cider. Yorick eats. Ulla tells him, in one sentence, why the Bend won't sign, and nobody laughs at him. In the morning he takes his notice on to the next village. The final beat is Ulla doing something she has never done on her rounds: going in and staying for the cup.

### Stage 1 — THINK

**Reasoning allocation:** structured single path. The risk is tone, not mechanism: the story walks right up to public shaming, which the [tone guardrails](../Notes/Rules.md#tone-guardrails) forbid, and has to turn away from it convincingly.

**Decomposition.**

1. Why Ulla has to lead.
2. The notice and how the Bend half-agrees to it.
3. The route of the round, and why the Kembles' door is last.
4. What the seventh verse is about.
5. The turn at the door.
6. What happens to Yorick and his statement.

Connection to keep: the seventh verse's history (4) has to arrive before the door (5), or the turn is a speech instead of a memory.

**Assumption mapping.**

| Assumption | Status | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Root Cellar Night is a village round | `ASSUMED` → **narrowed** | The festivals page calls it intimate: stored food, family stories, old scarcity. The round of seven household cellars stays intimate, a door at a time, with no civic ceremony. The round is a provisional custom. |
| The Blight lasted seven years | `VERIFIED` with a hedge | [Greenvale](../../Regions/Greenvale.md#historical-identity): "whether exactly seven years or later mythologized". The song has seven verses either way, and nobody in the story settles it. |
| Reformers would circulate a notice | `VERIFIED` | Posted text and guild notices are ordinary, and printing doesn't exist, so the notice is hand-copied ([Records #28](../../Records-Literacy-and-Authentication.md#28-public-notice-does-not-require-universal-literacy)). |
| The buyers engineered the crisis | `UNKNOWN` | Left open, as in One Square. Only the accusation happens on the page. |
| Who funds the reform circle | `UNKNOWN` | Left open, and never raised. |
| The Kembles still own their cellar | `UNKNOWN` | Deliberately unclear. It's Jessop's fear in the climax, and the story doesn't answer it. |

**First principles.** The story needs a public moment where shaming is possible and refused. It needs a reason the refusal comes from the protagonist and not a speech, and a warm way back for everyone, the reformer included. Removed convention: a villain. The buyers never appear, and Yorick is wrong only about where to aim. The smallest mechanism: one song with a fixed order of doors, so the last door is chosen by history, not by anyone tonight.

**Counterexample search.**

| Attack | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Isn't singing a verse at someone's door still shaming, even the seventh? | **Narrows.** The seventh verse praises a Kemble of long ago; nobody's sure which one, and nobody dates the Blight by it. It's sung *to* them, as it is every year, so the custom itself is the kindness. |
| Would the Bend really follow Ulla's bad singing? | **Survives.** It's the bad singing that does it: nobody wants to leave her alone out there. |
| Does Yorick get humiliated? | **Contradicts the tone; fixed.** He's fed and answered in one sentence, and he leaves with his dignity. The "cats" error is laughed at, and he laughs too, eventually. |
| Is this One Square again (a Greenvale village resisting the buyers)? | **Risk.** One Square solves a scheduling clash and the agent leaves. Here nobody resists the buyers; the village decides how to treat its own. Different engine, register and resolution. |
| Can the story mock reform? | **Contradicts.** The grievance is real and the story says so. Only the eighth verse is refused, not the anger. |

### Stage 3 — DO

Drafted 2026-11-03: [The Eighth Verse](../Drafts/The-Eighth-Verse.md), about 4,500 words, in the spoken register from the first pass ([D14](../Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)). Every plan item and both ledger rows are kept.

- **DO: the verses.** The Cellar Song is given only in fragments, one line or two per door. Seven full verses would turn a song for remembering into a performance, and the lines are the author's to rewrite ([who writes what](../Notes/Craft.md#ai-and-the-author-who-writes-what)). Each verse names a Blight year by what was eaten or done ("we ate the seed", "we boiled the harness"); the round comes in on the shared last line, *and we kept, and we kept, and we kept*. The seventh is the one whose middle the Bend has never sung.
- **DO: the door order.** Seven doors: the co-op cellar, then the Wicks, the Fallons, old Pettigrew, the Yeos, the Elwins and the Kembles. These household names are provisional minor names (registered).
- **DO: Yorick's reason** (a reconsideration trigger). His uncle sold downriver two harvests back. It's one line, said before the Kemble door, so his wanting the verse there reads as anger, not cruelty.
- **DO: Ulla answers Jessop** with a practical line ("That cheese is ready. Are you going to make us stand out here?"), so Jessop's sad line stays the only one and nobody explains it.
- **DO: the last scene.** Oda fills two cups and doesn't offer one. Ulla sits down on the potato bin and picks it up. The doorway habit is never named as changed.
- **After the plan check:** the Blight is never dated. The cellar that fed the Bend was "a Kemble's, some Kemble, Jessop's great-great-somebody".
- **Checker:** clean after one pass. Seven five-word phrases shared with other stories were varied, and a "said anything" tic (five times in the first pass) was cut to one.
