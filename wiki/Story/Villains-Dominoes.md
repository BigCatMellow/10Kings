# Villain's Dominoes

See [Grounded Domino Design Method](../Reference/Grounded-Domino-Design.md) for the active non-canon method extracted from the legacy domino research.

## Concept

The Villain's plan is not one giant conspiracy in which every event is directly controlled.

It is a **domino architecture**.

He identifies people, institutions, and tensions that already possess momentum. He gives them small pushes so their independent decisions create the next condition he needs.

## Six provisional character-dominoes

These names and exact biographies remain provisional pending final naming systems.

### Orin Slatehallow — Stone Hills

Role:
talented young metalworker caught between innovation, guild restriction, and patronage.

Strategic value:
access to production networks and information about strategic metal supply.

Manipulation:
anonymous funding, selective obstruction by rivals, and opportunities that make him dependent on a benefactor.

Important rule:
Orin should not invent fantasy super-steel. His value is organizational and technical: improved quality control, repair methods, production knowledge, or access.

### Maris Bleakshore — North Coast

Role:
respected coastal/clan leader shaped by piracy and loss.

Strategic value:
can influence convoy behavior, harbor cooperation, and perceptions of maritime threats.

Manipulation:
credible but selectively framed intelligence makes defensive action look necessary.

Domino:
North Coast patrol changes are interpreted elsewhere as aggression.

### Rosana Meadowcroft — Low Rivers

Role:
agricultural researcher/seed steward working on crop resilience.

Strategic value:
her work changes expectations about harvest, land value, and dependence on existing seed/storage systems.

Manipulation:
fund research, create distribution bottlenecks, allow rivals to overreact.

Domino:
an agricultural innovation becomes a political fight over control.

### Samir Tareh — High Roads

Role:
caravan negotiator seeking fair and predictable routes.

Strategic value:
his route recommendations can redirect enormous amounts of trade without appearing political.

Manipulation:
provide true information about dangers but omit who created them.

Domino:
"safe" trade concentration makes one route strategically vulnerable and impoverishes another.

### Naruin Mossglade — Longwood

Role:
forest warden/sentinel defending local rights.

Strategic value:
can mobilize resistance to roads, logging, or outside extraction.

Manipulation:
leak real Council-backed expansion plans without context; encourage preemptive defense.

Domino:
a local conservation dispute becomes an inter-regional sovereignty crisis.

### Bahriyya Nazar — Old Cities

Role:
reformist diplomat trying to coordinate city-states around water/trade.

Strategic value:
successful cooperation could create a powerful bloc.

Manipulation:
secretly help her coordination, then ensure neighbors interpret it as expansionist consolidation.

Domino:
a peace/reform coalition becomes the apparent military/economic threat that justifies counter-alliances.

## Why these characters work

None needs to be evil.

Each does something consistent with personal values.

Their stories allow the audience to see the Villain's intelligence while preserving everyone else's agency.

## Wurdren's effect

Wurdren does not "knock over the dominoes backward."

He changes relationships:
- proves an accusation false;
- rescues people who become witnesses;
- brings two groups into direct contact;
- returns stolen evidence;
- helps a character survive long enough to reconsider;
- makes cooperation possible where the plan required isolation.

Those human links are difficult for the Villain to model.
