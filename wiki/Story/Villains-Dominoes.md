# Villain's Dominoes

## Status

**Active story architecture.**

This page owns the Villain's current domino plan.

The older six-character / one-per-region domino model is superseded.

[Grounded Domino Design Method](../Reference/Grounded-Domino-Design.md) remains the method reference.

[Current Events](Current-Events.md) owns what is actually happening in the world now.

---

# 1. Core concept — braided domino architecture

The plan is not:

> six regions → six agents → six crises → one war.

It is a **braided system attack**.

Several real pressures move independently.

The Villain identifies where they can be coupled so that:

1. one institution's rational response becomes another institution's crisis;
2. the Council is forced to coordinate across domains;
3. that coordination produces visible winners and losers;
4. true evidence of coordination becomes politically explosive;
5. alternative networks begin performing functions the Council claimed were indispensable.

The target is not chaos for its own sake.

The target is:

> **the legitimacy of hidden coordination.**

---

# 2. The strategic equation

The Villain needs all four conditions.

~~~
REAL STRESS
+ COUPLING PUSH
+ COUNCIL RESPONSE
+ SELECTIVE EXPOSURE
= LEGITIMACY CRISIS
~~~

If there is no real stress, the plot becomes conspiracy theater.

If there is no Villain push, he is only an observer.

If there is no Council response, he cannot expose the political meaning of hidden coordination.

If there is no exposure, the crises remain local.

---

# 3. Phase 0 — build capacity before breaking anything

Before the major domino chain, the Villain's movement needs mundane capacity.

## Information

- merchants;
- dissatisfied clerks;
- guild contacts;
- local political allies;
- smugglers;
- travelers;
- copied contracts;
- Council-linked public records.

## Movement

- secondary roads;
- minor ports / landings;
- Underpass paths;
- small carriers;
- trusted inns / warehouses;
- direct local agreements.

## Finance / relief

- strike support;
- emergency loans;
- purchases from distressed producers;
- legal defense;
- food / medicine movement.

## Political legitimacy

The network must solve some real problems before asking people to accept its political interpretation.

**Design rule:** some Phase 0 work should be genuinely beneficial even if the larger strategy never existed.

---

# 4. Phase 1 — let the world provide the first shocks

The Villain does **not** create:

- the fish-stock shift;
- the Low Rivers bumper harvest;
- the Longwood ecological disruption;
- the Old Cities drought anxiety;
- the Spine land movement;
- the existence of SH-M labor grievances.

These are gifts of circumstance.

His intelligence lies in seeing that they can be connected.

---

# 5. Braid A — maritime / finance / food / labor

## Domino A1 — make maritime risk politically expensive

Starting condition:

- real piracy;
- fishery conflict;
- NC-M pressure to coordinate defense.

Villain push:

- selected threat information;
- perhaps one redirected / financed attack later;
- encourage confidence in the claim that wider convoy restrictions are necessary.

Expected rational response:

- NC-M expands convoy / patrol coordination;
- harbor interests resist delay / cost;
- NC-I disputes obligations or levy terms.

Strategic result:

- maritime security becomes a constitutional issue rather than just a policing issue.

---

## Domino A2 — let Port transmit the shock

Starting condition:

- losses / delay;
- convoy uncertainty.

No direct Villain command is required.

Port actors respond:

- risk terms rise;
- credit shortens;
- marginal voyages are delayed;
- warehouses / merchants become cautious.

Council-linked Finance / Routes interests may selectively stabilize important clients.

Strategic result:

> a North Coast security problem becomes a Port credit problem.

---

## Domino A3 — collide Port tightening with Low Rivers abundance

Starting condition:

- oversupply;
- full storage;
- producer debt.

Port / trade shock reduces or delays some distant purchases.

Expected response:

- prices fall further;
- merchants refuse marginal cargo;
- defaults rise;
- land / debt consolidators appear.

Villain push:

- support a distressed-buyer alternative in selected places;
- release evidence linking some consolidators / lenders to Council-associated houses;
- amplify true cases where emergency support favors systemically important clients.

Strategic result:

> a successful harvest becomes evidence of political dispossession.

---

## Domino A4 — transmit the crisis into SH-M

Starting condition:

- SH-M labor grievances;
- ordinary orders weaken;
- strategic metal demand remains opaque.

Villain push:

- time strike support / evidence release;
- redirect a small volume of high-quality metal through suspicious private contracts;
- expose real Council-linked purchasing where useful.

Expected response:

- SH-A investigates;
- mine owners seek order;
- guild factions split;
- export / inspection pressure rises.

Strategic result:

> workers can reasonably believe "ordinary people are being cut back while hidden buyers still receive strategic production."

The labor dispute is now connected to the same hidden coordination story as Low Rivers.

---

# 6. Braid B — route confidence / infrastructure / Longwood rights

## Domino B1 — break confidence, not every road

Starting condition:

- real banditry / disappearances;
- fragmented High Roads governance.

Villain push:

- a few selected incidents;
- selective accurate warnings;
- confidence manipulation around one or more routes.

Expected response:

- caravans reroute;
- corridor authorities argue;
- HR-C-like arbitration functions face more disputes;
- bypassed places lose income.

Strategic result:

> security uncertainty changes the economic map without conquering territory.

---

## Domino B2 — force a redundancy project

Starting condition:

- concentrated traffic;
- damaged routes;
- Spine / Underpass disruption.

Expected response:

- polities;
- corridor compacts;
- merchants;
- Council-linked Routes / Infrastructure interests

seek an alternate connection.

The exact project remains map-dependent.

It may involve:

- repair;
- widening;
- a forest-edge connector;
- a secondary river-road link;
- an Underpass stabilization.

Strategic result:

> a route problem becomes an infrastructure / land-rights problem.

---

## Domino B3 — make Longwood the cost bearer

Starting condition:

- layered use rights;
- external demand for timber / passage;
- ecological stress.

A legally plausible project crosses rights no single authority completely controls.

Villain push:

- leak the broad concession;
- omit or delay limiting terms;
- help affected communities coordinate before outside parties have reached a final settlement.

Expected response:

- LW-W / LW-I / LW-S or local communities invoke different rights;
- specialists / wardens issue warnings;
- outside actors demand reliability;
- route security may become militarized.

Strategic result:

> "necessary resilience" becomes evidence that distant interests decide which local rights are expendable.

---

# 7. Braid C — water cooperation / bloc anxiety

## Domino C1 — support the thing rivals already fear

Starting condition:

- drought anxiety;
- OC-U / OC-D shared water dependency.

Expected response:

- technical coordination;
- repairs;
- gate / warning agreements;
- emergency planning.

Villain action:

- no sabotage required;
- perhaps quietly help communication succeed.

Why?

Because successful cooperation can itself become threatening.

Strategic result:

> a useful functional institution becomes politically more visible.

---

## Domino C2 — expose the hidden sponsors

Council-linked interests prefer stability.

They may:

- finance repair;
- guarantee contracts;
- quietly pressure compromise;
- coordinate shipping / purchases around expected water conditions.

Villain push:

- publish selected records showing repeated Council-linked involvement.

Expected response:

- OC-C / rivals seek guarantees;
- defensive trade / security arrangements expand;
- proponents of cooperation deny bloc ambitions.

Strategic result:

> everyone begins taking precautions against a bloc that may not originally have existed.

---

## Domino C3 — reaction makes the accusation partially true

Once rivals:

- form counter-arrangements;
- change patrols;
- secure suppliers;
- coordinate diplomatically;

OC-U / OC-D have new reasons to cooperate more broadly.

Strategic result:

> interpretation creates structure.

The Villain's best domino is often a self-fulfilling political expectation.

---

# 8. Hidden braid — alternative logistics

The Spine / Underpass disruption gives the Villain a chance to expand non-Council circulation.

He does not need to own the Underpass.

He needs relationships with people who can move:

- small high-value cargo;
- documents;
- people;
- emergency supplies;
- information

through routes larger institutions treat as marginal.

At the same time his movement can support surface alternatives:

- guild mutual aid;
- faith relief;
- direct producer-buyer contracts;
- local credit;
- independent warehouses;
- secondary merchants.

This braid is not a crisis chain.

It is the **replacement-capacity chain**.

Without it, the Villain can only destroy.

With it, he can claim to offer another order.

---

# 9. Convergence point — the Council must choose

The braids converge when several things happen at once:

- shipping risk;
- credit stress;
- Low Rivers default;
- strategic-metal uncertainty;
- route rerouting;
- contested infrastructure;
- Old Cities diplomatic suspicion.

The Council sees the systemic interaction earlier than most public institutions.

That is its advantage.

But it cannot stabilize everything invisibly.

It must choose among actions such as:

- guarantee one merchant network;
- prioritize one route;
- release one stock;
- delay one project;
- finance one repair;
- pressure one polity;
- redirect one material stream.

Every choice produces local losers.

The Villain is waiting for those choices.

---

# 10. The exposure domino

This is the first truly world-scale domino.

The Villain assembles a package of **mostly genuine evidence** showing that several apparently independent interventions share:

- the same houses;
- related agents;
- coordinated timing;
- common information;
- private correspondence or contracts.

The evidence does not need to prove:

> the Council caused every crisis.

It only needs to prove:

> **the same private networks secretly decided how several crises would be managed.**

That claim is already politically explosive.

## Why mostly true evidence is stronger

A pure forgery can be disproved.

Selective truth creates a harder argument:

- the Council can explain the context;
- the public can still reasonably object to the authority.

The Villain wins even when the Council's factual rebuttal is accurate.

# 11. Phase 3 — defensive politics creates the next dominoes

After exposure, institutions do not simply "join the rebellion."

They protect themselves.

Possible responses:

## SH-A

- investigations;
- contract restrictions;
- strategic export controls;
- pressure on Council-linked creditors.

## NC-M / NC-I

- demand disclosure around convoy finance / shipping influence;
- constitutional dispute over who committed whom to what.

## LR-A / LR-M

- debt / land investigations;
- emergency purchasing rules;
- restrictions on outside acquisition or warehousing.

## High Roads actors

- dispute whether route recommendations were manipulated;
- revisit corridor contracts / arbitration neutrality.

## Longwood actors

- suspend or reopen disputed concessions.

## Old Cities

- demand disclosure of hidden financing;
- rivals reinterpret water cooperation again.

## Port

- faces pressure to investigate houses that are legally ordinary merchant actors but politically implicated in hidden coordination.

These responses are individually rational.

Collectively they damage the mechanisms the Council normally uses to stabilize the next crisis.

That is the second world-scale domino.

---

# 12. Phase 4 — coordination crisis

Now the Council's tools are politically contaminated.

A Council-backed loan may be refused because:

> accepting it looks like submission.

A useful route recommendation may be ignored because:

> the source is suspected.

A necessary warehouse release may be treated as manipulation.

The Council faces the exact trap the Villain wanted:

## Stay hidden / restrained

Risk:

- real instability worsens;
- alternative networks gain legitimacy.

## Act openly

Risk:

- confirm that an unelected private government exists in practice;
- provoke political retaliation;
- expose member houses.

This is the operational climax of the domino plan.

---

# 13. Phase 5 — the Villain's proposed alternative

The Villain does **not** need to walk into a room and declare himself ruler.

His network has already become useful.

By this stage it may include or connect:

- producers;
- small merchants;
- guild factions;
- local political movements;
- relief networks;
- smugglers;
- secondary routes;
- reformers;
- communities harmed by Council choices.

He can demand:

> a new settlement.

The exact demand remains open until his constituency / grievance is finalized.

But his leverage is concrete:

- people can move some goods without the Council;
- evidence of secret coordination is public;
- existing institutions are afraid of dependence;
- the old stabilization machinery is politically constrained.

---

# 14. The plan's failure modes

The Villain is not omniscient.

His plan can fail because:

- piracy falls naturally;
- harvest buyers recover;
- a corridor reopens;
- local authorities reach a fair Longwood agreement;
- OC-U / OC-D communicate openly enough to prevent bloc panic;
- a Council member refuses a damaging intervention;
- alternative networks choose independence from him;
- evidence is released before he is ready;
- ordinary people compare notes directly.

The architecture should survive some failures.

It should not require every prediction to be correct.

---

# 15. Wurdren's interference points

Wurdren is strongest when he changes the **information / trust topology**, not when he defeats one named domino-person.

Examples:

## Witness survives

A survivor of an attack can show that the public explanation is incomplete.

## Direct contact

People from two jurisdictions meet before intermediaries can frame each other as enemies.

## Original record recovered

A contract, water record, cargo manifest, or route notice reveals omitted context.

## Local solution succeeds

Two communities solve a practical problem without Council coercion or Villain direction.

## Alternative network refuses capture

A guild, faith, merchant, or community accepts useful aid but rejects becoming part of the Villain's political machine.

## Human obligation overrides prediction

Someone accepts a real cost to protect people outside their normal constituency.

These events are hard for a model built on leverage to predict.

---

# 16. Superseded six-character model

The old page assigned one provisional named "domino character" to each major cultural region.

That structure is superseded because it:

- reproduced six-region symmetry;
- treated cultural regions as political units;
- made the plan depend too heavily on specially chosen people;
- used provisional names from an outdated naming approach.

Characters can still emerge from the decision points above.

But they should emerge because a real institution needs a human face.

Do **not** rebuild the cast as six replacements.

---

# 17. Design rule for a new human domino

Add a recurring character only when the architecture produces a decision that cannot remain abstract.

Ask:

1. Which institution are they actually part of?
2. What do they want before the Villain notices them?
3. What information do they possess?
4. Why would their action be reasonable without the larger plot?
5. What choice can they make that changes the chain?
6. Can they refuse the Villain?
7. Can Wurdren affect them through an ordinary relationship rather than plot destiny?

If the character exists mainly to "represent a region," do not add them.
