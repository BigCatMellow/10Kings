# Current Events

## Purpose

This is a snapshot of the world shortly before or during the opening movement of the story.

Many events are linked, but most participants do not know that.

Some are caused by the Villain.

Some are Council responses.

Some are ordinary events opportunistically used by both.

That distinction is essential.

## Stone Hills

### Labor unrest

Mine and metalworking workers are organizing around wages, safety, debt, and control of guild leadership.

Possible hidden layers:
- some grievances are entirely real;
- Council-linked owners are trying to prevent a production shock;
- Villain agents encourage timing that makes the strike strategically damaging.

### Unusual metal movements

High-quality metal is disappearing into private contracts or being redirected.

Different observers blame:
- smuggling;
- war preparation;
- corrupt guild masters;
- foreign buyers.

## North Coast

### Fish stocks and access

Some fisheries are declining or shifting.

This creates:
- price increases;
- clan disputes;
- illegal fishing;
- pressure for new agreements.

### Piracy and convoy politics

Raids are increasing.

A response that looks purely defensive to North Coast can look like maritime militarization to Old Cities or Port merchants.

## Low Rivers

### Abundance crisis

A new strain, unusually good weather, or both have produced unexpectedly high yields in some districts.

Instead of simple prosperity:
- prices fall;
- storage fills;
- poorer farmers cannot cover debts;
- rumors spread that grain is unsafe;
- merchants delay purchases.

### Land and seed politics

Large interests attempt to acquire distressed farms.

Reformers accuse them of engineering the crisis.

## High Roads

### Caravan attacks

Attacks and disappearances make some routes seem unreliable.

Trade is redirected.

Towns bypassed by new routes suffer immediately.

### Political debate

High Roads intellectual disputes are increasingly tied to real patrons and commercial interests.

## Longwood

### Logging and road conflict

New extraction or infrastructure proposals cross disputed local boundaries.

Some work is legal under old treaties; locals dispute those interpretations.

### Fungal/ecological disruption

A disease or ecological change affects important forest foods/commodities.

This may be natural, accidentally human-caused, or exploited by manipulators.

## Old Cities

### Drought anxiety

Water is not yet universally scarce, but fear changes behavior:
- hoarding;
- canal guards;
- secret agreements;
- speculative prices.

### City-state coordination

Some leaders are trying to cooperate.

Rivals interpret the effort as the beginning of a dominant bloc.

## Port

### Smuggling surge

Higher prices and route disruption increase demand for illegal transport.

### Merchant conflict

Shipping losses, insurance disputes, warehouse fires/thefts, and political accusations are making neutral commerce harder.

### Refugee/worker pressure

Displaced people from border and economic crises arrive faster than housing and charity systems can absorb them.

## The Spine

### Earthquake/land movement

A recent event damages roads and exposes older ruins, caves, or sealed routes.

This produces:
- treasure/resource rushes;
- religious claims;
- territorial disputes;
- archaeological interest;
- Tunnels instability.

## The Underpass

### Route wars

Collapse and discovery have changed which enclaves control profitable corridors.

Smuggling and legitimate trade compete for the same space.

## Religious networks

See [Religions](../Politics/Religions.md).

Faiths are beginning to interpret the crises trans-regionally:
- Illuminated scholars compare records;
- Weaver communities document ecological damage;
- Compass pilgrims carry rumors;
- Harmonious mediators build alternative dispute systems;
- Dual Flame counselors notice patterns of radicalization.

## Example ripple chain

One possible chain:

1. North Coast piracy raises shipping risk.
2. Port insurance/credit tightens.
3. Low Rivers merchants cancel distant grain purchases.
4. Low Rivers grain prices collapse locally.
5. farmers default.
6. Council finance tries to stabilize lenders.
7. land consolidators buy distressed farms.
8. religious and guild reformers call it coordinated theft.
9. protests affect tool orders from Stone Hills.
10. Stone Hills workshops reduce hours.
11. strike leaders blame owners.
12. Villain agents amplify evidence of Council involvement.
13. a local economic shock becomes proof, in several regions, that another group is acting against them.

This is the kind of causal chain the story should prefer.
