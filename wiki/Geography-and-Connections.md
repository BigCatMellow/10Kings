# Geography and Connections

## Status

**Working canon at the topology level.** Relative regional placement, the central role of The Spine, and the main contact network are now defined well enough to support politics, trade, history, and story work. Exact coastlines, rivers, travel times, Port's precise site, and some narrow border/corridor questions remain open.

## Macro-geography

The known setting is organized around:

- a **Western Continent**;
- a **Northern Continent**;
- an **Eastern Continent**;
- the central mountain system called **The Spine**;
- subterranean routes called **The Underpass**;
- the neutral commercial city **Port**.

The Spine is both barrier and connector. Routes funnel toward passes, valleys, tunnel mouths, navigable rivers, coastal approaches, and the central waters between the major landmasses.

## Source reconciliation

The preserved [continent map](../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/Map_-_The_Continent.png) is useful physical evidence, but it predates the fully developed six-region layout.

A later six-region synthesis preserved in the source corpus gives a clearer relative placement:

- **Ironcrest** — primarily Western Continent, facing The Spine;
- **Northwind** — primarily Northern Continent;
- **Highridge** — central, at or near The Spine, bridging the western, northern, and eastern route systems;
- **Greenvale** — Eastern Continent heartland;
- **Deepwood** — eastern forest belt;
- **Sunplains** — southern/southeastern Eastern Continent;
- **Port** — neutral maritime hub on a strategically useful bay or strait linking northern, southern/eastern, and western shipping.

That later topology is more useful than the old one-region-per-continent assumptions.

The legacy image still supports:

- three large landmasses arranged around a central mountain-and-water zone;
- The Spine occupying the central connective/dividing geography;
- narrow channels, bays, inland/coastal waters, and islands that make water travel structurally important;
- multiple approaches to the central zone;
- a strong reason for passes, tunnels, harbors, and channel access to matter politically.

The map should therefore be **redrawn to fit the current six-region topology**, not used as a literal political map.

## Working regional layout

### Northwind — north

Northwind occupies the Northern Continent and northern maritime system.

Its key physical relationships are:

- cold outer coasts and islands;
- southern access toward Highridge and The Spine;
- sea routes toward Port;
- alternate coastwise routes that do not require Port.

Its main land connection to the rest of the world is through the **Northwind ↔ Highridge** transition and associated passes.

### Ironcrest — west

Ironcrest occupies most of the Western Continent's upland/mineral country.

Its eastern side rises toward The Spine and Highridge.

Its important contacts are:

- **Highridge** through mountain/plateau approaches;
- **Greenvale** through at least a narrow foothill/agricultural transition preserved by current border-town canon;
- **Deepwood** through a narrow forest-mineral frontier preserved by current Darkroot Gulch material.

The last two contacts create a cartographic constraint: the redraw must allow western/eastern transition corridors near the central/southern Spine rather than leaving Ironcrest isolated behind open water.

### Highridge — central bridge

Highridge occupies the central plateau/pass system at or near The Spine.

It should not be pictured as one neat block sitting wholly on one side of the mountains. Its importance comes from controlling or serving several **approach basins, passes, saddles, valleys, and caravan corridors**.

Highridge connects most directly to:

- Northwind;
- Ironcrest;
- Greenvale;
- Deepwood.

A direct Highridge ↔ Sunplains border remains optional; a trade corridor is enough.

Highridge is therefore the main **overland interchange** of the known world.

### Greenvale — eastern heartland

Greenvale occupies broad fertile country on the Eastern Continent.

Its important contacts are:

- Highridge to the west/northwest through plateau descent and caravan roads;
- Deepwood along a broad field-to-forest transition;
- Sunplains along a warmer agricultural/orchard transition;
- Ironcrest through a narrower western/southwestern foothill corridor preserved by current canon.

Greenvale is the most important inland agricultural connector between several otherwise different environments.

### Deepwood — eastern/central forest belt

Deepwood occupies a large forest zone on the Eastern Continent, especially where the eastern lowlands meet foothills, wetter uplands, and older forest.

Its important contacts are:

- Greenvale;
- Sunplains;
- Highridge;
- a narrower Ironcrest-linked forest/mineral frontier required by current Darkroot Gulch material.

The later source corpus did not require a direct Ironcrest border, so this should be a **narrow arm, corridor, or near-direct frontier**, not a huge shared boundary unless later map work earns it.

### Sunplains — south/southeast

Sunplains occupies the warmer southern and southeastern portion of the Eastern Continent.

Its important contacts are:

- Greenvale;
- Deepwood;
- the southern/eastern coast and maritime network;
- Highridge by caravan corridor, with a direct land border optional.

Its coast provides a major southern maritime alternative to purely overland trade.

## Working topology

The map should preserve this relationship structure:

```text
                 NORTHWIND
                     |
                 HIGHRIDGE
                /    |     \
         IRONCREST   |    DEEPWOOD
             \       |      /  \
              \   GREENVALE --- SUNPLAINS
               \_____/
```

This diagram is **topological, not geometric**. It says which relationships the map must make plausible; it does not prescribe shape, distance, scale, or political border length.

### Required or strongly supported contacts

- Northwind ↔ Highridge
- Ironcrest ↔ Highridge
- Highridge ↔ Greenvale
- Highridge ↔ Deepwood
- Greenvale ↔ Deepwood
- Greenvale ↔ Sunplains
- Deepwood ↔ Sunplains

### Current-canon contacts that constrain the redraw

- Ironcrest ↔ Greenvale
- Ironcrest ↔ Deepwood

These need not be long borders. Narrow foothill, forest, valley, or central-Spine corridors are enough.

### Provisional

- Highridge ↔ Sunplains direct land border

A reliable caravan route may connect them without a substantial shared frontier.

## Port

See [Port](Places/Port.md).

Port should be placed on a **central bay, strait, estuary, island-edge, or peninsula system** where several maritime routes naturally converge and where an inland connection to Highridge/The Spine is plausible.

It should have direct sea access toward:

- Northwind;
- Sunplains and the eastern/southern coast;
- western coastal ports.

Port does **not** need to sit on every route.

Its importance comes from being the easiest place to combine:

- shipping;
- repair;
- warehousing;
- finance;
- arbitration;
- information;
- onward land transport.

Other routes must remain possible so Port has leverage rather than magical monopoly.

## The Spine

See [The Spine](Places/The-Spine.md).

The Spine is the land-side counterpart to the central waters.

It should contain:

- high barriers;
- major passes;
- lesser local passes;
- inhabited valleys;
- resource zones;
- seasonal closures;
- pilgrimage paths;
- old forts and toll points;
- multiple Underpass entrances.

Its importance comes from concentrating movement, not from being uniformly impassable.

## The Underpass

See [The Underpass](Places/The-Underpass.md).

The Underpass crosses beneath parts of The Spine and creates functional adjacency among western, northern, and eastern approach zones.

It is a network, not one tunnel.

Its branches provide costly alternatives when:

- surface passes close;
- rulers restrict roads;
- armies mobilize;
- tolls rise;
- smugglers need to avoid checkpoints.

## Route skeleton

### Maritime network

The core sea system should include:

- Northwind ↔ Port;
- Sunplains/eastern coast ↔ Port;
- western ports ↔ Port;
- coastwise routes capable of bypassing Port at greater time, risk, or transaction cost.

### Overland network

The core land system should include:

- Ironcrest ↔ Highridge;
- Northwind ↔ Highridge;
- Highridge ↔ Greenvale;
- Highridge ↔ Deepwood;
- Greenvale ↔ Sunplains;
- Greenvale ↔ Deepwood;
- local/narrow Ironcrest ↔ Greenvale and Ironcrest ↔ Deepwood corridors.

### Underpass network

At minimum, important branches should connect:

- western approaches;
- northern/Highridge approaches;
- eastern approaches.

Exact entrances remain to be placed.

## Route stress test

The working topology supports multiple plausible long-distance paths instead of one universal route.

### Greenvale grain → Northwind

**Preferred:** Greenvale → Port-facing road/river system → Port → sea north.

**Alternative:** Greenvale → Highridge → Northwind overland.

**Pressure points:** Port handling/finance, Highridge passes, Northwind seasonal conditions.

### Ironcrest metal → Sunplains

**Preferred:** Ironcrest → Highridge → eastern/southern caravan system → Sunplains.

**Alternative:** Ironcrest western harbor → central/coastwise sea network → Port or direct southern shipping → Sunplains.

**Pressure points:** Spine approaches, Highridge tolls, central waters, shipping risk.

### Northwind fish/salt → Ironcrest

**Preferred:** Northwind sea route → western ports and/or Port-linked shipping → Ironcrest.

**Alternative:** Northwind → Highridge → Ironcrest overland.

**Emergency bypass:** selected Underpass branches where surface routes fail.

This is the desired pattern: **a preferred route, a costly alternative, and different chokepoints**.

## Border towns

See [Border Towns](Places/Border-Towns.md).

Established examples should guide the redraw:

- Stonefield Forge — Ironcrest / Greenvale
- Harveston Vale — Greenvale / Sunplains
- Twilighthollow — Deepwood / Highridge
- Icestep Summit — Northwind / Highridge
- Darkroot Gulch — Deepwood / Ironcrest

These towns are not decoration. Their existence constrains the physical map.

## What remains to draw

The next cartographic pass should decide:

1. exact coastline shapes and scale;
2. exact Port site and neutral territory;
3. the land/corridor geometry that permits Ironcrest ↔ Greenvale and Ironcrest ↔ Deepwood;
4. major rivers and watersheds;
5. named major Spine passes;
6. named major Underpass entrances;
7. primary maritime lanes;
8. approximate travel times and seasonal closures.

The **network topology is now usable before those details are fixed**.
