# Geography and Connections

## Status

**Working canon.** The broad relationships are established, but an exact map has not yet been locked. Earlier brainstorming sometimes treated possible adjacencies as settled facts. This page separates strong connections from provisional ones.

## Macro-geography

The known setting is organized around:

- a **Western Continent**;
- a **Northern Continent**;
- an **Eastern Continent**;
- the central mountain system called **The Spine**;
- subterranean routes called **The Underpass**;
- the neutral commercial city **Port**.

The Spine is both barrier and connector. Routes funnel toward passes, valleys, tunnel mouths, navigable rivers, and coastal approaches.

## Strongly established regional relationships

### Ironcrest ↔ Greenvale

Repeatedly used in border-town discussions. Upland/mineral country grades into productive foothill and lowland agriculture. Their economic relationship is old: metal, tools, timber, food, animals, fuel, labor, and investment move both ways.

Representative working border settlement: **Stonefield Forge**.

### Greenvale ↔ Sunplains

Repeatedly used. Temperate mixed farmland transitions toward warmer, drier agriculture with orchards, vineyards, irrigation systems, and southern trade.

Representative working border settlement: **Harveston Vale**.

### Deepwood ↔ Highridge

Repeatedly used. Forested uplands thin toward exposed plateau routes. This boundary is important for timber, herbs, caravan access, water, philosophy/religion, and arguments over extraction.

Representative working border settlement: **Twilighthollow**.

### Deepwood ↔ Ironcrest

Used in discussions of logging, charcoal, ore, tools, forest-edge conflict, and the town **Darkroot Gulch**. This implies at least one direct or near-direct frontier, probably where Ironcrest's eastern or southeastern uplands meet a western arm of the forest.

### Northwind ↔ Highridge

Used repeatedly through **Icestep Summit** and trade-pass discussions. The cold northern interior/coast transitions toward elevated routes leading into Highridge.

## Connections that may be corridors rather than long borders

### Ironcrest ↔ Highridge

Logically strong and repeatedly implied by ore caravans, mountain routes, and the central position of The Spine. Likely a meaningful direct border or a very short mountain corridor.

### Greenvale ↔ Deepwood

Strong ecological logic and repeatedly discussed as field-to-forest transition, including historical encroachment. Treat as a direct border unless the final map contradicts it.

### Deepwood ↔ Sunplains

Used in forest-edge/orchard expansion conflicts. Likely a southern forest transition into drier woodland and cultivated Sunplains.

### Greenvale ↔ Highridge

Likely. Highridge trade must reach the eastern agricultural interior somewhere. The exact length of border is not settled.

### Highridge ↔ Sunplains

Possible through southern caravan routes. Could be a direct boundary, a narrow corridor, or mediated through Greenvale/Deepwood. Do not assume until the map is locked.

## Port

Port is not simply "between" two regions. Its importance comes from **network position**.

Its exact site should satisfy several conditions:

- sheltered deep-water harbor or navigable estuary;
- access to major maritime lanes;
- reasonable overland or river connection to interior routes;
- difficult for one kingdom to seize without provoking all others;
- enough surrounding food/water to sustain a large city;
- historical reason for neutral status.

Northwind and Sunplains are major maritime partners, but Port belongs to neither.

## The Underpass

The Underpass crosses beneath parts of The Spine and has multiple branches. It should not function like a single straight tunnel.

Some sections are:
- public and maintained;
- locally controlled;
- seasonal;
- flooded;
- collapsed;
- religiously restricted;
- mined;
- inhabited;
- criminally controlled;
- known only to specialist guides.

The Underpass can create "functional adjacency": two places not close on the surface may trade intensely because a reliable tunnel branch connects them.

## Travel logic

A useful rule: **routes matter more than straight-line distance**.

Two neighboring valleys can be culturally distant if a ridge blocks travel. Two far ports can be closely connected if seasonal winds make voyages easy.

When the map is finalized, it should show:
- navigable rivers;
- major passes;
- Underpass entrances;
- caravan roads;
- seasonal closures;
- coastwise sailing routes;
- old pre-Convergence roads;
- pilgrimage paths;
- smuggling corridors.
