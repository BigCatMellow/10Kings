# Magic Baseline

## Status

**Working world constraint. Specific magical phenomena remain unresolved unless explicitly promoted elsewhere.**

This page prevents old fantasy brainstorming from silently becoming infrastructure in the current world.

The setting leaves room for real magic.

It does **not** currently require any specific magical phenomenon for geography, politics, trade, religion, warfare, medicine, information, or daily life to function.

---

# 1. Current baseline

The authoritative rule remains [World Rule 7](World-Rules.md):

> **Magic is scarce, uncertain, limited, or costly.**

Combined with the rest of the current world:

- magic is not a universal convenience;
- ordinary institutions do not depend on routine magic;
- material limits remain real;
- magic cannot erase logistics;
- magical equivalents of modern infrastructure should not be assumed;
- any specific magical ability must be established before another page relies on it.

~~~text
MATERIAL WORLD FIRST
+
POSSIBLE RARE MAGIC
+
NO UNSTATED MAGICAL INFRASTRUCTURE
~~~

# 2. What the current world actually depends on

Travel depends on:

- roads;
- rivers;
- ships;
- animals;
- weather;
- passes;
- The Underpass;
- human labor.

No teleportation or magical transport is required.

## Information

See [Movement and Information](Movement-and-Information.md).

Information depends on:

- merchants;
- sailors;
- pilgrims;
- caravans;
- guilds;
- messengers;
- itinerant people;
- records;
- spies;
- physical travel.

No magical broadcast, telepathy, scrying, or instant communication is required.

## Food and agriculture

Food depends on:

- ecology;
- seed;
- water;
- labor;
- preservation;
- storage;
- trade;
- institutions.

No magical crop system is required.

## Medicine

Current worldbuilding assumes injury and disease remain real constraints.

No magical healing system is currently established.

## Warfare

Current [Weapons and Elite Troops](Culture/Weapons-and-Elite-Troops.md) emphasizes:

- training;
- discipline;
- logistics;
- equipment;
- terrain;
- communication.

It explicitly avoids supernatural competence as the explanation for elite troops.

## Politics

Political influence works through:

- legitimacy;
- law;
- force;
- money;
- contracts;
- routes;
- information;
- institutions.

The Council is explicitly not a supernatural conspiracy.

## Religion

See [Religions](Politics/Religions.md).

Religions currently function through:

- belief;
- ritual;
- moral interpretation;
- archives;
- charity;
- mediation;
- pilgrimage;
- community.

A religious claim is not automatically proof of a supernatural event.

# 3. Current owners actively resist unnecessary supernatural explanations

Examples:

- [The Underpass](Places/The-Underpass.md) says strange animal activity may indicate ecological disruption rather than supernatural monsters.
- [Longwood](Regions/Deepwood.md) warns against implying supernatural wisdom.
- [Weapons and Elite Troops](Culture/Weapons-and-Elite-Troops.md) rejects supernatural competence as the explanation for elite forces.
- [Audience Movements](Culture/Audience-Movements.md) keeps magical broadcasting **UNKNOWN** and provides mundane alternatives.
- [World Rules](World-Rules.md) prohibits magic from erasing logistics or becoming cheap technological replacement.

This is a consistent design direction.

# 4. Legacy magic contains several different ideas

Preserved source material contains many magical concepts.

They should not be treated as one coherent canon system.

## A. Subtle practical magic

The legacy world-rules source proposes:

- low-key practical magic;
- modest trade or survival advantages;
- artifacts with benefits and drawbacks;
- creation through rare materials, ritual, sacrifice, or specialized knowledge.

This is the legacy magic direction most compatible with current World Rules.

It still does not establish any specific artifact or ability.

## B. Mythic and religious supernatural claims

Legacy lore includes:

- the Spine containing the Mother's essence;
- star-blessed energies;
- sacred valleys;
- prophetic experiences;
- relics;
- legendary champions;
- cosmic blessings.

These can currently function as:

- religion;
- myth;
- contested history;
- folklore;
- political symbolism.

Their supernatural truth remains unresolved.

## C. Operational fantasy systems

Other legacy notes propose:

- illusions;
- scrying;
- enchanted messengers;
- magical communication;
- arcane laboratories;
- magical research;
- powerful artifacts;
- supernatural creatures;
- magically altered warfare.

These have much larger systemic consequences.

They are **not current canon**.

# 5. No confirmed magical communication

Current information geography depends on physical carriers.

Not established:

- telepathy;
- long-distance scrying;
- enchanted message systems;
- magical broadcast;
- instant artifact communication.

Any future magical communication must explain:

1. range;
2. reliability;
3. cost;
4. rarity;
5. access;
6. interception;
7. why existing messenger and merchant networks still matter.

Until then, use physical information networks.

---

# 6. No confirmed magical transport

Not established:

- teleportation;
- portals;
- reliable magical flight;
- instant cargo movement;
- magical roads that ignore geography.

This matters because the current world depends on:

- chokepoints;
- Port;
- High Roads;
- The Spine;
- The Underpass;
- shipping;
- weather;
- route security.

Any magic strong enough to bypass these systems is a world-system redesign.

Default:

> **Travel remains physical.**

---

# 7. No confirmed magical healing

Current disease, injury, dangerous labor, childbirth, and medicine remain materially consequential.

If magical healing is later introduced, define:

- what it can treat;
- what it cannot;
- practitioner scarcity;
- cost;
- failure rate;
- training;
- class access;
- why ordinary medicine still matters.

Reliable major healing would affect:

- warfare;
- mining;
- childbirth;
- labor;
- inheritance;
- religion;
- insurance-like systems.

It cannot be added as flavor.

---

# 8. No confirmed magical agriculture

The current food system depends on real:

- land;
- rainfall;
- water control;
- seed;
- labor;
- storage;
- preservation;
- disease;
- trade.

Do not casually introduce:

- guaranteed magical harvests;
- enchanted seed;
- weather control;
- crop-growth spells;
- magically preserved food.

Even rare agricultural magic could alter Low Rivers politics and the world's food dependencies.

---

# 9. No confirmed magical industrial production

Stone Hills and other craft systems rely on:

- furnaces;
- fuel;
- ore;
- skilled labor;
- water power;
- hand and animal power;
- tools.

Do not introduce:

- effortless transmutation;
- magically perfect metal;
- unlimited heat;
- magical mass production;
- cheap magical precision;

without explaining why the existing production system still matters.

---

# 10. No confirmed magical weapons system

Legacy notes contain enchanted or legendary weapons.

Current world does not require them.

If one is promoted later, test:

- rarity;
- replicability;
- maintenance;
- ownership;
- military consequences;
- regulation;
- production inputs.

One exceptional object is easier to integrate than a repeatable magical weapons industry.

---

# 11. No confirmed magical monster ecology

The current world does not depend on monsters.

Where older material attributes danger to supernatural creatures, first test mundane explanations:

- animals;
- disease;
- environmental change;
- human violence;
- rumor;
- misidentification;
- folklore.

A confirmed supernatural creature would require ecology and human adaptation.

---

# 12. No confirmed prophecy system

Religions and myths may contain:

- prophets;
- omens;
- dreams;
- visions;
- sacred interpretations.

Current canon does not establish reliable foresight.

~~~text
BELIEF IN PROPHECY
≠
CONFIRMED PREDICTIVE MAGIC
~~~

If prophecy is real and repeatable, it changes politics, markets, warfare, religion, and Council behavior.

---

# 13. The Spine is physically real; its supernatural meaning is unresolved

See [The Spine](Places/The-Spine.md).

The Spine currently functions through:

- geology;
- elevation;
- passes;
- rivers;
- caves;
- chokepoints.

Legacy material also contains:

- Mother's-body mythology;
- mystical energy;
- star-blessing;
- dreams;
- prophecy;
- magical origins.

These can remain religious or mythic traditions without requiring literal supernatural geography.

Do not use magic to explain a physical feature current geology already explains unless later canon deliberately adds a second layer.

---

# 14. Religious experience remains epistemically open

People can sincerely report:

- visions;
- blessings;
- curses;
- answered prayer;
- sacred presence;
- prophecy;
- spirits.

Useful classifications include:

- confirmed mundane cause;
- likely mundane cause;
- unexplained;
- religious interpretation;
- fraud;
- coincidence;
- possibly magical;
- confirmed magical.

Do not collapse all religious experience into fraud.

Do not collapse all unexplained experience into magic.

---

# 15. Any real magic must create middle-worldbuilding

A magical phenomenon should not stop at:

> cool power.

Use:

~~~text
phenomenon
→ limitation / cost
→ repeated use
→ coordination problem
→ institution / profession / taboo
→ ordinary consequence
~~~

If a rare person can detect contaminated water, for example, ask:

- accuracy;
- rarity;
- training;
- trust;
- employment;
- false positives;
- replacement when the practitioner dies;
- effects on public health.

If none of those consequences exist, the magic is probably decorative or too new to matter.

---

# 16. Magic must preserve existing bottlenecks unless we deliberately redesign them

Current bottlenecks include:

- travel time;
- shipping capacity;
- food storage;
- pass closures;
- labor;
- credit;
- information delay;
- fuel;
- water;
- injury;
- disease.

If magic removes one, ask what happens to the institutions built around that constraint.

Reliable long-distance magical messages, for example, would reduce the importance of:

- couriers;
- merchant news;
- pilgrim information;
- Port aggregation;
- rumor delay.

That is not a small addition.

---

# 17. Magic must have distribution

For any real magical effect, define:

- how many people can do it;
- where practitioners live;
- how they learn;
- who can afford access;
- whether governments regulate it;
- whether guilds exist;
- whether religions claim it;
- whether criminals use it;
- whether the effect can be copied.

A power can be individually strong while socially minor if access is sufficiently rare or unreliable.

---

# 18. Magic and class

If magic has practical value, unequal access matters.

Ask:

- do elites monopolize it?
- do guilds license it?
- do villages pool money for it?
- do faiths provide it?
- do black markets exist?
- can an ordinary worker access it?

Magic that affects everyone equally without an access mechanism is under-worldbuilt.

---

# 19. Magic and law

See [Law, Jurisdiction, and Dispute Resolution](Law-and-Jurisdiction.md).

Do not assume courts accept supernatural evidence.

Abilities such as:

- lie detection;
- identity verification;
- guilt detection;
- locating hidden objects;
- reliable foresight;

would transform law.

They are not established.

Any future magical evidence system needs rules for reliability, fraud, interpretation, access, and appeal.

# 20. Magic and the Council

See [Economic Council](Politics/Economic-Council.md).

The Council does not currently require a magic portfolio.

Its power is explained through:

- finance;
- contracts;
- storage;
- routes;
- infrastructure;
- information;
- political relationships.

Do not reintroduce an arcane domain or magical surveillance system merely because legacy notes had one.

If magic later becomes economically important, the Council may try to influence access through its normal dependency logic.

# 21. Magic and religion are separate questions

The nine current faith traditions do not require demonstrable magic.

Religious authority currently comes from:

- community;
- history;
- moral teaching;
- ritual;
- service;
- archives;
- charity;
- mediation;
- identity.

A magical event could affect religion.

Religion does not require magical proof.

---

# 22. Magic and technology

The world is pre-industrial and craft-scale.

Do not use magic to smuggle modern infrastructure into the setting.

Warning signs:

- magical telephones;
- magical electricity grids;
- universal magical refrigeration;
- magical mass transit;
- magical factories;
- cheap magical precision manufacturing;
- universal magical medicine.

A rare artifact performing one unusual function is different from a scalable infrastructure system.

---

# 23. Artifact rule

The legacy World Rules provide a compatible design principle.

If magical artifacts are retained, they should have:

- a specific function;
- a reason to exist;
- limited availability;
- a cost or drawback;
- a creation history;
- material requirements;
- cultural context.

Avoid:

> enchanted because fantasy.

Prefer:

> created because someone faced a specific problem and had access to a rare method.

Artifacts should undergo the same Middle Worldbuilding test as any technology.

# 24. Current classification

## Working canon constraints

- setting is low fantasy;
- material limits matter;
- magic cannot erase logistics;
- technology remains pre-industrial and craft-scale;
- magic, if real, must be scarce, uncertain, limited, or costly;
- any operational magic must have systemic consequences;
- no owner should rely on an unstated magical capability.

## Confirmed specific phenomena

**None are required as confirmed magic by the active world model.**

## Unresolved candidates

- rare practical magic;
- rare artifacts;
- supernatural creatures;
- prophecy or visions;
- magical communication;
- magical healing;
- magical agriculture;
- magical industrial effects;
- magical weapons;
- supernatural aspects of the Spine;
- magical broadcasting in Audience Movements.

## Legacy-only unless promoted

- arcane research systems;
- magic research institutions;
- scrying networks;
- enchanted messenger systems;
- routine illusions;
- magical mass communication;
- star-powered relic systems;
- broad supernatural monster ecology;
- domain-scale magical industry.

# 25. Promotion test

Before adding a magical phenomenon to canon, answer:

1. What exactly happens?
2. Is it definitely supernatural?
3. How reliable is it?
4. What does it cost?
5. How rare is it?
6. Can it be taught?
7. Can it be reproduced?
8. Who has access?
9. Which current bottleneck does it alter?
10. Which owner pages would change?
11. What institution would form around it?
12. Why has it not transformed the world more broadly?
13. What mundane alternative still exists?
14. What happens when the magic fails?
15. What distinguishes it from fraud, religion, coincidence, or misunderstood natural phenomena?

If these cannot be answered, keep the phenomenon mythic, unexplained, provisional, or legacy.

---

# 26. Current design direction

The world does **not** need magic to justify its complexity.

That is an advantage.

Magic can later be added only where it produces something genuinely valuable rather than patching holes in logistics or lore.

Current default:

> **Use mundane causality unless magic has been specifically established.**

That protects everything already built while leaving room for rare genuine wonder.
