# South Country

## Status

**Working canon.**

South Country is the warmer, generally drier southern/eastern region associated with old city-states, irrigated agriculture, orchards, coastal exchange, and civic competition.

It should not be reduced to wine, olive oil, and festivals.

## Geography

South Country includes:
- dry plains;
- river valleys;
- irrigated basins;
- orchard belts;
- grazing country;
- coast;
- interior towns;
- scrub and open woodland;
- fertile deltas in some areas.

Water availability varies sharply, which makes water law politically important.

## Historical identity

Before The Convergence, South Country consisted of competing:
- city-states;
- estate regions;
- port towns;
- irrigation leagues;
- noble houses;
- merchant dynasties;
- rural client territories.

Borders often followed waterworks and tributary networks rather than obvious terrain.

Wars could be fought over:
- canal gates;
- springs;
- river diversions;
- orchard land;
- ports;
- trade rights.

Temporary alliances were common and betrayal was normal.

## Cultural inspirations

Useful pools include:
- Maghrebi and Levantine societies;
- Anatolian, Persian, Iberian, Greek, and southern Italian dry-climate traditions;
- South Asian irrigation and market cultures;
- Andean and other South American arid/coastal agriculture;
- historical city-states and merchant republics globally.

## Language

South Country urban languages may contain:
- formal status registers;
- public/private speech differences;
- elaborate traditions of rhetoric and legal argument;
- indirect refusal and face-saving forms.

This helps explain why political speech is a craft and why public humiliation can matter politically.

## Settlement and architecture

### Cities

South Country city-states can have:
- dense walled cores;
- courtyard houses;
- shaded streets;
- cisterns;
- fountains;
- baths where culturally appropriate;
- markets;
- assembly spaces;
- warehouses;
- garden districts;
- elite estates;
- crowded poor quarters.

Different cities should look meaningfully different.

### Towns

Small towns may center on:
- a spring;
- irrigation gate;
- market;
- road junction;
- estate;
- shrine;
- fishing harbor.

### Rural homes

Farmsteads adapt to heat:
- thick earthen/stone walls;
- courtyards;
- shade structures;
- roof terraces;
- water storage;
- enclosed animal spaces.

## Food

South Country cuisine is diverse:
- wheat and barley breads;
- pulses;
- vegetables;
- fruits;
- nuts;
- cheeses;
- lamb/goat/poultry;
- fish near coasts;
- preserved lemons or similar acidic preserves where appropriate;
- oils;
- spice blends;
- dried fruit.

Wine is important in some places, forbidden or uncommon in others depending on faith and local custom.

Do not make all South Country food uniformly "Mediterranean."

## Politics

The region remains politically plural.

City-states compete for:
- water;
- port access;
- prestige;
- trade;
- labor;
- alliances;
- cultural influence.

A permanent South Country union would be powerful enough to worry both neighbors and the Council.

## Current pressures

- drought fears;
- private control of canals;
- city-state blocs forming and breaking;
- merchant-family espionage;
- artistic/civic rivalry masking real political competition;
- Council efforts to prevent dangerous consolidation;
- Villain attempts to make defensive alliances appear expansionist.

## Story function

South Country shows how **public culture and private power** can coexist.

Its political sophistication makes it hard to manipulate crudely — but also creates more channels through which skilled manipulators can work.
