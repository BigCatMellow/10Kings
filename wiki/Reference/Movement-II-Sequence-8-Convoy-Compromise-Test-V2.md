# Prose test

The captain hated the convoy before it left the harbor.

This was useful because it meant they could not later accuse themselves of changing principle for convenience.

The schedule required departure after midday.

The wind had been better in the morning.

The contribution charge had been collected before anyone knew whether the escort would actually sail.

The lead escort came from a harbor the captain did not trust.

And the paper from Port contained three separate places where somebody else could tell them to wait.

The captain had underlined all three.

"Still going?" the harbor clerk asked.

The captain folded the schedule.

"Obviously."

"You've complained for two days."

"I can complain and sail."

"That is becoming the regional settlement."

The captain did not dignify that.

Their vessel carried:

- preserved fish;
- two passengers;
- a small load of hides;
- one crate of tools going south.

Nothing worth a song.

Everything worth losing if a raider took the boat.

That was the problem with ordinary trade.

Nobody wrote songs about it until it stopped arriving.

At the outer harbor, five vessels waited.

Three were local.

Two were not.

The larger merchant ship arrived late.

The captain watched it enter and said:

"Of course."

Their mate knew better than to ask.

A patrol vessel moved past the line and raised the departure signal.

Then lowered it again.

The captain swore.

A boat came alongside with a message.

"Hold for the eastern pair."

"We are the eastern pair."

"Other eastern pair."

"There are too many directions in this agreement."

The messenger had no opinion.

The captain looked at the sky.

Then at the tide.

Then at the larger merchant ship whose owner could apparently arrive whenever they pleased and still receive protection.

"This is why it won't last," the captain said.

Their mate adjusted a line.

"You said that yesterday."

"I was correct yesterday."

The other two vessels arrived.

The convoy left.

For the first hour, the captain remained angry enough to notice every way the formation was inefficient.

The escort lead kept the smaller vessels tighter than they would have chosen.

One merchant captain repeatedly drifted out of place.

A fishing boat carried more sail than the signal allowed.

The captain could have made better time alone.

The captain had made better time alone.

That was not the same question anymore.

Near the outer headland, they passed a stretch of water where a cargo boat had been taken earlier in the season.

The crew grew quieter.

Nobody had to tell them why.

The patrol vessel signaled a course change.

The captain objected immediately.

"Why?"

The mate looked through the glass.

"Something north."

"What?"

"Can't tell."

The captain took the glass.

A sail.

Maybe two.

Far enough away to be ordinary.

Close enough to become an argument.

The escort turned the convoy slightly seaward.

The captain calculated:

- lost time;
- worse angle;
- evening arrival;
- market delay.

Then calculated:

- one hull;
- one crew;
- two passengers;
- everything else.

"Fine," they said.

The mate looked at them.

"I said fine."

"I heard you."

"Then stop looking pleased."

The distant sails did not follow.

That proved nothing.

It was also exactly what everyone wanted.

They reached the next harbor after dusk.

The market was closed.

The captain had predicted this.

They repeated the prediction to anyone who would listen.

The cargo still arrived.

Nobody was injured.

Nothing had been taken.

The harbor clerk at the receiving end recorded:

- departure;
- delay;
- route change;
- sighting;
- arrival.

The captain watched the clerk write.

"Do you record that we missed market?"

"If you want."

"I want."

"Do you want it under security delay or commercial consequence?"

The captain stared.

"Both."

"I need one."

"Why?"

"Because the report has one line."

The captain looked at the form.

"Your new system is excellent."

"Thank you."

"That was not praise."

"I know."

The clerk marked:

> security delay.

The captain went to find a bed.

---

The next convoy left three days later.

The captain considered sailing outside it.

The wind was good.

The cargo was ready.

The schedule was not.

They waited.

This time the escort left on time.

No strange sails appeared.

A ship lost a steering line and the whole convoy slowed while the crew repaired it.

The captain spent the delay explaining to their mate that this was exactly why independent sailing was superior.

Their mate listened.

The repaired ship rejoined.

They arrived before market closed.

Nothing happened.

That was the second voyage.

Then a third.

A raider was reported farther north.

The convoy route shifted before departure.

A small harbor refused one contribution demand and sent a patrol boat instead.

The coordinating office accepted the substitution after two days of argument.

An island captain refused the assigned formation, sailed anyway, and still arrived safely.

Both sides claimed this proved their position.

The captain began recognizing the schedule clerk's handwriting.

That bothered them more than it should have.

---

By the fifth convoy, the harbor board had added a permanent hook for the temporary schedule.

The captain noticed.

"Take that down."

The clerk looked at the hook.

"Why?"

"It's temporary."

"The hook?"

"The schedule."

"We still need somewhere to hang it."

"Use a nail."

"What is the political difference between a hook and a nail?"

"A hook expects another paper."

The clerk considered this.

"Fair."

They did not remove it.

The captain paid the convoy contribution.

It was lower than the first week.

Not by much.

Enough to notice.

"Why?"

"Fewer losses."

"That fast?"

"Less replacement cargo. Fewer emergency hires. Lenders are changing terms."

"Good."

The clerk looked surprised.

The captain corrected themselves.

"Not good. Reasonable."

"Of course."

The captain folded the receipt.

Across the harbor, an escort crew was repainting one of the signal boards.

Temporary work.

On a permanent board.

The captain disliked that too.

---

At home, the captain's spouse asked:

"So?"

"So what?"

"Does it work?"

The captain took off their wet outer layer.

"That's not the question."

"It was mine."

They hung the garment near the stove.

The room smelled of:

- fish;
- damp wool;
- something boiling with onions.

The captain sat.

"It works enough."

"Enough for what?"

"Enough that I have to complain more carefully."

Their spouse laughed.

The captain did not.

Not immediately.
