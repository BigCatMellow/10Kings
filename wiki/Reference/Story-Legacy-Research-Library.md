## What's in Volume 11, and its disposition

- **Elevator pitch & outline.** A hidden Council maintains balance; a Villain engineers chaos to install himself as "peacemaker"; Wurdren is an aging, unglamorous adventurer whose small kindnesses keep undoing the Villain's plans without either side knowing why. **Premise is compatible** with current [Main Conflict](../Story/Main-Conflict.md) and [Villain](../Story/Villain.md) — both already use this three-force shape — but the outline's specific beats and act breaks were never promoted scene-by-scene.
- **Hero profile.** Wurdren: late fifties, from a farming family, widowed, children drifted, carries a plain sword engraved with their names. **Offered 2026-09-29, declined by the author.** Stays open per [Open Questions](../Open-Questions.md); the only trace in canon is that the question remains unanswered.
- **Villain profile.** Placeholder name **Arivel**; origin in Port; **unaware the Council exists**. *Since [D28](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions) the origin and the unawareness match bt 7274's ruling; the name stays declined.* **Conflicted with the canon of the time** — [Villain](../Story/Villain.md) treats the Council relationship as structurally important to the plot, and this origin was noted as conflicting on 2026-09-29 (only the Wurdren profile and the name "Arivel" were offered and declined; see the [Source Register](Source-Register.md#12-2026-09-29-thin-areas-settled-magic-language-families-council-houses)).
- **20-entry "Blacklist Style Villains" brainstorm** (Garion "Blacktongue" Harth, Shala Zemir, Azhar Rivenswell, and 17 more named alternates with one-line hooks). Pure naming brainstorm; **never evaluated against canon, never promoted**. Useful only as a name/flavor pool if a future villain concept is needed.
- **Council, Villain & Hero" dynamics section.** Elaborates how the Council tries to contain the Villain without exposing itself, and how Wurdren becomes a variable neither side modeled. Largely consistent with current Main Conflict's resolution logic; not separately promoted because the current page already covers this ground.
- **Real-World Analogue: The Monday Night Wars.** A structural comparison to the 1990s WWF/WCW ratings war (backstage cliques, talent raids, the Montreal Screwjob, escalating spectacle to win an audience) used as a model for how two power centers can drag a system into escalating, performative conflict. **Never evaluated or promoted.** Interesting as a pacing/escalation reference, not as setting fact.

## What's in Volume 12, and its disposition

- **Domino structure, four tiers:** Nails (tiny, local) → Horseshoes (intermediate disruptions) → Steeds (major domino failures) → Catastrophe (open war + the Villain's big move). **Only the "tiny nail dominoes" idea was carried forward** into the live [Villain's Dominoes](../Story/Villains-Dominoes.md) and the Sunday Morning stories' domino map. The Horseshoe/Steed/Catastrophe tiers and the full cascade mechanics were **not used**.
- **The Villain's actual goal: broker peace to found his new kingdom.** Ignite a war severe enough that only he can end it; demand territorial concessions or a recognized new realm as the price of peace. **Explicitly rejected** — conflicts with or goes beyond the live wiki per Source Register. *Reopened 2026-11-09 and ruled in a different form ([D28](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)):* not one man's kingdom but a real voice for the Port-born, the Sea People's heirs, read through the Danzig sections ([The Whole Road](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/The-Whole-Road.md#the-villain-alternatives-judged-separately), [Villain](../Story/Villain.md#homeland-and-grievance-d28)).
- **The final peace summit.** War reaches stalemate; the Villain steps in with supplies/logistics/trade routes; kingdoms cede land or resources to him as "the lesser evil"; Wurdren witnesses or attempts a last-minute confrontation he can't win alone. **Rejected along with the goal above** — no replacement ending has been promoted; the current direction is [Main Conflict](../Story/Main-Conflict.md#possible-resolution-model)'s possible resolution model, adopted as the end goal by [D18](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions).
- **The Villain's ten-step playbook** (research weak points → exploit local tension → manipulate communication → leverage guild discontent → incite outrage → corrupt key figures → engineer a crisis → keep the Council distracted → fan the flames → reap rewards), plus named guilds the plan depends on. **Not used.** Its plan-specific guilds are unused; the general named guild roster (from Volume 08) came in as provisional by D24 ([Guilds](../Politics/Guilds.md#the-named-guilds)).
- **Multi-POV narration notes** (why center the Villain's perspective; the Council as antagonist-to-the-antagonist; Wurdren as ground-level witness). Craft guidance, not setting fact; never formally adopted but doesn't conflict with anything live.
- **Port internal dynamics** (the source is [World Dynamics](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/direct-uploads/two_sons_world_dynamics.md), not Volume 12, whose Danzig sections are real-world history; now on the [backlog](README.md#legacy-reconciliation-backlog)). Population structure (mixed/displaced, "sailors' droppings," "mud dogs," 80%+ identify as Port-born first), the economic trap (total food dependence, demilitarized harbor, chronic unemployment), the smuggling economy as survival mechanism, the sovereignty/identity problem, and three political factions (Harbor Masters' Alliance, Workers' Collectives, Merchant Factors). **Partially rejected:** the absolutist claims ("only protected deep-water bay," "no military," total food dependence) were explicitly not promoted because current [Port](../Places/Port.md) is more nuanced. The texture pieces — factions, slang terms, smuggling-as-survival framing — were **never evaluated**, just never pulled forward.

## Reconciliation principles

1. A legacy beat is evidence of an earlier concept, not a draft waiting for approval.
2. Where a legacy element has already been explicitly accepted, declined, or rejected (see tables above), treat that as settled — don't re-litigate it without the author raising it again.
3. Where a legacy element was never evaluated (the WWF analogue, the Villain's narration notes, Port's faction/texture detail), it's a candidate for future review, not a gap in current canon.
4. If any legacy beat is promoted later, it goes into its owning Story or Places page directly, and this entry should be updated to point at the new canon rather than duplicating it.
