# 8. Current thematic statements

## Story-level declarative thesis

> **Being right about what must change does not give you the right to decide everyone else's future.**

## Expanded institutional thesis

> **Necessary change does not make every means necessary. Power may be useful, competent, and historically transformative without becoming legitimate. A future is only meaningfully free if the people who must live in it remain participants rather than instruments, and no founder, council, or savior earns ownership of what comes after.**

## Wurdren personal thesis

> **A life does not become worthwhile when history notices it.**

Expanded:

> **A life worth living is not proved by one great accomplishment; it is built by becoming the kind of person whose values, actions, relationships, and responsibilities increasingly belong to the same life.**

Shared bridge:

> **Meaning does not require ownership.**

# 9. Current ending direction

Working structural ending:

> **The Villain wins the structural argument and loses ownership of the result.**

The immediate story produces:

- real Port agency;
- Council exposure without erasing the need for coordination;
- Villain exposure without erasing the truth of Port's grievance;
- a negotiated pause;
- a minimum successor settlement.

The long-horizon epilogue returns to Port at Three Moons **98 years later / fourteen festival cycles later**.

The later world:

- retains local sovereignty;
- publicly coordinates some shared problems;
- remains politically contested / imperfect;
- no longer belongs to the person who forced its first rupture.
