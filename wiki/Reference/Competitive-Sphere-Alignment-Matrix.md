# Competitive Sphere Alignment Matrix

## Status

**Reference / provisional historical reconstruction — not canon.**

This page tests the [Convergence to Competitive Spheres](Convergence-to-Competitive-Spheres.md) hypothesis actor by actor.

The question is not:

> Which side does each region belong to?

The question is:

> Given what an actor gained, lost, learned and resented before and during the Convergence, which present-day competitive sphere would it most plausibly lean toward—and where would that alignment split internally?

Legacy polity names are used as evidence because they preserve older political relationships. Their existence and exact modern form remain subject to the current owner pages.

## Working sphere definitions

### Charter / Convergence sphere — WWF structural role

The older establishment:

- recognized capitals;
- treaty institutions;
- route and arbitration systems;
- rulers whose legitimacy was stabilized by the Convergence;
- established guild relationships;
- inherited expectations about who counts as a serious power.

Its central problem is **institutional prestige under pressure**.

### Ascendant / revisionist sphere — WCW structural role

The challenger network:

- secondary cities whose wealth grew after the Convergence;
- guild-run powers;
- merchant leagues;
- ambitious patrons;
- independent city-states;
- actors who obey the post-Convergence system but believe its prestige hierarchy is obsolete.

Its central advantage is **money, recruitment and momentum**.

Its central danger is that imported prestige and powerful patrons can eventually overwhelm internal coherence.

### Port — ECW + Danzig

Port remains outside both spheres.

It is the shared market, cultural laboratory, recruitment ground and politically constrained neutral space used by both.

# 1. Summithold / central Highridge institutions

## Before the Convergence

Highridge predecessor politics revolved around:

- toll lords;
- caravan families;
- fortified pass towns;
- merchant coalitions;
- competing route systems.

The legacy pre-Convergence notes describe Highridge pass-lords changing tolls, closing trails and receiving outside backing from Ironcrest and Sunplains interests.

This means Summithold's later authority did **not** emerge from an eternally unified Highridge state.

It emerged from a region where controlling movement was political power.

## What the Convergence gives it

Highridge is the strongest current candidate for the principal Convergence congress.

The settlement also creates precisely the institutions Highridge is suited to dominate:

- route rules;
- arbitration;
- protected movement;
- standardized negotiations;
- repeated interregional congresses.

Legacy polity notes later place Summithold at the center of Highridge authority and give it final influence over important caravan tolls.

So the Convergence turns a contested route center into a **recognized institutional center**.

## What it gives up

The same settlement that legitimizes Highridge also limits arbitrary route power.

A post-Convergence system of protected movement and agreed access means a major Highridge authority cannot simply behave like the old toll-lords without undermining the settlement that legitimizes it.

It is therefore trapped by its own success:

**Summithold has enormous power because the rules matter, but it must continue pretending that the rules matter more than Summithold.**

## Present rivals

The clearest is **Cliffhaven**, explicitly described in legacy notes as attempting to undercut Summithold's toll dominance.

That is not merely a local feud.

It is exactly the kind of post-Convergence rivalry that can become sphere politics.

## Working alignment

**Strong Charter core candidate.**

Summithold is the cleanest current WWF-side anchor because its prestige is tied to:

- history;
- recognized procedure;
- institutional centrality;
- route legitimacy;
- inherited expectations.

### What would humiliate it

- a famous arbitrator leaving for Cliffhaven;
- major caravan houses shifting routes to a rival system;
- scholars choosing challenger patronage;
- another city hosting negotiations previously assumed to belong in Summithold;
- a rival toll regime becoming the de facto commercial standard.

# 2. Verdanthearth / central Greenvale authority

## Before the Convergence

Greenvale predecessor politics repeatedly destabilized under crop failure.

Legacy history includes:

- famine raids;
- fragmented farmland barons;
- expansion into healthier territory;
- Ironcrest's temporary Iron-Field conquest;
- retaliation funded partly by Sunplains orchard powers.

Greenvale therefore enters the Convergence with a particularly strong memory of what unstable borders and insecure food systems cost.

## What the Convergence gives it

The settlement provides:

- recognized agricultural territory;
- safer export routes;
- reliable access to metal tools;
- predictable markets;
- reduced risk of another Ironcrest-style territorial push;
- a framework in which grain obligations become contractual rather than military.

Legacy polity notes describe Verdanthearth as friendly with Summithold and Aurorashore and pragmatic with Cindermarch.

That is the strongest recovered pre-existing relationship cluster resembling a Charter network.

## What it gives up

Stability also constrains Greenvale actors.

Possible inherited constraints include:

- recognized borders limiting opportunistic farmland expansion;
- trade commitments during shortages;
- external expectations that Greenvale will stabilize food supply;
- treaty interpretations around forest edges;
- central crop taxation and storage systems becoming more politically important.

The current **abundance crisis** makes this especially relevant.

A system designed to protect food stability can become politically vulnerable when surplus, storage, debt and finance—not famine—are the problem.

## Working alignment

**Strong Charter partner candidate at the central-government level.**

Verdanthearth has more reason than many powers to value the settlement itself.

But Greenvale should not be uniformly Charter-aligned.

Potential Ascendant openings exist among:

- distressed farmers;
- independent baronies;
- river towns;
- reformist cooperatives;
- merchants angry at established storage or finance systems.

### What would humiliate it

- a famous agricultural innovator accepting patronage from a Sunplains challenger;
- an independent Greenvale market town joining a rival grain network;
- a challenger solving the abundance crisis faster than Charter institutions;
- major farm cooperatives rejecting the central export/storage framework.

# 3. Aurorashore / central Northwind leadership

## Before the Convergence

Northwind politics were unusually fragmented.

Pre-Convergence power included:

- coastal clans;
- harbor confederacies;
- fishing-right alliances;
- raiding fleets;
- overlapping maritime claims.

The legacy Great Coastal Skirmish also shows Northwind clans cooperating with an Ironcrest forging faction against Sunplains-backed maritime pressure.

This is important: Northwind already has a history of **practical cross-regional alignment without ideological loyalty**.

## What the Convergence gives it

The settlement provides:

- recognition of a broader Northwind political voice;
- safer maritime trade;
- expanded markets;
- predictable grain and metal access;
- agreed maritime rules;
- reduced incentive for destructive clan competition.

Legacy notes also describe Verdanthearth as friendly toward Aurorashore.

## What it gives up

The settlement may be particularly ambiguous for Northwind.

Maritime rules and neutral Port access benefit its traders, but they also restrain:

- exclusive fishing or shipping claims;
- coercive harbor control;
- unilateral convoy rules;
- raiding traditions;
- any old claim to special control over Port.

[Contested Historical Memory](../History/Contested-Memory.md) preserves an unresolved tradition in which Northwind interests once controlled Port before coalition pressure produced withdrawal.

That is not canon chronology, but it gives a credible reason why some Northwind families might view Convergence-era neutrality as **a loss disguised as peace**.

## Working alignment

**Central Aurorashore institutions: Charter-leaning.**

**Northwind as a whole: divided.**

That distinction is important.

The Clanhold benefits from recognized diplomacy.

Individual harbors and clans can resent exactly the rules that made the Clanhold more useful internationally.

### Likely internal splits

- central Clanhold diplomacy → Charter;
- traditional fishing-right factions → mixed;
- independent harbor powers → potential Ascendant;
- Whitepeak-type elites seeking industrial ties → swing;
- pirate-adjacent or fiercely autonomous ports → nonaligned / transactional.

### What would humiliate it

- a famous navigator taking a challenger contract;
- a harbor recognizing a rival convoy standard;
- a clan bypassing Aurorashore in negotiations;
- a challenger-funded fleet becoming more prestigious than the official maritime institutions.

# 4. Ironcrest Sovereignty / Cindermarch

## Before the Convergence

Ironcrest predecessor politics were divided among:

- mine-holding clans;
- fortified smith towns;
- valley lordships;
- merchant-backed warbands;
- craft alliances.

Legacy history repeatedly shows Ironcrest projecting power outward:

- ore conflicts;
- the Iron-Field conquest into Greenvale;
- intervention around Highridge passes;
- temporary military-industrial cooperation with Northwind.

Ironcrest therefore enters the Convergence as both an indispensable producer and a power other regions have reason to fear.

## What the Convergence gives it

The gains are enormous:

- recognized territory;
- secure food imports from Greenvale;
- safer export routes;
- predictable demand for tools and metal;
- reduced cost of defending distant commercial interests;
- wider guild markets.

The Convergence may be one of the major reasons Ironcrest can become **industrial rather than merely militarized**.

## What it gives up

It also loses tools of expansion:

- conquest for farmland;
- coercive route opening;
- uncontrolled extraction beyond recognized boundaries;
- easy use of military force to settle commercial disputes.

And post-Convergence guild growth creates a new internal threat.

The same system that enriches the Crown also enables industrial actors that do not need to accept total Crown control.

## Blackbellow changes the equation

Legacy notes describe the **Blackbellow Consortium** as:

- a guild-run city-state;
- ruled by wealthy forging families;
- technologically advanced;
- able to attract workers through higher wages;
- tense with the Ironcrest crown.

This may be one of the most important recovered relationships in the entire alignment problem.

It is a WWF/WCW talent war **inside Ironcrest**.

## Working alignment

**Ironcrest is the major hinge, not a clean sphere member.**

Best current hypothesis:

- Ironcrest Crown / old state institutions → Charter-leaning;
- Blackbellow and some industrial challengers → Ascendant;
- mine owners and guild branches → contested;
- labor movements → issue-based rather than automatically aligned.

Ironcrest could become the place where the sphere rivalry becomes impossible for ordinary people to ignore.

### Major strategic possibility

If the **Ironcrest Sovereignty itself** ever shifts significantly toward the Ascendant network, that could be the equivalent of a marquee defection that transforms the challenger from an irritating coalition into a true peer.

Do not assume that has happened yet.

### What would humiliate the Crown

- a royal engineer defecting to Blackbellow;
- workers preferring challenger workshops;
- a major state forge accepting Ascendant finance;
- an industrial guild refusing Crown policy because rival patrons can protect it.

# 5. Cliffhaven

## Historical position

Cliffhaven is a semi-republican Highridge city whose merchants and scholars elect leadership.

Its defining recovered political fact is simple:

**Cliffhaven tries to undercut Summithold's toll dominance.**

That gives it an unusually clean historical motive.

## What the Convergence gives it

Cliffhaven benefits from everything that makes secondary trade cities viable:

- safer routes;
- standard contracts;
- reduced petty warfare;
- broader merchant networks;
- recognition of commercial norms;
- freedom to compete economically rather than militarily.

Without the Convergence, Summithold's route rivals can be crushed, bypassed or isolated.

Under the Convergence, a rival can challenge the center **while still claiming to obey the shared system**.

## What constrains it

Summithold retains inherited prestige and route authority.

Cliffhaven can become prosperous while still being treated as secondary.

That is exactly the status mismatch needed for the Ascendant argument:

> We use the same roads, finance the same trade and attract the same people. Why does Summithold still get to decide what counts as legitimate?

## Working alignment

**Very strong Ascendant core candidate.**

Cliffhaven is probably the cleanest political counterpart to Summithold.

It provides:

- merchant capital;
- scholar networks;
- competing toll structures;
- a civic identity built around doing things differently from the established center.

### WCW-style recruitment tools

Cliffhaven can offer:

- lower tolls;
- freer scholarly patronage;
- elected civic office;
- merchant backing;
- access to alternative caravan networks;
- prestige built around being modern rather than traditional.

### What would count as a major win

- a Summithold caravan house relocating;
- a famous scholar accepting a Cliffhaven chair;
- a regional congress choosing Cliffhaven;
- a new route standard becoming more widely used than Summithold's.

# 6. Blackbellow Consortium

## Historical position

Blackbellow is not a normal monarchy.

It is a wealthy guild-run industrial city-state inside the broader Ironcrest ecosystem.

Legacy notes explicitly make it competitive with the Crown.

## What the Convergence gives it

Blackbellow benefits from:

- cross-domain guild expansion;
- predictable trade;
- larger markets for high-quality metal;
- safer movement of workers and specialists;
- access to finance and imported food;
- reduced need for territorial control.

The Convergence lets industrial power become **portable and networked**.

That is ideal for Blackbellow.

## What constrains it

Its formal political importance may lag behind its economic importance.

It still exists in the shadow of the Ironcrest Sovereignty.

That produces the same basic grievance as Cliffhaven:

**wealth without equivalent inherited prestige.**

## Working alignment

**Very strong Ascendant core candidate.**

Blackbellow may be one of the sphere's main financial and recruitment engines.

Its higher wages are especially important because they translate the wrestling analogy almost literally:

```text
established institution develops skilled person
→ Blackbellow offers more money / freedom / workshop rights
→ person leaves
→ Crown calls it disloyalty
→ Blackbellow calls it merit
→ everyone else watches
```

### WCW-style risk

Blackbellow also contains the seeds of the challenger sphere's eventual internal problem.

If famous engineers, guild masters and defectors gain special privileges, they may crowd out Blackbellow's own people.

The institution that once attracted talent by offering opportunity can become dominated by expensive imported prestige.

That is a particularly strong fantasy translation of WCW's backstage failure mode.

# 7. Solanterra / the Sunplains central Mandate

## Historical position

Sunplains is the least useful region to assign wholesale.

Its old political structure is explicitly plural:

- city-states;
- merchant ports;
- irrigation leagues;
- estates;
- rival patrons.

Pre-Convergence orchard-lords competed violently and sometimes financed conflicts outside the region.

That means cross-border patronage is already part of Sunplains political culture.

## What the Convergence gives the central Mandate

A central Sunplains authority benefits from:

- external recognition;
- common trade rules;
- security against raiders;
- protected caravan access;
- boundaries around orchard / forest expansion;
- a framework for coordinating otherwise competitive city-states.

## What it gives up

The Mandate cannot simply absorb every independent oasis, city or ancient local kingdom without consequences.

Convergence recognition of plural political actors may preserve precisely the local autonomy a central ruler would prefer to weaken.

## Working alignment

**Central Solanterra / Mandate: Charter-leaning or swing.**

It has reasons to defend the order that recognizes it as the major external representative of Sunplains.

But the region surrounding it is one of the strongest Ascendant recruitment pools.

This is another valuable internal split.

# 8. Rashira, Dunespire and Zalhar-type Sunplains powers

## Rashira

Legacy description:

- wealthy merchant league;
- controls caravan taxes;
- militarily weak;
- often depends on the Mandate for protection.

### Working alignment

**Transactional swing actor.**

Rashira may economically sympathize with Ascendant arguments while relying on Charter security.

That is exactly the kind of divided alignment the sphere model needs.

---

## Dunespire

Legacy description:

- independent emirate;
- valuable specialist production;
- ruler suspicious of Solanterra;
- plausible ties to rebellious actors.

### Working alignment

**Ascendant-leaning candidate.**

It has a direct incentive to support a sphere that weakens the assumption that Solanterra speaks for Sunplains.

---

## Zalhar

Legacy description:

- claims lineage older than the Mandate;
- jealously protects independence;
- fears Solanterra expansion.

### Working alignment

**Ascendant-leaning, but for different reasons than Blackbellow.**

Blackbellow's case is modern wealth.

Zalhar's case can be **old legitimacy resisting newer centralization**.

That distinction keeps the challenger sphere from becoming ideologically neat.

Some members want the future.

Others want their older privileges back.

They can still cooperate because both dislike the present hierarchy.

# 9. Deepwood

## Historical position

Deepwood repeatedly resisted outside extraction and frontier expansion.

The Convergence's recognized boundaries can therefore be genuinely valuable.

## Why clean sphere membership is weak

Both major spheres can threaten Deepwood for different reasons.

Charter institutions may defend treaty boundaries while also interpreting old extraction rights in ways local communities reject.

Ascendant industrial and merchant actors may challenge old political hierarchy while demanding even more access to timber, roads and specialist resources.

## Working alignment

**Nonaligned / issue-by-issue.**

Specific Deepwood communities, wardens or market towns can align temporarily.

Deepwood as a whole should not become the environmental member of one convenient coalition.

That would flatten the region.

- route-focused;

A route enclave may support whichever side keeps traffic moving or pays best.

Another may deliberately serve both.

**Nonaligned / fragmented.**

- a talent farm;

# The two emerging coalitions

## Charter sphere — current strongest skeleton

```text
                  SUMMITHOLD
                  /        \
                 /          \
       VERDANTHEARTH ---- AURORASHORE
              \             /
               \           /
             IRONCREST CROWN?
                   |
             contested internally
```

This is not necessarily a formal alliance.

It is an inherited web of:

- recognized capitals;
- treaty habits;
- route systems;
- food / metal / maritime interdependence;
- officials who have worked together for generations.

## Ascendant sphere — current strongest skeleton

```text
                CLIFFHAVEN
                /       \
               /         \
      BLACKBELLOW ---- SUNPLAINS PATRONS
            |                |
       guild money      city-state money
            \                /
             \              /
        ambitious secondary powers
```

Again, not a formal alliance.

Its cohesion comes from a shared experience:

**being economically or culturally more important than the old settlement says they are.**

# The most important hinge: Ironcrest

Ironcrest can give the rivalry its first truly dramatic geopolitical turn.

The Crown has strong reasons to defend Charter stability.

But the region contains some of the strongest Ascendant economic forces.

That creates several possible present states:

### A. Crown securely Charter

The rivalry remains established capitals versus rising secondary powers.

### B. Crown publicly Charter, court deeply split

This is probably the richest early-story state.

Ascendant money and personnel are already inside Ironcrest politics.

### C. Ironcrest shifts toward Ascendant

This is a **major defection event**.

The Ascendant sphere stops looking like a coalition of ambitious second-tier actors and suddenly gains one of the world's major industrial powers.

That could be the equivalent of the moment WCW's recruitment strategy became impossible to dismiss.

**Current recommendation: B.**

Do not promote it yet.

# The likely current phase of the rivalry

The source material does not identify an exact Monday Night Wars year to reproduce.

The current world state best supports a structural phase rather than an exact date analogue:

## **The challenger has crossed from nuisance to credible peer, but has not won.**

Signs:

- Cliffhaven and Blackbellow already have independent economic bases;
- Sunplains contains wealthy competitive patrons;
- current crises create opportunities to recruit dissatisfied people;
- Charter institutions still retain greater formal legitimacy;
- Port is already a shared cultural and commercial arena;
- no current source establishes that the Ascendant network has already displaced the older order.

That resembles the useful **early competitive-breakthrough period** of the wrestling notes:

```text
old establishment still controls legitimacy
→ challenger starts landing visible defections
→ establishment publicly minimizes threat
→ privately, competition changes decisions
→ public begins comparing them constantly
→ every major recruitment becomes news
```

Do not import the later WWF comeback or WCW collapse as predetermined history.

The analogy should create pressure, not prophecy.

These are tests, not canon events.

## 1. Summithold → Cliffhaven

A famous:

- arbitrator;
- scholar;
- caravan master;
- mapmaker;
- public intellectual

leaves a prestigious Summithold position for Cliffhaven.

**Meaning:** the challenger can now offer more prestige or freedom than the old institutional capital.

## 2. Ironcrest Crown → Blackbellow

A celebrated:

- master smith;
- engineer;
- mine administrator;
- military artificer;
- guild leader

takes a Blackbellow contract.

**Meaning:** money and workshop freedom can defeat Crown prestige.

This is probably the cleanest direct fantasy translation of a wrestling talent defection.

## 3. Charter agriculture → Sunplains patronage

A celebrated Greenvale agricultural or storage expert accepts a wealthy Sunplains patron.

**Meaning:** even the Charter's practical expertise is becoming mobile.

## 4. Aurorashore → independent maritime network

A respected navigator or harbor official leaves central service for a challenger-aligned port, patron or convoy system.

**Meaning:** Northwind's prestige does not guarantee loyalty from the people who create its maritime advantage.

# What the rivalry is actually about

The deepest distinction is becoming:

### Charter argument

> The Convergence works because durable institutions restrain everyone—including us. If ambitious new powers rewrite the hierarchy whenever they become rich, the world returns to competitive disorder.

### Ascendant argument

> The Convergence was supposed to create fair rules, not freeze yesterday's winners permanently on top. If our cities, guilds and people now do the work, why should inherited capitals keep the prestige and authority?

Both arguments can be sincere.

Both can also be excuses for self-interest.

That is probably the correct level of ideological coherence.

The spheres do not need manifestos.

They need a recognizable disagreement about **whether the post-Convergence order is a living framework or a protected hierarchy**.

# Story consequences

This model gives the Villain much better material.

He can make unrelated events look like proof of sphere aggression:

- Blackbellow recruits during an Ironcrest strike → Crown calls it economic sabotage.
- Summithold tightens route rules after caravan attacks → Cliffhaven calls it protectionism.
- Sunplains patrons rescue distressed Greenvale farms → Charter politicians call it foreign capture.
- Aurorashore expands convoys → Ascendant merchants call it a bid for maritime control.
- Port gives refuge to defectors → both sides accuse it of helping the other.

The Villain does not invent either interpretation.

He only selects the moment when people are most willing to believe it.

Do not lock these yet:

- exactly which Sunplains city-states become Ascendant leaders;
- whether Aurorashore's central leadership is firmly Charter or increasingly divided;
- the historical event that made the Ascendant network self-conscious;
- the first famous defection;
- whether ordinary people use one shared label for each sphere or several competing names;
- how much the Economic Council quietly shaped sphere balance.

The next discriminating question is:

**What event turned several separate challenger disputes into one recognized movement?**

The old provisional **Merchant Guild War** is one candidate.

Other possibilities should be tested against the Convergence history before choosing it.
