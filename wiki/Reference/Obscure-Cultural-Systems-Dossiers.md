# Obscure Cultural Systems — Expanded Research Dossiers

## Scope and evidence

**NON-CANON comparative research, researched 2026-11-04.** This file adds source-backed notes to the preexisting [Cultural Practices and Institutions](Cultural-Practices-and-Institutions.md), [Community Institution Casebook](Community-Institution-Casebook.md), and [Nomadic Peoples Research](Nomadic-Peoples-Recovered-Research.md). It covers **17** specific practices and institutions, from living communities across different geographies. Links provide institutional descriptions; these are *starting sources*, not exhaustive ethnography, community consent, or proof of uniform practice.

**Read these as local systems, not traits of whole ethnic groups.** Each dossier distinguishes observed context, function, social dependencies, a worldbuilding question and safeguards. Two Sons questions are hypothetical and **cannot establish canon**. Do not copy sacred texts, restricted techniques, recognizable designs, songs, or named historic events. The repositories' current [Culture owner](../Culture/Culture-and-Influence.md) and [World Rules](../World-Rules.md) govern later decisions.

## Source-grounded dossiers

### 1. Aka contrapuntal polyphony

**Community/place:** Southwestern Central African Republic.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** In several Aka communities, contrasting vocal lines and interlocking responses are learned in collective life and performed in social and ceremonial settings. UNESCO describes improvisation, dance and early learning through participation.

**Mechanism worth studying:** The group can be a flexible memory-and-coordination system; no single singer owns the whole performance.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** Who is allowed to start? Who teaches new singers? What happens when a settlement disperses and crucial vocal parts are absent?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** Avoid UNESCO's outdated generalized label as a category for multiple peoples; never assume all Central African forest communities share the practice.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/polyphonic-singing-of-the-aka-pygmies-of-central-africa-00082).

### 2. Corsican cantu in paghjella

**Community/place:** Corsica, France.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** Three ordered vocal registers, performed in local, sacred and social occasions, have historically been transmitted through observation and participation. Emigration can weaken transmission when the ordinary spaces for singing disappear.

**Mechanism worth studying:** An art survives because recurring occasions create apprentices, not just because someone preserves a score.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** Which tavern, workshop or community assembly routinely produces future performers? What if the group starts touring without local learners?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** Do not treat male historical participation as an essential rule for another culture; do not copy liturgical repertoire.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/cantu-in-paghjella-polyphonic-singing-of-corsica-00315).

### 3. Semeiskie oral culture

**Community/place:** Transbaikal, Russia.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** Communities descended from Russian Old Believers displaced into Siberia maintained religious practices, household arts, regional language influences and polyphonic singing, while encountering social and economic change.

**Mechanism worth studying:** A displaced group can become distinctive through shared institutions and selective change rather than staying a frozen copy of an origin country.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** What survives after migration: a family calendar, school, language register, construction tradition or reputation for mutual aid? What changes first?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** UNESCO's description is a historical snapshot; do not assume a single uniform Old Believer identity.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/cultural-space-and-oral-culture-of-the-semeiskie-00017).

### 4. Yaaral and Degal

**Community/place:** Inner Niger Delta, Mali.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** Peul pastoralists mark seasonal river crossings with gatherings featuring herders' poetry, decorated cattle and meetings with farming and fishing communities. Timing responds to river level and grazing conditions.

**Mechanism worth studying:** An event can be a calendar, animal market, cross-group peace mechanism and public archive of migration.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** Which floodplain crossing holds negotiated obligations among herders, growers, boatmen and toll authorities? Who loses if the river changes course?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** Not all Fulani practice these festivals; do not detach them from named river ecology or inter-community history.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/cultural-space-of-the-yaaral-and-degal-00132).

### 5. Mongolian camel coaxing

**Community/place:** Mongolia.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** Experienced herders use a skilled combination of vocal performance, patient handling and attention to the mother camel's response when encouraging acceptance of a newborn or orphan calf.

**Mechanism worth studying:** An expert's seemingly artistic talent can be practical husbandry, a scarce service and a social bond between households.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** What difficult animal or crop process requires rare intergenerational skill? When is a specialist borrowed and what compensation is customary?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** Do not claim the music has guaranteed effects, or transform the ritual into magical mind control.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/USL/coaxing-ritual-for-camels-01061).

### 6. Sanké mon collective fishing

**Community/place:** San, Mali.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** An annual community fishing event associated with the founding of the town joins water rites, coordinated harvesting and public performance; UNESCO notes risks from declining lake conditions and attendance.

**Mechanism worth studying:** A seasonal harvest can allocate community access, renew founding memories and link ecological health to civic ritual.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** What communal resource is used only at certain times, and who decides when harvesting is permitted? What if it dries up?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** Some elements are sacred and community-specific: extract governance questions, not masked dances or offerings.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/sanke-mon-collective-fishing-rite-of-the-sanke-00289).

### 7. Kyrgyz akyn epic tellers

**Community/place:** Kyrgyzstan.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** Epic performers combine recitation, composition, memory, voice and performance at gatherings; masters teach younger narrators. Epics can contain remembered history and moral disputes.

**Mechanism worth studying:** Historical memory can travel with specialized performers who improvise while preserving recognizable story frameworks.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** What is lost when an itinerant narrator dies? How do competing performers relate to local authorities and archives?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** Do not directly copy the Manas cycle or infer that an oral epic is a literal chronicle.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/fado-00065).

### 8. Kazakh/Kyrgyz Aitysh or Aitys

**Community/place:** Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** Two performers publicly improvise witty, musical exchanges; audience choices and social topics shape the contest. Participation has expanded beyond its historically male performers.

**Mechanism worth studying:** Public art can be a recurring mechanism for criticism, reputation and informal political accountability.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** Could a market contest embarrass officials without possessing formal legal authority? Who controls the performance venue?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** Do not equate artistic competition with a universally safe or unrestricted 'free press'.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/aitysh-aitys-art-of-improvisation-00997).

### 9. Sosso-Bala custodianship

**Community/place:** Nyagassola, Guinea.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** An important balafon is kept in the care of a specific family and guardian, with performance and teaching bound to a historical lineage and ceremonial setting.

**Mechanism worth studying:** Custodianship links objects, authorized expertise, family claims and public memory; ownership is not always simple commodity ownership.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** How does a council resolve a contested artifact's custodianship when a hereditary bearer and a public institution disagree?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** Sacred guardianship and particular epic repertoire are not transferable design assets.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/cultural-space-of-sosso-bala-00009).

### 10. Wooden movable-type family genealogies

**Community/place:** Rui'an, Zhejiang, China.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** Specialized teams traditionally traveled with reusable wooden type to compile and print clan genealogies, combining craft, historical literacy, apprenticeship and preservation ceremonies.

**Mechanism worth studying:** A record-making trade can move between households, control access to ancestry evidence and build reputational power.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** Who certifies inheritance or descent when the recorders move among villages? Who may challenge a missing name?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** The UNESCO entry contains historical gender-role descriptions; do not project them as universal or immutable.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/USL/wooden-movable-type-printing-of-china-00322).

### 11. Mahadra community education

**Community/place:** Mauritania.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** Tent-based education transmits oral literature, religious study, navigation, plants and other knowledge through teachers, listening, memorization and community relationships.

**Mechanism worth studying:** A school can be mobile, low-infrastructure and connected to survival knowledge, without being lesser than a stone academy.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** Where do different households send apprentices? Who feeds teachers and learners? Can a young traveler bring knowledge home?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** Do not transplant Islamic religious teachings or erase contemporary educational variation.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/mahadra-a-community-system-for-transmission-of-traditional-knowledge-and-oral-expressions-01960).

### 12. Hezhen Yimakan storytelling

**Community/place:** Heilongjiang river systems, northeastern China.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** Hezhen stories mix prose and verse and preserve accounts of heroes, river life and locally significant knowledge. UNESCO's 2025 listing documents safeguarding and renewed transmission.

**Mechanism worth studying:** An art form can carry work ecology, names and community identity beyond its surface plot.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** If the last skilled storyteller in a river village relocates, what fragments remain in fishing sayings, apprenticeship and local testimony?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** Do not imply all knowledge is preserved unchanged or reproduce community stories as fantasy texts.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/hezhen-yimakan-storytelling-02224).

### 13. Wooden arch bridge craft

**Community/place:** Fujian and Zhejiang, China.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** Experienced builders use interlocking carpentry and locally adapted engineering; bridges also operate as gathering places and links among communities. Craft transmission depends on mentoring and raw materials.

**Mechanism worth studying:** A bridge can be technology, classroom, place of assembly and disputed responsibility.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** When a bridge is repaired, who authorizes timber use, maintains passage rights and receives credit for skilled work?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** Do not infer that the specific beam-weaving design can be copied without engineering.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-design-and-practices-for-building-chinese-wooden-arch-bridges-02156).

### 14. Chinese junk watertight bulkheads

**Community/place:** Fujian shipbuilding, China.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** Shipwrights developed compartmented hull construction so some flooding could be contained. The practice entails specialist carpentry, marine construction and training.

**Mechanism worth studying:** Safety technology changes who bears shipping risk, who trusts captains, and how merchants finance journeys.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** Could two shipbuilding traditions coexist with different accident reputations, insurance-like agreements and repair costs?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** Exact engineering requires separate technical evidence; avoid implying invulnerability or anachronistic industrial manufacturing.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/lists?RL=00321).

### 15. Manden Charter oral tradition

**Community/place:** Mali / Upper Niger historical memory.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** A charter associated with early Mali Empire traditions is transmitted and commemorated orally. Its remembered teachings address rights, social relations and responsibilities; dating and interpretation require historical care.

**Mechanism worth studying:** Public law can have multiple carriers: ceremonial recitation, family knowledge, written records and local practice.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** What if the public version of an old charter contradicts a landholder's archive? Who can summon a respected reciter to arbitrate?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** Do not treat UNESCO's historicity framing as uncontested proof of a modern-style constitution; avoid a direct copy of legal articles.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/manden-charter-proclaimed-in-kurukan-fuga-00290).

### 16. Ngondo among the Sawa

**Community/place:** Cameroon, Wouri River.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** Annual practice includes public activities involving districts and waterways, alongside sacred elements transmitted through initiation and mediated by authorized specialists.

**Mechanism worth studying:** A shared river event can connect many neighborhoods while different kinds of knowledge remain public and restricted.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** How can a recurring civic event gather petitions from different river users, and why might officials fear its public attention?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** Sacred rites and oracle messages are community-specific; do not convert their detailed ceremonies into exotic spectacle.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ngondo-worship-of-water-oracles-and-associated-cultural-traditions-among-the-sawa-02140).

### 17. Kyrgyz, Kazakh and Karakalpak yurt craftsmanship

**Community/place:** Central Asia.

**Observed practice / institutional context:** Collapsible wooden frames, felt, ropes and interior textiles are produced by different trained specialists and transmitted through families or apprentices; dwelling-making connects craftsmanship and family memory.

**Mechanism worth studying:** Mobility needs whole chains of raw material, skilled labor, repair, storage and social cooperation.

**Two Sons design question (not canon):** What happens when a mobile household can no longer obtain one key craft input? Who rents, owns, repairs and inherits a shelter?

**Important exclusion / uncertainty:** Regional fabrication and household customs differ; do not borrow decorative motifs as mere identity shorthand.

**Source:** [Institutional reference](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-knowledge-and-skills-in-making-kyrgyz-kazakh-and-karakalpak-yurts-turkic-nomadic-dwellings-02284).

## Why these examples should NOT simply be combined

A tradition often exists because several pressures coincide: local ecology, work schedule, rights to resources, institutions, religious authority, material costs, apprenticeship, memory and historical rupture. Copying its visible expression but transplanting it into a different climate and social order breaks its causal logic.

The [Original Culture Generation Method](Original-Culture-Generation-Method.md) uses these cases to generate new societies **from a setting problem**. Its test is whether the same resulting institution could be justified even if the real-world analogue's proper name, costume, and ritual form were all removed.

- **Source-grounded:** The 17 practice summaries above have one named institutional source each; some entries are UNESCO nomination summaries, whose scope and chronology are not the same as independent long-term field studies.
- **Earlier casebooks:** Existing sourced research remains in its original owner files to avoid duplicating full treatments.
- **Open leads:** The old thread's other names still lack robust, community-specific sources. Do not label them “verified.”
- **Contemporary practice:** Heritage inscriptions often describe practices at inscription time. Do not read historical counts or risk assessments as current.
- **Sensitive practices:** Some sacred practices have restricted knowledge; learn about their institutions rather than replicating ritual content.
- **Next scholarship:** Community-produced accounts, ethnographies, dissenting practitioners' perspectives, class/gender/generation variation, technological change, and material costs.
