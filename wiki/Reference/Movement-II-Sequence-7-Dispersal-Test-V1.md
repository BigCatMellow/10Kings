# Prose test

Port did not become less crowded when people began leaving.

It became crowded in a different direction.

The roads toward the gates filled first.

Then the ferry landings.

Then the river quays.

People who had spent days fighting to enter the city now fought to get out of it with:

- trunks;
- contracts;
- samples;
- instruments;
- children;
- arguments they had not arrived with.

Wurdren left with papers.

This seemed appropriate.

The Longwood delegate had the originals in their leather case.

Wurdren carried one certified copy packet because the delegate had looked at him the previous evening and said:

> "If one bag goes missing, I want the argument to survive."

He had not asked whether that was a joke.

The route-side representative traveled with a separate copy.

That was more important.

Nobody could arrive home and claim the other side had changed the paper after Port.

At the departure yard, a clerk checked their names against a hired cart list.

The clerk looked at Wurdren's.

Then at him.

"Guild?"

"Sometimes."

"Which one?"

"Whichever one is paying."

The clerk did not find this useful.

The Longwood delegate said:

"Independent witness."

The clerk wrote something that probably meant:

> difficult to classify.

Wurdren preferred that.

Behind them, two men were carrying a rolled banner out of a guild office.

A third person stood in the doorway arguing with the landlord.

"We only need the front room."

"You said that seven days ago."

"Seven days ago we were leaving."

"Now?"

"We are leaving eventually."

The landlord named a monthly rate.

The guild representative swore.

The landlord named the same rate again.

Wurdren looked at the Longwood delegate.

"Permanent institution being born."

"Or bad judgment."

"Those are often related."

The delegate checked the cart straps instead of answering.

Across the yard, a group of North Coast sailors were loading sealed packets into a chest.

One of them held up a sheet.

"Harbor copy."

Another said:

"Escort office."

A third:

"Captain."

"Which captain?"

"Both."

"That's not how captains work."

"It is this week."

The first sailor folded the sheet.

"Contribution table?"

"Other packet."

"Command conditions?"

"Other other packet."

The sailor stared.

"How many agreements did we make?"

"Enough to sail."

That appeared to settle it.

The chest was closed.

No one cheered.

Wurdren watched it go toward the river.

The Longwood delegate noticed.

"You know them?"

"No."

"Then why are you watching?"

"I spent yesterday hearing people argue about escorts."

"And?"

"Apparently they finished."

"People do that sometimes."

The delegate climbed into the cart.

Wurdren considered telling them they sounded surprised.

He did not.

The road out of Port was slow.

Not because anyone had closed it.

Because everyone had chosen the same morning to possess a future somewhere else.

A family ahead of them had tied a chair to the back of a cart badly.

It fell off twice.

The second time Wurdren helped retie it.

The father thanked him.

The child did not.

The child was wearing a Netman mask.

The teeth had increased again.

"That thing is spreading," Wurdren said when he caught up.

The delegate looked at the child.

"What thing?"

"Never mind."

At the last major warehouse lane, Wurdren saw the Old Port Hand arguing with a supplier.

They did not see him.

The broken lift was still half-useful.

One arm moved.

The other did not.

A crew worked around it as though partial machinery were a weather condition.

Near the office door, a runner with a leather message case was speaking to a westbound carrier.

Wurdren recognized the case.

The procurement packet had not gone far yet.

That was somehow reassuring.

Not every important document escaped the city immediately.

Some of them waited for a wagon like everyone else.

---

The Records Worker did not see Wurdren leave.

They saw his paperwork leave.

The Longwood packet received:

- two certified copies;
- one acknowledgement of incomplete Port commercial abstract;
- one signed statement that the cutting question remained unresolved.

The worker checked the copy references, then passed the packet to outgoing registry.

On the next desk sat:

- the closed grain transfer;
- a reduced commercial extension;
- three warehouse-recognition changes;
- a notice from a guild branch asking how to register a permanent Port address.

The festival had produced more records than conclusions.

The worker preferred records.

Conclusions were harder to file.

A junior clerk appeared beside the desk.

"Do temporary offices need a permanent number if they sign a year lease?"

The Records Worker looked at them.

"Say that again slowly."

The junior clerk did.

The worker closed their eyes.

"Get the address first."

"They don't have one."

"Then they do not yet have a permanent number."

"They say they need the number to finalize the address."

The Records Worker opened their eyes.

"Of course they do."

The junior clerk waited.

The worker stood.

"Show me."

The grain papers remained where they were.

So did the two correspondent marks the worker had noticed the day before.

There would be time later.

Or there would not.

The queue did not care.

---

At a river quay farther north, a captain unfolded the temporary convoy schedule that had come out of Port.

The first thing they checked was not the route.

It was who had authority to delay departure.

The second thing was who paid if delay protected another harbor's ships.

The third was whether the schedule had actually been signed by everyone who claimed to have signed it.

It had.

That was irritating.

The captain handed it to the harbor clerk.

"Try it."

"For how long?"

"Until it stops working."

The clerk looked at the escort conditions.

"That could be tomorrow."

"Then tomorrow we argue again."

The next convoy left under the new schedule.

It did not get attacked.

Neither did the one after that.

People began saying:

> **the arrangement was working.**

Other people began saying:

> **the arrangement was becoming permanent.**

Both statements were premature.

Both were useful.

---

By midday, Port had lost enough visitors that the streets no longer felt impossible.

They still felt full.

The Old Port Hand finished a shift and discovered:

- one temporary worker had decided to stay;
- a rented room had not dropped its festival price;
- the north lift still needed pins;
- somebody had removed two of the temporary signs and left the posts.

They looked at the empty posts.

Then at the broken lift.

Then toward the road Wurdren had taken.

"Festival," they said.

Nobody nearby knew which problem they meant.

That was fine.

---

Outside the city, Wurdren's cart finally reached a stretch of road where the traffic thinned.

Port stayed visible behind them longer than he expected.

The delegate sat opposite him with the leather case under one hand.

"Do you always carry papers like that?" Wurdren asked.

"Like what?"

"As if they might run."

"People do worse things to papers than run."

"Fair."

The delegate looked past him toward Port.

"You like it."

"The city?"

"Yes."

"I complain about it."

"So does everyone who likes it."

Wurdren thought of the Old Port Hand.

"Apparently."

The delegate tapped the case.

"These are not going to solve it."

"I know."

"You say that quickly."

"I learned yesterday."

That earned the smallest possible nod.

The cart turned.

Port disappeared behind a low rise.

Wurdren felt the loss of it more than he expected.

Then the road ahead opened.

That felt familiar.

He settled back against the cart side.

Somewhere behind them:

- ships were leaving under new rules;
- clerks were making temporary things permanent;
- messages were moving west;
- somebody was probably still arguing over the price of a bed.

Wurdren did not know any of that.

He knew only:

> the people beside him had a dispute waiting at home, and he had said he would go.

For the moment, that was enough.

# V1 assessment

## Dispersal test

**PASS.**

The sequence makes outward movement material through:

- Longwood certified copies;
- maritime schedule packets;
- Stone Hills procurement message;
- guild lease / permanent-address problem.

## Port persistence

**PASS.**

Port does not reset after the festival.

It retains:

- high rents;
- broken infrastructure;
- workers who stay;
- offices that stay;
- records.

## Maritime containment seed

**PASS.**

The convoy arrangement begins working without claiming the constitutional issue is solved.

Important distinction survives:

> operational success ≠ settled legitimacy.

## Wurdren continuity

**PASS.**

He leaves because of:

- a specific promise;
- a specific dispute.

He does not leave because he has decoded the current-events board.

## Records Worker continuity

**PASS.**

Their later evidence function grows naturally through ordinary filing.

The two correspondent marks remain:

- noticed;
- not investigated.

## Main risk

The line:

> "Somewhere behind them: ships were leaving under new rules; clerks were making temporary things permanent; messages were moving west..."

briefly gives Wurdren / narration knowledge he does not possess.

The prose immediately corrects:

> Wurdren did not know any of that.

But this is still authorial summary.

A challenge should likely remove it.
