## Summary

**The security-dilemma turn works. The intervention should rely on information latency rather than another selective-document leak, and OC-U's readiness measure should remain more civilian / contract-bound.**

The sequence already succeeds at:

- making delay itself costly;
- producing precaution before certainty;
- creating reciprocal interpretation without requiring either side to want war;
- letting the Villain stop pushing once the branch becomes self-moving.

Three corrections improve it.

---

# 1. Distinguish the operation from Sequence 15

Sequence 15 already uses:

> restricted true record + timed disclosure.

Sequence 19 should use a different craft:

> **true commercial information outruns slower diplomatic clarification.**

The Villain does not need to suppress or conceal OC-C's reassurance.

Instead:

1. commercial contract overlap is already moving quickly through merchant / Port networks;
2. OC-C's diplomatic explanation moves through a slower official channel;
3. the Villain pays / encourages a normal commercial-intelligence contact to get the accurate overlap report to the most worried OC-U audience immediately;
4. he knows fuller context is coming later;
5. he wants OC-U to face the decision **before** it arrives.

That is manipulation of:

- timing;
- carrier;
- audience;
- network latency.

It is meaningfully different from Sequence 15.

---

# 2. Keep OC-U registration tied to existing relationships

V1's registration is close to a proto-mobilization census.

Narrow it.

OC-U can require or request information from actors it already has legal / contractual relationships with:

- city-chartered / licensed major warehouses;
- contractors already serving public works;
- merchants holding city emergency / supply contracts;
- carriers under city-linked agreements;
- publicly supported storage;
- major buyers voluntarily seeking future city guarantees.

It can ask for:

- available / already-committed storage;
- current contracted food;
- transport capacity under existing agreements;
- major repair dependencies.

Do not require a general count of:

- private wagons;
- all reserve animals;
- all shipping;
- all civilian assets.

This remains:

> **commercial contingency planning**

with later mobilization relevance.

---

# 3. Give the Villain a partial failure

The packet should **not** convince OC-U that OC-C is hostile.

A water / civic faction should insist:

- OC-U / OC-D technical cooperation continues;
- OC-C's contracts are not evidence of attack;
- the registration should explicitly avoid military / requisition interpretation.

This narrows the measure.

The Villain gets:

- better capacity legibility;
- reciprocal precaution.

He does **not** get:

- immediate Old Cities polarization.

That is important.

---

# 4. Preserve OC-C's reciprocal precaution

OC-C can still confirm part of future shipping before the fuller OC-U notice arrives.

This is plausible because:

> a commercial intermediary gives them a real deadline.

When the real notice arrives, OC-C discovers:

- rumor overstated it;
- but the underlying capacity planning was real.

Their earlier reservation therefore remains defensible.

---

# 5. Remove coined "protected counterparties"

The phrase is too neat and author-visible this early.

Let identity harden through ordinary language already established:

- our carriers;
- our guarantor;
- their buyers;
- their route.

No new label required.

---

# 6. Villain result

He should mark the branch:

> **SELF-MOVING**

but with a note / awareness that one intended interpretation failed.

Working result:

- OC-U did **not** treat OC-C as an enemy;
- OC-U did begin mapping contractual capacity;
- OC-C did preserve more shipping access;
- each action now becomes evidence for future caution.

That is enough.
