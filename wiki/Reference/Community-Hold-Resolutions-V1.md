# 1. SH-1 industrial mine district — migration / neighborhood / faith resolution

## Existing constraints

Stone Hills already supports:

- old mine / craft settlements;
- post-Convergence territorial consolidation;
- guild mobility;
- Low Rivers food / labor connections;
- Western Gate / High Roads movement;
- mine owners;
- workers;
- apprentices;
- religious / civic institutions.

Therefore migration does not need a new historical event.

The industrial district can emerge through **ordinary expansion over generations**.

---

## Working migration layers

### Layer 1 — older mine / craft households

Oldest surviving urban layer.

Families tied historically to:

- mine work;
- workshops;
- carting;
- food supply;
- local administration.

Their claims to seniority may matter socially.

They do not need to be wealthy.

Some old worker families may possess:

- reputation;
- guild access;
- housing rights;
- local burial plots;

while remaining materially modest.

---

### Layer 2 — internal Stone Hills migration during expansion

As mines / workshops scale up, workers arrive from:

- smaller valleys;
- upland farms;
- depleted / declining workings;
- nearby craft towns.

This creates:

- boarding districts;
- regional speech variation;
- kin recruitment;
- old-town resentment toward "new mine people."

This is likely the largest migration layer.

It avoids making industrial Stone Hills culturally dependent on outsiders.

---

### Layer 3 — cross-regional skilled / casual migration

Post-Convergence route stability makes longer movement easier.

Plausible arrivals include:

- High Roads skilled repair / transport workers;
- Low Rivers laborers during weak agricultural seasons / debt pressure;
- itinerant repair families temporarily settling;
- guild-trained specialists from other Stone Hills towns / Port.

No one migration group needs to dominate.

The point is:

> **the district has become a labor market as well as a mine settlement.**

---

### Layer 4 — current strategic-demand migration

Recent strategic orders / high wages attract:

- temporary labor;
- recruiters;
- skilled specialists.

This is small enough to be current-story pressure rather than deep culture.

It intensifies:

- rent;
- food demand;
- apprentice poaching;
- neighborhood friction.

---

## Working neighborhood history

### Old mine-road quarter

Likely contains:

- older worker / craft households;
- mutual-aid hall;
- older guild / faith buildings;
- mine memorials.

### Lower cart / boarding district

Grew around:

- transport;
- cheap lodging;
- casual work;
- migrant households.

More crowded / polluted / unstable.

### Workshop / skilled-labor belt

Contains:

- attached workshops;
- apprentices;
- repair services;
- technical schools / guild houses;
- mixed-origin skilled workers.

### Owner / administrative district

Cleaner water / air, larger housing, offices.

This need not be one walled elite quarter.

---

## Faith / institution test

Do **not** assign one mining faith.

Instead test several overlapping functions.

### Eternal Forge institutions

Plausible roles:

- apprenticeship ethics;
- craft discipline;
- repair;
- rebuilding after accidents.

Not every miner / smith belongs.

### Radiant Path institutions

Plausible roles:

- injury / widow / orphan charity;
- public moral arguments about safety / justice.

### Harmonious Path institutions

Possible:

- mediation;
- labor / neighborhood mutual aid;
- reconciliation.

### Other traditions

Can exist normally.

Especially:

- Cyclic Order burial / mourning;
- Great Weaver environmental / extraction concerns;
- Illuminated Circle technical learning.

The important point:

> religious service institutions overlap worker / guild institutions without replacing them.

---

## Myth result

The Knocking Below has multiple neighborhood meanings because the district itself has history.

- old mine-road families → inherited worker testimony;
- technical belt → acoustic / engineering explanation;
- boarding district → mixed imported versions;
- memorial halls → deaths / warnings preserved.

This is stronger than one "miners believe X."

---

## Resolution

SH-1 now has:

- migration history;
- neighborhood differentiation;
- institutional / faith pluralism.

**Disposition: P2-READY at community-type level.**

Exact settlement history remains open.

# 2. LR-4 floodplain settlement — concrete hydrology test

## Map constraint

Current map work establishes:

- G1 as the major Low Rivers trunk river;
- **G1-A** as the lower / tidal-to-floodplain transport zone upstream from Port;
- broad lower floodplain conditions;
- major bulk navigation;
- side channels / estuarine transition effects;
- Port downstream as a strong market.

## Working site

Test one LR-4 community on:

> **a G1-A side channel / backwater several ordinary river stages upstream from Port, above the strongest salt / tidal mixing but still inside the lower floodplain.**

This is deliberately:

- not Port suburb;
- not open delta;
- not upper-river market town.

The settlement occupies a **backwater / seasonal-overflow landscape** beside the main navigable trunk.

---

## Hydrology

Normal seasonal cycle:

### Lower-water period

- exposed banks / paths;
- more land usable for grazing / cultivation;
- side channel shallower;
- road / foot movement easier.

### Rising-water period

- side channels reconnect;
- boat movement increases;
- low paths disappear;
- fish / wetland productivity shifts.

### High-water period

- some lower fields intentionally inundated;
- certain structures / paths unusable;
- elevated storage / refuge matters.

### Receding-water period

- silt / wet soil creates planting opportunity;
- stranded pools / fish / disease hazards;
- boundaries may need re-marking.

This creates a community adapted to **expected change**, not merely emergency floods.

---

## Settlement form

Test:

- core homes on slightly higher natural levee / rise;
- lower work sheds / animal areas more sacrificial;
- elevated common storage / refuge;
- boat landings at multiple seasonal elevations;
- paths with alternate wet / dry versions.

Do not canonize floating gardens yet.

---

## Economic relation

The community can supply:

- vegetables;
- fish;
- animals;
- nursery plants / seedlings;
- wetland products

into G1 traffic and Port markets.

But its dependence on one side-channel connection creates transport risk when:

- water too high;
- water too low;
- channel silts.

---

## Political conflict

Possible local divisions:

### embankment faction

Wants:

- stronger flood protection;
- more stable fields;
- reliable road connection.

### seasonal-water faction

Depends on:

- fish;
- grazing;
- flood fertility;
- wetland products.

Too much embankment harms them.

Therefore:

> flood control is not automatically neutral improvement.

---

## Marsh Lure test

This exact hydrology gives the legend practical grounding.

A light seen where:

- yesterday's path existed;
- today a side channel has reconnected

can kill someone who assumes the landscape is fixed.

Local saying / exact name remains open.

The creature story may preserve:

> **water changes the map faster than memory does.**

---

## Resolution

LR-4 now has a concrete working hydrological type.

**Disposition: P2-READY as a G1-A lower-floodplain community type.**

Exact site / settlement name remain provisional.

# 3. M-1 portable repair network — home-base / legal-route test

## Geographic problem

The network needs:

- a real recurring home attachment;
- enough settlements needing periodic repair;
- legal / commercial access across jurisdictions;
- route variety;
- fairs / markets.

## Working branch test

Do not define the whole M-1 network.

Test **one branch** whose family bases lie in:

> **lower eastern Stone Hills / western approach country near the R1 Western Gate system.**

This location works because it is close enough to:

- Stone Hills craft / mine demand;
- High Roads route traffic;
- Low Rivers markets

without forcing the families to belong culturally to only one of them.

The exact settlement is not locked.

---

## Annual circuit — one branch

### Winter / deep bad-season base

Return to:

- kin households;
- stored tools / materials;
- marriage / family gatherings;
- repair of their own wagons / gear;
- training / song / story concentration.

Some family members may remain permanently.

### Spring — nearby farms / Stone Hills valleys

Demand:

- tool repair;
- harness / container repair;
- preparation for agricultural / transport season.

Legal basis:

- customary customer relationships;
- local market permission;
- guild reciprocity where relevant.

### Early / mid-summer — Western Gate / High Roads markets

Demand:

- caravan gear;
- tack;
- wagon fittings;
- portable household / merchant repairs.

Legal basis:

- temporary market license;
- recognized guild / craft credential for some specialists;
- contracted caravan work.

### Late summer / autumn — G1 head-of-navigation / Low Rivers market circuit

Demand:

- harvest tools;
- carts;
- containers;
- mill / household repairs;
- fair trade.

Return follows before worst mountain travel.

### Three Moons exception

Every seventh year, some households extend toward Port because:

- concentrated demand;
- guild contacts;
- buyers;
- family / social gathering.

Not every branch does.

---

## Legal relationships

This route deliberately uses **different access forms**.

### Home base

Ordinary residence / citizenship / household rights under whichever local polity the base belongs to.

### Stone Hills stops

- repeat customers;
- guild-recognized temporary work in some towns;
- informal work elsewhere.

### High Roads

- market permits;
- caravan contracts;
- corridor rules.

### Low Rivers

- fair / market permission;
- private customer invitation;
- possible local craft restrictions.

There is no universal traveling right.

---

## Cultural consequence

The network's identity comes from:

- repeated return;
- customer memory;
- kin;
- craft;
- song;

not placeless wandering.

A family may know the same:

- farm;
- inn;
- market yard;
- mine town

for generations.

---

## Road Hound test

This circuit gives the Hound a specific transmission path.

A ballad / story can travel:

~~~text
Stone Hills base
→ High Roads caravan yard
→ Low Rivers fair
→ back to winter base
~~~

Each place adds a verse / interpretation.

This is exactly the oicotype mechanism desired.

---

## Resolution

One M-1 branch now passes:

- home attachment;
- economic route;
- legal-route diversity;
- myth transmission.

**Disposition: P2-READY as a network model.**

Do not treat this one circuit as the only M-1 route.

# 4. M-2 pastoral circuit — seasonal route / herd ecology test

## Geographic anchor

Current High Roads political work already places HR-N around:

- North Shoulder roadhead;
- surrounding settled valleys;
- herding routes;
- local markets.

This is the strongest first test site.

## Working branch

One pastoral network uses:

- a **sheltered HR-N lower valley / shoulder settlement** as cold-season anchor;
- **higher northern / central High Roads pasture** in warm season.

The exact valley / pasture names remain open.

---

## Herd ecology

Working default:

> **mixed sheep / goat-like small stock with a smaller number of pack / larger grazing animals.**

Why this is safer than one exotic herd:

- current High Roads already supports sheep / goat / yak-like possibilities;
- small stock can use varied slopes;
- larger animals connect households to:
  - transport;
  - trade;
  - milk / fiber;
  - caravan work.

Do not canonize a yak analogue yet.

Use ecology first.

---

## Seasonal rhythm

### Cold season

Most household members concentrate near lower sheltered base.

Activities:

- stored fodder;
- household craft;
- market trade;
- births / marriages / teaching;
- equipment repair.

Some animals may be housed / closely managed.

### Spring ascent

Movement begins as:

- snow / ground;
- pasture;
- water

permit.

Not one date.

Different households leave at different times.

### Warm-season high pasture

Household may split:

- herding party moves;
- some members remain lower for:
  - crops;
  - elderly relatives;
  - market;
  - children / schooling.

### Autumn descent

Possible use of:

- post-harvest stubble;
- negotiated lowland grazing;
- market sales;
- fairs.

Return before dangerous weather.

---

## Access relations

At different points:

- inherited customary pasture;
- compact-recognized passage;
- leased grazing;
- water agreement;
- crop-residue bargain with farmers;
- kin-host relationship.

This creates route politics without a pastoral state.

---

## HR-N political consequence

Because herding routes cross:

- roads;
- markets;
- settled valleys,

HR-N government must negotiate among:

- merchants wanting wider / more predictable roads;
- herders protecting passage / water;
- farmers protecting fields.

Pastoralists are therefore ordinary political constituents, not outsiders.

---

## Weather Animal test

This is the strongest version of the legend.

Local observation:

- rare animal appears lower than expected;
- herd animals become restless;
- pressure / wind changes follow.

Interpretations:

> it predicts the storm.

versus:

> it is simply better at noticing the storm.

The practical response is the same:

> move.

This is exactly the desired natural/supernatural middle.

---

## Resolution

The M-2 circuit now has:

- real seasonal geography;
- herd logic;
- split household;
- access relations;
- political interface;
- myth role.

**Disposition: P2-READY as a pastoral network model.**

Exact animal species / route names remain later decisions.
