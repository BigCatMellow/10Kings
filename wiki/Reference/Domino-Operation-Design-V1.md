# Domino Operation Design — V1

## Status

**Active story-design method.**

This page strengthens the Villain's dominoes using:

- [Current Events](../Story/Current-Events.md);
- [Grounded Domino Design](Grounded-Domino-Design.md);
- [Crime and the Underworld](../Politics/Crime-and-Underworld.md);
- [Underworld Legacy Reconciliation](Underworld-Legacy-Reconciliation.md);
- the preserved legacy **Blacklist-style villain** design material.

The purpose is not to import the old villain roster.

It is to recover one useful design principle:

> **each major operation should have a distinctive craft, logic, and human footprint rather than feeling like the same manipulation repeated in a different region.**

---

# 1. Blacklist-style elements worth keeping

The legacy source repeatedly values:

- memorable introduction;
- complex motive;
- exceptional competence;
- connection to larger systems;
- moral code / self-justification;
- distinctive modus operandi;
- elaborate multi-layered schemes;
- recurrence / adaptation.

For the current story, apply those qualities to **operations**, not necessarily to one themed villain per region.

A strong domino should therefore answer:

1. What makes this operation recognizable?
2. What specialized competence does it require?
3. What legitimate system does it exploit?
4. What would the public initially think happened?
5. What does the Villain actually gain?
6. What evidence survives?
7. What moral line does the operation cross?
8. How can it recur or mutate later?

---

# 2. Distinct operational crafts available in the current world

## A. Selective truth / rumor brokerage

Legacy analogue:

- Whisperer;
- Sparrow's Court;
- information brokers.

Current grounded mechanisms:

- release a true document without its limiting appendix;
- circulate accurate route danger without confidence bounds;
- reveal a real payment while hiding the emergency purpose;
- give different audiences different true fragments;
- allow rumor to outrun verification.

Strength:

the operation remains dangerous even after the facts are confirmed.

---

## B. Market / debt engineering

Legacy analogue:

- Tax Collector;
- Merchant King;
- Coin Gambler.

Current grounded mechanisms:

- buy distressed claims;
- extend rescue credit selectively;
- corner storage / transport capacity;
- create liquidity at one point and scarcity at another;
- exploit debt covenants;
- redirect purchasing at a critical moment.

Strength:

nobody needs to believe a lie.

The market outcome itself creates political meaning.

---

## C. Contract / document manipulation

Legacy analogue:

- Glass Sculptor;
- Chain Broker;
- Painted Moon;
- forged-record networks.

Current grounded mechanisms:

- copied seals;
- altered schedules;
- missing annexes;
- duplicate contracts;
- forged authorizations;
- substituted manifests;
- stolen originals;
- contradictory authenticated copies.

Strength:

the world already depends heavily on records, jurisdiction, certification, and reputation.

A document can move more power than a weapon.

---

## D. Conflict brokerage

Legacy analogue:

- Iron Duke / Gavel of War;
- Ebony Doves.

Current grounded mechanisms:

- mediate while benefiting from unresolved conflict;
- sell security to both sides;
- pass warnings that make both sides mobilize;
- arrange a temporary solution that increases long-term dependency;
- ensure each actor sees the other's precaution as aggression.

Strength:

the Villain does not need to command either side.

He profits from rational defensive behavior.

---

## E. Logistics substitution

Legacy analogue:

- Caravan Phantom;
- Black Tapestry;
- Tallow Runners.

Current grounded mechanisms:

- warehouse substitution;
- false manifests;
- small repeated loads;
- Underpass handoffs;
- secondary roads / landings;
- direct producer-buyer links;
- cargo reclassification;
- trusted carriers outside dominant systems.

Strength:

this can build **replacement capacity**, not just cause disruption.

---

## F. Engineered accident / maintenance failure

Legacy analogue:

- Furnace Puppeteer.

Current grounded version:

- deferred maintenance exploited at the worst time;
- a repair delayed rather than a bridge magically destroyed;
- a safety rule quietly bypassed;
- an already stressed system pushed beyond tolerance;
- a replacement part / crew / inspection intentionally withheld.

Strength:

the event can initially look like ordinary institutional failure.

Use sparingly.

If every accident is sabotage, the world stops feeling alive.

# 3. Minimum-push rule

A Villain intervention should usually be **smaller than the consequence it eventually helps produce**.

Preferred pattern:

~~~text
small deliberate push
→ ordinary actor reacts
→ another system receives the cost
→ ordinary actor reacts
→ existing historical fear changes interpretation
→ another precaution
~~~

Avoid:

~~~text
Villain personally sabotages everything
→ everyone does exactly what he wanted
~~~

For most major branches, test:

> **If the Villain disappeared immediately after this push, would the next two or three dominoes still plausibly fall?**

If no, the intervention is probably too large or too dependent on mastermind control.

The conspiracy can still be enormous because it may contain:

- many agents;
- many payments;
- many reports;
- many small delays;
- many selective disclosures;
- many background observations.

But the *causal leverage points* should remain small.

---

# 4. Visibility split

Each domino has two versions.

## Author-side

The causal graph records:

- the real pressure;
- the Villain's exact nudge;
- the institutional response;
- the downstream effect.

## Reader / Wurdren side

The scene usually contains only:

- an inconvenience;
- a rumor;
- a changed price;
- a delayed shipment;
- a strange document;
- a nervous official;
- a repair crew;
- an empty inn;
- an unexpectedly armed convoy.

Do not reveal the plan merely because the author knows it.

The reader should first notice **repetition**, then **connection**, then **intent**.

---

# 5. Operation card

Every major domino should now have an operation card.

## Existing pressure

What was already moving before the Villain intervened?

## Desired next condition

What exact state does he need after the operation?

Not:

> cause chaos.

Instead:

> make NC-M adopt a convoy rule that raises Port risk costs.

## Distinctive craft

Which operational skill makes this move different from the others?

Examples:

- market engineering;
- record theft;
- route-intelligence manipulation;
- conflict brokerage;
- logistics substitution;
- maintenance sabotage.

## Minimal push

What is the smallest intervention that can plausibly produce the desired response?

## Visible story

What do ordinary actors think happened?

## Hidden strategic function

Why does this matter to the Port settlement plan?

## Independent beneficiaries

Who profits or advances their own agenda without being controlled by the Villain?

## Evidence residue

What survives that can later be recognized?

Examples:

- repeated handwriting / accounting convention;
- the same intermediary;
- matching payment timing;
- duplicated seal error;
- unusual cargo classification;
- common warehouse;
- identical risk language in unrelated contracts.

Avoid theatrical calling cards unless the world itself gives a reason.

The best signature is often an **operational habit**.

## Human cost

Who pays first?

## Wurdren entry point

What ordinary problem could bring him into contact with the operation before he understands it?

## Failure mode

How could the operation fail without requiring the Villain to be stupid?

## Moral threshold

What makes this move more ethically serious than the previous one?

# 6. Signature rule

The Villain should develop a recognizable strategic fingerprint across otherwise different operations.

Not:

> he leaves the same symbol everywhere.

Better:

> he repeatedly creates situations where the official record is technically true but the practical meaning is false because one crucial piece is missing.

or:

> he prefers to make institutions commit themselves publicly before releasing the information that changes how their decision is interpreted.

or:

> he builds a useful alternative before sabotaging confidence in the old system.

A consistent **decision pattern** is more credible than a decorative calling card.

---

# 7. Current recommended fingerprint

The strongest current fingerprint for the Port-centered Villain is:

> **He creates an asymmetry between what institutions know when they act and what the public learns afterward.**

Typical sequence:

~~~text
real pressure
→ institution receives selected / incomplete information
→ institution makes a defensible decision
→ decision creates a loser
→ missing context or hidden coordination is revealed later
→ the same defensible decision now looks corrupt / captured
~~~

This supports his political case without requiring fabricated evidence.

A secondary fingerprint is:

> **he tries to have a replacement channel ready before the official channel fails publicly.**

That makes his movement look competent rather than merely destructive.

---

# 8. Underworld use

Do not make the Villain personally execute every operation.

The current underworld material supports specialist services such as:

- forged marks / seals;
- manifest fraud;
- information brokerage;
- blackmail;
- smuggling;
- warehousing;
- conflict mediation;
- local loans;
- refugee movement;
- enforcement.

Use specialists for **capabilities**, not themed regional henchmen.

A fixer may work for the Villain once without becoming a loyal follower.

A smuggling network may move relief cargo and contraband for the same economic reason.

A document forger may never know the political consequence of the document.

This preserves compartmentation.

---

# 9. Escalation ladder for operations

## Level 1 — reveal

- buy information;
- expose records;
- help people route around a bad system;
- fund legitimate opposition.

## Level 2 — bias

- selectively release information;
- redirect contracts;
- time purchases / withdrawals;
- favor one route.

## Level 3 — deceive

- omit crucial context deliberately;
- forge a limited record;
- plant false route information;
- create a misleading transaction trail.

## Level 4 — endanger

- knowingly create conditions likely to cause violence / deprivation;
- redirect attackers;
- withhold safety information;
- trigger a preventable infrastructure failure.

## Level 5 — sacrifice

- allow or cause serious harm because the political effect is worth more than preventing the harm.

The Villain should not begin at Level 5.

The reader should be able to see the progression.

---

# 10. Domino quality gate

Before a domino is accepted, ask:

1. Could the underlying crisis exist without him?
2. Is his desired next condition precise?
3. Is this operation mechanically different from the previous one?
4. Does it exploit an established institution / route / market / right?
5. Does at least one actor benefit independently?
6. Is there a plausible visible explanation?
7. Is there evidence residue that can matter later?
8. Does Wurdren have a human-scale way into it?
9. Can it fail?
10. Does it serve the Port settlement strategy?
11. Does it move the Villain one step further down his moral slope?
12. Would the operation still make an interesting local story if the larger plan were removed?

If several answers are weak, the domino is not ready.
