# The world's structure, checked against the plan: reviser's pass, 2026-11-26

## Status

**Review record, not canon.** It follows the [plan check](Story-Plan-Logic-2026-11.md). bt 7274 hoped that check would "see some structural flaws in our organization of the world", then said "Continue" ([D57](../../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)). This pass looks at how the world is *organized*: what kinds of thing it has pages for, and what kinds it doesn't. It asks whether that organization supplies everything the saga's plan needs.

This is the reviser's own pass. B0's ninth test checks the same question independently ([B0](../../../briefs/B0-Domino-And-Story-Plan.md#the-nine-tests)).

What was read: [Kingdoms and Politics](../../Politics/Kingdoms-and-Politics.md), [The World as a System](../../World-System.md), [Peoples and Land](../../Peoples-and-Land.md), [Port](../../Places/Port.md), [The Convergence](../../History/The-Convergence.md), the six region pages and the [Character Roster](../../Story/Character-Roster.md) (searched by grep), and [Open Questions](../../Open-Questions.md). All are read against the plan as the [plan check](Story-Plan-Logic-2026-11.md) laid it out.

## Result

**The world is organized by region, but its story runs on states, a settlement and a calendar the world doesn't have yet.**

The wiki is rich wherever the stories stay small: peoples, places, trade, crafts, faiths, the sky. It's thin exactly where the main story turns. Five structural flaws:
1. no layer of actual polities;
2. a central settlement with no terms;
3. no time depth;
4. hidden actors with no visible traces;
5. world facts living only in story slates.

Every gap the plan check found traces back to one of them. None needs a page rewritten; four need new owner content that only bt 7274 can rule on. **The decisions are in *Questions*, the first two most of all.**

## The five structural flaws

### 1. "Region" does three jobs, and no page does the job of rule

D43 split the world into three layers that don't line up: land, people and rule ([Peoples and Land](../../Peoples-and-Land.md#three-layers-that-dont-line-up)).
- **Land** now has owners: [Geography](../../Geography-and-Connections.md), the [Gazetteer](../../Places/Gazetteer.md), [Sky](../../Sky-Seasons-and-Sea.md) and [Ecology](../../Ecology-Flora-and-Fauna.md).
- **People** has the six region pages.
- **Rule** has 416 words of "likely" and "could be" on [Kingdoms and Politics](../../Politics/Kingdoms-and-Politics.md), and nothing else.

There is no list of the actual states: how many there are, which ground each holds, who rules each, or who can make war or sign a treaty.

**What it breaks.** The plan's main story is about states:
- "a limited war at one border" (D28);
- "kingdoms distrust each other";
- "the guarantor nations" the Villain fights ([Villain](../../Story/Villain.md#relationship-to-the-council));
- "leaders [who] fear losing legitimacy" ([Kingdoms and Politics](../../Politics/Kingdoms-and-Politics.md#how-war-can-begin-against-council-wishes));
- the talks in Port.

**None of these actors exists by name or shape.** No region page names a ruler, a crown, or the seat of a government. The Character Roster has no ruler. The guarantor nations aren't listed anywhere. This is also why the plan check couldn't find the war's border (its Finding 4). After D43 there are no culture borders, and nothing has replaced them with political ones. The only border object in the world, the Treaty Stones, is deliberately unplaced.

**The repository's own name points at it.** The project is *10Kings*, and the world has no kings on the page. [Places of Interest](../../Places/Places-of-Interest.md#for-bt-7274) already asks whether the Sitting Kings at the Kings' Gate should number ten.

**What would fix it.** A rule layer, as a provisional owner page, called *Polities* or *Kingdoms*. For each state it gives:
- its form (crown, federation, city-state, clan league);
- the ground it claims, which can overlap D43's peoples;
- who decides war and treaties;
- its relation to Port and the Convergence.

The region pages keep their people and link to it. Kingdoms and Politics already has the right shape per region; it lacks the actual list. Ten crowned or titled rulers across the six regions would give the title its meaning, if bt 7274 wants that.

### 2. The settlement at the centre has no terms

The [Convergence](../../History/The-Convergence.md) is the treaty order everything else depends on: Port's neutrality, the guarantors, "recognized claims", route security. Its "exact signatories and treaty machinery remain open" ([World System §3](../../World-System.md#3-historical-glue-from-contested-frontiers-to-managed-interdependence)). Port's own charter is a list of what it "should … involve", and "local citizens" are on it ([Port](../../Places/Port.md#neutrality)).

**What it breaks.** The saga's ending changes exactly this structure: the Council forced into the open, and Port's people getting "a real voice" (D18, D32). The plan can't say what changes, because nothing says what Port's people have now. The charter already lists local citizens, so as written, the Villain's grievance is already partly answered. Finding 2 of the plan check (how a "regular dude" reaches six regions) and Finding 8 (who brokers the peace) both run into the same missing piece: who governs Port, and who stands behind its neutrality.

**What would fix it.** A minimum the story needs, and no more:
- who guarantees Port;
- what the Port-born have under the charter now (lean: courts and a name, but no vote on the board that governs the harbour);
- who would sit at talks in Port.

### 3. The world has no time depth

Nothing dates the Convergence, the last empire's fall, the Council's founding, the Ages of Silence or the Seven-year Blight. History gives their order, not their distance. Searching the wiki finds no "years ago", "generations" or "since the Convergence" anywhere that fixes one.

**What it breaks:**
- **Season 4's war.** Is it the first war in living memory, or one more? A "limited war" lands very differently each way.
- **The Council.** How long has it worked unseen? That decides whether its exposure is a scandal or the end of an age.
- **Port's people.** Whether they remember being the empire's city decides how the Villain's grievance feels.
- **The stories.** The grandmother's "fifty years" at the stall, and anyone old enough to remember, need something to measure against.

**What would fix it.** Three numbers: years since the Convergence, since Port was taken, and since the Council began. *Lean:* about two hundred years since the Convergence. Then nobody alive remembers a war between the kingdoms, the Season 4 war is the first in living memory, and the Council is many generations old, older than any family's memory of it.

### 4. Hidden actors have no visible traces

The stories show only the ground (D21). So every hidden actor needs a specification of what the ground sees.
- **The Villain** has one: cream paper, a ship's-lantern watermark, a phrase, offers that arrive and are refused.
- **The Council** has none. It is specified by its portfolios and houses, which the ground never sees.
- **The Villain's own people,** whoever carries his letters and pays for his workshop, have no page at all.

**What it breaks:** Season 2's "more than one hand" (Finding 3) and his reach (Finding 2).

**What would fix it.** A *What the ground sees* section on [Economic Council](../../Politics/Economic-Council.md) and on [Villain](../../Story/Villain.md): the ordinary, repeatable traces each one leaves, which no character ever connects.

### 5. World facts live only in story slates

Several things the main story depends on exist only as lines in [The Whole Road](../../Sunday-Morning/Notes/The-Whole-Road.md)'s slates:
- a Port-born mutual society that leases warehouses;
- requisitioned hulls;
- ships whose draught doesn't match their papers;
- the safe road the troops take.

The world pages don't record them. If a slate line changes, the world fact silently disappears, and pages written later won't know it was ever there.

**What would fix it.** Apply the [promotion rule](../../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Rules.md#promotion-rule) to the plan, not just to written stories. When a slate relies on a fact about the world, the fact goes on its world page as Provisional, with the slate as its source. The Port-born society would go on [Port](../../Places/Port.md).

## What's sound

The organization is right where the stories live:
- six peoples with their own textures;
- systems pages (law, money, couriers, records) that make small stories credible;
- a physical layer that holds;
- one owner per concept.

The flaws are all one level up: the actors and terms the main story moves between. That is the expected shape for a world built story-first, and it's cheaper to fill in now, while Seasons 3–5 are still slates.
