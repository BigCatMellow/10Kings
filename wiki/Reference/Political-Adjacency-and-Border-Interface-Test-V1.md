# 1. Border types

A single line on a future map should not hide several different relationships.

V1 uses five border / interface types.

## A. Recognized territorial border

A reasonably understood division between polities for:

- taxation;
- courts;
- military authority;
- ordinary land administration.

It can still contain local disputes.

## B. Fuzzy frontier

Authority weakens or overlaps across:

- forest;
- mountain;
- pasture;
- sparsely settled country;
- shifting settlement.

The frontier has no need to be lawless.

It is simply less line-like.

## C. Functional overlap

Territorial authority is clear enough, but a route, water system, fishery, or other institution crosses the boundary.

## D. Route interface

Two political systems meet because travelers, cargo, animals, or messages must pass through a specific corridor or transfer point.

The authorities may not share a long territorial border.

## E. Maritime-use interface

Political territory on land does not settle:

- fishing;
- anchorage;
- convoy;
- rescue;
- navigation.

This is especially important in North Coast and Port politics.

# 3. Stone Hills interfaces

## SH-A ↔ LR-A

**Type:** recognized territorial border with broad contact zone.

This frontier is required by:

- food moving west;
- tools and metal moving east;
- mixed foothill communities;
- older territorial exchanges;
- current dependency.

### V1 character

Do not make the whole border militarized.

A stronger model is:

- old recognized political line or set of jurisdictional markers;
- dense local crossing;
- mixed households;
- market roads;
- water and smoke disputes;
- land records that do not always match lived use.

Most days this should be a **boring working border**.

That makes any later closure politically significant.

### Implied contact-zone settlement

V1 requires at least one substantial foothill market / craft town near this interface.

It does not need sovereignty.

Its identity can become strongly local and mixed.

---

## SH-A ↔ HR-W

**Type:** route / mountain interface.

The Western Gate corridor creates a narrower political relationship than SH-A ↔ LR-A.

Important recurring issues:

- tolls;
- road maintenance;
- snow / landslide closure;
- guides;
- security;
- military passage;
- Underpass substitution.

### V1 character

The boundary can be geographically clearer because terrain concentrates traffic.

This is the strongest candidate for a historically fortified or administratively marked frontier.

It should still have ordinary commerce.

---

## SH-A ↔ LW-W

**Type:** fuzzy extraction frontier.

This is the border most likely to remain spatially untidy.

Pressure comes from:

- timber;
- charcoal / fuel;
- roads;
- mining support;
- migrants;
- settlements expanding into managed forest.

### V1 character

Expect:

- overlapping old claims;
- use rights crossing political jurisdiction;
- forest-edge villages with mixed identities;
- concessions that one side considers legal and another considers overreach.

This is a better current conflict zone than making every Stone Hills border contested.

# 4. North Coast interfaces

## NC-M ↔ NC-I

**Type:** maritime-use relationship plus uncertain constitutional link.

This is intentionally not reduced to a land border.

Important questions include:

- fishery access;
- winter anchorage;
- rescue;
- customs;
- convoy levies;
- mainland military demands;
- island local law.

### V1 character

The relationship should be politically routine enough to function but structurally ambiguous enough that crises reveal different assumptions.

NC-I may say:

> we cooperate because we agreed to.

NC-M may say:

> cooperation is part of the political order.

That disagreement can exist without constant rebellion.

---

## NC-R ↔ HR-N

**Type:** paired route interface.

This is one of the most concrete cross-polity boundaries in the current world.

It is already supported by the North Shoulder test.

### V1 character

The two roadheads should be distinct settlements with:

- different courts;
- different local taxes / fees;
- different political patrons;
- overlapping commercial relationships.

The corridor between them may include:

- pass authority;
- guide rights;
- shelters;
- seasonal checkpoints.

The border itself does not need to sit at the physical summit.

---

# 5. Low Rivers interfaces

## LR-A ↔ HR-C

**Type:** river-road / elevation transition.

This should not look like a hard line across open farmland.

The political change may become legible through:

- road jurisdiction;
- toll practice;
- local court reach;
- river navigation rules;
- upland versus lowland administration.

### Important distinction

LR-M is inside LR-A in V1.

HR-C is outside it.

The head-of-navigation market therefore becomes a place where Low Rivers authority is still strong but High Roads commercial and legal practice is increasingly present.

That is useful cultural bleed.

---

## LR-A ↔ LW-W / LW-I

**Type:** fuzzy territorial frontier plus watershed overlap.

The Low Rivers / Longwood boundary should vary.

Some stretches may be:

- cleared and settled;
- marked by old roads;
- controlled by estates.

Others may be:

- wetland;
- forest edge;
- river corridor;
- low-density settlement.

G2 makes ecological decisions cross the political line.

### Important result

The political border and watershed boundary should not coincide neatly.

That is what allows Longwood land-use decisions to create downstream Low Rivers consequences.

---

## LR-A ↔ OC-U

**Type:** recognized but culturally mixed agricultural transition.

The current world already supports a Low Rivers ↔ Old Cities contact zone.

V1 places the first major political interface between LR-A and OC-U.

### V1 character

Likely sources of ordinary friction:

- seasonal labor;
- irrigation versus rainfall farming;
- grain / orchard trade;
- water rights;
- tax treatment of migrants;
- marriage / inheritance across jurisdictions.

This border should produce a distinctive local culture.

It should not be a line separating two pure populations.

---

## LR-A ↔ Port

**Type:** special-jurisdiction estuary boundary.

This is one of the most important legal boundaries in the setting.

Port must be separate enough that:

- local law matters;
- outside authorities cannot simply police the city;
- treaty access is meaningful.

But the boundary also needs to allow:

- food;
- workers;
- carts;
- river craft;
- mainland services

to cross constantly.

### V1 character

The border should be politically sensitive and operationally routine.

It is a good candidate for:

- customs disagreement;
- ferry / bridge jurisdiction;
- warehouse boundary questions;
- extradition disputes later.

Do not turn it into a permanently closed checkpoint.

---

# 6. High Roads internal interfaces

## HR-N ↔ HR-C

**Type:** corridor relationship, probably fuzzy territorial frontier.

The exact political border can be weak because the route compact matters more than a perfectly surveyed line.

Recurring politics:

- maintenance contributions;
- tolls;
- herding rights;
- caravan security;
- route-status information.

## HR-W ↔ HR-C

**Type:** corridor relationship.

Similar logic applies, but the western route may be more militarily sensitive because it links toward SH-A.

### Important rule

HR-C should not own HR-N and HR-W merely because it is the largest switching / arbitration center.

If every dispute ends with HR-C dictating the answer, the networked High Roads model has collapsed into a capital-centered state.

---

## HR-C ↔ LW-I

**Type:** forest / upland contact frontier.

This interface is required by the existing Longwood ↔ High Roads contact zone.

It should support:

- forest goods entering caravans;
- guides;
- herding / forest-use conflicts;
- religious exchange;
- technical vocabulary exchange;
- road disputes.

The political border can remain fuzzy while the route itself is well known.

---

# 7. Longwood internal interfaces

## LW-W ↔ LW-I

**Type:** local-polity frontier with use-right overlap.

Do not assume a surveyed border through continuous forest.

Political distinction may follow:

- watershed;
- old settlement claims;
- hunting / cutting zones;
- river crossing;
- recognized meeting place.

## LW-I ↔ LW-S

**Type:** local-polity frontier / transition.

The southern polity should have greater routine exposure to:

- Old Cities markets;
- seasonal labor;
- southern road traffic.

LW-I should not automatically share those priorities.

### Important result

Longwood gets internal politics from geography rather than invented ideological factions.

---

# 8. Old Cities interfaces

## OC-U ↔ OC-D

**Type:** recognized political border plus shared-water jurisdiction.

This should be one of the clearest examples of:

> territorial separation + functional interdependence.

Their ordinary border can be stable.

The S1 water system cuts across it.

### V1 character

The current drought crisis does not require the territorial border itself to be disputed.

Conflict can arise because a recognized border fails to solve:

- water allocation;
- maintenance;
- upstream leverage.

That is stronger than another border war.

---

## OC-D ↔ OC-C

**Type:** ordinary city-state frontier / rivalry.

This border should be comparatively boring unless a later story requires otherwise.

The political value of OC-C comes from:

- independent diplomacy;
- M2 access;
- watching OC-U / OC-D coordination;
- merchant competition.

It does not need an active territorial dispute.

### Design rule

Not every rival needs disputed land.

Old Cities politics can be intense through:

- trade;
- prestige;
- alliances;
- water;
- patronage;
- league formation.

---

# 9. Port maritime interfaces

Port has important political relationships with actors it does not border territorially.

## P ↔ NC-M / NC-I

M1 creates:

- harbor access;
- convoy politics;
- rescue / shipping information;
- foreign merchant presence.

## P ↔ OC-D / OC-C

M2 creates:

- shipping;
- warehouse use;
- legal arbitration;
- migrant communities;
- merchant rivalry.

## P ↔ SH-A interests

M3 and western feeder routes create:

- metal traffic;
- imported food / goods;
- finance;
- alternative routing during Western Gate problems.

These should appear on a political map as **network relationships**, not fake borders across the sea.

# 11. Which borders should be politically boring?

V1 needs ordinary borders so every crossing is not a story hook.

Good candidates:

## SH-A ↔ LR-A

Mostly recognized.

Conflict usually concerns:

- trade;
- water;
- labor;
- local property.

Not territorial sovereignty.

## OC-D ↔ OC-C

Mostly recognized.

Rivalry is usually diplomatic / commercial.

## HR-N ↔ HR-C

Routine internal-High-Roads political separation.

Most travelers care more about:

- tolls;
- road condition;
- records

than ideological identity.

## LW-W ↔ LW-I

Often socially porous even when political authority differs.

### Why this matters

A world where every border is disputed feels designed for conflict rather than historically inhabited.

Some boundaries should be old enough that most people complain about paperwork, not sovereignty.

# 12. Which interfaces should remain politically sensitive?

## LR-A ↔ Port

Because neutrality and outside access depend on it.

## SH-A ↔ HR-W / Western Gate

Because route access and military movement matter.

## NC-M ↔ NC-I maritime authority

Because the constitutional relationship remains open and sea-use rights cross it.

## SH-A ↔ LW-W

Because extraction and settlement pressure change the physical frontier.

## LR-A ↔ LW-I watershed relationship

Because ecological effects cross the boundary.

## OC-U ↔ OC-D water relationship

Because drought reveals upstream leverage.

Sensitivity does not mean constant violence.

# 13. Convergence border test

The current topology now suggests several kinds of Convergence-era settlement problems.

## Recognized territorial claims

Likely needed for some version of:

- SH-A ↔ LR-A;
- LR-A ↔ OC-U;
- OC-U ↔ OC-D;
- OC-D ↔ OC-C;
- Port's mainland boundary.

## Attached route agreements

Likely needed for:

- NC-R ↔ HR-N / North Shoulder;
- SH-A ↔ HR-W / Western Gate;
- major Port approach rights.

## Attached resource / infrastructure agreements

Likely needed for:

- OC-U ↔ OC-D S1 water;
- cross-border watershed or river systems where later required.

## Maritime access

Likely needed for:

- Port;
- North Coast / Port shipping;
- possibly recognized harbor access.

### Important result

The Convergence can have solved different border problems with different instruments.

It does not need one universal border article.

**PASS.**

# 15. Main correction to Working Political Geography V1

The first topology diagram underemphasized one already-established contact zone:

> **SH-A and LR-A need a direct foothill political / social interface.**

They should not interact only through HR-W / Western Gate.

This is required by:

- the Stone Hills ↔ Low Rivers dependency;
- the foothill craft / farming contact-zone owner;
- pre-Convergence back-and-forth occupation of western lowlands.

Therefore V1 political geography should explicitly preserve both:

1. direct SH-A ↔ LR-A foothill contact;
2. SH-A ↔ HR-W Western Gate mountain-route contact.

Those are different relationships.

# 16. Strong adjacency conclusions

V1 now supports a political map where:

1. SH-A directly interfaces with LR-A, HR-W, and LW-W.
2. NC-M's main overland political connection runs through NC-R ↔ HR-N.
3. LR-A directly interfaces with SH-A, HR-C, Longwood edges, OC-U, and Port.
4. High Roads internal actors connect primarily through corridor relationships rather than one territorial hierarchy.
5. Longwood political geography follows several external interfaces rather than one national border.
6. OC-U and OC-D are territorially separate but functionally joined by S1.
7. OC-C can remain outside the shared-water system while participating in the same regional rivalry and maritime network.
8. Port's most important territorial adjacency is to the G1 / Low Rivers mainland, while many of its strongest political relationships are maritime rather than bordering.
9. Contact-zone towns are required by repeated movement but do not need independent sovereignty.
10. Several borders should be politically ordinary so the world does not become a map of permanent crises.

# 17. Remaining adjacency questions

Still open:

- whether SH-A and HR-W share a long border or only a narrow corridor;
- whether LR-A directly borders HR-W anywhere;
- whether HR-N touches any North Coast authority beyond NC-R's corridor;
- exact shape of LR-A ↔ Longwood interfaces;
- whether LW-W touches HR-W or HR-C directly;
- whether LR-A ↔ OC-U is a long frontier or narrow transition;
- whether OC-C borders OC-U as well as OC-D;
- Port's exact mainland footprint;
- which Underpass enclaves lie under, beside, or outside surface sovereignty;
- exact maritime-use boundaries around NC-I;
- exact treaty-recognized lines.

These should be resolved only when physical geometry or a concrete historical event requires them.

# 18. Next highest-value pass

The political topology now has actors and required interfaces.

The next useful step is:

> **political history of the borders**

For each major interface, ask:

- what existed before the current border;
- why the current line or relationship won;
- what older claims survived;
- what the Convergence recognized;
- which border customs developed afterward;
- which old institutions outlived their original purpose.

That will let final political geography feel inherited rather than drawn at one historical moment.
