# 1. G1 — river name must predate LR-A

## Existing constraints

G1:

- rises toward High Roads / The Spine-facing uplands;
- crosses the Low Rivers basin;
- supports settlement, farming, mills, crossings, and bulk navigation;
- reaches Port's estuary;
- predates the current LR-A political structure.

## Strongest naming rule

The river should **not** be named after LR-A.

The causal order is the reverse:

> river  
> → settlement / basin identity  
> → later territorial administration

The G1 hydronym should therefore be one of the oldest durable names in the eastern lowlands.

## V1 historical source

The best source is an **older R1 or pre-R1 hydronym attached to the river as a whole or its lower navigable course**.

Its original meaning may have been ordinary:

- broad water;
- long water;
- main river;
- slow river;
- another directly observed feature.

The exact meaning may be partly opaque now.

## Naming consequence

Later actors can derive:

- district names;
- basin labels;
- political shorthand;
- settlement names

from the river root.

This makes G1 the strongest plausible source for LR-A's eventual territorial name.

## Result

**SOURCE READY; LEXICAL ROOT STILL OPEN.**

A river name can be generated next without deciding LR-A's constitution or capital.

# 2. NC-M — political center should be a sheltered mainland harbor

## Existing constraints

NC-M is:

- mainland-centered;
- maritime;
- dependent on harbors / crews;
- connected to inland food / road systems;
- historically built from recurring maritime coordination.

Its political center needs to make that history plausible.

## Alternatives

### Largest fishing port

Too narrow.

A state center cannot be explained only by one fishery.

### Inland royal stronghold

Possible but weakens the maritime-compact origin.

### Sheltered mainland harbor with inland access

Best fit.

It combines:

- defensible / reliable anchorage;
- ship repair;
- market;
- access to inland valleys / food;
- communications along the coast;
- ability to host multi-harbor meetings.

## V1 site history

NC-M's principal political center begins at a **well-sheltered southern or central mainland bay / inlet with a reliable freshwater outlet and practical road connection inland**.

### Stage 1

Harbor settlement:

- fishing;
- coastal trade;
- repair;
- inland exchange.

### Stage 2

Repeated defense / rescue coordination makes it a common meeting port.

### Stage 3

Records, contributions, convoy planning, and foreign envoys accumulate there.

### Stage 4

As NC-M's compact becomes durable, the harbor becomes the ordinary central political seat without needing to govern every other harbor directly.

## Naming consequence

The center's oldest name should derive from:

- bay / inlet;
- freshwater stream;
- headland;
- older lineage / settlement.

The **polity** might later take:

- the harbor's name;
- a compact name;
- a ruling-office / house name.

Do not decide which until the compact's constitutional history is more specific.

## Result

**CENTER SOURCE READY; POLITY-NAME RELATIONSHIP STILL OPEN.**

# 3. SH-A — ruling seat should not be Kavel

## Existing constraints

SH-A is:

- composite;
- post-Convergence;
- larger than one industrial center;
- built by incorporation / succession among older jurisdictions.

Kavel is an important industrial civic jurisdiction, not necessarily the central seat.

## What the seat needs

It should give a ruling authority reasons to survive before modern industrial consolidation.

Strong needs:

- defensible site;
- agricultural / tax hinterland;
- road access to several western valleys;
- enough distance from one mine interest to bargain among them.

## V1 site history

The strongest site is a **raised confluence / valley-junction settlement in the lower western uplands**, where:

- two or more valley routes meet;
- reliable water exists;
- surrounding land can feed a court / town;
- upland mining districts are reachable;
- eastbound routes are reachable without making the seat itself a mine city.

### Stage 1

Local valley stronghold / market at a confluence.

### Stage 2

A ruling house / jurisdiction gains influence because it can mediate / tax movement among several valleys.

### Stage 3

Marriage, inheritance, protection, and debt bring nearby jurisdictions into a larger political orbit.

### Stage 4

Post-Convergence consolidation turns the seat into SH-A's central court / administrative center.

## Naming consequence

The seat's old name should derive from:

- confluence;
- raised ground;
- older lineage;
- valley junction.

SH-A may eventually take:

- this seat's name;
- ruling-house name;
- older territorial label.

### Important result

Kavel remains politically important without becoming the capital by default.

## Result

**SEAT SOURCE READY; FINAL SEAT AND POLITY NAMES OPEN.**

# 4. OC-D — lower-basin city should grow from crossing + managed water

## Existing constraints

OC-D is:

- downstream of Naret / OC-U in the S1 shared system;
- heavily dependent on reliable flow;
- densely irrigated;
- market-oriented;
- potentially near coastal access.

It needs a founding site distinct from Naret's spring / terrace origin.

## Alternatives

### Delta port

Would overemphasize maritime identity and blur OC-C's coastal role.

### Canal city built from scratch

Makes later water politics too intentional.

### Lower-basin crossing on an older managed channel / river

Best fit.

## V1 site history

OC-D begins at a **firm crossing / raised bank where the lower river or major managed channel can be crossed before the wetter / more divided lower basin**.

### Stage 1

Crossing settlement supports:

- local market;
- agriculture;
- travelers;
- water access.

### Stage 2

Irrigation branches expand downstream and around it.

### Stage 3

The settlement becomes the practical center for:

- lower-basin maintenance;
- markets;
- storage;
- legal disputes.

### Stage 4

Civic sovereignty consolidates around the town.

### Stage 5

Political boundary with Naret / OC-U later cuts across older shared water infrastructure.

## Naming consequence

The oldest name should derive from:

- crossing;
- firm bank;
- local channel / river;
- old lineage.

It should not mean:

- downstream;
- rival of Naret;
- lower irrigation capital.

## Result

**SITE SOURCE READY FOR S1 LEXICAL TEST.**
