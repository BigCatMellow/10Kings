# Stone Hills Polity Vertical Slice — V1

## Status

**Provisional polity test — not final canon.**

This page tests the SH-A / SH-M actors introduced in:

- [Working Political Geography — V1](Working-Political-Geography-V1.md)
- [Stone Hills Functional Governance Test](Stone-Hills-Functional-Governance-Test.md)
- [Political Border History Test — V1](Political-Border-History-Test-V1.md)

It does not establish:

- polity name;
- ruler title;
- dynasty name;
- capital;
- exact territorial border;
- exact succession law;
- exact charter language;
- named offices;
- exact mine / guild legal code.

The purpose is to answer:

> **Can one actual Stone Hills polity be made concrete enough to govern, tax, fight, negotiate, and survive crisis without erasing the independent institutions that make Stone Hills function?**

---

# 1. Starting constraint

Stone Hills is now more territorially consolidated than its pre-Convergence past.

But it still depends on:

- mine holders;
- skilled workers;
- guilds;
- towns;
- lenders;
- food imports;
- forests / fuel;
- roads;
- local legitimacy.

Therefore the strongest V1 solution is neither:

- fragmented mine lords with no meaningful state;

nor:

- an absolute ruler who directly administers every mine and workshop.

V1 tests:

> **SH-A as a post-Convergence composite territorial polity.**

Its central authority became stronger over time by incorporating, recognizing, bargaining with, and sometimes subordinating older jurisdictions rather than replacing them all with one bureaucracy.

---

# 2. Historical formation model

## SUPPORTED

Pre-Convergence Stone Hills contained:

- mine holders;
- fortified towns;
- valley rulers;
- merchant families;
- warbands;
- craft alliances.

Modern Stone Hills is more consolidated.

The Convergence recognized practical political claims without creating one state.

## PROVISIONAL V1 sequence

~~~
fragmented western authorities
→ expensive resource / route conflict
→ Convergence recognizes several practical western claims
→ trade / route stability rewards authorities that can coordinate larger areas
→ one territorial ruler / house accumulates adjacent jurisdictions through:
   - marriage;
   - inheritance;
   - debt assumption;
   - negotiated submission;
   - protection agreements;
   - arbitration;
   - limited warfare;
→ older towns / mine rights survive inside the expanding polity
→ SH-A emerges as the strongest western territorial polity
~~~

This makes SH-A a product of the post-Convergence world rather than an ancient ethnic kingdom.

---

# 3. Convergence succession problem

The strongest V1 historical model is:

> **The modern SH-A polity may be the successor to several western Convergence-era authorities rather than one original signatory.**

This creates useful legal consequences.

A town may say:

- our privileges predate SH-A;
- our predecessor signed directly;
- the central ruler inherited obligations, not unlimited authority.

The ruler may answer:

- SH-A is the recognized successor to those predecessor powers;
- their external rights now belong to the central polity;
- local privileges survive only within that larger succession.

This produces real constitutional politics from history.

### Important limit

Do not yet canonize:

- how many predecessor signatories existed;
- whether SH-M was one of them;
- exact succession documents.

The mechanism is the useful part.

---

# 4. Central authority

SH-A requires one recognized central political authority capable of:

- diplomacy;
- military mobilization;
- major taxation;
- serious criminal enforcement;
- major-road policy;
- strategic-metal regulation;
- cross-district arbitration;
- Convergence representation.

V1 does not require a modern cabinet or ministry system.

The central ruler can govern through:

- household / court officers;
- appointed district officials;
- negotiated local elites;
- chartered towns;
- recognized mine / guild institutions.

The polity should feel administratively uneven.

---

# 5. Succession legitimacy

The legacy reconciliation preserves a useful mechanism:

~~~
dynastic claim
+
institutional confirmation
→ legitimate succession
~~~

V1 tests this as the strongest Stone Hills fit.

## Why pure hereditary succession is too simple

SH-A is composite.

A ruler who inherits the central title still needs cooperation from:

- major territorial districts;
- important towns;
- military elites;
- large mine / land interests;
- institutions whose recognition affects tax / production / roads.

## Why full election is also unnecessary

The polity can still expect the ruler to come from a recognized ruling house or narrow dynastic pool.

## V1 model

A successor normally needs:

1. credible dynastic / inherited claim;
2. formal recognition or confirmation by a set of major political jurisdictions / institutions.

The confirmation body need not be a sovereign parliament.

It may exist mainly to certify:

- lawful succession;
- inherited obligations;
- continuity of charters;
- tax / military expectations.

### Failure condition

If confirmation becomes automatic ceremony with no ability to reject a disastrous claimant, it adds nothing.

If it can routinely govern ordinary policy, it risks becoming an invented national legislature.

V1 keeps its function narrow.

---

# 6. Territorial administration

SH-A should not use one uniform local-government template.

Possible internal units include:

- old valley jurisdictions;
- chartered towns;
- mining districts;
- rural estates;
- market towns;
- forest-edge districts;
- directly administered royal / central lands.

Different units can owe the center different combinations of:

- tax;
- troops;
- road labor;
- mine royalty;
- customs;
- negotiated annual contribution.

This unevenness is a feature.

It records how consolidation happened.

---

# 7. Fiscal base

The central polity needs enough revenue to be real.

V1 supports a mixed fiscal base.

## Land / household revenue

Agricultural and settled districts provide some regular taxation or obligation.

## Mine / extraction revenue

The ruler may receive some combination of:

- royalty;
- lease payment;
- production share;
- licensing fee;
- tax on sale.

This does not require central ownership of every mine.

## Road / market / customs revenue

Major corridors and external trade can provide:

- toll;
- customs;
- market fees.

## Chartered contributions

Some towns may owe negotiated fixed or formula-based payments rather than ordinary district taxation.

## Extraordinary levies

War, mine disaster, road collapse, or food emergency can create special demands.

### Political consequence

Because the central state depends on several revenue streams, no single sector is identical to state power.

A mine strike can hurt the ruler without immediately bankrupting the polity.

A food shock can weaken tax collection even when metal output remains high.

---

# 8. Mine rights

V1 should avoid one simple category called mine ownership.

At minimum distinguish:

## Surface / land authority

Who controls the land above the deposit?

## Extraction right

Who possesses the recognized right to remove ore?

## Operating control

Who actually runs:

- shafts;
- workers;
- equipment;
- safety;
- storage?

These can belong to different actors.

### Why this matters

A central ruler may recognize an extraction right without owning the operation.

A town may control streets / housing around a mine without owning the seam.

A mine holder may operate underground without controlling the forest needed for fuel.

This preserves the Stone Hills distinction between sovereignty and productive control.

---

# 9. Strategic-metal power

SH-A requires some ability to intervene in metal flows.

V1 gives the center authority to attempt:

- emergency requisition;
- export restriction;
- inspection;
- military procurement;
- licensing of some strategic contracts.

But this authority is not self-executing.

It depends on:

- records;
- town officials;
- mine owners;
- guild knowledge;
- warehouse access;
- road control.

## Compensation and legitimacy

A strong V1 rule:

> **Emergency seizure is politically easier to sustain when the state recognizes compensation / debt rather than treating all metal as ruler property.**

Otherwise every strategic-metal measure becomes indistinguishable from confiscation.

Exact compensation law remains open.

---

# 10. SH-M — major industrial civic jurisdiction

SH-M is not merely the place where mines happen.

It is a real city / district with:

- households;
- markets;
- local infrastructure;
- workshops;
- warehouses;
- guild halls;
- poor districts;
- merchants;
- civic institutions.

V1 gives SH-M meaningful local authority over:

- streets;
- markets;
- water;
- fire;
- local watch;
- building;
- ordinary local disputes;
- some local taxes / fees;
- local commercial regulation.

The central authority retains or claims stronger authority over:

- serious crime;
- military matters;
- external diplomacy;
- high-level land / title disputes;
- strategic-metal rules;
- Convergence obligations.

Exact boundary between local and central law remains intentionally messy.

---

# 11. SH-M political status

V1 tests SH-M as:

> **a charter-like civic jurisdiction inside SH-A, with privileges that the central ruler cannot revoke casually.**

The source of those privileges remains open.

They could derive from:

- pre-SH-A independence;
- Convergence-era recognition;
- later bargain with the central ruler;
- financing of consolidation;
- control of essential infrastructure.

### Why this model works

If SH-M has no protected local authority:

- the central ruler becomes too administratively strong;
- civic politics becomes decorative.

If SH-M is fully sovereign:

- SH-A becomes less consolidated than current owners imply.

A strong internal charter / privilege layer fits between them.

---

# 12. Guild privilege inside SH-M

Guilds can possess recognized authority over:

- apprenticeship;
- certification;
- workmanship;
- professional discipline;
- some contract standards;
- mutual aid.

They should not automatically control:

- city government;
- criminal law;
- taxation;
- mine ownership.

Guild privilege can vary by craft.

A metalworking guild may be politically important while a food / transport guild has different legal rights.

This keeps guilds plural.

---

# 13. Labor organization

Current labor unrest should not require a modern union system.

Workers can coordinate through several overlapping channels:

- guild factions;
- mutual-aid funds;
- neighborhood networks;
- religious institutions;
- workshop crews;
- mine crews;
- informal strike committees;
- political reformers.

Some master artisans may support labor demands.

Some ordinary workers may oppose a strike.

That prevents class from perfectly mapping onto guild membership.

---

# 14. Central ruler versus mine owners

The relationship is bargaining.

The ruler needs owners because they provide:

- capital;
- production;
- records;
- taxes;
- contracts.

Owners need the ruler because the state provides:

- recognized title;
- roads;
- security;
- courts;
- external diplomacy;
- protection against private seizure.

Neither side should be able to ignore the other indefinitely.

---

# 15. Food dependence

SH-A's industrial districts rely substantially on Low Rivers trade.

This creates a constitutional fact:

> **food security limits the ruler's freedom to treat eastern routes as purely military questions.**

During a Low Rivers shock, SH-A may need to:

- lower tolls;
- buy grain;
- prioritize freight;
- borrow;
- negotiate with LR-A;
- pressure merchants.

The ruler cannot solve food shortage by commanding mines harder.

---

# 16. Military structure

V1 does not require one huge standing army.

A plausible layered structure includes:

## Central force

A permanent or semi-permanent core loyal to the ruler.

Functions:

- palace / court security;
- rapid response;
- important fortifications;
- enforcement against major rebellion;
- military leadership.

## District / local obligations

Territorial districts may owe:

- levy;
- militia;
- equipment;
- money.

## Town contingents

Chartered cities may provide:

- guards;
- specialists;
- money;
- limited troops.

## Contracted specialists

Possible:

- engineers;
- miners;
- guides;
- mercenaries;
- transport.

### Important limit

Mine owners may have guards.

They should not automatically field private armies comparable to the state without that becoming a major political crisis.

---

# 17. Roads and Western Gate

SH-A needs strong interest in:

- the foothill border with LR-A;
- Western Gate;
- forest-edge roads;
- western feeder routes.

But different internal constituencies want different things.

Mining interests want:

- cheap freight;
- reliable imports.

Military officials may want:

- inspection;
- strategic control.

Border towns may want:

- lower friction.

Smugglers want:

- alternate routes.

The road policy is therefore political.

---

# 18. Internal constituencies

These are not necessarily formal parties.

## Centralist officials / court interests

Want:

- predictable taxation;
- military capacity;
- stronger inspection;
- strategic coordination.

## Chartered civic interests

Want:

- local law;
- control of city revenue;
- charter protections;
- predictable central demands.

## Mine / large property interests

Want:

- secure rights;
- labor stability;
- credit;
- favorable export policy.

## Guild masters / professional institutions

Want:

- standards;
- training control;
- legal privilege;
- bargaining voice.

## Worker / reform networks

Want varying combinations of:

- safety;
- pay;
- debt relief;
- guild reform;
- legal standing;
- injury support.

## Rural / forest-edge communities

Care about:

- food prices;
- land;
- taxes;
- fuel;
- road expansion;
- extraction pressure.

## Merchants / lenders

Want:

- contract stability;
- routes;
- repayment;
- access.

No one bloc should permanently dominate all issues.

---

# 19. Ordinary legitimacy

People obey SH-A for more than fear.

The central polity can provide real benefits:

- recognized land / mine claims;
- protection against private war;
- safer roads;
- external defense;
- cross-district courts;
- stable currency / measures where applicable;
- Convergence standing;
- famine / disaster response;
- limits on arbitrary private coercion.

People can resent taxes while still preferring SH-A to the older world of mine wars and shifting allegiance.

That creates plausible legitimacy.

# 20. Test 1 — mine disaster

A major mine collapses.

## Mine operator

Provides:

- maps;
- crews;
- equipment.

## Guild / workers

Provide:

- technical knowledge;
- rescue skill;
- witness testimony.

## SH-M

Provides:

- local emergency response;
- shelter;
- public order;
- local investigation.

## SH-A

Can:

- send specialists;
- compel evidence;
- suspend operation;
- hear higher claims;
- organize relief.

**PASS.**

No one institution has to do everything.

---

# 21. Test 2 — current labor strike

Workers across several operations stop work.

## SH-M

Faces:

- demonstrations;
- shortages;
- local violence risk;
- business pressure.

## Mine owners

Want reopening.

## Guild factions

Split.

## SH-A

Wants:

- strategic production;
- tax revenue;
- public order.

### Strong V1 response

The ruler cannot simply order everyone back without risking:

- civic resistance;
- guild refusal;
- wider unrest.

Nor can SH-M ignore central strategic pressure.

The crisis becomes negotiation over:

- safety;
- wages;
- debt;
- guild governance;
- state emergency powers.

**STRONG PASS.**

---

# 22. Test 3 — unusual metal movement

High-quality metal disappears into private contracts.

SH-A can:

- inspect;
- subpoena / demand records where law allows;
- restrict roads;
- question warehouses;
- pressure mine holders.

But:

- records are distributed;
- Council-linked finance can obscure ownership;
- U-W / secondary routes exist;
- guild expertise is needed to identify grades / uses.

**STRONG PASS.**

The polity is powerful enough to investigate and weak enough that the mystery remains plausible.

---

# 23. Test 4 — strategic export restriction

SH-A bans export of a high-quality metal class.

## Expected effects

- mine holders lose buyers;
- merchants seek rerouting;
- guilds dispute classification;
- SH-M loses revenue;
- smuggling rises;
- foreign buyers protest.

## Political test

If SH-A cannot affect exports at all, it is too weak.

If it can stop every shipment perfectly, the distributed-economy model fails.

V1 produces partial enforcement.

**PASS.**

---

# 24. Test 5 — Low Rivers food-price shock

Grain prices rise sharply.

SH-M workers demand higher wages.

Mine owners resist.

SH-A can:

- cut tolls;
- subsidize freight;
- purchase grain;
- borrow;
- negotiate with LR-A.

But dependence remains.

**PASS.**

This proves economic interdependence constrains the central state.

---

# 25. Test 6 — SH-M refuses an extraordinary levy

SH-A demands emergency money for:

- war;
- road repair;
- food purchase.

SH-M claims its charter / inherited obligation does not permit the amount.

## Possible escalation

negotiation  
→ legal / historical argument  
→ withholding  
→ central pressure  
→ merchant / guild involvement  
→ settlement

The central ruler may possess coercive authority but still hesitate to use it because damaging SH-M damages the polity.

**STRONG PASS.**

This is exactly the kind of politics the composite model should create.

---

# 26. Test 7 — succession crisis

The central ruler dies with a disputed successor.

Possible claimants have different:

- dynastic standing;
- military support;
- town support;
- mine-owner support;
- guild relationships.

The confirmation mechanism matters.

## Why it works

The polity has a way to distinguish:

- private dynastic claim;
- publicly recognized succession.

A disputed confirmation can create crisis without requiring immediate civil war.

**PASS.**

### Design warning

Do not make every major institution possess an equal vote.

The exact recognition process should emerge from specific history.

---

# 27. Test 8 — Western Gate crisis

HR-W closes or restricts the corridor.

SH-A wants reopening.

SH-M merchants and mine owners pressure the ruler.

Military leaders may favor force.

Food importers favor negotiation.

Convergence obligations and route compact matter.

**PASS.**

External policy emerges from internal dependency rather than pure ruler preference.

---

# 28. Strong V1 polity conclusion

The best current SH-A model is:

> **a post-Convergence composite territorial polity with a central dynastic ruler / ruling house whose authority is real but historically layered over older towns, mining rights, local jurisdictions, guild privileges, and negotiated obligations.**

The central polity is strong in:

- diplomacy;
- military force;
- major taxation;
- serious law;
- strategic roads;
- strategic-metal policy.

It is constrained by:

- chartered civic authority;
- mine / property rights;
- guild expertise and privilege;
- labor;
- credit;
- food imports;
- inherited local arrangements.

SH-M works best as:

> **a major charter-like industrial city / jurisdiction inside SH-A whose political importance comes from concentrated productive capacity, civic institutions, and inherited privileges rather than sovereignty.**

# 30. Promotion gate

Before promoting SH-A / SH-M from map IDs toward named canon, V1 should survive one comparison test:

> **Can a different polity model solve the same constraints with fewer assumptions?**

Main alternatives:

## A. Unitary monarchy

Fails because it makes inherited civic / productive institutions too easy to override.

## B. Loose western confederation

Fails because current owners imply more territorial consolidation and stronger ruler-level action than a purely loose league naturally supplies.

## C. Guild / merchant state

Fails because Stone Hills contains major rural, landed, military, and civic interests not reducible to production guilds.

## D. Composite territorial polity

Fits:

- consolidation;
- local privilege;
- ruler capacity;
- distributed production;
- messy history;
- Convergence succession;
- current crises.

**V1 winner: composite territorial polity as the working design model.**

This is still a reference conclusion, not a final constitutional canon label.

- post-Convergence consolidation;

Low Rivers should be tested independently rather than copied.
