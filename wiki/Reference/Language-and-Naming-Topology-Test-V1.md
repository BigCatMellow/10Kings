# Language and Naming Topology Test — V1

## Status

**Provisional historical-linguistic test — not canon language inventory.**

This page supplies the minimum language-history structure needed before the V1 political actors can receive defensible local names.

It follows:

- [Language Families, Contact, and Mutual Intelligibility](../Language-Families-and-Contact.md)
- [Naming](../Culture/Naming.md)
- [Language History and Naming Audit](Language-History-and-Naming-Audit.md)
- [Working Political Geography — V1](Working-Political-Geography-V1.md)
- [Political Border History Test — V1](Political-Border-History-Test-V1.md)

It does **not** establish:

- final language names;
- exact family trees;
- phoneme inventories;
- scripts;
- full grammars;
- one language per polity;
- one language per cultural region.

The working question is:

> **What is the smallest plausible linguistic topology that explains the world's settlement history, political borders, major contact routes, and future naming layers without turning the six cultural cores into six language boxes?**

This page uses family / cluster IDs as design handles only.

---

# 1. Method

The political-map method worked because it used temporary IDs before names.

Use the same rule here.

Do not begin with:

- Stone Hills language;
- North Coast language;
- Old Cities language.

Begin with:

- physical isolation;
- old migration;
- river corridors;
- island chains;
- mountain routes;
- settlement density;
- long-term contact.

V1 therefore asks where language relationships are **most likely to need continuity or separation**.

---

# 2. Strong geographic constraint

The three-continent model creates a useful broad asymmetry.

## Western Continent

Long periods of relative geographic concentration make one major western family / cluster plausible.

It need not contain every western community.

## Northern Continent

Coastal and island chains make dialect continua plausible, but maritime movement can connect places that are physically distant.

## Eastern Continent

Its size and ecological diversity strongly argue against one eastern family.

At minimum it should contain several old linguistic populations whose later cultural histories overlap.

## The Spine / High Roads

This is a contact zone, not a natural candidate for one dominant family.

## Port

This is a contact-language engine rather than a clean ancestral homeland.

---

# 3. V1 minimum topology

V1 tests six **unnamed historical family / cluster handles** plus Port contact varieties.

These are not six cultural-region languages.

---

## W1 — western upland / valley family cluster

### Core

Most strongly associated historically with:

- much of Stone Hills;
- western upland valleys;
- older western towns.

### Expected spillover

Long contact and changing borders make W1 varieties plausible in:

- SH-A ↔ LR-A foothill communities;
- western Low Rivers towns;
- Stone Hills ↔ Longwood edge settlements;
- Port / High Roads diaspora.

### Why V1 needs W1

The Western Continent has enough historical continuity to justify a major related cluster.

But W1 should contain:

- valley divergence;
- town standards;
- craft registers;
- older substrate pockets.

It is not one uniform Stone Hills language.

---

## N1 — northern coastal / mainland chain

### Core

Historically associated with:

- major NC-M coastal populations;
- connected mainland harbors;
- some island communities.

### Structure

N1 should behave like a dialect / language chain rather than one standardized language.

Maritime mobility can spread:

- vocabulary;
- songs;
- legal terms;
- crew language

farther than ordinary household intelligibility.

### NC-I caution

V1 does **not** require NC-I to speak the same branch as NC-M.

NC-I may contain:

- a divergent N1 branch;
- older substrate;
- mixed island speech.

That remains open.

---

## R1 — eastern river / lowland family cluster

### Core

Historically associated with:

- much of the G1 / Low Rivers settlement corridor;
- river towns;
- lowland villages / estates.

### Expected spillover

Because rivers and agriculture create long settlement continua, R1 varieties can extend into:

- foothill border communities with Stone Hills;
- lower High Roads valleys;
- northern Old Cities transition;
- Port's mainland / migrant population.

### Why V1 needs R1

LR-A's basin administration should not create its language from scratch.

A preexisting river continuum is a strong substrate for:

- record standards;
- legal spelling;
- market forms.

---

## P1 — plateau / interior High Roads family cluster

### Core

Historically associated with:

- older plateau communities;
- herding populations;
- some interior High Roads settlements.

### Important limit

P1 is **not the High Roads language**.

HR-N / HR-W / HR-C should also contain speakers of:

- N1;
- W1;
- R1;
- F1;
- S1;
- mixed contact varieties.

### Why V1 needs P1

Without at least one older local linguistic population, High Roads risks becoming a region populated only by travelers and imported speech.

P1 gives the plateau its own historical settlement depth.

---

## F1 — forest / river family cluster

### Core

Historically associated with:

- parts of Longwood interior;
- forest river communities;
- G2-related settlement.

### Expected spillover

F1 varieties can cross into:

- Low Rivers forest margins;
- High Roads / Longwood junctions;
- southern Longwood / Old Cities transition.

### Important limit

Longwood should still contain **more than one language community**.

F1 is a working major cluster, not a claim that every forest community shares ancestry.

Older substrate pockets remain explicitly possible.

---

## S1 — southern urban / irrigation family cluster

### Core

Historically associated with:

- several Old Cities urban / rural populations;
- S1 irrigation basin;
- southern / eastern coastal towns.

### Structure

S1 should contain multiple civic standards and dialects.

OC-U and OC-D may plausibly use relatively related forms because:

- they share old infrastructure;
- populations moved along the basin;
- technical records crossed later borders.

OC-C may belong to:

- a more divergent S1 branch;
- or another coastal substrate later heavily influenced by S1.

V1 leaves that choice open.

### Why V1 needs S1

The Old Cities requires language history older than the current city-state borders.

One family cluster with strong civic differentiation is a useful minimum.

---

# 4. Why this is not six regions = six languages

The topology crosses the current political / cultural map.

Examples:

- W1 crosses Stone Hills into Low Rivers / Longwood contact zones.
- R1 crosses Low Rivers toward High Roads, Old Cities transition, and Port.
- P1 exists in High Roads alongside several imported / neighboring languages.
- F1 crosses Longwood into Low Rivers / High Roads / southern transition zones.
- S1 contains several rival city-state standards rather than one regional language.
- N1 follows coast / island movement rather than NC-M's political border.
- Port develops new contact varieties from several sources.

The mapping is therefore historical / geographic, not cultural-administrative.

---

# 5. Contact belts

Some of the most important naming areas are **between** clusters.

## W1 ↔ R1 — SH-A / LR-A foothills

Long-term:

- trade;
- conquest;
- mixed families;
- craft / agricultural exchange

should create:

- borrowed settlement terms;
- duplicate place names;
- translated names;
- surnames crossing borders.

This is a strong source of double naming.

---

## N1 ↔ P1 / High Roads registers — North Shoulder

NC-R / HR-N should be highly bilingual / multilingual in practical terms.

Possible pattern:

- home language on each side;
- shared route vocabulary;
- standardized contract / date forms;
- names represented differently in each roadhead's records.

---

## W1 ↔ P1 — Western Gate

Military / trade history supports:

- borrowed fort / toll vocabulary;
- translated route names;
- official treaty spellings different from local speech.

---

## R1 ↔ F1 — G2 / Longwood edge

Water / forest communities can produce:

- old river names surviving in a newer political language;
- Longwood place terms entering Low Rivers maps;
- mixed village naming systems.

---

## F1 ↔ P1 — HR-C / LW-I junction

This should be a productive zone for:

- guide vocabulary;
- route / forest compound translations;
- bilingual households;
- professional exonyms.

---

## R1 ↔ S1 — LR-A / OC-U transition

Long agricultural contact supports:

- partial mutual intelligibility in some border communities if later family work allows;
- or heavy borrowing if genealogically unrelated.

V1 does not decide relatedness.

It does require strong contact.

---

## F1 ↔ S1 — LW-S / OC-U transition

Likely:

- market bilingualism;
- botanical / agricultural borrowing;
- alternate names for the same water / forest features.

---

# 6. Port contact complex

Port should not be assigned one ancestral family.

V1 expects:

- R1 mainland influence;
- S1 maritime / merchant influence;
- N1 sailor / harbor influence;
- W1 merchant / craft communities;
- High Roads professional register;
- later locally native Port contact varieties.

### Naming consequence

A Port legal record may preserve a person's name in a form no one in the family uses at home.

A foreign city may be globally known by its Port shortening.

This can explain common-language names without declaring Port speech the universal language.

---

# 7. Underpass linguistic overlay

The Underpass should not receive a separate universal family.

U-W / U-N / U-F instead preserve:

- nearby surface languages;
- enclave drift;
- mixed route cants;
- occupational terminology.

Some enclaves may preserve older forms lost on the surface.

That creates naming fossils without inventing an underground ethnicity.

---

# 8. Political actors and likely naming sources

The topology now constrains where each V1 political actor should draw its names.

## SH-A

Likely naming strata:

1. W1 older territorial / seat name;
2. ruling-house / administrative form;
3. Convergence legal standard;
4. common-language exonym.

Do **not** call the polity Stone Hills.

Stone Hills is broader than SH-A.

---

## SH-M

Likely:

1. older W1 river / valley / settlement name predating industrial dominance;
2. mine / district / guild commercial nickname;
3. SH-A administrative form;
4. worker / merchant shortening.

This directly supports the grounded Blackbellow replacement mechanism.

---

## NC-M

Likely:

1. N1 territorial / principal harbor or old ruling-center name;
2. compact / dynastic political form;
3. Convergence standard;
4. foreign maritime exonyms.

Do **not** call it North Coast.

---

## NC-I

Likely:

1. island geography / older local name;
2. political / lineage title;
3. NC-M administrative / diplomatic form;
4. sailors' shortening.

The local and mainland forms may differ significantly.

---

## NC-R / HR-N

Each should have an ordinary local settlement name.

The pass / corridor can have:

- N1 name;
- P1 / High Roads-side name;
- caravan translation;
- Convergence legal form.

Do not force both roadheads to share one name.

---

## LR-A

Likely naming sources:

1. R1 river / old central settlement;
2. administrative / ruling form;
3. Convergence standard;
4. foreign common-language form.

The polity may be named after:

- its seat;
- a river;
- a dynasty / house;
- an older territorial designation.

Do **not** use Low Rivers as the state name.

---

## LR-M

Likely:

1. older R1 village / ford / crossing name;
2. market / quay nickname;
3. administrative form;
4. caravan / Port shortening.

Its current economic importance should not require a grand name.

---

## HR-N / HR-W / HR-C

These are the strongest candidates for **multiple parallel names**.

Each can have:

- older P1 or other local name;
- route / traveler translation;
- treaty spelling;
- merchant shorthand.

HR-C especially should not receive a name meaning:

- capital;
- summit;
- grand crossroads

unless later history literally supports it.

---

## LW-W / LW-I / LW-S

Naming should be highly local.

Possible sources:

- rivers;
- older settlements;
- species / ecological landmarks where ordinary and literal;
- lineages;
- religious sites;
- translated outsider forms.

Because F1 is only one major cluster, neighboring Longwood communities may not share one naming morphology.

---

## OC-U / OC-D / OC-C

These are the strongest candidates for **old opaque city names**, but only after local language history exists.

Each city can have:

- old civic endonym;
- rival-city exonym;
- formal civic title;
- Port merchant shortening;
- dynastic / regime-era historical name.

The political unit may simply inherit the city's name.

Do not invent an opaque form until an S1 branch / substrate can generate it.

---

## Port

Keep **Port** as the interregional common form.

Still open:

- local formal civic name;
- older geographic name;
- district forms.

Port is a good demonstration that the best common name can be extremely plain.

---

# 9. Administrative standardization after the Convergence

The new political map makes naming standardization more concrete.

## SH-A

Central records may standardize W1 spellings while leaving local forms alive.

## NC-M

Maritime / harbor records may preserve several accepted variants because captains move among dialect chains.

## LR-A

Basin administration may standardize:

- land;
- river;
- district;
- market names

more aggressively than household speech.

## High Roads

Contracts may use transcription conventions rather than replacing local names.

## Longwood

Outside maps may standardize names much more than local communities do.

## Old Cities

Each city may maintain its own official spelling / prestige form.

## Port

Port clerks may generate internationally durable transliterations / shortenings.

This gives political systems different effects on naming.

---

# 10. Mutual intelligibility remains deliberately unresolved

V1 does **not** claim:

- W1 and R1 are related;
- R1 and S1 are related;
- F1 is isolated;
- N1 is one language;
- P1 has one standard.

Those require a later family-tree / sound-change pass.

The current topology only says:

> these are the historical contact / continuity units that future language design must explain.

That is enough to avoid random naming.

---

# 11. Minimum next language work

Before final local names can be generated, each major cluster needs only a **small naming profile**, not a complete conlang.

For W1, N1, R1, P1, F1, and S1 establish:

1. broad sound inventory tendencies;
2. common syllable shapes;
3. how compounds / genitives work in place names;
4. common ordinary geographic nouns;
5. major historical sound / spelling changes if needed;
6. how Convergence / Port / High Roads scribes represent the forms.

This is the minimum necessary to generate names that are inherited rather than decorative.

---

# 12. Test against the political map

## SH-A / LR-A border

W1 / R1 contact naturally produces mixed place-name layers.

**PASS.**

## NC-M / NC-I

N1 chain plus possible island divergence supports constitutional and cultural distinction without requiring a separate ethnicity.

**PASS.**

## High Roads

P1 local depth plus several contact languages prevents "merchant language region."

**STRONG PASS.**

## Longwood

F1 major cluster plus explicitly open substrate / neighboring communities prevents one forest language.

**PASS.**

## Old Cities

S1 civic differentiation supports city-state prestige standards older than present alliances.

**PASS.**

## Port

Multi-source contact naturally generates local speech and international name standardization.

**STRONG PASS.**

---

# 13. Strong V1 conclusion

The naming problem does **not** require six finished conlangs.

It requires a historical linguistic substrate.

V1's minimum working topology is:

- W1 — western upland / valley cluster;
- N1 — northern coastal / mainland chain;
- R1 — eastern river / lowland cluster;
- P1 — plateau / interior High Roads cluster;
- F1 — forest / river cluster;
- S1 — southern urban / irrigation cluster;
- Port — contact complex;
- Underpass — local/enclave overlays.

These handles are provisional.

Their purpose is to make the next naming pass historical rather than aesthetic.

---

# 14. What remains open

- actual family count;
- whether any V1 clusters split into multiple families;
- genealogical relationships among clusters;
- language names;
- phonologies;
- scripts;
- mutual intelligibility;
- prestige standards;
- local endonyms;
- final polity / settlement names.

---

# 15. Next gate

The next pass should build **minimal naming profiles** for the six V1 clusters.

Do not write full grammars.

The output should be just enough to generate and reject place names consistently.

After that, test the profiles on six politically important names:

1. SH-A / its seat;
2. NC-M / principal political center;
3. LR-A / its seat or river-derived state name;
4. HR-C;
5. LW-W or its main market town;
6. OC-U.

Only names that survive history + language + ordinary-use tests should be promoted.
