# Political Border History Test — V1

## Status

**Provisional historical synthesis — not canon chronology.**

This page applies [Writing the Sequel](Writing-the-Sequel.md) to the political interfaces established in:

- [Working Political Geography — V1](Working-Political-Geography-V1.md)
- [Political Adjacency and Border-Interface Test — V1](Political-Adjacency-and-Border-Interface-Test-V1.md)
- [Pre-Convergence History](../History/Pre-Convergence.md)
- [The Convergence](../History/The-Convergence.md)
- [Contested Historical Memory](../History/Contested-Memory.md)

The goal is not to invent wars for every border.

It is to answer:

> **Why does this interface look the way it does now, and what older political arrangements still survive inside it?**

Each section distinguishes:

- **SUPPORTED** — directly consistent with current owners;
- **PROVISIONAL** — the smallest historical sequence that explains those constraints;
- **OPEN** — details not yet earned.

---

# 1. General historical rule

Pre-Convergence control followed:

- roads;
- rivers;
- mines;
- fields;
- harbors;
- fortifications;
- kinship;
- local bargains.

The Convergence then made many existing compromises harder to change by force.

The safest general sequence is:

~~~
fluid local control
→ repeated conflict / bargaining
→ practical compromise
→ Convergence recognition or attached agreement
→ later generations inherit the compromise
→ original reason becomes partly forgotten
~~~

Therefore modern borders should often preserve **old practical bargains**, not elegant geographic logic.

# 2. SH-A ↔ LR-A — foothill frontier

## SUPPORTED

Current owners establish:

- repeated Stone Hills / Low Rivers movement across western lowlands;
- Low Rivers rulers sometimes held western lowlands;
- Stone Hills powers sometimes pushed into food-producing country;
- mixed foothill craft / farming communities;
- food / metal dependency;
- remembered western-linked expansion and burning traditions.

The frontier was therefore not historically fixed.

## PROVISIONAL sequence

~~~
productive foothills + ore routes
→ small valley / estate / mining authorities overlap
→ stronger western and lowland rulers alternately claim districts
→ local towns survive by changing allegiance / patrons
→ repeated campaigns fail to create durable total control
→ a practical frontier emerges around what major rulers can actually hold
→ Convergence recognizes the broad political settlement
→ local land, marriage, water, and market ties continue across it
~~~

## Present residue

The modern border can be recognized politically while containing:

- families with property on both sides;
- old boundary stones inconsistent with current administration;
- villages with mixed court / property traditions;
- market exemptions;
- old road rights;
- shrines or cemeteries serving both sides.

## Writing-the-Sequel result

The border's greatest political significance is no longer:

> who owns the foothills?

It is more often:

> which side's law applies to the old relationships that survived the settlement?

## OPEN

- exact location;
- which side gained / lost territory at the Convergence;
- whether one major campaign became the later Ember Wars memory;
- exact surviving local exemptions.

# 3. SH-A ↔ HR-W — Western Gate

## SUPPORTED

The High Roads predecessor system contained:

- pass lords;
- toll towns;
- fortress communities;
- caravan families.

Stone Hills requires a major eastbound corridor.

The Convergence regularized route security and passage.

## PROVISIONAL sequence

~~~
local pass authorities control pieces of the corridor
→ western rulers repeatedly attempt greater control
→ toll conflict / military occupation changes hands
→ merchants and High Roads interests resist unpredictable monopoly
→ one or more practical route settlements emerge
→ Convergence recognizes territorial claims separately from passage obligations
→ corridor compact survives even as surrounding political power consolidates
~~~

## Present residue

Possible inherited features:

- old fort maintained for customs rather than war;
- toll rights older than the current ruler;
- guide privileges that survived dynastic change;
- maintenance obligations tied to villages no longer politically central;
- different legal treatment for military versus commercial passage.

## Writing-the-Sequel result

An institution created to prevent armies or toll lords from choking the pass may now spend most of its time on:

- bridge repair;
- freight disputes;
- snow closure;
- receipts.

That is exactly the kind of institutional afterlife the setting should preserve.

# 4. SH-A ↔ LW-W — extraction frontier

## SUPPORTED

Current owners establish:

- forest edges historically expanded and contracted;
- outsiders repeatedly pushed into Longwood for fuel, timber, farmland, and roads;
- Longwood groups sometimes resisted, cooperated, or profited;
- present logging / road conflict remains active.

## PROVISIONAL sequence

This frontier should not have one clean settlement moment.

Better:

~~~
forest-edge communities + western demand
→ seasonal cutting / grazing / trade
→ roads and permanent extraction expand
→ local agreements multiply
→ some settlements become politically tied to SH-A predecessors
→ Longwood communities recover or reassert use rights after contractions
→ Convergence recognizes broad territorial authority but does not erase layered forest rights
→ later concessions cite different historical layers
~~~

## Present residue

This border should still contain:

- overlapping cutting rights;
- old concessions;
- roads crossing disputed use zones;
- communities politically inside one jurisdiction but economically tied to another;
- arguments over whether a right is territorial, customary, or contractual.

## Writing-the-Sequel result

The most dangerous document on this frontier may be an old legal concession everyone agrees is authentic but disagrees about how broadly it applies.

# 5. NC-M ↔ NC-I — mainland / island relationship

## SUPPORTED

North Coast predecessor politics included:

- island rulers;
- coastal clans;
- fishing-right alliances;
- harbor confederacies.

Modern owners require strong island autonomy and maritime-use rights that do not follow shore borders.

## PROVISIONAL sequence

V1 should not decide whether NC-I was conquered, confederated, protected, or voluntarily associated.

The safest historical pattern is:

~~~
island authority develops independently
→ kin / trade / fishery obligations tie island to mainland networks
→ periods of stronger mainland coordination alternate with island resistance
→ practical defense / rescue / market arrangements accumulate
→ Convergence recognizes relevant political actors and maritime obligations without eliminating local island authority
→ later mainland governments claim more continuity than islanders necessarily accept
~~~

## Present residue

The constitutional relationship may contain phrases or obligations whose original context is disputed.

Examples:

- mainland duty to defend;
- island duty to contribute ships;
- shared fishery rules;
- tax exemptions;
- rights to mainland markets.

## Writing-the-Sequel result

A centuries-old emergency defense contribution could now be treated by NC-M as ordinary taxation and by NC-I as a temporary obligation that somehow never ended.

This is a useful later constitutional dispute.

# 6. NC-R ↔ HR-N — North Shoulder corridor

## SUPPORTED

The paired roadheads are already physically developed.

The pass requires:

- route information;
- transshipment;
- guides;
- rescue;
- seasonal closure;
- storage.

High Roads predecessor politics included pass / toll authorities.

## PROVISIONAL sequence

~~~
local crossing path
→ repeated merchant / migrant use
→ roadheads grow on both sides
→ specialists professionalize
→ disaster / closure produces shared rules
→ repeated agreements become a corridor compact
→ Convergence protects broader passage and recognizes some cross-border obligations
→ later commerce turns emergency practices into ordinary bureaucracy
~~~

## Present residue

Likely historical residue:

- old rescue obligations;
- shared signal / warning customs;
- route-status practices older than current polities;
- families with businesses at both roadheads;
- reciprocal guide recognition.

## Writing-the-Sequel result

The pass's most important surviving institution may have begun as a disaster response rather than a diplomatic design.

# 7. LR-A ↔ HR-C — G1 / River Road transition

## SUPPORTED

The physical network requires:

- G1 bulk river movement;
- a head-of-navigation transfer point;
- High Roads overland switching;
- river-road connectivity toward Port.

## PROVISIONAL sequence

This boundary may be younger and less militarized than the SH-A frontier.

~~~
upland and lowland communities trade through G1
→ head-of-navigation market grows
→ LR-A predecessor consolidates much of lower basin
→ HR-C predecessor remains politically separate because route networks support local autonomy
→ commercial practices become more important than frontier warfare
→ Convergence regularizes passage / claims
→ LR-M develops as the practical translation zone between systems
~~~

## Present residue

The frontier may be easier to see in:

- toll practice;
- court jurisdiction;
- records;
- road maintenance;
- commercial measures

than in walls or forts.

## Writing-the-Sequel result

A border once defined by where rulers could project force may now be experienced by merchants as the point where paperwork changes.

# 8. LR-A ↔ Longwood — forest / watershed frontier

## SUPPORTED

Current world requires:

- Longwood forest margins;
- G2 tributary flowing toward Low Rivers;
- forest-edge settlement pressure;
- cultural contact;
- ecological downstream effects.

## PROVISIONAL sequence

~~~
river / forest communities interact before large state consolidation
→ clearing and settlement move the edge repeatedly
→ Low Rivers estates / towns expand along accessible land
→ Longwood communities retain rights in forest / water systems
→ political authority stabilizes unevenly
→ Convergence recognizes territorial claims but leaves many use rights local
→ later watershed consequences ignore the political settlement
~~~

## Present residue

Possible residues:

- upstream forest communities with downstream water obligations;
- Low Rivers land titles that exclude timber rights;
- Longwood customary access through LR-A territory;
- old ferry / logging rights.

## Writing-the-Sequel result

The Convergence can stabilize the border while making environmental disputes more legal, not less real.

# 9. LR-A ↔ OC-U — agricultural transition

## SUPPORTED

Current owners establish:

- Low Rivers ↔ Old Cities agricultural transition;
- mixed water law;
- seasonal migration;
- historical Low Rivers famine expansion into healthier land;
- Old Cities city-state / irrigation politics.

## PROVISIONAL sequence

~~~
mixed farming / orchard transition contains many local authorities
→ drought / famine periodically changes migration and military pressure
→ estates and towns switch patrons
→ larger Low Rivers and Old Cities actors attempt to stabilize productive districts
→ repeated conflict proves expensive
→ Convergence recognizes political claims while preserving local water / property arrangements
→ contact-zone culture becomes more stable than either state's official narrative admits
~~~

## Present residue

Likely:

- mixed inheritance customs;
- seasonal-worker rights;
- old irrigation obligations;
- bilingual market law;
- claims based on pre-Convergence marriages or land grants.

## Writing-the-Sequel result

A border created partly to stop famine-era expansion may now be crossed every year by workers who treat it as ordinary.

# 10. LR-A ↔ Port — mainland / neutral city boundary

## SUPPORTED

Current owners establish:

- Port existed before the modern treaty order;
- the Convergence did not create Port from nothing;
- Port neutrality / access was formalized or strengthened by the Convergence;
- Port needs meaningful local self-government;
- right to use Port is not right to rule Port.

## PROVISIONAL sequence

Avoid locking the legacy Port seizure yet.

The minimum sequence is:

~~~
commercial harbor / local settlement develops at G1 estuary
→ outside powers increasingly depend on access
→ local / foreign authority becomes repeatedly contested
→ monopoly by any one nearby power becomes strategically unacceptable
→ Convergence formalizes or strengthens a separate neutral status
→ mainland boundary and access rights become legally important
→ Port grows more locally distinct after neutrality stabilizes
~~~

## Present residue

Possible inherited friction:

- mainland road / bridge rights;
- old property claims inside Port;
- foreign warehouses older than current treaty rules;
- Port families who remember outside rule differently;
- disagreement about where harbor jurisdiction ends.

## Writing-the-Sequel result

The treaty boundary may now matter most for customs, arrests, labor, and warehouses rather than armies.

# 11. High Roads internal boundaries

## SUPPORTED

High Roads predecessor politics was fragmented around:

- tolls;
- passes;
- towns;
- caravan families;
- herding territories.

Modern governance remains networked.

## PROVISIONAL sequence

Do not invent three ancient nations corresponding exactly to HR-N / HR-W / HR-C.

Better:

~~~
many local authorities
→ some towns / route families grow more durable than others
→ corridor agreements bundle smaller jurisdictions
→ certain urban / route centers become recognized political actors
→ Convergence strengthens passage and arbitration
→ post-Convergence practice gradually makes HR-N / HR-W / HR-C-like blocs more legible
~~~

## Present residue

The current V1 actors may be **historical accumulations**, not original states.

Their internal borders can therefore remain:

- fuzzy;
- layered;
- locally interpreted.

## Writing-the-Sequel result

High Roads political geography may actually be clearer now than it was at the Convergence because repeated route coordination consolidated identities afterward.

# 12. HR-C ↔ LW-I — upland / forest junction

## SUPPORTED

The Border Towns owner already requires a Longwood ↔ High Roads route-contact zone.

## PROVISIONAL sequence

~~~
forest paths and upland trails meet
→ guides / traders / herders build repeated exchange
→ market settlement grows
→ road improvement increases outside use
→ local rights become politically contested
→ corridor arrangements emerge without one side annexing the other
~~~

## Present residue

Expect:

- mixed guide families;
- route terms borrowed into forest law;
- Longwood use-right claims crossing a High Roads-administered road;
- High Roads commercial records used as evidence in local disputes.

# 13. Longwood internal boundaries

## SUPPORTED

Longwood is historically a patchwork, not one polity.

## PROVISIONAL sequence

LW-W, LW-I, and LW-S should not be treated as ancient permanent blocks.

They are better understood as modern V1 political concentrations that emerged through:

- settlement;
- alliance;
- trade;
- ecological pressure;
- outside contact.

### LW-W

May become more coherent because R4 / extraction pressure forces several communities to bargain together.

### LW-I

May preserve older local alliance structures because external administrative pressure is lower.

### LW-S

May become more town / market oriented through R5 and Old Cities contact.

## Writing-the-Sequel result

Different kinds of outside pressure can create different kinds of internal political consolidation without producing one Longwood state.

# 14. OC-U ↔ OC-D — shared S1 system

## SUPPORTED

This is the strongest existing historical border mechanism because the shared-irrigation test already supplies it.

The water system can predate the current political boundary.

## PROVISIONAL sequence

~~~
older shared water infrastructure
→ settlement and agriculture expand around it
→ political control changes repeatedly
→ current predecessor polities emerge on different parts of system
→ boundary cuts across inherited infrastructure
→ neither side can cheaply replace the water system
→ Convergence or attached agreements recognize territorial claims and preserve practical water cooperation
→ later generations argue about which rules are ancient, treaty-based, or customary
~~~

## Present residue

Strong candidates:

- joint maintenance access;
- flow records;
- inherited shares;
- emergency-warning customs;
- old canal workers with cross-border standing.

## Writing-the-Sequel result

The border can be politically stable precisely because the canal remains politically messy.

# 15. OC-D ↔ OC-C — ordinary civic frontier

## SUPPORTED

Old Cities needs multiple city-states and long-lived civic rivalry.

No current owner requires every pair to have a major territorial dispute.

## PROVISIONAL sequence

Treat this as one of the world's more ordinary old borders.

Possible history:

~~~
rival civic territories stabilize through repeated wars / bargains
→ neither gains enough from continued frontier warfare
→ merchants / estates adapt
→ Convergence recognizes the existing practical division
→ rivalry moves toward trade, prestige, alliances, and influence
~~~

## Present residue

The border may contain:

- old forts now used administratively;
- competing market days;
- civic monuments;
- jokes;
- slightly different weights / legal procedure.

## Writing-the-Sequel result

A former military frontier becomes a place where people complain about customs forms.

That contrast is useful world texture.

# 16. Underpass / surface relationships

## SUPPORTED

The Underpass:

- predates current political borders;
- has fragmented authority;
- crosses surface systems;
- can bypass some chokepoints.

## PROVISIONAL sequence

Surface rulers repeatedly try to claim entrances.

Actual underground control remains local because:

- distance;
- collapse;
- route knowledge;
- enclave communities;
- toll holders;
- maintenance

make effective rule discontinuous.

The Convergence may recognize some surface access obligations without solving underground sovereignty.

## Present residue

A surface border may cross an underground route whose users recognize entirely different authority.

This is a strong source of:

- smuggling;
- extradition problems;
- toll disputes;
- old privileges.

# 17. What the Convergence probably froze

V1 does not yet identify exact articles.

But the border-history tests support the following **categories** of settlement.

## Territorial recognition

Likely important for:

- SH-A ↔ LR-A;
- LR-A ↔ OC-U;
- OC-U ↔ OC-D;
- OC-D ↔ OC-C;
- Port's local footprint.

## Route settlement

Likely important for:

- Western Gate;
- North Shoulder;
- major G1 / High Roads passage.

## Functional attached agreements

Likely important for:

- S1 shared irrigation;
- maritime access;
- possible fishery / anchorage relationships;
- Port access.

## Local exceptions

Likely preserved:

- old roads;
- seasonal rights;
- water shares;
- market privileges;
- religious access;
- family / property claims.

### Strong conclusion

The Convergence should not be imagined as drawing new borders on a blank map.

It more plausibly:

> **recognized broad political realities while preserving or attaching enough older local rights to stop recognition itself from breaking everyday life.**

That is a strong historical fit with the current world.

# 18. Contested memory opportunities

The border histories generate memory without requiring new canonical wars.

## SH-A / LR-A

Later populations may compress many campaigns into one Ember Wars narrative.

## SH-A / LW-W

Longwood closure traditions and western development narratives can describe the same concessions differently.

## NC-M / NC-I

Mainland histories may call political coordination unification.

Island histories may call it alliance.

## Port

Some traditions may treat neutrality as liberation from outside domination even if the exact Port Seizure remains unresolved.

## OC-U / OC-D

Each city may claim the shared water rules prove older sovereignty.

The same maintenance records can support opposite political stories.

---

# 19. Strong V1 historical conclusions

The political map works best if:

1. modern borders mostly descend from **practical pre-Convergence control**, not cultural boundaries;
2. the Convergence recognized many arrangements that already existed rather than inventing them;
3. functional rights frequently survived across newly stabilized territorial lines;
4. SH-A ↔ LR-A is an old fluctuating frontier that became a routine working border;
5. Western Gate and North Shoulder retained corridor institutions older than current political structures;
6. Longwood edges remain historically layered because forest use did not freeze as cleanly as territorial claims;
7. Port's separate status grew from preexisting commercial importance and later treaty protection;
8. High Roads V1 actors may have consolidated after the Convergence rather than existing unchanged before it;
9. OC-U ↔ OC-D is a successor-state boundary cutting across older shared infrastructure;
10. some old military frontiers should now feel bureaucratically ordinary.

# 21. Next highest-value pass

The political geography now has:

- actor topology;
- required adjacency;
- plausible inherited border history.

The next useful step is to develop **one concrete polity vertical slice**.

The best candidate is **SH-A / SH-M** because current events already place labor unrest and unusual metal movement there.

A vertical slice should establish only what the story and system need:

- center of government;
- territorial reach in rough terms;
- fiscal basis;
- relationship to SH-M;
- mine / guild / town privileges;
- military capacity;
- succession legitimacy;
- internal factions;
- how it participates in the Convergence;
- why ordinary people accept or resist it.

That would test whether the current political method produces a believable actual state before repeating the process elsewhere.
