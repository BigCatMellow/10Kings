# Conspiracy Capability Ledger — V2

## Status

**Active author-side capability / access map.**

Supersedes [Domino Conspiracy Ledger — V1](Domino-Conspiracy-Ledger-V1.md) as the active model.

V1 remains provenance. Its 79 micro-actions demonstrated that a large conspiracy could be decomposed into mundane tasks, but it still read too much like a hidden master checklist.

V2 records **capabilities the Villain can draw on when circumstances create a useful intervention window**.

A capability is not a planned action.

---

# 1. Core rule

The Villain maintains options.

He does not pre-order ninety future moves.

The relevant questions are:

- What can he learn?
- What can he move?
- What can he finance?
- What can he reveal?
- Who can he reach?
- How quickly?
- At what exposure cost?

---

# 2. Information capability

Possible access:

- shipping schedules;
- route-condition reports;
- public / semi-public contracts;
- debt notices;
- procurement records;
- warehouse availability;
- political meeting schedules;
- market prices;
- copied correspondence.

Sources can include:

- merchants;
- clerks;
- sailors;
- guides;
- lenders;
- guild members;
- travelers;
- Port workers;
- brokers.

Important:

Most information sources are **not conspirators**.

They may be:

- selling information;
- helping a reform movement;
- doing ordinary work;
- sharing gossip;
- unaware where the information goes.

---

# 3. Commercial capability

The Villain / aligned movement may be able to:

- make limited purchases;
- provide small emergency loans;
- rent warehouse space;
- hire carriers;
- buy distressed cargo;
- retain legal / accounting help;
- prepay for transport.

Limits:

- cannot move whole regional markets at will;
- cannot sustain unlimited losses;
- large positions create visibility;
- competitors can outbid him.

Use:

at bottlenecks where a modest allocation matters.

---

# 4. Logistics capability

Possible channels:

- small carriers;
- secondary roads;
- Underpass contacts;
- minor landings;
- independent warehouses;
- direct producer-buyer relationships;
- guild / faith hospitality.

Limits:

- slower / lower capacity;
- dependent on local relationships;
- vulnerable to inspection and disruption.

Strategic value:

- relief;
- documents;
- people;
- small high-value cargo;
- proof that Port can retain some independent capacity.

---

# 5. Political / legal capability

Possible access:

- reform politicians;
- labor organizers;
- local rights-holders;
- commercial litigants;
- treaty critics;
- journalists / chroniclers / public speakers where appropriate to media baseline;
- legal advocates.

He can:

- fund legitimate cases;
- provide evidence;
- make introductions;
- support travel / copying / publication.

He cannot assume:

- loyalty;
- message discipline;
- willingness to escalate.

Some beneficiaries should later oppose him.

---

# 6. Underworld capability

Possible purchased services:

- forged seals / marks;
- manifest fraud;
- covert carriage;
- bribed access;
- blackmail;
- stolen records;
- quiet enforcement.

Use sparingly.

Every criminal service:

- increases traceability;
- adds witnesses;
- can betray him;
- can pursue its own interests.

True criminal operations should be the exception, not the backbone.

---

# 7. Observation capability

One of his strongest capabilities may simply be:

> **seeing changes sooner than most political actors.**

This requires:

- many low-level reports;
- good record keeping;
- cross-domain comparison;
- people capable of distinguishing signal from noise.

That allows him to exploit events without causing them.

---

# 8. Strategic reserve capability

He should keep some resources uncommitted.

Examples:

- money;
- warehouse capacity;
- transport;
- trusted messengers;
- access to a document specialist;
- emergency relief stock;
- one or two politically useful relationships.

Why:

unexpected events create better opportunities than preplanned ones.

A mastermind with every resource already assigned cannot adapt.

---

# 9. Exposure budget

Every deliberate touch has an exposure cost.

## Low exposure

- public purchases;
- ordinary donations;
- paying for information;
- legal representation;
- introductions.

## Medium exposure

- selective financing across several connected actors;
- concealed ownership;
- advance positioning before a crisis;
- acquiring restricted documents.

## High exposure

- forgery;
- bribery;
- directing criminal action;
- facilitating violence;
- deliberate withholding that causes harm.

He should prefer lower-exposure tools whenever they can work.

---

# 10. Compartmentation

## Tier A — strategic

Very few people know:

- Port is the ultimate political objective;
- war is being cultivated as a forcing mechanism.

## Tier B — political movement

People know:

- Port needs more agency;
- the current settlement is unjust;
- the movement is building alternatives.

They do not necessarily know about covert escalation.

## Tier C — coordinators

Know a small portfolio:

- commercial relief;
- information collection;
- legal activism;
- logistics.

They may suspect more without knowing it.

## Tier D — specialists

Know only the task.

## Tier E — ordinary counterparties

Not members at all.

The lower tiers should contain almost all people who interact with the network.

---

# 11. No permanent asset assumption

A useful contact today may disappear tomorrow.

Possible reasons:

- changes jobs;
- gets arrested;
- refuses;
- becomes frightened;
- joins an opponent;
- dies naturally;
- moves away;
- realizes the political stakes;
- is bought by someone else.

The Villain must continually rebuild access.

This also creates story evidence and failure.

---

# 12. Capability use test

Before using a capability:

1. Is there a real intervention window?
2. Is the strategic gain larger than the exposure cost?
3. Can the same pressure advance without intervention?
4. Does using this capability burn it for later?
5. Does this require someone to know too much?
6. Can the action be explained by the participant's own motive?
7. What happens if they refuse?
8. What happens if the action becomes public immediately?

If refusal or exposure destroys the entire plan, the plan is too brittle.
