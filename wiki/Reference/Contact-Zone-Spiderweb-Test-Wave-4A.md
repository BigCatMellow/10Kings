## Test standard

A contact-zone community passes only if:

1. its livelihood depends on the interface;
2. at least one institution exists because systems meet;
3. household / food / language reflect repeated contact;
4. its local culture cannot be reproduced by simply placing two regional communities beside each other;
5. the border / interface creates actual friction;
6. cultural transmission has identified carriers / routes.

# 1. CZ-1 — Stone Hills ↔ Low Rivers foothill

## Causal chain

~~~text
foothill agriculture
+
nearby industrial / mine demand
→ seasonal wage movement
→ mixed household calendars
→ cross-border wages / debts / apprenticeships
→ witnesses / brokers / lodging / market workarounds
→ local work food + speech + labor identity
→ current abundance + strategic-demand shocks hit same households differently
~~~

## Neighboring institutions

Stone Hills side:

- guilds;
- mine / workshop employers;
- SH-A law / tax;
- craft credentialing.

Low Rivers side:

- villages / estates / cooperatives;
- LR-A law / tax;
- agricultural markets.

The contact community does not need one cross-border government.

It needs people who repeatedly make the systems **legible to one another**.

## Transmission carriers

- seasonal mine workers;
- repair workers;
- market traders;
- apprentices;
- cross-border households.

## Third-culture test

Passes because:

- dual agricultural / industrial calendar is structurally central;
- local foods / speech / law arise from that rhythm;
- neither a Stone Hills mine town nor a Low Rivers farm village would produce the same household economy.

## Weakness

Exact polity boundary / customs treatment is still provisional.

This does not block type-level promotion.

## Disposition

**P2-READY AS CONTACT-ZONE TYPE.**

# 2. CZ-2 — Longwood ↔ High Roads forest-pass

## Causal chain

~~~text
pass / road
+
forest / pasture use rights
→ travelers need local maintenance / guides
→ route authority overlaps but does not replace land-use authority
→ jurisdiction translators / route custodians
→ mixed forest / route / pastoral household livelihoods
→ hybrid food / speech / legend
→ road expansion creates conflict over what passage actually authorizes
~~~

## Neighboring institutions

High Roads:

- corridor compact;
- route authority;
- caravan / carrier systems;
- HR local polity.

Longwood:

- local use-right communities;
- wardens / specialists;
- compact / town authorities where relevant.

The key local rule:

> **road authority ends at the road unless another right has actually been granted.**

This is recoverable from existing Longwood / High Roads law.

## Transmission carriers

- guides;
- caravan workers;
- pastoralists;
- local forest workers;
- repair / lodging households.

## Third-culture test

Very strong.

The local role of **jurisdiction translation** does not exist in the same form inside either parent core.

The hybrid creature tradition also follows naturally from people who hear:

- forest;
- route;
- weather

stories from different users of the same terrain.

## Weakness

Exact pass site unresolved.

The type survives multiple site choices.

## Disposition

**P2-READY AS CONTACT-ZONE TYPE.**

# 3. CZ-3 — Low Rivers ↔ Old Cities water-market corridor

## Causal chain

~~~text
older irrigation / labor / market ties
→ political border formalized across working water landscape
→ households retain cross-border land / kin / work
→ canal maintenance requires recurring cooperation
→ brokers / witnesses translate legal traditions
→ mixed market cuisine / water vocabulary / household practice
→ drought + abundance increase pressure on same corridor
~~~

## Neighboring institutions

Low Rivers side:

- water-user associations;
- villages / estates;
- LR-A records / courts.

Old Cities side:

- irrigation compact / tribunals;
- city-state records;
- canal technical officials.

The contact zone can produce people trusted by both without creating a supranational bureaucracy.

## Transmission carriers

- canal labor;
- growers;
- produce merchants;
- seasonal workers;
- mixed households;
- technical water specialists.

## Third-culture test

Exceptional.

The local water vocabulary / maintenance practice / food economy derive from **cross-system use**, not aesthetic mixture.

The Marsh Lure / Charter Beast hybrid also follows from actual local conditions.

## Weakness

Exact canal / river relationship still needs future map/site choice.

Not a blocker to community type.

## Disposition

**P2-READY AS CONTACT-ZONE TYPE.**

# 4. Overall result

All three contact-zone types pass.

This materially strengthens the world rule:

> **cultures are not hard-bordered regional packages.**

The strongest refinement is:

> **contact zones are often old functional landscapes that political borders later cut across.**

That gives their mixed culture historical depth.

# 5. New E/I candidates

## A. Border specialists are an information network

People who repeatedly translate:

- contracts;
- measures;
- route permissions;
- water shares;
- wages

can know about cross-regional pressure unusually early.

This may create a decentralized information layer parallel to:

- guilds;
- merchants;
- pilgrims.

## B. Border law creates cultural vocabulary

Loanwords / mixed registers may concentrate around:

- obligations;
- measures;
- work;
- rights

before spreading into ordinary speech.

Language contact should therefore follow institutional need as well as marriage / fashion.

## C. Hybrid myth can preserve hybrid jurisdiction

The strongest contact-zone myths now encode:

- mine/farm subsidence;
- forest/road permission;
- cross-border water allocation.

This reinforces the existing E/I candidate:

> myth often preserves contested jurisdiction more strongly than cosmology.
