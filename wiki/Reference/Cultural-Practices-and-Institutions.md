## Source-checked cases

### 1. Sardinian *canto a tenore*: a group can be the instrument

**Real setting:** Within Sardinian pastoral culture, groups of four singers perform different vocal roles, including especially resonant low voices. Singing can happen spontaneously in local social places and also at weddings, shearing and religious gatherings.

**Mechanism:** A performance tradition is simultaneously leisure, collaborative trained skill, shared memory, social recognition and a marker of locality. Different roles matter: it is not 'everyone produces a spooky drone.'

**Questions for Two Sons:** Who knows each part? Can the singers perform while working? How do repertoire and social status vary across districts, age groups and migration routes? What happens when a guild or tavern imports one part but not the whole tradition?

**Evidence:** [UNESCO — Canto a tenore, Sardinian Pastoral Songs](https://www.unesco.org/archives/multimedia/document-1748). **No fictional singer style is canonized.**

### 2. Georgian polyphonic traditions: several techniques within a single country

**Real setting:** UNESCO distinguishes multiple regional forms, including complex harmonies, dialogue against a supporting bass, and contrasting or improvised parts. Singing crosses ceremonial and secular life.

**Mechanism:** A nation need not have one shared 'national music system'; different communities can develop historically distinct ensembles and transmission paths.

**Questions:** Which occupations, family networks, languages, religious settings or traveling performers transmit particular techniques? Can neighboring towns disagree over the 'correct' way to sing?

**Evidence:** [UNESCO — Georgian Polyphonic Singing](https://www.unesco.org/archives/multimedia/document-1737).

### 3. *Silbo Gomero*: geography changes the medium of speech

**Real setting:** In La Gomera, a whistled register historically transmitted Spanish language information across difficult terrain. It is taught and maintained as a named cultural practice, not a generic 'secret bird language.'

**Mechanism:** Communication technology need not be a new device. It may be a specialized register of a living language, transmitted through particular teachers and social conditions.

**Questions:** How far can a signal plausibly travel given real sound conditions? How do wind, interpretation, secrecy, class and education affect use? Is literacy required? Which institution trains specialists?

**Evidence:** [UNESCO — Silbo Gomero](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/whistled-language-of-the-island-of-la-gomera-canary-islands-the-silbo-gomero-00172). Avoid direct import of its actual sound system into a fictional language without careful design.

### 4. Inka *khipu* / *quipu*: records can be physical and trained

**Real setting:** Knot and cord arrangements were used for records in the Inka world, and specialists interpreted them. Knots, placement, material and colors can encode information. Scholars continue to debate the extent of non-numerical meaning; **do not treat all quipus as fully decoded narrative books**.

**Mechanism:** A society's trusted records can require tactile material, expert training, storage practices, authorized custody and institutional protocols different from modern paper offices.

**Questions:** Who may make, inspect, duplicate and challenge a record? What is forgery? Can a witness disagree with a trained recordkeeper? What does moisture or fire destroy?

**Evidence:** [Smithsonian — Quipu](https://www.si.edu/collections/snapshot/quipu); [Smithsonian NMAI catalog object](https://americanindian.si.edu/collections-search/object/NMAI_154591); [UNESCO — Khipu Database](https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/lac/khipu-database-khipu-archives).

### 5. Balinese *subak*: ritual and irrigation are also governance

**Real setting:** Cooperative water management networks of farmers, canals, weirs, terraces and water temples govern distribution across connected watersheds. Religious meaning, engineering, coordination and landscape are intertwined.

**Mechanism:** Technical infrastructure creates rights, dependencies, calendars, shared maintenance, conflicts and ritual forms. Neither 'priests control water magically' nor 'farmers build a canal' captures the complete social institution.

**Questions:** Who decides upstream/diversion priority? What obligations maintain a sluice? Who arbitrates drought? How does a seasonal festival reinforce collective maintenance? What happens when an absentee owner withdraws labor?

**Evidence:** [UNESCO World Heritage — Balinese Subak System](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1194/); [UNESCO case study — Water Temples](https://ich.unesco.org/en/individual-case-study-00988?id=00013).

### 6. Trobriand *Kula*: not every exchange is a purchase

**Real setting:** Ethnography distinguishes ceremonial prestige gift circulation from ordinary bargaining for consumable goods, sometimes described with separate terms (Kula and gimwali).

**Mechanism:** Valuables can circulate to maintain alliances, status and obligations without being simply bought for a fixed commodity price. Trade and ceremonial reciprocity may coexist.

**Questions:** Which objects carry histories? Can a trader be commercially wealthy but excluded from the prestige circuit? What obligation persists after an object moves? Who keeps track?

**Evidence:** [Yale Human Relations Area Files — Kula Ring case study](https://hraf.yale.edu/teach-ehraf/reciprocity-exchange-the-kula-ring/index.html). Ethnography has its own history and should be read critically.

### 7. Yoruba *Ifá*: apprenticeship, corpus and interpreted signs

**Real setting:** An extensive corpus of verses and signs is interpreted by trained specialists. The practice has a specific Yoruba religious and philosophical context and is also found in diaspora communities.

**Mechanism:** Skilled knowledge can depend on long learning, recital, a symbolic grammar, contested interpretation and trusted advisers. It is **not** a generic mechanical 'fortune-telling machine'.

**Questions:** How is a specialist credentialed and corrected? Who can request advice? How can interpretations shape decisions without making the world magically deterministic? What role does oral learning play?

**Evidence:** [UNESCO — Ifá Divination System](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ifa-divination-system-00146). **Sacred content should not be lifted into fantasy incantations.**

### 8. Indonesian *wayang*: traveling stories are mutable public institutions

**Real setting:** Indonesian puppet theatre encompasses different material forms, regional styles and music. Performance traditions flourished in court and village contexts and change as stories travel.

**Mechanism:** A storytelling practice contains equipment, skilled craft workers, musicians, patronage, apprenticeships, performance locations and changing interpretations. It can address contemporary anxieties through older narratives.

**Questions:** Who funds a performance? Who builds and repairs figures? Can a character become political satire? How far does one troupe's interpretation spread?

**Evidence:** [UNESCO — Wayang Puppet Theatre](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/wayang-00063); [UNESCO safeguarding programme](https://ich.unesco.org/en/projects/implementation-of-the-national-action-plan-for-the-safeguarding-of-the-wayang-puppet-theatre-of-indonesia-00020).

### 9. Garifuna traditions: migration can yield a new culture rather than a 'blend' on a map

**Real setting:** Garifuna communities in Central America preserve language, oral stories, song and dance shaped by Indigenous Caribbean and African histories and forced displacement. Songs can carry accounts of work, livelihoods, history and satire.

**Mechanism:** A durable cultural identity may arise from displacement, generation-to-generation practice, language, music, work and shared memory. The result is a culture in its own right, not a permanent 50/50 mixture.

**Questions:** How do fishing methods, remembered origins, institutions and regional neighbors shape music? How does migration change the words but not necessarily social function? Who remembers what others omit?

**Evidence:** [UNESCO — Language, Dance and Music of the Garifuna](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/language-dance-and-music-of-the-garifuna-00001).

### 10. Bedu cultural space at Petra and Wadi Rum: mobile and settled neighbors share knowledge

**Real setting:** UNESCO describes complementary settled and nomadic Bedu communities whose environmental expertise includes pastoral knowledge, camel husbandry, tent craft, tracking, climbing, and orally transmitted poetry/history tied to places. Settlement and tourism affect the transmission of skills.

**Mechanism:** Movement is part of a negotiated relationship among households, seasonal environments, permanent installations, and visitors. People can change residence patterns without simply losing their identity.

**Questions:** Who may use a cistern? Who controls springs or shelter? Which stories mark inherited access? When do tourism or commercial demand distort a craft's function?

**Evidence:** [UNESCO — Cultural Space of the Bedu](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/cultural-space-of-the-bedu-in-petra-and-wadi-rum-00122); [UNESCO safeguarding project](https://ich.unesco.org/en/projects/action-plan-for-the-safeguarding-promotion-and-development-of-the-cultural-space-of-the-bedu-in-the-regions-of-petra-and-wadi-rum-00029).

### 11. Traditional Ainu dance: ecological relationships without reduction to 'forest mysticism'

**Real setting:** Ainu dance is performed in varied contexts including ceremonial, festive and daily settings; traditions include circle dances, imitations of animals and ritual forms. Connections between specific religious ceremonies, human-animal relationships and local performance matter.

**Mechanism:** One form of performance may reinforce spiritual meaning, social identity and common enjoyment simultaneously, with internal variations rather than a single universal ritual.

**Questions:** Which celebrations have sacred restrictions? Who learns dances and from whom? How do changing livelihoods transform public performance?

**Evidence:** [UNESCO — Traditional Ainu Dance](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-ainu-dance-00278). Sensitive ritual detail should be understood from Ainu perspectives before any fictional borrowing.

### 12. Vanuatu sand drawing: memory and communication can be temporary

**Real setting:** Some communities use complex geometric drawings in sand, ash or clay, created in connection with communication, stories and other transmitted knowledge.

**Mechanism:** A medium can be **ephemeral but rigorously structured**. Destroying the drawing may not destroy its orally carried meaning; the performer and audience are part of the record.

**Questions:** Who is qualified to draw? Who has permission to reproduce a pattern? Can a visual form function as a mnemonic for stories, law or routes? How does transmission survive schooling changes?

**Evidence:** [UNESCO — Safeguarding of Vanuatu Sand Drawings](https://ich.unesco.org/en/projects/safeguarding-of-vanuatu-sand-drawings-00022). Some designs can be culturally restricted; this is a research example, not a library of patterns to copy.

### 13. Tongan *lakalaka*: ceremonial speech can be choreographed

**Real setting:** *Lakalaka* combines choreography, sung speech and layered vocal expression, with public ceremonial functions.

**Mechanism:** A community's formal public discourse can be performed collectively, with skilled coordination and significance beyond entertainment.

**Questions:** Does a political announcement need witnesses, singers or a patron? How would a ceremony change after dynastic conflict or migration? Who gets invited or excluded?

**Evidence:** [UNESCO — Lakalaka](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/lakalaka-dances-and-sung-speeches-of-tonga-00072).

## Research discipline

For any future addition record: (a) source and date accessed; (b) who is described and whose perspective is recorded; (c) what the practice accomplishes; (d) where and when it varies; (e) any sacred/restricted dimensions; (f) what is uncertain or contested; (g) what design question it might answer in Two Sons. Keep source quote lengths minimal and record interpretation in your own words.
