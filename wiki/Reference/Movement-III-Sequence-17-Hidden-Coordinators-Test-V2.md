# Prose test

The Finance representative knew the meeting was real because nobody had prepared a room for it.

The table belonged to a shipping house.

The chairs did not match.

Someone had moved a ledger cabinet far enough to fit another person against the wall.

Three principals were present.

A fourth house had sent an agent with authority to negotiate one part of the problem.

Other networks had sent written summaries.

That was enough.

Usually.

---

The Routes representative arrived last.

"Your roads are counting guards now."

"They are not my roads."

"They are counting guards."

"Yes."

"Cargo too."

"Broad class."

"Departure windows."

"Yes."

"Available escort providers."

"Yes."

The Materials representative said:

"Useful."

The Finance representative looked at them.

"Everyone keeps saying that."

"Because it is."

"That is not reassurance."

"No one offered reassurance."

Good.

They were all tired.

That improved meetings.

---

The Finance representative spread four summaries on the table.

Low Rivers:

- weak prices;
- uneven storage;
- forward purchasing rising.

Old Cities buyers:

- moving earlier.

Port guarantors:

- favoring contracts with recognized storage and protected transport.

High Roads:

- safer on guarded departures;
- more expensive;
- more legible.

Stone Hills:

- protected allocation becoming formal enough that outsiders were asking what it meant for access.

The Routes representative read the transport column.

"Half of this is timing."

"More."

"How much more?"

"We do not know."

The Materials representative tapped another sheet.

"Metal buyers are doing the same thing."

"Metal is not grain."

"No. Fear is."

The Finance representative disliked the phrase.

It was too neat.

They kept it anyway.

---

The Provision report had arrived by courier.

It listed:

- grain under contract;
- estimated open storage;
- distressed seller volume;
- large buyer options;
- public reserve positions where known.

The Finance representative read one line aloud.

"Several Old Cities buyers have advanced purchases beyond normal timing."

The Routes representative asked:

"Drought?"

"Partly."

"Actual shortage?"

"Not yet."

The Old Cities agent against the wall said:

"Water concern is real. Buying is ahead of the measurements."

"Why?"

The Materials representative asked it as if they did not know.

The Finance representative answered:

"Because the buyer who waits may find storage, carriage, or guarantees already committed."

"Reasonable."

"Yes."

The Routes representative looked at the storage figures.

"And if everyone is reasonable?"

The Finance representative pointed.

"That."

---

No one called it panic.

Panic was emotional.

This was contractual.

That made it harder to stop.

The Materials representative said:

"Let price clear it."

The Provision agent answered:

"Price is clearing land before grain."

"That is not the same thing."

"It is if you are the seller."

"Not institutionally."

The Routes representative said:

"Everything becomes somebody else's problem if the circle is small enough."

The Materials representative looked at them.

"Everything becomes ours if it is large enough."

That was also true.

Nobody liked both truths in the same room.

---

They rejected the first idea quickly.

More guarantees alone would:

- reward early buyers;
- consume more storage;
- make the same actors even easier to finance.

The second idea survived longer.

Release some reserved storage.

Provision could do that through its networks.

Not much.

Enough to change one market week.

The cost was real.

Materials supported the sacrifice enthusiastically because it was not theirs.

Provision ignored them.

Storage alone would refill.

So Finance proposed limited staged-purchase credit.

Not:

> buy everything now.

Instead:

> financing remains available if delivery is spread across agreed dates.

Routes could support some future transport windows through ordinary contracts.

Not command them.

Not reserve the whole corridor.

Enough to make later delivery believable.

Old Cities technical contacts could accelerate circulation of already-public measurements.

No unified statement.

No Council reassurance.

Just:

- faster copies;
- trusted commercial circulation;
- data that already existed.

The Materials representative committed almost nothing.

Finance noticed.

"You are enjoying this."

"I am watching the rest of you subsidize food."

"Your turn comes."

"It always does."

---

No formal vote followed.

Relevant networks either acted or did not.

They acted.

Provision released several storage blocks.

One was taken almost immediately by a large buyer with better clerks than everyone else.

The second remained available long enough for several smaller sellers to use it.

The third was never fully released because a warehouse partner refused the loss.

Finance offered staged-purchase credit.

Large, well-recorded buyers accepted quickly.

Some smaller buyers did not qualify.

One complained publicly that the new "relief" simply gave the best-connected houses cheaper patience.

They were not entirely wrong.

Routes secured several later carriage windows.

That eased immediate booking pressure.

It also made those particular carriers more attractive counterparties.

The first contracts using them received better financing.

That was not neutral either.

Old Cities measurements circulated faster.

Some merchants reduced extreme reserve assumptions.

Others said:

> of course the cities say there is no emergency.

A rumor did not disappear merely because a measurement contradicted it.

Still, the worst buying spiral weakened.

That mattered.

Not everywhere.

Enough.

---

Then one buyer did something the model did not explain.

An Old Cities house continued buying early even after:

- water reports improved slightly;
- storage opened;
- later finance became available.

Finance assumed:

- bad information;
- stubborn risk preference.

The Old Cities agent checked.

Neither.

The house had received:

> a separate political assurance that future food access would be protected through a particular external relationship.

The Finance representative read the note.

"From whom?"

"Not confirmed."

"Council?"

"No indication."

"State?"

"Maybe."

"Commercial house?"

"Could be."

The Materials representative smiled.

"What?"

"You built a market model."

"Yes."

"They built a political one."

The Finance representative disliked that more than they should have.

Not because it was clever.

Because it meant the early buying pattern had begun changing categories.

Some buyers were no longer only asking:

> will grain exist?

They were asking:

> **who will still sell to us if access becomes political?**

That was different.

---

Before leaving, the Routes representative put High Roads movement counts on the table.

Not all of them.

Enough.

The Materials representative read.

"That many private guards?"

"Inside guarded windows."

"Available escorts?"

"Recognized providers."

"Same difference if someone pays."

"No."

"Close."

The Finance representative placed the sheet beside:

- Stone Hills protected-share notices;
- North Coast convoy renewals;
- Low Rivers guarantees;
- Old Cities early-purchase commitments.

Several systems were learning the same grammar.

Protected.

Recognized.

Priority.

Guaranteed.

"Are we looking at blocs?" Finance asked.

Materials answered:

"No."

Too quickly.

Routes said:

"Not yet."

Better.

Provision asked:

"What makes it yes?"

Finance looked at the papers.

"Same partners."

Materials frowned.

"When the same people start guaranteeing more than one essential thing."

Nobody argued immediately.

That was the first worrying part.

---

A house clerk brought the loss summary the next week.

"Storage release cost us."

"I know."

"Bridge credit too."

"I know."

"Routes charged us for the later windows."

"I know."

The clerk looked at the page.

"Who approved taking all three losses?"

Finance answered automatically:

"We did."

The clerk waited.

Finance realized the question had meant:

> which principal;
> which customer;
> which authorized account.

Not:

> which private group decided the market needed this.

"Charge them where the agreements say," Finance added.

The clerk left.

The answer remained in the room.

> **We did.**

That was true too.

Finance returned to the market reports.

The intervention had reduced risk.

It had also favored:

- legible buyers;
- recognized carriers;
- people already inside networks capable of being coordinated.

They had not intended that as a political choice.

Intent was becoming less useful.
