# Current Events Legacy Reconciliation

## Status

**Active reference / event quarry — not an alternate current-events canon.**

Source:

- [Volume 10 — Current Events](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/10_Current_Events.md)

Current owners:

- [Current Events](../Story/Current-Events.md)
- [Villain's Dominoes](../Story/Villains-Dominoes.md)
- [Grounded Domino Design Method](Grounded-Domino-Design.md)
- [World System](../World-System.md)

The current-events owner remains authoritative for what is actually happening near the opening of the story.

Volume 10 is useful mainly as:

- alternate pressure-chain research;
- second- and third-order consequence quarry;
- possible local incidents;
- examples of how guilds, religions, merchants and political actors respond differently to the same event;
- examples of what **not** to do when every crisis becomes part of one conspiracy.

---

# 1. Main salvage result

The best legacy current-events material uses this pattern:

```text
ordinary disruption
→ economic consequence
→ institutional response
→ rumor / blame
→ political consequence
→ opportunistic exploitation
```

That remains compatible with the current story.

The weakest material uses:

```text
spectacular event
→ secret Villain plot
→ secret Council plot
→ guild conspiracy
→ magical complication
→ hero quest
```

for nearly everything.

The world should contain causality that exists independently of both the Council and the Villain.

# 2. Event classes

A useful classification for current events is:

## Natural / material

Examples:
- bad winter;
- fish-stock shift;
- flood;
- crop disease;
- mine collapse;
- landslide;
- Underpass gas pocket;
- drought;
- storm damage.

These can be:
- genuinely natural;
- worsened by human decisions;
- misinterpreted as sabotage;
- exploited after the fact.

## Economic / institutional

Examples:
- guild walkout;
- tariff dispute;
- failed contract;
- debt crisis;
- convoy refusal;
- insurance tightening;
- warehouse loss;
- merchant summit;
- route repricing.

These can emerge from legitimate conflicting incentives.

## Political

Examples:
- emergency council;
- rival city-state coalition;
- disputed border enforcement;
- ruler bypassing a treaty mechanism;
- competing relief plans;
- jurisdictional conflict.

## Deliberately manipulated

Examples:
- false documents;
- timed rumor;
- selective financing;
- targeted sabotage;
- covert support to an existing faction;
- strategic release of true information.

This category should be a **subset**, not the explanation for everything.

# 3. Strong alternate incident seeds

The following legacy events remain useful as provisional local incidents.

They are **not established current events** unless promoted into [Current Events](../Story/Current-Events.md).

## Mine collapse

Useful because it creates:
- rescue pressure;
- worker anger;
- safety investigation;
- production loss;
- insurance / debt problems;
- family hardship;
- accusations of negligence or sabotage.

It can intersect Stone Hills labor unrest without being caused by the Villain.

## Severe winter

Useful because it can:
- interrupt shipping;
- increase food demand;
- expose poor reserves;
- create political pressure for emergency imports;
- alter migration.

Do not automatically make it a continent-wide famine.

## Flood / crop rot

Useful for:
- local food loss;
- transport damage;
- blame over water management;
- relief politics;
- temporary labor displacement.

This is a different pressure from the current Low Rivers abundance crisis and should not be merged into it unless deliberately chosen.

## Underpass gas leak / bad air

One of the stronger physical-event seeds.

Possible consequences:
- tunnel closure;
- worker deaths;
- rerouting;
- quarantine-like access rules;
- argument over ventilation / maintenance;
- smuggling through unsafe passages;
- competing technical explanations.

No supernatural cause is required.

## Guild walkout

Useful at any large works project, mine, port, construction site or workshop district.

The important questions are:
- what grievance;
- who owns the work;
- who loses money;
- who can replace the workers;
- whether another guild honors the walkout.

## Emergency merchant summit

Useful as an institutional response to:
- insurance failure;
- route disruption;
- tariffs;
- missing cargo;
- unstable credit.

A summit can fail because participants have incompatible interests without sabotage.

## Independent rerouting

Legacy merchants repeatedly try to bypass:
- taxes;
- Council-linked routes;
- unsafe corridors;
- political restrictions.

That is useful because successful bypasses can permanently weaken old chokepoints.

## Refugee / migrant movement

Useful second-order effect of:
- conflict;
- economic collapse;
- route closure;
- ecological loss.

Migration should create:
- housing pressure;
- labor supply changes;
- new cultural scenes;
- charity demands;
- political backlash;
- new kin / trade connections.

# 4. Guild reactions worth keeping

The legacy volume repeatedly treats guilds as actors with their own incentives.

Keep that.

A guild may:
- strike;
- remain neutral;
- accept emergency contracts;
- refuse dangerous work;
- split internally;
- use a crisis to demand legal privileges;
- build an alternate supply relationship;
- provide relief to members;
- cooperate across borders.

Do not reduce guild reactions to:
- "supports Council";
- "supports Villain";
- "supports monarchy."

The same guild can contain all three positions plus members who care mainly about wages and safety.

---

# 5. Religious reactions worth keeping

Volume 10 is useful when religions respond to events through their actual institutions.

Possible responses include:
- charity;
- mediation;
- record comparison;
- ecological documentation;
- counseling;
- pilgrimage disruption;
- public sermons / interpretation;
- shelter;
- funeral work.

The current [Current Events](../Story/Current-Events.md) owner already uses this approach.

Do not restore legacy plots where religious sects exist only as secret conspiracy nodes.

---

# 6. Rumor and attribution

A strong recurring legacy mechanism is disagreement over cause.

For one event, different people may blame:

- Council manipulation;
- a rival polity;
- guild corruption;
- smugglers;
- divine judgment;
- environmental mismanagement;
- bad luck;
- the Villain;
- ordinary incompetence.

Some explanations can contain pieces of truth.

The story becomes stronger when characters act on **plausible but incomplete attribution**.

# 7. Second-order consequences

Volume 10 contains many useful ripple ideas even when the original event is obsolete.

Examples:

```text
shipping disruption
→ higher insurance / credit cost
→ merchants cancel marginal orders
→ producers lose buyers
→ debt defaults rise
→ land / workshops consolidate
→ protests spread
```

```text
route closure
→ traffic shifts
→ bypassed town loses income
→ replacement route gains labor demand
→ housing prices rise there
→ smuggling changes geography
→ old route constituency demands intervention
```

```text
guild strike
→ project delay
→ another industry lacks inputs
→ emergency subcontractors enter
→ quality dispute
→ legal fight over liability
```

The exact chain should be rebuilt from current owners, but the **cross-domain consequence habit** is worth retaining.

# 8. Wurdren's role

The legacy material often makes Wurdren solve a local problem whose consequences ripple outward.

That structural idea is useful.

But avoid:
- every local act secretly defeating part of the Villain's master plan;
- Wurdren being the only person capable of restoring systems;
- quest-like assignments that exist only to move him to the next region.

A better model:

Wurdren can:
- help a household;
- uncover evidence;
- prevent one immediate harm;
- choose between legitimate obligations;
- accidentally strengthen one institution;
- create consequences he does not see.

The world keeps moving without him.

---

# 9. Council use of crises

The legacy source often makes the Council create crises.

The later Council review is better.

Preferred pattern:

```text
real crisis
→ Council identifies coordination problem
→ intervention solves one problem
→ intervention creates dependency / resentment / side effect
→ someone exploits that consequence
```

The Council can still deliberately manipulate events.

It simply should not need to manufacture every disruption.

---

# 10. Villain use of crises

Likewise, the Villain is strongest when exploiting:

- grievances;
- bad timing;
- existing factionalism;
- shortages;
- fear;
- contradictory records;
- failed institutions.

Useful actions include:
- encouraging a strike at a strategically damaging moment;
- funding one faction;
- releasing selected evidence;
- redirecting smuggling;
- creating a forged document;
- exploiting a real disaster.

This keeps the Villain capable without turning the setting into a stage set.

---

# 11. Legacy event seeds to reject / hold

Do not promote without separate support:

- **Starlight Harvest** / cosmic-energy economy;
- large-scale mechanical harvesters;
- advanced rail sabotage where railways are not established;
- alchemical miracle cures that revolutionize entire industries;
- magical crop / forest attacks;
- ancient prophecies driving present politics;
- powerful relic hunts inserted merely to add fantasy spectacle;
- magical assassins / secret superweapons.

The newer [Magic Baseline](../Magic-Baseline.md) is authoritative.

---

# 12. Avoid conspiracy saturation

Volume 10 repeatedly layers:

- Council conspiracy;
- Villain conspiracy;
- guild conspiracy;
- religious conspiracy;
- ruler conspiracy

onto the same event.

That weakens the world.

Use three categories instead:

1. **causal actor** — actually produced the event;
2. **responsive actor** — acts because of the event;
3. **opportunist** — takes advantage of the event.

Different actors can change categories during the chain.

---

# 13. Event-state rule

Any legacy event should be labeled before use:

- **CURRENT** — promoted into Current Events;
- **POSSIBLE** — compatible but not happening yet;
- **HISTORICAL** — happened earlier;
- **DISCARDED** — incompatible;
- **MECHANISM ONLY** — useful causal pattern, event itself rejected.

This prevents old outlines from silently becoming present-tense canon.

---

# 14. Promotion test

Before promoting a Volume 10 event:

1. Does it fit the current technology baseline?
2. Does it fit the current magic baseline?
3. Does it fit current geography?
4. Which polity / institution owns the problem?
5. Is there a mundane explanation?
6. Is the Villain necessary?
7. Is the Council necessary?
8. What happens to ordinary people first?
9. What second-order effect follows?
10. Does it duplicate an existing current event?
11. Does it create a new kind of pressure?
12. Does it remain interesting if no conspiracy is revealed?

If not, leave it in the quarry.
