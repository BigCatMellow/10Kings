## Summary

**Local-politics structure passes. The alternate-turn discovery is too convenient.**

Everything important is working:

- delegate ≠ community;
- road is useful;
- local rights are specific;
- pro-road and anti-expansion locals coexist;
- Wurdren learns by correction;
- physical site changes the argument.

The weak point:

> the upslope turn appears only when someone happens to ask "what about there?"

That makes compromise feel like a solution the world forgot to notice until the protagonist arrived.

---

# 1. Make the alternate route already known

The local guide / road custodian should say:

> **"We showed them the upper turn last season."**

The route surveyor / representative should answer:

> it was rejected because:
> - steeper grade;
> - more retaining work;
> - slower carts;
> - harder winter maintenance;
> - higher cost.

This transforms the choice from:

> nobody thought of the obvious solution

into:

> **everyone knew the solution, but the cheaper plan externalized its cost onto a local right / wet ground.**

That is much stronger.

---

# 2. Keep Wurdren away from technical discovery

Wurdren should not:

- spot the route;
- solve drainage;
- become an engineer.

His role remains:

- ask people to state actual claims;
- keep conversation intact;
- accept corrections;
- notice when "impossible" means "more expensive."

That last point is social / linguistic, not technical.

---

# 3. Increase alternate-route cost

The upper line should have at least two real drawbacks:

- more retaining work;
- steeper approach / slower loaded carts;
- more maintenance after heavy rain / frost;
- additional construction time.

Do not make it obviously superior.

A later compromise can still select it because:

> avoiding a recurring rights / drainage conflict has value.

---

# 4. Preserve the wet-ground stakes

The lower turn should remain attractive because it is:

- flatter;
- cheaper;
- faster to construct.

The local objection is therefore not:

> outsiders chose a stupid route.

It is:

> outsiders chose the cheapest route because the ecological / use-right cost landed on people outside the contract.

That fits the larger story extremely well without becoming allegory.
