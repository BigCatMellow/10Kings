# Prose test

The Villain learned about the OC-C arrangements through commercial channels.

That was normal.

Food contracts moved quickly.

Shipping reservations moved quickly.

Guarantee references moved quickly.

Diplomatic explanations did not.

That difference was not his invention.

It was merely useful.

The commercial picture was accurate:

- OC-C had secured future Low Rivers food;
- a familiar Port guarantor supported staged purchases;
- several North Coast-linked carriers appeared in its future shipping arrangements.

No defense agreement.

No secret military clause.

No formal bloc.

But commercial reports had begun describing the relationships together because people needed to know:

> who could still deliver if ordinary access tightened.

He read the latest report.

Then the note that OC-C had been asked for a diplomatic explanation.

No reply had reached OC-U yet.

It would.

Eventually.

The question was what happened first.

---

He sent no secret contract.

Nothing stolen.

Nothing altered.

A commercial-intelligence broker already sold:

- market comparisons;
- foreign contract notices;
- shipping commitments

to several clients.

The Villain paid for speed.

That was all.

The broker's OC-U client received the verified overlap report before the ordinary diplomatic channel delivered OC-C's reassurance.

The Villain knew that would happen.

That was why he paid.

---

The OC-U official who received the report did not think:

> enemy.

They thought:

> how much time do we have?

That distinction mattered.

The commercial report showed three separate facts:

- future food;
- future credit;
- future carriage.

The same names did not appear everywhere.

Enough names repeated to suggest reliability.

A merchant official read the page.

"Good planning."

The first official looked up.

"For them."

"For anyone."

"If we wait until access actually tightens, can we assemble the same?"

"Probably."

"Probably when?"

The merchant official did not answer.

That was the problem.

---

They met with:

- a food official;
- a water-system official;
- two merchant representatives;
- one public-works contracting clerk.

Not soldiers.

Not military command.

The first official proposed:

> a temporary review of commercial capacity already tied to the city.

The water official objected immediately.

"Do not call this an emergency inventory."

"I did not."

"It will be called that."

"Then give me a better term."

"Contract review."

The merchant representative said:

"That sounds like taxation."

"Everything sounds like taxation if you dislike the clerk."

The public-works clerk ignored them all.

"What do you actually want?"

The official answered:

"For major actors already inside city agreements:

- how much contracted storage is free or committed;
- how much future food is already promised elsewhere;
- what carriage capacity our own contracts can actually claim;
- which emergency repairs depend on outside material;
- which guaranteed purchases overlap with the same counterparties."

"Why?"

"Because we keep saying we can buy elsewhere."

The official tapped the commercial report.

"I want to know what 'elsewhere' still means."

---

The water official drew the boundary.

"This does not become an assessment of private households."

"No."

"Not ordinary carts."

"No."

"Not reserve animals."

"No."

"No requisition authority."

"We do not have it."

"Good."

"And the water compact with OC-D continues."

The first official looked at them.

"Nobody proposed ending it."

"I am saying it before somebody reads this report and does."

The merchant representative nodded.

"Put that in the order."

They did.

The temporary review applied only to:

- major city-licensed storage;
- merchants already holding city supply / emergency contracts;
- carriers under city-linked commercial agreements;
- contractors responsible for large public repairs;
- counterparties voluntarily seeking city guarantees.

Some reporting was required by existing contract.

Some requested.

Some refused.

The city did not force the refusal.

The result would be incomplete.

That was accepted.

---

The diplomatic reply from OC-C arrived the next morning.

> **No defense obligation is created by these commercial arrangements.**

The water official held it up.

"There."

The first official read it.

"I believe them."

That was not the response the Villain's commercial contact had expected.

The merchant representative said:

"So cancel the review?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because the contracts still exist."

"Commercially."

"Yes."

The first official looked at the original report.

"The question was never whether they promised war."

The water official said:

"Good. Then say that publicly too."

They did.

A short notice accompanied the contract review:

> **The city has no evidence that OC-C's commercial continuity arrangements create a defense obligation or hostile commitment. Existing technical cooperation with OC-D continues.**

That mattered.

The first interpretation had failed to harden into hostility.

The review continued anyway.

---

OC-C heard about the review through merchants.

The first account said:

> OC-U is counting grain.

The second:

> OC-U is checking every wagon.

The third:

> OC-U is preparing requisitions.

The OC-C official rejected all three and requested the actual notice.

That took time.

A shipping intermediary arrived before the notice did.

"Your future windows."

"What about them?"

"I can still hold the current number."

"Still?"

"Other buyers are asking."

"Because of OC-U?"

"Because of everything."

The official disliked answers that could not be disproved.

"How long?"

"Tomorrow."

The official looked at the existing shipping arrangement.

Then at the empty place where the OC-U notice should have been.

They confirmed:

- part of the future carriage;
- not the full option;
- no new exclusivity.

"Preserve the rest if you can."

"I can if someone pays."

"Of course."

The intermediary left.

---

The real OC-U notice arrived later.

It was narrower than rumor.

Much narrower.

No wagons counted unless they were already under city-linked agreement.

No household assets.

No requisition.

No military category.

The OC-C official read the accompanying reassurance.

Then the contract-review categories.

A colleague said:

"They said we're not hostile."

"Yes."

"So we moved early for nothing."

The official looked at the shipping confirmation.

"No."

"Why?"

"They still want to know what they can count on."

"So?"

"We just did the same thing."

The colleague considered that.

"Then maybe both are sensible."

"That is the problem."

---

OC-U's review produced less than its strongest supporters wanted.

Several merchants refused voluntary detail.

One carrier relationship turned out to be far less available than officials had assumed.

A warehouse recorded as major emergency capacity had already promised most of its open space to private contracts.

The city learned:

- less than it wanted;
- more than it had known.

Most importantly, officials discovered which answers depended on:

- the same guarantors;
- the same carriers;
- the same handful of future sellers.

Not a war map.

A dependence map.

Still useful.

---

The Villain received two reports.

OC-U:

- review narrowed;
- no hostile declaration;
- water cooperation reaffirmed;
- commercial-capacity picture improving.

OC-C:

- partial future shipping confirmed before clarification;
- no exclusivity;
- no defense commitment.

He read them both.

One intended line had failed.

Good.

OC-U had refused the easiest interpretation.

That meant the people involved were still thinking.

He preferred dangerous systems populated by competent people.

They produced better evidence.

The part that mattered had survived:

- OC-U knew more about what it could count on;
- OC-C had paid to preserve more access;
- each now possessed a reason to watch the other's next commercial precaution.

He marked the branch:

> **SELF-MOVING**

Then stopped touching it.

---

In Port, a broker told a buyer:

"Use the North Coast carriers. They already know the guarantor."

The buyer asked:

"Whose guarantor?"

"Yours if you sign."

At another desk, a merchant said:

"OC-C has first call on some later grain."

"All of it?"

"No."

"Then we still have supply."

"If the seller honors us too."

Nobody called these relationships alliances.

They were simply becoming the names people reached for when asked:

> who will still answer if the ordinary market does not?

That was enough.
