# Realism Check, 2026-11

## Status

**Review record, not setting canon.** bt 7274 asked on 2026-11-13 ([D48](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)): "is this world actually funtional and realistic? could it actually work for people in this world to survive and thrive? I want ot make sure that the world is coherent". This page records what the check found. The map fixes it made are listed below; everything marked *lean* is for bt 7274 to overrule, and the open decisions are also on [Open Questions](../Open-Questions.md#map).

## Verdict

**Yes, the world works.** How people feed themselves, what they trade, and why they need each other all hold up, and each region's way of life has real precedents. What the world lacked was a physical layer: no scale, no stated source for its weather, and a map whose water didn't behave. The map's water errors are fixed. Five decisions remain, and the first, the map's scale, is the one that matters most.

## How it was checked

- **The lore:** [World Rules](../World-Rules.md), [World Overview](../World-Overview.md), [Geography](../Geography-and-Connections.md), [Trade and Dependencies](../Economy/Trade-and-Dependencies.md), the [Economic Council](../Politics/Economic-Council.md), [Port](../Places/Port.md), the six region pages and [Peoples and Land](../Peoples-and-Land.md). Each region was checked for food, water, fuel and what it trades, against how real pre-industrial societies managed the same problems.
- **The [map draft](../../atlas/map/README.md), measured:**
  - whether each named sea, sound and channel actually reaches the ocean;
  - whether each named river runs from high ground to water;
  - whether the land is one piece;
  - travel distances, using the cheapest route over land and by sea.

**The systems hold too.**

- The Council's leverage through storage, credit and routes is how the Fuggers, the Medici and the Hanseatic League really held power.
- Magic is too rare and too uncertain to replace logistics, so nothing industrial needs explaining away.
- Highridge's caravans have a reason to exist. At the lean scale, a voyage from Port round to Northwind's north coast takes about as long as the road over the plateau. So the two routes compete, and every storm season or pirate scare shifts trade onto Highridge's roads.

## Decisions for bt 7274

### 1. The map's scale

The map has no scale, and its placements imply two different ones.

- **A small world.** These placements imply a world about 250 miles across:
  - the Straight Road, which the wiki gives as "forty miles", drawn as 98 pixels;
  - the Ship on the Hill, "forty miles from the sea", drawn 90 pixels inland;
  - Fairmarket, "a day above Port";
  - Larchmere, "a day's walk" from Twilighthollow (from [The Blank Square](../Sunday-Morning/Stories/The-Blank-Square.md)).

  A world that size runs out of room for its climate: seas that partly freeze in the north and dry orchards that need irrigation in the south would sit less than 250 miles apart.
- **A larger world.** Cold northern seas and a dry, Mediterranean south need roughly the distance from the Baltic to Provence.

*Lean:* **about 1.8 miles to the map's pixel** (revised from 1.5 by [D50](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions): it puts Northwind's coast near 56° north, far enough for its Aurora Nights, and the Sunplains tip near 44°, a Mediterranean summer; [Sky, Seasons and Sea](../Sky-Seasons-and-Sea.md#the-year-and-the-light)). The land would be about 970 miles north to south and 1,070 miles east to west, roughly France, Spain, Italy and Germany together.

| What follows from the lean | |
| --- | --- |
| The climate needs no special pleading | the latitudes do most of the work |
| Population | about 20 to 25 million at medieval densities. Port 100,000–150,000, the size of Venice at its height |
| The 145 settlements on the map | the notable ones only: real country at this size holds thousands of villages |
| Travel between the great cities | weeks, not days (table below), which suits "travel takes time" ([World Rules](../World-Rules.md#5-low-fantasy-means-material-limits-matter)) |

The map has a scale bar. No Season 1 story changes |

Riders and courier relays move three to six times faster than carts. Barges move faster downstream than upstream.

### 2. Where the rain comes from

Nothing says it yet. The map's order, cold in the north, temperate in the middle and warm and dry in the south, fits latitude. One spot would make a geographer look twice: Deepwood's wet forest sits right beside Sunplains' irrigated country, with nothing between them.

**Tested with a rain model** ([D49](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)). `python3 atlas/tools/rain_model.py map <bearing>` blows wet air in off the sea from one direction over the map's height model, rains it out where the land rises, and lets land (forest most) hand some back. It writes [`rain-model.png`](../../atlas/map/rain-model.png).

| Wet air from | Wettest → driest, at each region's settlements | Fit with the wiki (rank correlation) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the west-north-west (300°) | Northwind, Greenvale, Deepwood, Highridge, Ironcrest, Sunplains | **0.66, the best** |
| the north (0°) | Northwind, Ironcrest, Greenvale, Deepwood, Sunplains, Highridge | 0.60 |
| due west (270°) | Greenvale, Northwind, Sunplains, Highridge, Deepwood, Ironcrest | 0.09: Sunplains wetter than Deepwood |
| the east (90°) | Northwind, Sunplains, Greenvale, Ironcrest, Deepwood, Highridge | 0.26 |

What it shows:
- **North-west weather fits.** Northwind's coasts and Greenvale's western shores are wettest, the plateau's interior dries out (which fits the Salt Mirror), and Sunplains is driest.
- **Due west doesn't.** It soaks the south-west and leaves Deepwood dry.
- **Deepwood is never the wettest**, under any wind. It lies in the lee of the Spine and the Ironcrest hills, far from the windward coast. So its forest has to stand on wet ground rather than wet sky: slow rivers from the Ironcrest hills spreading into the Alderfens, Blackwater Marsh and the lakes, the way the Pripet Marshes or Białowieża stay wooded and wet on moderate rain. Nothing on the [Deepwood](../Regions/Deepwood.md) page says rainforest, so nothing needs to change there.

*Lean:*
- the weather comes in off the ocean from the north-west;
- the cold White Current chills Northwind's seas;
- Deepwood is a river-fed lowland forest;
- Deepwood thins southward into the "scrub and open woodland" that [Sunplains](../Regions/Sunplains.md) already lists.

This would be one sentence on [Geography](../Geography-and-Connections.md).

### 3. Port and the eastern sea

[Port](../Places/Port.md) is said to be "convenient to both northern and southern sea routes". On the map, though, the Inner Sea's only way out is the Narrows, to the west. A ship from Port to Accord sails about 1,300 miles round the south. Overland, it's about 125 miles from Port across the Spine's southern foot to Footwater, then down the Long Channel. That neck of land between the Inner Sea and Footwater is also the only land route south.

*Lean:* a haul road over that neck. It would make Port the meeting point of two seas, the way Corinth hauled ships across its isthmus and Suez carried goods between the seas by caravan. The other choice is to reword the Port page.

### 4. Northwind's grain and salt

- **Grain.** [Geography](../Geography-and-Connections.md#macro-geography) sends Northwind's grain overland through Highridge at Icestep, or by sea through Port. But a month of carting grain over a pass costs more than the grain. Along the Salted Coast from Greenvale's west coast, the voyage takes about a week. *Lean:* the Salted Coast is Northwind's grain road. Icestep carries light, valuable goods, and grain only in years when the sea is closed.
- **Salt.** Cold, cloudy coasts can't make salt by drying seawater in the sun. They boil it over peat or driftwood, or buy it. The Hanseatic herring trade ran on French bay salt. *Lean:* Northwind exports salt fish, and buys much of the salt it cures them with from Sunplains' pans (Saltpan) and Highridge's Whitepan. That gives the Council one more thing it can squeeze. It would change one cell of the [Trade](../Economy/Trade-and-Dependencies.md#core-model) table.

### 5. The twin suns

Now worked out, with the year, the moon question, the tides and the waves, on [Sky, Seasons and Sea](../Sky-Seasons-and-Sea.md) (D50). Nothing says how the two suns behave. *Lean:* a close pair that rises and sets together, so there are no extra seasons. If the second sun were distant, it would cross the sky part of the year, and that would change calendars, farming and religion.

## Is it sustainable?

Asked next ([D49](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)): "does the world ecosystem work / sustainable?"

**Yes as a steady state, with exactly the strains you'd expect,** and the [current events](../Story/Current-Events.md) already name most of them. Nothing here needs magic to hold together. The weak points are the ones real pre-industrial societies hit, which makes them good story pressure rather than holes.

| Cycle | How it renews itself | Where it strains | Real precedent | In the lore? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Soil** (Greenvale's grain) | legumes, manure, pasture and fallow in turn, hedges | grain shipped to Port carries the soil's goodness away with it; a single high-yield strain invites the next blight | European mixed farming; the Irish potato famine | yes: the Seven-year Blight, the new seed strain |
| **Water and salt** (Sunplains) | the Sallow's floods flush and refeed the delta | canal irrigation without drainage slowly salts the fields white | Mesopotamia's southern fields, lost to salt | **no.** *Lean:* old abandoned basins scarred white, and water law that includes drainage duties |
| **Forest and fuel** (Ironcrest and Deepwood) | coppice cut in turns regrows (the Charcoal Woods) | coppicing holds steady; growth doesn't. Every new furnace wants more woodland, and the nearest is Deepwood's | England's timber shortage and its turn to coal | yes: logging beyond limits, and Coalbrook and Coalglen as the coal turn |
| **Fish** (Northwind) | the stocks breed back where the fishing is seasonal and customary | inshore banks fished out; whole shoals shifting grounds | the Baltic herring moving to the North Sea in the 1400s | yes: "declining or shifting" stocks |
| **Grazing** (Highridge, the Barrens) | herds moving between seasonal pastures | herds kept too long in one place strip the slopes and the soil washes away | Mediterranean hill erosion; Himalayan transhumance | partly: seasonal pasture is in the reference research |
| **Mine waste** | rivers carry it off | Cinder Brook "black with washings" and the Ember "warm below the foundries" foul the water for everyone downstream, down to the North Gut's fishing | the Harz, Cornwall's tin streams | yes, as texture, not yet as a dispute |
| **Disease and pests** | distance and season slow them | Port mixes every region's seed, rats, insects and fevers | the Black Death's route through the ports | the [reference](README.md#legacy-reconciliation-backlog) flags quarantine; not in canon |

**People against land.** At the lean scale, 20 to 25 million people on about 670,000 square miles is 12 to 15 to the square kilometre. Mixed farmland supports more than that, so there's slack. That is why a bad harvest is a crisis rather than a collapse, and why the true collapses in the history (the Blight, the Ages of Silence) needed several failures at once.

**What's missing is an owner page.** Flora, fauna and food webs have none. The 2026-09-24 legacy notes hold a grounded draft per region, with its biology errors already corrected, and the [Reference index](README.md#legacy-reconciliation-backlog) calls it the highest risk of loss.
