# Prose test

The OC-C official first asked about grain.

Not ships.

Not credit.

Grain.

The Low Rivers merchant representative said:

"We can reserve future supply under conditions."

"What conditions?"

"Price band. Storage availability. Delivery schedule."

"Guarantee?"

"Separate."

"Why separate?"

"Because I sell grain."

That was reassuring.

For three days.

---

The guarantor's office asked different questions.

"How much of the purchase do you intend to lock now?"

"Not all of it."

"Good."

"Why good?"

"Because locking the whole season now is expensive."

"For us?"

"For everyone."

The official disliked financial people who spoke that way.

"Can you guarantee later purchases?"

"Within limits."

"Against the same Low Rivers sellers?"

"Some."

"Same storage?"

"Not necessarily."

"Same routes?"

"Not our office."

Again:

separate.

That was reassuring too.

Until the guarantor named the shipping houses it already trusted for staged delivery.

The official recognized two names from the food discussions.

They made a note.

Nothing more.

---

Shipping took another week.

The maritime intermediary offered:

- future cargo windows;
- recognized convoy participation;
- alternate carriage if one harbor route tightened.

The official read the carrier list.

Several were North Coast operators.

One had already appeared in the guarantor's preferred-delivery appendix.

The official looked up.

"Do all of you know each other?"

The intermediary smiled.

"That is usually cheaper."

Not an answer.

Also an answer.

---

OC-U and OC-D had not formed a union.

Everyone in OC-C agreed on that.

This did not prevent everyone in OC-C from asking what repeated water cooperation might become.

The upstream and downstream cities now shared more:

- measurements;
- maintenance schedules;
- warning procedures.

All sensible.

All limited.

All difficult to abandon because the water itself did not care about civic rivalry.

One councilor asked:

"If access tightens elsewhere while those two are cooperating, who guarantees us?"

The official answered:

"Nobody guarantees a city."

The councilor looked at the food papers.

"Apparently several people are trying."

That ended the discussion badly.

---

OC-C authorized the food agreement first.

Limited.

No exclusivity.

Then the staged credit.

Also limited.

Then shipping reservations under separate terms.

No one document said:

> these three relationships belong together.

Administratively, they did not.

Politically, people began putting them in the same folder.

That was new.

---

In the Low Rivers, the future OC-C contract looked simpler.

Reliable buyer.

Staged delivery.

Recognized guarantor.

One cooperative representative asked the buyer:

"Why do they get the better term?"

"Guaranteed settlement."

"We settle."

"You settle when your buyer pays."

"They have a buyer."

"They have someone standing behind the buyer."

"Difference?"

"Ask your lender."

The cooperative representative did.

Their lender knew exactly what the buyer meant.

That answer traveled.

---

At a North Coast harbor, the independent convoy captain received a future-cargo offer from a shipping intermediary.

"Southern food."

Their mate said:

"Good cargo."

"Reliable."

"Better."

The captain turned the paper over.

"Same guarantor."

"As what?"

"The last convoy finance."

Their mate shrugged.

"Maybe they're competent."

The captain considered that.

"That is how they get you."

"By paying?"

"Usually."

They signed.

---

In Port, the Records Worker received:

- an OC-C food guarantee reference;
- a shipping recognition under a separate file;
- a later Stone Hills inquiry asking whether the same guarantor was accepted for a high-grade commercial purchase.

The junior clerk put the folders together.

"Same group?"

The Records Worker looked at them.

"No."

"They keep appearing together."

"That is not the same thing."

"What is it?"

"Repeated counterparties."

"That sounds like group with more syllables."

The Records Worker took the Stone Hills file.

"File it under the guarantor."

The junior clerk did.

"Anything else?"

"No."

They hesitated.

"Those are our shipping people now, aren't they?"

The Records Worker looked up.

"Our?"

"The office."

"No."

"Port?"

"No."

"The merchants."

"Which merchants?"

The junior clerk stopped.

Good.

The Records Worker returned to the file.

Words were getting ahead of institutions again.

---

OC-D noticed the OC-C arrangements through ordinary merchant reporting.

An official asked:

"Why are they buying ahead?"

The merchant answered:

"Continuity."

"Against drought?"

"Against access."

"Access from whom?"

The merchant shrugged.

"Whoever stops selling first."

That answer traveled badly.

By the next civic discussion, the story had become:

> OC-C is securing supply outside the basin relationship.

True.

Another speaker said:

> OC-C is choosing northern shipping and Port finance against us.

Too strong.

Not entirely invented.

OC-U heard a still different version.

No one needed to lie.

Each retelling selected the part that mattered locally.

---

The OC-C official eventually received a diplomatic note asking whether the new food / shipping arrangements created:

- exclusivity;
- political conditions;
- defense obligations.

They pulled all three contracts.

Separate files.

Separate counterparties.

Separate terms.

No defense clause anywhere.

They wrote:

> **No defense obligation is created by these commercial arrangements.**

Then stopped.

That was the first time they had needed a diplomatic sentence to describe three ordinary contracts.

They sent it anyway.
