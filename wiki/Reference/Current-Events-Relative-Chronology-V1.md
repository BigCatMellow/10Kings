# Current Events Relative Chronology — V1

## Status

**Author-side working chronology. Reference only; not an in-world calendar and not a fixed Villain script.**

This page sequences the current pressure field without undoing the adaptive model.

It should be read with:

- [Current Events](../Story/Current-Events.md) — authoritative present-tense pressures;
- [Villain's Dominoes](../Story/Villains-Dominoes.md) — hidden strategic architecture;
- [Adaptive War Escalation Graph — V2](Adaptive-War-Escalation-Graph-V2.md) — pressure states and alternate routes;
- [Current-Event Stakeholder Reaction Matrix — V1](Current-Event-Stakeholder-Reaction-Matrix-V1.md) — constituencies and internal splits;
- [Grounded Domino Design](Grounded-Domino-Design.md) — causal design method;
- [Three Moons Opening Construction — V1](Three-Moons-Opening-Construction-V1.md) — active author-side opening beat architecture derived from the ending.

The purpose is not to decide that event A must always cause event B.

The purpose is to answer:

> **What has to be true before the opening, what is happening around Three Moons, what can be contained, what residue remains after containment, and what later conditions must exist before war becomes plausible?**

---

# 1. Chronology rule

Use **hard ordering only where causality requires it**.

Examples:

- baseline ecological / economic stresses must predate the responses they provoke;
- the Villain's Port guild-centralization work must predate the information benefit he receives from it;
- reserve behavior must become visible before it can be interpreted as strategic preparation;
- security conversion must occur before a small armed incident can plausibly broaden into war;
- the Port settlement crisis belongs after Port's neutrality is tested by an already war-permissive system.

But many branches can overlap or change order.

The chronology therefore has:

- **fixed prerequisites**;
- **working default sequence**;
- **flexible braid order**;
- **explicit containment / failure points**;
- **residue that survives even when a branch dies**.

A later outline may reorder flexible events without breaking canon if those causal prerequisites still hold.

---

# 2. Working Three Moons placement

The current planning default is:

> **Three Moons overlaps the opening movement of the main story.**

This is an author-side default, not yet a locked calendar fact.

The festival works best here because it can simultaneously:

- overload Port;
- bring already-existing crises into the same conversational space;
- make guild centralization unusually visible;
- accelerate the Villain's information gathering;
- let Wurdren encounter distant problems without touring the continent first;
- create emergency coordination meetings that are legitimate rather than conspiratorial;
- send people, contracts, rumors, and revised assumptions back outward when the festival disperses.

Three Moons therefore has two temporal functions:

~~~text
before / during festival
many networks converge on Port
→ information aggregation

after festival
those same people leave Port
→ information / rumor / policy dispersal
~~~

The festival does not create the pressure field.

It makes the field easier to compare.

# 3. Phase 0 — years / seasons before the opening

## Port institutional buildup

Long before the current crisis field becomes obvious, the Villain is already doing work he can sincerely defend as good for Port:

- encouraging guild branches / representatives to maintain a Port presence;
- making cross-guild communication easier;
- supporting shared contracting / arbitration / recognition;
- building direct producer-to-Port relationships;
- helping alternate carriers / warehouses / relief networks;
- retaining more professional knowledge and relationships inside Port.

This work serves his political goal even without a conspiracy.

It also later gives him:

- better information;
- more intermediaries;
- more legitimate reasons to meet people;
- more capacity to compare otherwise unrelated complaints.

## Council background

The Council already exists as a distributed coordination system.

It already knows how to influence:

- credit;
- provision;
- routes;
- materials;
- infrastructure;
- information.

The current story does not begin with either side suddenly acquiring these capabilities.

# 4. Phase 1 — several independent pressures mature

These stresses should already exist before the opening movement.

## North Coast

- fish access is shifting;
- piracy / raiding is a real problem;
- maritime-use disputes are politically brittle.

## Low Rivers

- abundance has become oversupply;
- storage is tight;
- prices are weak;
- debt / land pressure is beginning.

## Stone Hills

- labor grievances are real;
- production priorities are politically sensitive;
- strategic-grade output is becoming more valuable.

## High Roads

- route attacks / fraud / uncertainty exist;
- Spine damage complicates movement;
- corridor confidence is uneven.

## Longwood

- ecological disruption already affects local use / supply;
- layered rights make emergency access politically difficult.

## Old Cities

- drought anxiety already changes behavior before universal physical shortage exists;
- technical cooperation is becoming politically visible.

## Port

- ordinary throughput is already producing local capacity costs;
- festival preparation increases housing, labor, warehouse, food, sanitation, and security pressure.

### Chronology test

At this stage, every problem should still be explainable without the Villain.

If removing him makes the whole pressure field disappear, Phase 1 is wrong.

# 5. Phase 2 — defensive responses begin crossing systems

This phase begins before / during the festival buildup.

People are not preparing for war.

They are protecting themselves.

Examples:

- convoy / patrol proposals;
- tighter credit;
- early purchasing;
- storage reservation;
- advance metal contracting;
- route diversion;
- guard / inspection increases;
- water coordination;
- emergency repair planning;
- guild or merchant coordination sessions.

The important shift is:

> **one institution's sensible protection begins changing another institution's operating environment.**

Examples:

~~~text
maritime risk
→ tighter Port credit
→ weaker distant purchasing
→ Low Rivers debt pressure
~~~

~~~text
route unreliability
→ rerouting
→ bottleneck
→ Longwood access pressure
~~~

~~~text
water anxiety
→ visible cooperation
→ rival suspicion
~~~

This is still mostly self-generated.

The Villain should not need to touch every branch.

# 6. Phase 3 — Three Moons opening / information convergence

This is the current **working opening window**.

Port contains, at unusual density:

- guild representatives;
- merchants;
- sailors;
- carriers;
- political agents;
- faith / charity networks;
- performers;
- workers;
- visitors;
- people looking for contracts or relief.

The same city now hears, nearly at once:

- convoy changes;
- grain distress;
- metal priority orders;
- pack-animal shortages;
- route closures;
- Longwood access disputes;
- Old Cities reserve buying;
- Port housing / warehouse pressure.

## What ordinary people experience

Mostly:

- crowds;
- shortages;
- arguments;
- price changes;
- scheduling problems;
- rumor;
- delayed work;
- unfamiliar visitors.

## What guilds experience

The benefits and dangers of Port centralization become concrete.

Centralizers can say:

> this is exactly why we need permanent cross-guild coordination.

Autonomists can say:

> this is exactly how Port turns temporary coordination into permanent authority.

Small members can say:

> permanent representation mostly benefits houses wealthy enough to keep people here year-round.

## What the Villain learns

Three Moons should **change his model**, not merely confirm it.

He arrives with several hypotheses.

He discovers that:

- at least one expected branch is weaker than predicted;
- at least one unrelated bottleneck is stronger than expected;
- multiple sectors are competing for the same limited transport / storage / material capacity;
- some Council responses are genuinely effective;
- some local institutions are more competent than he assumed.

His advantage is synthesis.

His limitation is noise.

## What Wurdren can encounter

Wurdren can hear apparently unrelated complaints that later become meaningful:

- a Stone Hills smith angry about priority contracts;
- a Low Rivers merchant seeking a buyer;
- a carrier saying every good animal is booked;
- a sailor complaining about convoy rules;
- a Longwood representative disputing a copied agreement.

He does not need to know these are connected.

# 7. Phase 4 — containment, failure, and residue

This phase is mandatory.

The world must demonstrate that escalation is **not automatic**.

The following are working default outcomes, not yet fixed scene canon.

## Default containment A — maritime political branch partially succeeds

NC-M / NC-I / harbor actors reach a workable short-term convoy or escort compromise.

Result:

- the expected constitutional confrontation is smaller than the Villain hoped;
- immediate maritime losses may fall;
- one planned escalation route turns RED / contained.

But residue remains:

- convoy schedules;
- new reporting requirements;
- escort contracts;
- risk pricing;
- precedent for coordinated maritime security;
- earlier credit tightening that has already propagated elsewhere.

The Villain must accept:

> **the political branch failed.**

He may still exploit the residue.

He may not claim the agreement was secretly his plan.

## Default containment B — Longwood produces a real local compromise

A rights / access dispute is narrowed through:

- direct negotiation;
- compensation;
- route redesign;
- limited use rights;
- better comparison of actual documents.

This branch can die.

That success matters because it proves:

- local institutions can work;
- direct contact can stop a domino;
- the Villain is not entitled to sabotage every fair settlement.

This is a strong possible Wurdren counter-domino.

## Default containment C — Low Rivers stabilization works imperfectly

Local and/or Council-linked action prevents total producer collapse.

Possible tools:

- storage;
- credit extension;
- new buyers;
- temporary debt relief;
- redirected purchases.

The intervention is genuinely useful.

It also leaves political residue:

- some borrowers were saved first;
- some debt changed hands;
- some buyers gained durable relationships;
- some people now see Council coordination as proof of competence;
- others see the same intervention as hidden private power.

The crisis can be reduced without resetting the world.

# 8. Phase 5 — festival dispersal and adaptive redirection

When Three Moons ends, the information surge does not vanish.

People leave Port carrying:

- copied agreements;
- changed expectations;
- new contracts;
- rumors;
- suspicions;
- professional contacts;
- stories about who helped whom;
- stories about who refused.

This is the outward half of the festival's political importance.

## Villain adaptation

After containment, the Villain's board should look different from the one he entered with.

Working example:

- maritime constitutional conflict → **RED / contained**;
- Longwood route escalation → **RED / contained**;
- Low Rivers collapse route → **YELLOW / weakened but politically useful residue**;
- Stone Hills strategic-production anxiety → **YELLOW / strengthening**;
- Port capacity / neutrality stress → **GREEN / self-moving**;
- reserve behavior across several systems → **GREEN / convergence emerging**;
- Old Cities guarantee / bloc interpretation → **YELLOW / uncertain**.

The point is not those exact colors forever.

The point is that the plan **changes after evidence**.

## Villain's next question

Not:

> how do I revive the branches that failed?

Instead:

> which pressure is now closest to converting ordinary protection into security capability?

That moves attention toward:

- strategic allocation;
- reserve behavior;
- guarantees;
- inspections;
- escorts;
- control of routes / materials;
- Port emergency authority.

# 9. Phase 6 — protection becomes security infrastructure

This is the major mid-story category change.

Measures created for ordinary protection begin serving security functions.

Examples:

- convoy organization becomes maritime command capacity;
- route escorts become armed passage capability;
- reserve inventories become mobilization knowledge;
- advance contracts become strategic allocation;
- repaired posts become defensible positions;
- emergency records become usable logistics data;
- preferred counterparties become political guarantee networks.

The Villain may selectively push at:

- policy tipping points;
- expectation cascades;
- bottlenecks;
- attribution contests.

But much of the conversion should happen because actors are watching one another.

## Stakeholder effect

Every security measure creates new constituencies.

Example:

~~~text
strategic export control
→ security planners gain confidence
→ exporters lose flexibility
→ small workshops lose supply
→ rival buyers stockpile
→ smugglers gain margin
→ guild branches split
→ foreign officials read the control as evidence of preparation
~~~

This is where the recovered stakeholder matrix becomes plot machinery rather than background flavor.

---

# 10. Phase 7 — sphere sorting and readiness fear

Enough actors now prefer partners who can guarantee:

- food;
- metal;
- routes;
- shipping;
- access;
- credit;
- armed protection.

Commercial dependence begins hardening into security dependence.

The system becomes war-permissive only after enough of the following coexist:

- multiple supply / route insecurities;
- strategic controls;
- visible reserves;
- routine armed escorts;
- meaningful guarantees;
- partial mobilization capacity;
- fear that arbitration is slower than operational events.

The Villain does not need to create the final spark.

He needs the world to reach a state where a small spark cannot easily remain small.

---

# 11. Phase 8 — armed incident and failed containment

The final incident can still vary.

Candidate classes remain:

- disputed strategic cargo;
- convoy / harbor access;
- maritime detention;
- chokepoint crossing;
- retaliatory seizure.

The chronology requires only that:

1. the system is already war-permissive;
2. backing down has alliance / credibility costs;
3. armed actors are already in contact;
4. at least one attempt at containment fails;
5. retaliation broadens through existing guarantees.

War is therefore the **activation of accumulated capability**, not the product of one magical domino.

---

# 12. Phase 9 — Port neutrality crisis

Once war begins, Port can no longer postpone questions about:

- armed access;
- defense;
- military cargo;
- outside assistance;
- emergency revenue;
- inspection / blockade pressure;
- favoritism;
- treaty interpretation.

The Villain now has the forcing condition he wanted.

But this phase must also activate the recovered **Port rejection** problem.

Some Port constituencies may conclude:

- his diagnosis was right;
- his methods are now worse than the settlement he opposed;
- a partial reform is worth accepting;
- war is destroying the city he claimed to defend.

Possible reactions include:

- former allies accepting compromise;
- merchants withdrawing support;
- neighborhoods refusing assistance tied to his movement;
- guild branches leaving;
- families leaving Port.

The moral test becomes:

> **If Port itself says stop, does he listen?**

If he does not, his position has changed from:

> Port should control its own future.

to:

> I know Port's future better than Port does.

That is the clearest form of his moral collapse.

# 13. Phase 10 — public authority crisis / negotiated pause

See [Story Backward Construction — V1](Story-Backward-Construction-V1.md).

The Port crisis should not end merely with:

> Port rejects the Villain.

The stronger current direction is that Port **uses the crisis to exercise the agency the Villain said it lacked**.

Working sequence:

1. rival wartime demands make ordinary neutral operation increasingly contradictory;
2. Council-linked actors visibly coordinate enough shared systems that hidden authority becomes harder to deny;
3. the Villain exposes the broader pattern of private coordination;
4. evidence of his own escalation / withholding also becomes politically credible;
5. Port constituencies reject the binary of:
   - restore secret Council discretion;
   - let the Villain decide Port's future;
6. Port asserts a temporary **neutral-access / emergency operating regime** over systems it actually controls;
7. belligerents accept a negotiated pause because predictable Port access / shared systems now matter more than unrestricted escalation.

The exact emergency rules remain open.

The key category change is:

> **Port stops being only the object whose neutrality everyone interprets and becomes an actor capable of setting bounded terms for how neutrality functions.**

This is the first visible sapling of the later settlement.

---

# 14. Phase 11 — first Port settlement

The immediate postwar result should be a **minimum viable successor settlement**, not a complete final constitution.

Required direction:

- materially greater Port agency;
- continued neutrality;
- public recognition that some cross-border coordination is unavoidable;
- Council knowledge / capability transformed rather than erased;
- no successor body simply inherits the Council's whole hidden discretionary position;
- review / contestation / representation become part of the coordination problem;
- later amendment remains possible.

The Villain therefore wins the structural point:

> the old Port arrangement cannot return unchanged.

But he does not own the result.

Port factions, outside powers, guilds, workers, local authorities, and other affected actors bargain over what the new arrangement becomes.

That distinction is central:

> **Port choosing a reform the Villain does not fully control is not the defeat of Port agency. It is Port agency becoming real.**

A working long-horizon epilogue then returns to Port at Three Moons 98 years later, after fourteen festival cycles, to show how those first emergency / settlement arrangements have been revised and normalized.

Exact constitutional institutions and the Villain's personal fate remain open.

# 15. Wurdren's chronology — counter-domino rather than rival mastermind

Wurdren's story should cut across the phases through **successful local repair**.

He does not need to discover the entire architecture early.

A useful progression is:

## Early

He solves a problem that appears local.

Example classes:

- compare two records;
- protect a shipment;
- bring actual parties into one room;
- find the clerk / witness who explains an apparent conspiracy;
- stop a rumor from becoming violence.

## Middle

He begins noticing that his local fixes repeatedly interrupt pressures that somebody else seems to benefit from.

The Villain notices him for the opposite reason:

> this person keeps making brittle situations less brittle.

## Late

Wurdren's accumulated relationships matter because they preserve channels of trust after formal alignments harden.

He still does not become a superior strategic mastermind.

His strength is that people who would otherwise act on fear still have someone they can call, trust, or compare evidence with.

# 16. Intervention afterlife ledger

Every containment / response should leave something behind.

## Convoy compromise

Immediate result:

- safer / more predictable sailing.

Residue:

- reporting system;
- escort contracts;
- scheduling precedent;
- command / cost argument;
- new security providers.

## Low Rivers stabilization

Immediate result:

- fewer failures / less producer panic.

Residue:

- debt changes;
- new buyers;
- altered ownership;
- Council visibility;
- gratitude and resentment.

## Longwood compromise

Immediate result:

- local escalation stops.

Residue:

- clarified rights;
- precedent for negotiation;
- proof that outside emergency claims can be limited;
- political actors who can point to cooperation as an alternative to coercion.

## Old Cities technical cooperation

Immediate result:

- lower water risk.

Residue:

- shared records;
- repeated meetings;
- practical interdependence;
- rival suspicion.

## Stone Hills allocation / labor response

Immediate result:

- production continuity or labor settlement.

Residue:

- priority clauses;
- strategic procurement records;
- factional guild politics;
- workers who now understand where high-value output is going.

## Port emergency coordination

Immediate result:

- some crisis is managed.

Residue:

- precedent;
- contracts;
- dependency;
- new claims about Port authority;
- evidence for both centralizers and autonomists.

The rule is:

> **solved crisis ≠ restored old world.**

# 17. Chronology acceptance test

A final outline should pass all of these:

1. Baseline crises visibly predate Villain exploitation.
2. Three Moons adds information density, not causation.
3. At least one important Villain expectation fails.
4. At least one ordinary institution solves a real problem.
5. At least one Wurdren intervention kills or weakens a branch without revealing the whole conspiracy.
6. Council action genuinely prevents harm somewhere.
7. Every successful intervention leaves political / economic residue.
8. The Villain adapts to new evidence instead of forcing failed branches back on track.
9. Commercial protection changes category before war: credit / logistics → controls / guarantees → security capability.
10. A small final incident occurs only after the system is already war-permissive.
11. Port's own constituencies disagree over both the old settlement and the Villain's solution.
12. If Port rejects the Villain's methods, the story treats that as morally consequential rather than an inconvenience.

If those conditions hold, the chronology can remain flexible without becoming arbitrary.
