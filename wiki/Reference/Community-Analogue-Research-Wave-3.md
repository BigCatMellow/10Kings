# 1. SH-3 — small hereditary craft town

## Comparator A — Wajima lacquer production cluster

### Sources

Japan National Tourism Organization:

- https://www.japan.travel/en/japan-magazine/1909_wajima/
- https://www.japan.travel/en/luxury/detail/wajima-lacquerware/

Japan Tourism Agency:

- https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/en/R4-00537.html

Academic industry study:

- https://doi.org/10.4157/grj1984a.65.3_219

### Source-derived mechanisms

Wajima's craft economy demonstrates several useful mechanisms:

- high-skill production concentrated geographically;
- apprenticeship transmitting practical skill on site;
- production broken into specialized stages / workshops;
- merchants / material suppliers supporting artisans;
- local identity shaped by a craft without every resident performing the same task;
- material inputs can become nonlocal while the **skill cluster remains local**;
- disaster / rebuilding can become part of craft-neighborhood history.

The academic study is especially useful because it distinguishes:

- labor;
- apprenticeship;
- material supply;
- production organization.

The craft survives as a **local production system**, not just a style.

### 10Kings application

SH-3 should not be:

> a town where everyone is a smith.

A stronger form is a **specialized production cluster** in which several households / workshops perform different stages.

Possible chain:

~~~text
raw / semi-finished material
→ specialist preparation
→ shaping / assembly
→ finishing
→ inspection
→ merchant / guild distribution
~~~

Exact craft remains open.

The town's strongest asset may be:

> **the concentration of people who know how to do several interdependent things well.**

This creates local dependence among:

- masters;
- apprentices;
- subcontractors;
- material merchants;
- inspectors;
- carriers.

### Important consequence

A workshop can close without the craft disappearing.

But enough closures can break the cluster because:

- one stage loses specialists;
- training breaks;
- suppliers stop coming;
- apprentices leave.

That gives Stone Hills craft decline / guild centralization a strong systems model.

### Do not borrow

- lacquerware;
- Japanese aesthetics;
- Wajima identity;
- exact apprenticeship structure;
- named production stages.

## Comparator B — Echizen washi village

### Sources

Echizen Washi Village:

- https://www.echizenwashi.jp/english/index.php
- https://www.echizenwashi.jp/english/information/udatsu.html

Japan National Tourism Organization:

- https://www.japan.travel/en/experiences-in-japan/5922/

### Source-derived mechanisms

The official Echizen material documents:

- dozens of mills in one production area;
- different producers specializing in different products / qualities;
- craft knowledge tied to place;
- workshops, museums, cooperative organization, and ritual / public identity around the craft.

The useful point is:

> **local craft identity can coexist with strong internal specialization.**

### 10Kings application

A Stone Hills craft town can be famous for one category of product while locals care intensely about distinctions outsiders ignore.

Outsider:

> "They make blades."

Local:

> "That shop makes hinges. That family tempers springs. Those people grind surgical edges. Don't call them all bladesmiths."

This is culturally useful.

It also allows:

- shop rivalries;
- quality reputations;
- guild politics;
- apprenticeship movement between related specialties.

### Do not borrow

- paper production;
- Japanese shrine / deity practice;
- modern craft-tourism economy;
- exact cooperative model.

---

## SH-3 research result

**R2.**

Key mechanisms:

- geographic skill cluster;
- specialized workshop chain;
- apprenticeship;
- material merchants;
- quality reputation;
- local craft distinctions invisible to outsiders;
- vulnerability to loss of one production stage.

# 2. LR-2 — estate / tenant district

## Comparator A — rural Irish landlord / tenant order

### Sources

National Museum of Ireland:

- https://cms.museum.ie/en-IE/Museums/Country-Life/Exhibitions/The-Times
- https://source.museum.ie/en-IE/Museums/Country-Life/Exhibitions/The-Natural-Environment
- https://cms.museum.ie/en-IE/Museums/Country-Life/Visitor-Information/About-The-Museum/History-and-Architecture

### Source-derived mechanisms

The museum material documents a rural order in which:

- a relatively small landlord class could own large estates;
- tenant households worked leased farms;
- rent linked households to estate administration;
- estate agents / offices turned property relations into record systems;
- political conflict over landownership could become a mass social question;
- estate land could later be redistributed / converted toward owner occupation.

The key mechanism is:

> **formal sovereignty is not required for an estate to structure daily power.**

A tenant may experience:

- rent;
- lease;
- eviction risk;
- agent decisions;
- estate employment

more immediately than state institutions.

### 10Kings application

LR-2 can contain estates where tenants live under LR-A sovereignty but negotiate ordinary survival through:

- landlord / estate office;
- rent / crop obligation;
- access to storage;
- credit;
- local roads;
- employment.

This fits the current polity rule:

> estate can feel like government without being sovereign.

### Do not borrow

- Anglo-Irish identity;
- colonial land history;
- specific Land War politics;
- Big House aesthetic.

## Comparator B — Tuscan mezzadria / sharecropping

### Sources

Museo della Mezzadria Senese:

- https://www.museisenesi.org/en/museo/museo-della-mezzadria-senese-sharecropping-museum/

Academic household study:

- https://doi.org/10.1177/036319908400900201

Recent institutional analysis:

- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/rural-history/article/beyond-backwardness-sharecropping-as-an-institution-of-agrarian-colonization-in-mediterranean-europe-new-evidence-from-val-di-chiana-tuscany-19th20th-centuries/141C30C99FB79C37BBC80C9207E4E311

### Source-derived mechanisms

Mezzadria research is particularly useful because it shows:

- landowner supplied land / capital;
- tenant household supplied labor;
- output-sharing tied household economy to estate economy;
- estate management could coordinate:
  - skills;
  - improvements;
  - reclamation;
  - production;
- household size / composition could be influenced by farm labor requirements;
- debt / landlord lending could stabilize a household temporarily but increase dependency;
- tenancy insecurity could split or displace households.

The important mechanism is:

> **a land contract can shape household structure itself.**

### 10Kings application

LR-2 should not reduce tenancy to:

> pay rent or be evicted.

Different estates can use different mixes of:

- fixed rent;
- crop share;
- debt;
- seed advance;
- livestock / equipment supplied by estate;
- labor obligation.

That diversity can exist **inside the same polity**.

A household may therefore make marriage / residence / migration decisions partly around:

- available land;
- lease size;
- labor needs;
- inheritance prospects.

### Crucial safeguard

Do not import the historical patriarchal / patrilocal Tuscan household as 10Kings default.

That is source-specific.

Use only the causal point:

> contract / farm scale can influence household composition.

### Myth / folklore implication

Estate districts may preserve stories about:

- bad boundary;
- unjust measure;
- vanished tenant household;
- hidden grain;
- old landlord promises.

No new creature family is required.

The stronger cultural material may be **historical memory**, not monsters.

---

## LR-2 research result

**R2.**

Key mechanisms:

- estate power without sovereignty;
- tenant contracts varying locally;
- credit / grain advances;
- estate records / agents;
- household form affected by contract;
- reform creating winners / losers.

# 3. OC-3 — coastal merchant city

## Comparator A — Stone Town of Zanzibar

### Source

UNESCO World Heritage Centre:

- https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/173
- https://whc.unesco.org/en/urban-heritage-atlas/stone-town

### Source-derived mechanisms

UNESCO describes Stone Town as a long-lived coastal trading city shaped by repeated contact among:

- East African;
- Arab;
- Indian;
- European

traditions.

The key mechanism is not merely "multicultural city."

Its built environment shows long-term transformation through:

- trade;
- migration;
- merchant property;
- religious institutions;
- shop / residential forms;
- reuse of buildings.

UNESCO emphasizes that disparate influences became a **distinct local urban culture** rather than remaining a visible collage.

### 10Kings application

OC-3 should not look like:

> Old Cities + foreign quarter.

It should be a place where long-distance trade has produced **city-born forms**:

- merchant architecture;
- household layouts;
- food;
- contract practice;
- clothing;
- speech;
- religious / charitable institutions.

A family whose ancestors arrived generations ago can be fully OC-3 while maintaining:

- distant kin;
- merchant contacts;
- inherited ritual / cuisine.

This mirrors Port in mechanism but not political structure.

OC-3 is sovereign.

Port is constrained-neutral.

That difference matters.

### Do not borrow

- Swahili identity;
- Omani / Indian ethnic mapping;
- coral-stone architecture;
- slavery history;
- exact religious landscape.

> balance of power and commercial exclusion.

# 4. HR-1 — central switching / arbitration city

### Sources

UNESCO Silk Roads Programme:

UNESCO Samarkand:

- https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/603
- https://whc.unesco.org/en/urban-heritage-atlas/samarkand/

### Source-derived mechanisms

UNESCO emphasizes Silk Roads cities as places where travelers / merchants:

- stop;
- rest animals;
- exchange goods;
- exchange:
  - skills;
  - languages;
  - ideas;
  - religion;
  - knowledge.

Samarkand specifically demonstrates:

- long-term cultural layering;
- craft quarters;
- neighborhood organization;
- diverse populations tied to route history;
- social centers at neighborhood scale.

### 10Kings application

HR-C should not be merely:

> a market with translators.

Its switching function creates permanent urban communities around:

- lodging;
- animals;
- repair;
- warehouse;
- contract;
- arbitration;
- remittance / credit;
- education;
- religious hospitality.

The city can be cosmopolitan without being socially fluid everywhere.

People may live in tightly networked neighborhoods while working across languages daily.

### Strong distinction from Port

Port switches:

- sea;
- river;
- road;
- finance;
- neutrality.

HR-C switches primarily:

- roads;
- carriers;
- animals;
- jurisdictions;
- contracts.

Its prestige comes from **interoperability**.

# 5. HR-3 — northern high-route settlement

## Comparator — Khinalig high-mountain village / transhumance landscape

### Source

UNESCO World Heritage Centre:

- https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1696

### Source-derived mechanisms

UNESCO documents a continuing system combining:

- permanent high-mountain village;
- agricultural terraces;
- irrigation;
- high-altitude summer pasture;
- lower winter pasture;
- seasonal route;
- springs / wells;
- bridges;
- animal infrastructure;
- religious / burial sites;
- collective organization of transhumance.

The most useful corrective is:

> **high-route pastoral culture can have extremely strong permanent settlement attachment.**

Mobility and permanent village life are not opposites.

### 10Kings application

HR-3 should not be simply:

> the town pastoralists pass through.

It can itself contain households combining:

- highland cultivation;
- animal care;
- route service;
- seasonal herd movement;
- storage.

Some households move more than others.

Some people remain in the village year-round.

The village's built environment can therefore be shaped by:

- winter isolation;
- storage;
- animal shelter;
- terrace / slope management;
- route traffic.

### Relation to M-2

M-2 pastoral networks may use HR-3 as:

- base;
- market;
- kin center;
- route node.

But HR-3 is **not identical to the pastoral network**.

It contains:

- settled specialists;
- farmers;
- traders;
- clergy / teachers;
- older people;
- children;
- people whose household no longer moves.

This is important.

### Weather Animal implication

HR-3 can preserve one of the strongest local Weather Animal traditions because:

- residents observe elevation changes;
- animal behavior;
- pass weather;
- seasonal arrival / departure.

But resident and mobile pastoral versions may differ.

### Do not borrow

- Khinalig ethnic identity;
- exact 200 km route;
- Caucasus religion / architecture;
- language;
- transhumance organization.

---

## HR-3 research result

**R2.**

Key mechanisms:

- permanent mountain village + seasonal mobility;
- terrace / pastoral mixed economy;
- winter storage;
- collective route infrastructure;
- resident / mobile distinctions.

# 6. Cross-wave findings

## A. Specialization does not require monoculture

SH-3 can be famous for one craft while internally containing:

- many specialties;
- merchants;
- apprentices;
- suppliers;
- unrelated occupations.

## B. Contract can shape household

LR-2 adds a major cultural mechanism:

> legal / economic arrangements affect who lives together and why.

This should later be tested across:

- tenancy;
- apprenticeship;
- mobile work;
- Port boarding.

## C. Cosmopolitan cities become locally specific

OC-3 and HR-C both show:

> long-distance influence becomes local culture after generations.

This supports the existing anti-collage rule.

## D. Route cities and port cities are not interchangeable

HR-C:

- road / jurisdiction / animal / arbitration switching.

OC-3:

- maritime merchant sovereignty.

Port:

- multimodal neutral transfer / finance / constrained sovereignty.

All can be cosmopolitan for different reasons.

## E. Settled/mobile is a false binary

HR-3 reinforces M-2:

- permanent home;
- seasonal movement;
- partial-household mobility

can coexist.
