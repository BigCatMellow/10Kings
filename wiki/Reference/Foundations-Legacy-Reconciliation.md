# 1. Main salvage result

The strongest surviving material is:

- a plausible tectonic explanation for the three-continent topology;
- The Spine as an old mountain system formed by uplift / collision;
- faults, water erosion and geothermal activity as possible contributors to the Underpass;
- continued seismic instability;
- watersheds radiating from uplands;
- Port as a strait / harbor candidate because traffic converges there;
- pre-Convergence politics as fragmented, layered and locally compacted;
- the "parted Mother" as a possible creation / cultural myth distinct from geological fact.

The weakest material is:

- fixed placement of old capitals;
- one polity per old culture;
- exact old borders;
- old industrial technology;
- "Three Sons" cosmology presented as fact;
- exact historical dates and named polities unsupported by current history owners.

---

# 2. Geological hypothesis worth preserving

Volume 01 proposes a grounded geological model:

```text
older large landmass
→ rifting / plate separation
→ central uplift and faulting
→ The Spine
→ erosion / rivers / caves
→ separated landmasses + Underpass systems
```

This is a **working geological hypothesis**, not established deep-time canon.

It is useful because it can explain several current facts without magic:

- mineral-rich uplands;
- high mountain barriers;
- river headwaters;
- seismic activity;
- caves;
- fault-controlled passages;
- different coastal shapes;
- constrained cross-continent routes.

---

# 3. The ancient supercontinent

The old source names a primordial supercontinent:

- **Aria Magna**

Preserve the name as **deep-time / scholarly name quarry only**.

The underlying idea — that the current landmasses were once connected — is plausible and compatible with the old map logic.

It is not currently established that:

- scholars know this;
- the exact supercontinent shape is known;
- "Aria Magna" is the accepted term;
- the separation happened in exactly the old sequence.

This is especially useful because a myth of a once-whole land could independently resemble later geological reconstruction without proving the myth literally true.

---

# 4. The Spine

The legacy tectonic model is compatible with the current physical Spine:

- convergent uplift;
- folding and faulting;
- steep ridges;
- deep valleys;
- erosion;
- river headwaters;
- seismic zones.

Preserve this as a preferred mundane explanation candidate.

Do not establish:

- exact plate boundaries;
- exact geological age;
- active volcanism everywhere;
- magical origin.

The current [Magic Baseline](../Magic-Baseline.md) explicitly keeps supernatural interpretations unresolved.

---

# 5. The Underpass

Volume 01 provides a good mundane formation hypothesis:

- tectonic faults and fractures;
- cave formation;
- water erosion;
- geothermal effects in some areas;
- later human excavation / enlargement.

That fits the current model of The Underpass as **natural-and-worked**, not one constructed tunnel.

Useful consequence:

Different branches can have different origins.

One corridor may be:
- limestone cave;
- fault passage;
- abandoned mine;
- enlarged natural tunnel;
- later engineered connection.

This helps prevent The Underpass from feeling like one impossible ancient subway system.

---

# 6. Seismic instability

The old material repeatedly proposes earthquakes and cave-ins around The Spine.

That remains useful.

Possible consequences include:

- route closure;
- new cave exposure;
- water-course changes;
- damaged settlements;
- newly dangerous old tunnels;
- newly opened minor passages;
- political disputes over repair / access.

This already fits the current-event quarry.

Do not make every earthquake supernatural.

---

# 7. Climate and watersheds

The legacy geology tries to derive climate from:

- latitude;
- ocean currents;
- mountain rain shadows;
- elevation;
- drainage.

The exact old climate placements are too schematic.

But the **method** is correct.

Current ecology should continue to ask:

```text
latitude + elevation + prevailing weather + ocean + mountain barrier + watershed
→ local climate
→ ecology
→ settlement / production pressures
```

This is stronger than assigning climate because a culture "needs" a biome.

---

# 8. Port placement

Volume 01 strongly argues that Port should sit at or near:

- a natural harbor;
- strait;
- channel;
- other maritime bottleneck

where northern / southern and western / eastern traffic can converge.

That remains the strongest current placement candidate and is already reflected in [Geography and Connections](../Geography-and-Connections.md).

What remains open:

- island vs. peninsula vs. mainland shore;
- exact estuary / strait;
- exact neighboring polity geography;
- exact military defensibility.

Do not restore the old Port Lumière placement as fixed map canon.

---

# 9. "The Mother" / parted-land myth

Volume 01 contains a recurring mythic idea:

> the current land was once one Mother and later became divided.

This is worth preserving as mythology quarry.

Possible current use:

- creation myth;
- religious metaphor;
- poetic name for the old land;
- later geological scholars noting that the myth resembles evidence of an older joined landmass;
- political rhetoric about reunification or common origin.

Important:

**Mythic similarity to geology is not proof the myth is literally true.**

---

# 10. The old "Three Sons" problem

Legacy material repeatedly uses:

- **Three Sons**;
- three celestial Sons / stars;
- a Mother + Three Sons cosmology.

Current [World Overview](../World-Overview.md) instead says:

- the setting is called **Two Sons**;
- it is named for **twin suns**.

These are not the same cosmology.

Therefore the old Three Sons system is **superseded / unresolved legacy**.

Do not silently merge "three stars" into "two suns."

Possible future options if desired:

- Three Sons was an earlier abandoned concept;
- it survives as an archaic / regional myth unrelated to the literal suns;
- one "son" was metaphorical;
- the concept is discarded entirely.

No option is promoted here.

---

# 11. Pre-Convergence mechanisms worth keeping

The old history is much more useful as a **pattern bank** than as chronology.

Useful recurring structures include:

- wars over ore;
- water disputes;
- food insecurity;
- shifting borders;
- temporary dynasties;
- toll states;
- clan confederacies;
- local defense compacts;
- merchant agreements;
- small treaties that work locally;
- institutions surviving the collapse of larger states.

This supports the current idea that The Convergence did not emerge from a clean world of six kingdoms.

---

# 12. Failed large states

Legacy history experiments with older empires / large political formations that collapse through:

- overextension;
- succession;
- economic decline;
- local resistance;
- competing institutions.

The exact old empire names and dates are not promoted.

The mechanism is useful because current fragmentation should have history.

Some present institutions may be:

- remnants;
- successors;
- imitators;
- restorations;
- reactions against older centralized rule.

---

# 13. Local compacts before The Convergence

One useful legacy idea is that people did not wait for The Convergence to invent cooperation.

Before continent-scale settlement, communities could already have:

- defense compacts;
- river agreements;
- guild recognition;
- local truces;
- safe-passage conventions;
- seasonal market arrangements.

The Convergence can therefore formalize, connect, or standardize practices that already existed unevenly.

This aligns with the newer Convergence owner.

---

# 14. Historical dating

Legacy uses precise negative-year chronology.

Do not promote it yet.

The newer [Calendars, Timekeeping, and Seasonal Coordination](../Calendar-and-Timekeeping.md) explicitly says historical dating still needs a common reference solution.

Therefore:

- old dates are provenance;
- event order may remain useful;
- exact year numbers are not current canon.

- industrial technology that exceeds current craft-scale baseline;
- "Three Sons" as current literal cosmology;
- exact negative-year chronology;

# 17. Foundation questions still genuinely open

Volume 01 reveals several unresolved questions that remain worth answering later:

- Was there actually a primordial joined landmass?
- What is the geological age / formation history of The Spine?
- How much of The Underpass is natural versus excavated?
- Which seismic zones are active?
- Which old states left durable infrastructure?
- Which pre-Convergence compacts were incorporated into later treaty law?
- Does the "Mother" myth survive in any current faith or secular tradition?
- How do scholars date deep history?
- When did people first establish major Port settlement?
- How old are the oldest continuously occupied cities?

These are real gaps, not reasons to restore the legacy answers automatically.

---

# 18. Foundation promotion test

Before promoting a Volume 01 claim, ask:

1. Does a newer owner already answer this?
2. Is the old claim physical fact, historical claim, or myth?
3. Is it compatible with current geography?
4. Is it compatible with current technology?
5. Is it compatible with Magic Baseline?
6. Does it depend on an obsolete regional name?
7. Does it depend on one-region-one-state logic?
8. Is its exact date actually supported?
9. Does the claim improve causal understanding?
10. Would keeping it create downstream contradictions?

If a later owner exists, that owner wins.
