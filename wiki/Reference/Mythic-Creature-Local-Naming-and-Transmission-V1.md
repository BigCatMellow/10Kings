# 1. Naming rule — do not fake local etymologies

The current naming owner has a hard lexical-generation gate.

Therefore this pass does **not** invent W1 / N1 / P1 / F1 / Port words and then claim ancient roots.

Until reusable vocabulary exists, use:

- translated common-language gloss;
- occupational shorthand;
- legal / scholarly label;
- child nickname;
- traveler nickname;
- unresolved local endonym.

This is especially appropriate for folklore because one legend may never have had one authoritative name.

A useful naming stack is:

~~~text
local spoken description
→ occupational shorthand
→ neighboring translation
→ archive / scholar category
→ Port popular form
~~~

The standardized name may be the **least culturally authentic name** and still be the one outsiders know.

# 2. Estuary Captive

## Naming ecology

This legend begins in Port.

Unlike the other three, it does not require translation from one ancestral regional language before entering Port culture.

But different Port institutions classify the event differently.

---

## A. Old harbor-family form

### Working common-language gloss

**the Stranger in the Net**

Alternative ordinary shortening:

**the Net Stranger**

Status:

- provisional translated / Port-common form;
- not an invented local-language endonym.

### Why this form works

It foregrounds:

- encounter;
- uncertainty;
- possible personhood.

It does **not** decide that the captive was:

- animal;
- spirit;
- man;
- woman;
- species.

This aligns with the old harbor-family ethical interpretation:

> Port caught something it did not understand.

### Likely users

- old harbor-worker families;
- D-1 North Coast-descended Port households;
- storytellers emphasizing treatment rather than taxonomy.

---

## B. Legal / archival form

### Working label

**the Estuary Custody Case**

Possible dry archival shorthand:

**the Custody**

Status:

- professional / legal common-language label.

### Why this works

Later jurists care about:

- custody;
- personhood;
- property;
- foreign status;
- jurisdiction.

The legal label can sound almost absurdly dry beside the folklore.

That is desirable.

---

## C. Natural-history / scholarly standard

### Working standard label

**the Estuary Captive**

This remains the current wiki umbrella term.

### Naming history

Likely sequence:

~~~text
harbor incident
→ scattered custody / harbor records
→ later legal summaries
→ naturalists compare descriptions
→ archive catalog needs stable heading
→ "Estuary Captive" becomes scholarly / interregional standard
~~~

### Local objection

Harbor-family critics may dislike **Captive** because it turns:

- a moral story about what Port did

into:

- an interesting specimen category.

Naturalists may think **Stranger in the Net** prejudges personhood.

Neither term is neutral.

That is useful.

---

## D. Child / Three Moons form

### Working popular nickname

**Netman**

Status:

- provisional Port child / puppet / street-performance nickname;
- intentionally much cruder than the archival labels.

Important:

**Netman does not establish that the being was male.**

It may simply be the fossilized name of the popular figure.

A later generation can argue about that too.

### Cultural mechanism

~~~text
ugly old custody story
→ simplified street story
→ puppet / mask
→ comic character
→ children know character before history
~~~

This is exactly the sort of transformation Three Moons can accelerate.

---

## E. North Coast reinterpretation

North Coast fishers may not accept any Port name.

A common translated reaction may be:

> "the thing Port caught"

or:

> "Port's net-creature"

depending on speaker.

Some insist it was an ordinary marine animal badly described.

Others connect it to older sea-being traditions.

No single N1 endonym should be invented yet.

# 3. Estuary Captive transmission history

~~~text
Port fishermen / harbor workers
→ municipal / legal custody
→ harbor-family oral memory
→ legal archive
→ natural-history classification
→ Three Moons popular culture
→ Port-born children
→ visitors carry simplified story outward
→ North Coast / other regions reinterpret it
~~~

This is unusual because:

> **the popular myth spreads outward from an archival urban incident rather than arriving in Port from the periphery.**

# 4. Estuary Captive in-world fragments

## Archival fragment — provisional prose artifact

> **For damage to the west net, payment deferred pending judgment as to whether the catch was cargo, animal, or person.**

Purpose:

- shows legal category failure;
- does not describe anatomy.

## Harbor-family saying

> **They spent three days asking what it was before anyone asked whether it was afraid.**

This should remain a family / reformist saying, not an objective historical quotation unless later sourced in-world.

## Child rhyme — provisional

> **Netman under, Netman deep;  
> count the boats and go to sleep.**

The rhyme is deliberately almost useless as historical evidence.

## Scholarly note

> The popular figure called Netman shares little beyond capture, silence, and return to water with the earliest custody records.

# 5. Road Hound

## Naming problem

This legend is transregional.

Therefore no single local culture should own the "correct" name.

The current term **Road Hound** works best as:

> **an interregional / scholarly umbrella translation.**

It may be a Port / HR-C catalog term that groups stories locals do not necessarily think are identical.

---

## A. M-1 repair-network form

### Working translated gloss

**the Quiet Dog**

Alternative family shorthand:

**the Quiet One**

Status:

- translated occupational / family usage;
- local endonym unresolved.

### Why this fits

Stable M-1 anchors:

- solitary;
- silent;
- appears on routes;
- follows / leads;
- associated with stopping places and absent travelers.

The name does not decide:

- spirit;
- omen;
- animal.

### Family variation

One route lineage might say **Quiet Dog**.

Another might simply say:

> **the dog on the road**

and reject the idea that every such story refers to one being.

This is more realistic than one universal folk name.

---

## B. High Roads caravan form

### Working traveler term

**Road Dog**

Status:

- plain High Roads trade / traveler translation.

Possible saying:

> **Road dog ahead, road stays. Road dog behind, ask why.**

Do not promote this exact saying yet; it is a scene-seed.

The core idea is that caravan usage may encode **position relative to the traveler**.

---

## C. M-2 pastoral form

### Working translated gloss

**the Ahead Dog**

Status:

- provisional descriptive translation;
- deliberately awkward enough to signal that this may be a gloss, not a native phrase.

A cleaner common-language paraphrase:

> **the dog that goes ahead**

Pastoral versions may interpret it more as:

- crossing / weather warning

than death omen.

---

## D. North Coast / roadhead form

No single name yet.

Possible ordinary translated descriptions:

- **the shore-road dog**;
- **the waiting dog**.

The tradition can become associated with:

- sailors who did not return;
- people walking the final road home from harbor.

Do not invent a melodramatic "Drowned Hound" unless local evidence later earns it.

---

## E. Port form

### Child / popular name

**Home Dog**

Status:

- provisional Port children's form.

This is deliberately warmer than M-1 / North Coast variants.

It reflects a city need:

> lost festival child + crowded streets + road home.

### Scholarly umbrella

**Road Hound**

Port / HR-C folklorists may prefer this category because it is broad enough to index:

- dog-like route apparitions;
- guardian-dog stories;
- missing-traveler traditions.

### Local objection

M-1 route families may say scholars have:

> put every dark dog in the same book.

That should be a recurring critique of classification.

---

# 6. Road Hound transmission history

Strongest route:

~~~text
working / guardian dogs on old roads
+
M-1 recurring repair circuits
+
M-2 pastoral crossings
+
High Roads caravan traffic
→ structurally similar route stories spread
→ each community assigns different meaning
→ HR-C / Port scholars compare them
→ umbrella "Road Hound" category
→ printed / festival / traveler versions spread back outward
~~~

This creates a **classification feedback loop**.

After scholars declare several stories related:

- later storytellers may begin borrowing features from the standardized version.

Therefore scholarship can change folklore.

---

# 7. Road Hound in-world fragments

## M-1 route saying

> **Quiet dog in front, keep your road. Quiet dog behind, check your company.**

Status:

- provisional translated saying.

## Port child line

> **Home Dog knows the lane you forgot.**

This can be:

- rhyme fragment;
- puppet-show line;
- parental reassurance.

## Scholar note

> The silence of the animal is more stable across traditions than its size, color, sex, or supposed purpose.

## Young-worker joke

> **Every dog is the Road Hound after the second inn.**

Useful because believers and skeptics share the tradition.

# 8. Forest Owner

## Naming problem

The current umbrella **Forest Owner** is potentially misleading.

That is useful.

Longwood's actual rights system specifically rejects:

> one permission = total ownership.

Therefore the outsider name can encode the outsider misunderstanding.

---

## A. LW-I / interior use-right form

### Working translated gloss

**the Turner**

Alternative explanatory phrase:

**the one that turns you back**

Status:

- translated common-language gloss;
- F1 endonym unresolved.

### Why this is stronger than Forest Owner

The local story's stable function is:

- disorientation;
- wrong path;
- return;
- boundary / permission.

It does not require a supernatural landlord.

"The Turner" describes what happens.

It does not claim what the being **is**.

---

## B. LW-W western-edge form

### Working common term

**the Turning**

This can function less as a creature name and more as an event:

> "He got the Turning."

Meaning:

- became disoriented;
- violated a use boundary;
- was repeatedly returned to the same place.

Some speakers can believe in a being.

Others believe only in the phenomenon / story.

This gives Longwood grammatical / conceptual variation without inventing F1 vocabulary.

---

## C. High Roads / outsider form

### Traveler standard

**Forest Owner**

Likely origin:

- literalized translation of stories about the forest "refusing" unauthorized use;
- High Roads / merchant tendency to ask:
  - who owns this?
  - who can grant passage?

The term may then spread into:

- Port;
- travel accounts;
- scholarship.

### Local objection

A Longwood rights-holder can reasonably say:

> **That's the first mistake. Nothing in the story says it owns the forest.**

That single disagreement teaches the reader something about Longwood law.

---

## D. Port popular form

Port illustrations may turn Forest Owner into a literal:

- wild man;
- bark-covered person;
- antlered figure.

This is likely an outsider invention.

Do not make those visual features part of the shared core.

---

# 9. Forest Owner transmission history

~~~text
local use-right / path stories
→ F1-speaking interior communities
→ LW-W market contact
→ route-builders / High Roads merchants translate concept
→ "Forest Owner" becomes convenient outsider label
→ Port illustrations literalize it
→ outsider image returns to Longwood
→ locals mock / reject / sometimes adopt the new image
~~~

This is a particularly strong example of:

> **translation changing ontology.**

A story about:

- boundary;
- path;
- obligation

becomes a story about:

- a creature who owns a forest.

---

# 10. Forest Owner in-world fragments

## Old dispute-record fragment

> **They were granted the road, not the cutting. When they left it, the wood returned them until morning.**

Status:

- provisional stylized translation of an old record;
- deliberately ambiguous between metaphor and testimony.

## Interior saying

> **A path is not permission.**

This may be older than the creature story or produced by it.

## Child warning

> **Take what is yours and the path stays put.**

Again:

- moral simplification;
- not literal legal doctrine.

## Outsider scholar note

> "Forest Owner" is retained here as the conventional interregional label despite repeated objections from Longwood informants that ownership is precisely what the tradition denies.

This is probably the strongest scholarly naming note of the four dossiers.

# 11. Knocking Below

## Naming problem

This tradition may be strongest if miners **do not agree that there is a named creature at all**.

The current umbrella **Knocking Below** should therefore remain:

> a scholarly / author category for several occupational traditions.

---

## A. SH-1 industrial mine usage

### Worker terms

Use plain forms:

- **the knocks**;
- **deep knocks**;
- **answering knocks**.

These are not names of a species.

Examples:

> "We had answering knocks on third shift."

> "Those were timber knocks."

The important distinction is categorical / practical.

---

## B. Small mine / craft-community form

### Working personified gloss

**the Deep Worker**

Status:

- provisional translated folk form;
- not universal;
- W1 local endonym unresolved.

This fits the idea:

> someone working where no crew was sent.

It can appear in:

- child story;
- old family tale;
- small mine superstition.

Industrial workers may regard it as:

> village dressing around the knocks.

---

## C. Underpass maintenance usage

Possible functional distinction:

- **working knocks** — rhythm suggesting tools / intentional striking;
- **settling knocks** — ordinary structural sound;
- **answering knocks** — apparent response to human sound.

Exact professional terminology remains open.

Do not create one Underpass creature name.

The Underpass is linguistically fragmented.

---

## D. Port / scholarly form

### Umbrella category

**Knocking Below**

Possible catalog shorthand:

**Knocker traditions**

But **Knocker** should be treated as an outsider noun category, not proof miners themselves imagine a small humanoid being.

### Local objection

Miner:

> **It's a sound. Port gave it a face.**

That line captures the whole transmission failure.

---

# 12. Knocking Below transmission history

~~~text
mine acoustics / warning behavior
→ local worker vocabulary
→ accidents / rescue stories
→ mine songs / memorial records
→ migrant workers carry terminology
→ Underpass crews compare similar phenomena
→ Port / guild scholars classify traditions together
→ popular illustration invents creature body
→ illustrated creature feeds back into children's stories
~~~

Again:

> classification changes the folklore.

---

# 13. Knocking Below in-world fragments

## Worker saying

> **You don't have to believe the knocks. You have to hear them.**

This is strong because it rejects the belief / skepticism binary.

## Accident-record fragment

> **Work ceased after three answers from beyond the face. The east section failed before dawn.**

Status:

- provisional fictional archive fragment.

## Child version

> **Deep Worker taps where the roof is thin.**

This personifies the phenomenon more than adult mine culture does.

## Port scholar note

> No reliable mine testimony describes the conventional illustrated "Knocker"; the figure appears to be substantially younger than the acoustic tradition.

# 16. Language-cluster implications

## W1

Knocking traditions can diverge among:

- industrial standard speech;
- valley / mine dialects;
- guild technical vocabulary.

When W1 vocabulary becomes sufficiently developed, derive local forms from:

- striking;
- answering;
- deep;
- work;

only if those lexemes exist independently.

## F1

Forest Owner / Turner terminology should be especially sensitive to:

- path;
- permission;
- return;
- boundary;
- use.

Do not derive an F1 word for "owner" merely to translate the outsider name.

That would reproduce the outsider conceptual error.

## P1 / High Roads contact registers

Road Hound categories are likely to accumulate:

- route;
- ahead / behind;
- crossing;
- camp

terminology.

P1 is particularly important as a **borrower / adapter**, not universal source.

## Port contact varieties

Port is where:

- categories shorten;
- foreign labels become ordinary;
- child forms emerge;
- legal / commercial terms become common slang.

Therefore Port may popularize names that no source community prefers.

# 17. Story-use recommendations

## Three Moons

Best myth use:

- child with Netman puppet / mask;
- harbor worker dislikes the festival version;
- Wurdren later learns the custody history.

No exposition dump required.

## Wurdren on road

Best Road Hound use:

- someone casually mentions Quiet Dog / route rule;
- another traveler calls it Road Hound;
- they realize they are not even certain they mean the same thing.

## Longwood counter-domino

Best Forest Owner use:

- outsider invokes "Forest Owner";
- local corrects the term;
- correction reveals actual use-right logic.

The mythology teaches jurisdiction through conversation.

## Stone Hills

Best Knocking use:

- young worker laughs at it;
- experienced worker distinguishes timber from answering knocks;
- no one needs to announce whether the creature exists.

# 18. Promotion recommendation

Promote only the following **structural rules** later if desired:

1. important legends can have multiple simultaneous names by institution / community;
2. scholarly standardization can distort local folklore;
3. translation can change what kind of being a legend appears to describe;
4. some occupational folklore may never possess one creature name at all;
5. local endonyms remain unresolved until actual lexicon exists.

Keep all exact translated labels on this page **provisional** until scene testing proves they sound natural.

---

# 19. Next step

The next useful test is **scene-level folklore use**, not more naming.

Build four short encounter seeds:

1. Three Moons — Netman puppet beside someone who knows the custody story;
2. road / guild journey — Quiet Dog / Road Hound naming disagreement;
3. Longwood — "Forest Owner" correction exposes a rights misunderstanding;
4. Stone Hills — answering knocks during an ordinary work disagreement.

Stress-test:

- does the legend emerge naturally?
- does it reveal community?
- can the scene work without confirming magic?
- does anyone sound like they are explaining lore to the reader?

Only after those pass should any label move closer to working canon.
