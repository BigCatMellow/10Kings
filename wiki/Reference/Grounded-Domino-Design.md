# Core principle

Show the audience **effects before plans**.

A conspiracy or large strategy becomes believable when ordinary people encounter consequences that make sense locally before anyone can see the whole chain.

A useful pattern is:

```text
small plausible disturbance
→ ordinary people adapt
→ institutions react for their own reasons
→ second-order pressure
→ another actor exploits that pressure
→ escalation
→ pattern becomes visible only in retrospect
```

The mastermind should not need every participant to understand the larger plan.

---

# 1. Start at ground level

Legacy Volume 12 repeatedly recommends revealing systemic manipulation through ordinary scenes rather than conspirator exposition.

Useful examples of the method include:

- a caravan guard noticing damaged infrastructure;
- a farmer finding seed prices unexpectedly high;
- a shop closing early because new tolls destroyed its margin;
- workers arguing about a rule or wage change without knowing the larger cause.

The important design rule is:

> **The first visible consequence should be something an ordinary person would notice for an ordinary reason.**

This aligns with the current Wurdren-level story perspective.

---

# 2. Use incomplete information

People should receive:

- rumor;
- gossip;
- contradictory reports;
- partial documents;
- secondhand claims;
- local explanations;
- accusations shaped by existing prejudice or interest.

Do not make every clue accurate.

Do not make every clue false.

The audience should be able to reconstruct a pattern later because the fragments were real enough to matter.

---

# 3. Every actor needs an independent reason

A domino is weak if a character acts only because the Villain needs the plot to move.

For each step, ask:

- What does this actor think is happening?
- What do they personally gain or fear?
- What existing grievance does the event activate?
- Why is their response reasonable from their information?
- Would they still take this action if they never learned the Villain existed?

A strong chain uses manipulation of **incentives and interpretation**, not mind control.

---

# 4. Mix scales

Volume 12 experimented with several domino scales.

A useful modern version is to combine:

## Small / easily overlooked

- record discrepancy;
- damaged bridge;
- missing shipment;
- delayed messenger;
- debt dispute;
- local tax change;
- missing person;
- altered contract.

## Mid-scale / personal

- guild lockout;
- disputed inheritance;
- canceled marriage agreement;
- public accusation;
- religious dispute;
- local succession problem;
- serious market shortage.

## Large / publicly visible

- riot;
- route closure;
- assassination or apparent assassination where story-appropriate;
- military mobilization;
- diplomatic rupture;
- major strike;
- mass displacement.

The large event should usually be **fed by smaller pressures**, not dropped into the story as a spectacle.

---

# 5. Let micro-crises do narrative work

A domino should create immediate local stakes even if its systemic importance is still hidden.

Examples:

- a household cannot afford food;
- a caravan cannot leave;
- an apprentice loses work;
- a village loses access to a crossing;
- a merchant cannot meet a debt;
- a family waits for someone who never arrives.

This gives each step value before the reader understands the larger plot.

---

# 6. Trusted institutions should fail for understandable reasons

Legacy notes repeatedly proposed showing institutions fail or become compromised.

The current version should follow [World Rules](../World-Rules.md):

Institutions do not fail because everyone becomes foolish.

They fail because:

- information is incomplete;
- jurisdictions overlap;
- leaders protect constituencies;
- budgets are limited;
- incentives conflict;
- debt constrains choices;
- rival factions block action;
- emergency measures create new problems;
- people mistake a manipulated event for a familiar local conflict.

A guild, faith, court, kingdom or Council family can make a rational local decision that worsens the continental situation.

---

# 7. Escalation should change the problem

Do not repeat the same crisis at larger volume.

A strong domino chain changes category.

Example structure:

```text
transport problem
→ price problem
→ debt problem
→ labor problem
→ political blame
→ security response
→ diplomatic problem
```

That transformation is what makes the chain feel systemic.

---

# 8. Give the reader retrospective clarity

The final reveal should not depend on one secret document explaining everything.

The audience should be able to look backward and realize:

- this shortage altered that decision;
- that decision created a local grievance;
- the grievance gave another actor cover;
- the apparent coincidence was prepared;
- several events the characters blamed on unrelated enemies were connected.

The intended reaction is:

> "I saw all of those pieces, but I did not know they formed this shape."

---

# 9. Preserve moral choice

Legacy Volume 12 repeatedly recommends attaching dominoes to a moral or ethical decision.

A domino is stronger when a character must choose among competing legitimate obligations.

Examples:

- protect workers or keep a vital industry running;
- publish destabilizing evidence or preserve immediate peace;
- honor a contract or feed a community;
- enforce a border rule or shelter displaced people;
- expose corruption or protect someone who will be harmed by the exposure.

The Villain can exploit the choice without causing the underlying values.

---

# 10. Grounded legacy seed bank

The following **old proposals remain non-canon** but are useful as design prompts because they are structurally grounded:

- missing diplomatic envoy;
- canceled royal marriage / alliance;
- tampering in a scriptorium or records office;
- grain tax producing famine warnings;
- noble debt to a foreign lender;
- a local water / weir dispute escalating into a border issue;
- disputed succession in a secondary polity;
- religious schism around a dubious miracle;
- local bandits becoming a proxy militia;
- foreign scholar accused of espionage;
- guild lockout;
- suspicious ship loss;
- damaged or missing archive material.

Use the **shape**, not necessarily the original event.

Any event selected for current canon must be rebuilt against current geography, institutions, technology, culture and current-event owners.

---

# 11. Legacy material to reject unless current rules change

Volume 12 also contains more fantastical action variants such as:

- golem revolts;
- shadow plagues;
- kraken attacks;
- overtly magical treaties;
- magical beasts used as crisis engines.

These are not automatically invalid ideas forever.

They are simply **unsupported by the current low-magic / uncertain-magic framework** and should remain dormant unless later magic rules justify them.

---

# 12. Domino test

Before accepting a domino, answer:

1. What ordinary person notices it first?
2. What do they think caused it?
3. Which institution reacts?
4. Why is that reaction reasonable?
5. What second-order consequence follows?
6. Who benefits accidentally?
7. Who exploits it deliberately?
8. What existing grievance does it activate?
9. What evidence could later connect it to the larger chain?
10. Would the step still be interesting if the Villain were not revealed yet?
11. Does it create a new kind of pressure rather than simply "more chaos"?
12. Can the Council plausibly misread or partially contain it without becoming incompetent?

If several answers require "because the plot needs it," redesign the domino.

# 13. Relationship to operation design

[Domino Operation Design — V1](Domino-Operation-Design-V1.md) now adds a second gate.

This page asks:

> does the causal chain work?

The operation-design page asks:

> does each Villain intervention have a distinct craft, visible explanation, evidence trail, human cost, and failure mode?

Both are required.

A domino can be causally sound and still be dramatically weak if every Villain move is just:

- rumor;
- leak;
- sabotage;
- repeat.

The current plan deliberately varies:

- route-intelligence manipulation;
- credit engineering;
- alternative logistics;
- document manipulation;
- contract theft;
- conflict brokerage;
- procurement timing;
- withholding;
- curated evidence.

The Villain's strategic **fingerprint** should remain consistent even when the operational method changes.

# 14. Relationship to war-causality proof

[War Escalation Causal Chain — V1](War-Escalation-Causal-Chain-V1.md) applies this method across the full current-events field.

It adds a strict transition test:

~~~text
actor
+ known problem
+ locally rational action
+ material / legal capability change
+ outside interpretation
+ next incentive
→ next domino
~~~

No transition may use "tension rises" as sufficient explanation for war.

[Domino Conspiracy Ledger — V1](Domino-Conspiracy-Ledger-V1.md) then decomposes selected Villain nudges into small background tasks / micro-actions.

Together the layers are:

~~~text
CURRENT EVENTS
what ordinary people can notice

↓ author analysis

WAR CAUSAL CHAIN
why the system moves from local stress to war

↓ hidden execution

CONSPIRACY LEDGER
what small covert work makes selected transitions more likely
~~~

The large conspiracy should be visible to the author long before it is visible to the reader.

---

# 15. Relationship to Worldbuilding Breath

[Worldbuilding Breath](Worldbuilding-Breath.md) and this method reinforce each other.

Breath asks for:

- ordinary consequences;
- wrong information;
- irrelevant specifics;
- bureaucracy;
- off-camera relationships;
- faded fashions and grievances.

Grounded domino design uses those ordinary details to let a large hidden chain become visible indirectly.

The overlap is deliberate:

```text
systemic plan
→ ordinary disturbance
→ lived detail
→ local interpretation
→ consequence
→ later pattern recognition
```

This is the preferred scale transition for the story.
