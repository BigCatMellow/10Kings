# World Review, 2026-11

## Status

**Review record, not setting canon.** bt 7274 asked on 2026-11-04 to "expand, organize and refine our research and notes. Theres a lot here, and so that means theres a lot that can be lost. Also all this was made with older models, so maybe new insights can be found now that we are using more advanced models." This page records what that review found and what was done with each finding. The changes themselves live on their owner pages, and anything marked *lean* is a judgment for bt 7274 to overrule.

## How it was done

The wiki (about 100,000 words of setting, 26,000 of reference research, and the legacy notes) was split into six clusters, each read by a separate reviewer that had not written any of it. Each checked its pages against the order of authority (bt 7274's rulings D1–D25, then [World Rules](../World-Rules.md) and the established and working-canon pages, then the provisional candidates, then reference and legacy material) and reported contradictions, stale lines, duplicates, padding, material worth promoting, missing links and insights. The reports are kept as written:

1. [Information systems](World-Review-2026-11/1-Information-Systems.md): Movement, Couriers, Records, Education
2. [Law, money and time](World-Review-2026-11/2-Law-Money-Time.md): Law, Money, Calendar, Trade
3. [Social systems](World-Review-2026-11/3-Social-Systems.md): World System, Social Fabric, Daily Life, Folk Belief, Medicine and others
4. [Culture](World-Review-2026-11/4-Culture.md): arts, clothing, festivals, architecture, weapons, languages, food
5. [Core canon](World-Review-2026-11/5-Core-Canon.md): the overview, rules, regions, places, history, politics, story and Open Questions
6. [Reference and legacy](World-Review-2026-11/6-Reference-and-Legacy.md): the Reference folder and what the legacy notes still hold

The findings were then reconciled into the owner pages in three batches and the connections rechecked with the [Spiderweb Audit](Spiderweb-Audit.md).

## What it found, in short

- **No settled rule is broken** anywhere: magic, the gods, faiths crossing borders and the Council working through dependencies all hold.
- **The older pages fell behind the rulings.** The seventeen recovered system pages predate D24 and D25 and the 2026-09-29 language and Council rulings, so dozens of lines still called settled things open.
- **Eight real contradictions**, the biggest being Three Moon's cadence (yearly in the stories, every seven years on its own page) and printing (one story plan says it doesn't exist; another story has a printed sheet).
- **Heavy duplication and padding.** About half of the 45,000 words in the recovered system pages is generic list-making; some concepts are explained on four or five pages.
- **Most pages had no status label**, so neither a reader nor the Atlas could tell canon from candidate.
- **The legacy notes still hold the specific, spoken texture** the wiki lacks, because earlier passes turned concrete lines into category lists.

## Leans taken

Each is applied provisionally and can be overruled in one line.

1. **Three Moon is yearly**, spelled "Three Moon".
2. **No presses**; Port offices block-print forms.
3. **Ledger Closing is Highridge's own named day**, and Highridge's credit year runs from Pass Opening to Ledger Closing.
4. **The Current Events set is working canon at event level**, with names and hidden layers provisional, because fourteen drafted stories and the Domino Map rest on it.
5. **There is no standing Convergence court**; agreed forums sit as needed in Highridge and Port.
6. **The Council was founded before the Convergence**, out of idealism and more openly, and was driven into secrecy (from the legacy notes); an openness faction survives.
7. **Northwind's lack of a Council house is deliberate**, and a grievance the Villain can use without it being his homeland.
8. **House Luthain's power is lending backed by metal** and Ironcrest's skill with contracts.
9. **The gods' Latinate names are a shared liturgical register.**
10. **Writing and burning is one folk practice** that three faiths absorbed.
11. **The Fell Pantheon stays out**, except as heresy talk between faiths.
12. **Deepwood and Ridge-Roamer healers are mostly outside the Apothic Circle.**
13. **The local route authority may quarantine a pass**, contestably, on Port's model.
14. **Polities keep small forces, and the fighters' guild supplies specialists for hire.**
15. **Greenvale likely borders Highridge directly**, left open on Geography.

## Insights

New readings the review found, each with where it now lives. "Recorded" means it is on its owner page as a provisional reading.

1. **Convergence arbitration has no bailiff; credit is its enforcement.** Treaty awards hold because the Council's levers punish refusal, so its belief that it keeps the Convergence alive is literally true of this function, and a fair award refused by a popular ruler is the Villain's best target. Recorded on [Law](../Law-and-Jurisdiction.md#9-cross-border-enforcement-depends-on-recognition).
2. **Nobody owns classification.** Whoever succeeds in calling an incident "Convergence-level" makes retaliation legitimate. Recorded on Law.
3. **Leverage needs renewal.** Short, renewable, conditioned credit is power; long or uncallable credit is not (The Goat File against The Tree). Recorded on [Money](../Money-Credit-and-Measures.md#9-credit-is-structurally-central).
4. **In a panic the Council does the one central-bank job that reveals it.** Recorded on Money.
5. **Late autumn is the continent's settlement season**, so Council leverage and panic risk peak every year then. Recorded on [Calendar](../Calendar-and-Timekeeping.md).
6. **Records that aren't writing**: year-cloths, steel pillars, carved project signs, ballads and crate-lid marks are a second evidence system beside the ledger. Recorded on [Records](../Records-Literacy-and-Authentication.md#records-that-arent-writing).
7. **Records make false signals**: a rumor written into a register starts to count as verified. Recorded on [Movement](../Movement-and-Information.md#9-records-are-a-different-kind-of-power).
8. **A watermark names a stationer, never a buyer**, which protects the ship's-lantern letters and keeps Port paper from implying the Villain's homeland. Recorded on Records and [Villain](../Story/Villain.md).
9. **Trade Tongue strips evidential markers**, so rumor gains certainty in Port and can lose it again in Highridge's careful registers. Recorded on Movement.
10. **Information failure is what keeps D21 working**: in the stories, records cross regions only as bundles nobody reads whole. Recorded on Movement.
11. **The Overpath and the Underpass are everyday routes**, not only bypasses. Recorded on Movement.
12. **The continents must meet by land**, because every strongly established border is a land border in one chain. Recorded on [Geography](../Geography-and-Connections.md#macro-geography).
13. **Northwind borders none of its suppliers**, so its grain and metal come through Icestep or Port. Recorded on Geography.
14. **Northwind has no Council house; there is no Labor seat; the carrier is split from the content (Veynor and Rhaelen); Treasury sits away from the finance hubs; Greenvale distrusts what its own house does.** Recorded on [Economic Council](../Politics/Economic-Council.md#the-six-houses).
15. **The Council's founding answers the ending**: if it began open, losing its secrecy is partly a return. Recorded on Economic Council.
16. **The Northern language family has no recorded contact route** between Northwind and Ironcrest. Recorded on [Language and Thought](../Culture/Language-and-Thought.md#language-families).
17. **Trade Tongue builds a power gap into speech**: it can greet a land agent but may not be able to say "held for the family". Recorded on Language and Thought.
18. **Civic festivals had quietly brought back a faith per region** (all of Ironcrest's festivals are fire, Deepwood's are "quiet"); civic festivals and faith rites are now separated. Recorded on [Festivals](../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#cross-regional-effects).
19. **No festival marks the Convergence.** Open, recorded on Festivals.
20. **Three faiths share one write-and-burn rite**, best read as older folk religion beneath them. Recorded on [Religions](../Politics/Religions.md#working-life-and-gods) and Folk Belief.
21. **The faiths collect but never conclude, in the stories.** Recorded on Religions.
22. **Quarantine collides with hospitality**, and a rumor of plague closes a pass faster than plague. Recorded on [Medicine](../Medicine-and-Healing.md#7-public-health-exists-before-modern-medicine).
23. **The Apothic list collides with forest medicine**, and the pressure lands on the Ridge-Roamers. Recorded on Medicine.
24. **Wurdren works as a social chokepoint**: accumulated trust across groups is the one chokepoint the Council can't price. Recorded on [World System](../World-System.md#10-the-story-is-embedded-in-the-system).
25. **Weapons had no politics**: who recruits, pays and forbids. Recorded on [Weapons](../Culture/Weapons-and-Elite-Troops.md#elite-troop-rule).
26. **Mushroom scarcity is a cross-story thread with no food owner.** Recorded on [Food](../Culture/Food.md#food-and-story).
27. **The Cellar Song may be what fixed the Blight at seven years.** Recorded on [Contested Memory](../History/Contested-Memory.md#other-remembered-labels).
28. **Promotion has been lossy in one direction**: promote the line and the mechanism, never only the category. Recorded on the [backlog](README.md#legacy-reconciliation-backlog).

**Recorded here only, for later:**

- A dead Forge smith's tools are claimed three ways (faith says melt, heir says keep, creditor says sell): a world-story engine with no new lore.
- The Masoncraft three-region rule makes masons the most cross-regional workforce by rule: a natural point of view for a world story.
- The identity stack lacks a row for what other people call you, where stereotypes live (Port's "none stay" saying erases the Port-born).
- Faiths keep the dates: when a dispute turns on "since when", a shrine log may be the only evidence.
- The Deepwood logging fight is fed by Ironcrest's furnaces; which Council seat's plans leak (Materials for fuel, Works for roads) gives two different stories.
- D24 gives the history its first fixed anchor: negotiated passage in Highridge is older than the Convergence and the model for its route rules.
- The Circle of Mirrors gives the Council a presence in stories without action: unwitting critics whose remarks a stranger writes down.
- Documents are natural carriers of patterns: a story told partly as ledger entries would let the reader see what characters can't.
