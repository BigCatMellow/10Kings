# Working Political Geography Draft — V1

## Status

**Provisional design artifact — not canon.**

This page places the functional governance models onto the current physical network.

It does **not** assign final polity names, ruler names or titles, capitals, exact borders, constitutional forms, treaty signatories, succession rules, exact municipal status, or the sovereignty of every small jurisdiction.

Instead it asks:

> **What is the smallest concrete set of political actors and jurisdictions that can make the current world actually work on a map?**

The goal is to avoid two opposite failures:

1. turning the six cultural cores into six states;
2. filling the map with decorative kingdoms before the world needs them.

This page uses functional IDs so geography can be tested before naming.

# 1. Political-map vocabulary

V1 distinguishes four things.

## Polity

An authority capable of meaningful territorial government and some external political action.

A polity may later prove to be a kingdom, city-state, league, principality, clan coalition, local republic, or mixed system.

V1 does not decide those constitutional labels yet.

## Local jurisdiction

A town, estate, island, chartered district, market, or similar place with real local authority but uncertain or subordinate sovereignty.

## Functional jurisdiction

An authority whose competence follows a system rather than a territory.

Examples include route compacts, watershed bodies, fishery agreements, irrigation compacts, and convoy commands.

## Settlement node

A politically important city, town, or roadhead that may or may not be a polity.

The central distinction is:

> **Every important place does not need to be a state, and every important authority does not need its own territory.**

---

# 2. V1 minimum-complexity rule

Before adding a polity, ask:

> What existing world function fails without it?

If the answer is only that the map would look more politically varied, do not add it.

V1 therefore deliberately tests:

- **one major Stone Hills territorial polity** rather than several western kingdoms;
- **one major Low Rivers territorial polity** rather than splitting the grain basin for symmetry;
- political plurality where current owners already require it:
  - High Roads;
  - Longwood;
  - Old Cities;
  - North Coast islands and harbors;
  - Port;
  - route, water, and maritime jurisdictions.

If this simpler map fails, later drafts can split the larger polities.

# 4. Stone Hills — minimum political geography

## SH-A — major western territorial polity

**V1 status: required test actor.**

SH-A represents the minimum large-scale authority needed to satisfy the current Stone Hills model.

It should control a substantial share of:

- western uplands;
- settled mining and workshop districts;
- tax base;
- military capacity;
- major roads;
- external diplomacy.

It does not need to contain the entire Stone Hills cultural core.

It does not directly operate every mine or guild.

### Why V1 includes it

Current Stone Hills owners already require:

- greater consolidation than the pre-Convergence fragmented era;
- rulers capable of military and fiscal action;
- strategic interest in metal;
- meaningful diplomacy.

One substantial territorial polity is the smallest solution.

### What remains open

- monarchy, republic, or mixed form;
- exact size;
- whether rivals exist elsewhere in Stone Hills;
- capital;
- succession;
- border with The Spine and High Roads;
- exact relationship to western ports.

---

## SH-M — major mining / guild city jurisdiction

**V1 status: strongly useful, not necessarily sovereign.**

SH-M is a major industrial city or district inside, beside, or partially autonomous from SH-A.

It exists because the Stone Hills governance test needs a place where mine owners, guilds, workers, lenders, civic government, and ruler's officials all have to bargain.

SH-M can support the current labor-unrest storyline without requiring labor conflict to occur in the ruler's capital.

### V1 default

Treat SH-M as a real civic jurisdiction inside SH-A's wider political sphere, with enough local authority to resist complete administrative absorption.

Do not yet decide whether it is chartered, historically independent, directly governed, guild-weighted, or noble-controlled.

---

## Western Gate jurisdiction

The R1 / Western Gate corridor needs a political arrangement.

V1 does not create another kingdom for it.

Instead:

~~~
SH-A territory / influence
→ one or more pass / border authorities
→ HR-W and High Roads route compact
~~~

This may eventually support a march, principality, toll town, or mixed border district.

For now it remains a **functional / frontier jurisdiction**, not an additional required sovereign polity.

This preserves the useful legacy buffer-march / pass-polity mechanism without promoting a legacy state prematurely.

# 5. North Coast — minimum political geography

## NC-M — major mainland maritime polity

**V1 status: required test actor.**

NC-M is the minimum larger North Coast authority capable of:

- diplomacy;
- war;
- major convoy policy;
- strategic harbor investment;
- anti-piracy action;
- relations with Port.

It occupies a substantial part of the more connected southern / central mainland coast.

### Why one major polity is enough for V1

The current owners do not yet require multiple equal North Coast states.

A single larger mainland polity lets V1 test whether strong central maritime policy, clan obligations, autonomous harbors, and island jurisdictions can coexist.

If that produces too much centralization, V2 can split it.

---

## NC-I — island polity / highly autonomous island jurisdiction

**V1 status: required as a distinct political actor, sovereignty still open.**

At least one island or island cluster should possess enough separate authority to make the established North Coast island problem real.

NC-I needs meaningful control over some combination of:

- local law;
- harbor use;
- fishing access;
- taxes or fees;
- rescue obligations;
- local defense.

It may be sovereign, confederated with NC-M, a protectorate, tributary, or treaty-associated.

V1 leaves that open.

### Why it matters

Without an island actor distinct from the mainland center, North Coast's established island autonomy risks becoming only flavor.

NC-I gives the world a concrete place where seasonal rights cross political status, convoy demands are contested, mainland authority has limits, and food / shipping dependency matters.

---

## NC-R — North Shoulder northern roadhead

**V1 status: required settlement jurisdiction.**

NC-R is the northern roadhead already implied by the North Shoulder test.

It belongs somewhere inside North Coast political geography but does not need sovereign status.

It must have enough authority to manage:

- cart-to-pack transfer;
- storage;
- guide hiring;
- route information;
- animal yards;
- local security.

### V1 default

Place NC-R under NC-M's wider political umbrella while preserving strong municipal and route authority.

This is the simpler model.

If later route politics require NC-R to be politically separate, V2 can change it.

---

## Fisheries and convoy authority

North Coast also needs functional jurisdictions that do not map neatly onto NC-M / NC-I borders.

These can include:

- fishery access agreements;
- rescue obligations;
- seasonal anchorage rights;
- temporary convoy command;
- anti-piracy coordination.

Do not draw these as colored states on the map.

---

# 6. Low Rivers — minimum political geography

## LR-A — major G1 basin polity

**V1 status: required test actor.**

LR-A is a substantial territorial polity covering much of the central G1 agricultural basin.

It provides the minimum larger authority needed for:

- taxation;
- military action;
- major public works;
- emergency grain policy;
- higher enforcement;
- diplomacy.

### Important design choice

V1 deliberately does not split Low Rivers into several equivalent states.

The existing owners do not require that complexity yet.

LR-A therefore tests the strongest minimum model:

> a large agrarian polity whose internal institutions remain politically powerful enough that one polity does not mean one administrative machine.

---

## LR-M — G1 head-of-navigation market jurisdiction

**V1 status: required settlement jurisdiction.**

LR-M is the unnamed transfer city already developed by [G1 Head-of-Navigation Market](G1-Head-of-Navigation-Market-Test.md).

V1 places it inside LR-A's wider political sphere.

It still needs meaningful local authority over:

- quays;
- warehouses;
- markets;
- weighing;
- crossings;
- streets;
- fire;
- local commercial disputes.

### Why this matters

This lets the world distinguish:

- LR-A's territorial sovereignty;
- LR-M's local commercial power;
- watershed and navigation authority;
- merchant and guild control of actual cargo systems.

LR-M can therefore become politically consequential without becoming a city-state merely because it is important.

---

## G1 watershed / navigation jurisdiction

**V1 status: required functional layer.**

The G1 system needs institutions whose authority follows the river rather than ordinary administrative lines.

They may coordinate:

- navigation condition;
- water allocation;
- flood warnings;
- shared works;
- disputes.

V1 keeps exact organization open.

Because LR-A is the only major Low Rivers polity in V1, the G1 body first functions mainly across towns, estates, villages, and market jurisdictions.

It can become cross-polity where G1 reaches High Roads / Port or if V2 later splits Low Rivers.

---

# 7. High Roads — minimum political geography

High Roads cannot be reduced to one V1 polity without contradicting its functional model.

V1 therefore requires at least **three substantial local political actors**.

They are not three provinces of a hidden High Roads state.

---

## HR-N — North Shoulder / northern route polity

**V1 status: required.**

HR-N includes the High Roads-side roadhead and enough surrounding settlement / land authority to make the North Shoulder compact meaningful.

It needs:

- local government;
- road and pass interests;
- herding constituencies;
- storage and market interests;
- ability to bind at least some route obligations.

It interacts directly with NC-R.

---

## HR-W — Western Gate polity / route district

**V1 status: required.**

HR-W sits around the High Roads side of the R1 / Western Gate system.

It may be a pass town and hinterland, small principality, route lordship, city / fortress polity, or mixed district.

Its exact form remains open.

### Why V1 needs it

Without a distinct western actor:

- Western Gate politics collapses directly into SH-A versus High Roads;
- the route compact becomes abstract;
- toll and maintenance politics lose a local owner.

HR-W makes the corridor politically concrete.

---

## HR-C — central switching / arbitration polity

**V1 status: required.**

HR-C is a major interior High Roads city or local polity where several systems overlap:

- caravan routes;
- brokerage;
- records;
- arbitration;
- road information;
- access toward G1 and Port.

It does not become the capital of High Roads.

It is simply the strongest candidate for a recurring neutral or prestigious coordination center.

### Design value

HR-C can host multi-corridor meetings, major arbitration, merchants, guilds, religious lodging, and political delegates without ruling HR-N or HR-W.

---

## High Roads functional overlay

The three actors participate in overlapping compacts.

Example:

~~~
NC-R + HR-N
→ North Shoulder compact

SH-A frontier + HR-W
→ Western Gate compact

HR-N + HR-C
→ northern interior corridor arrangements

HR-W + HR-C
→ western interior corridor arrangements

HR-C + LR-M / LR-A interests
→ River Road / G1 access arrangements
~~~

A recurring High Roads assembly can emerge from these relationships later.

V1 does not make it sovereign.

---

# 8. Longwood — minimum political geography

Longwood's decentralization requires more than one local polity.

V1 uses **three different external interfaces** to avoid inventing arbitrary forest kingdoms.

---

## LW-W — western forest-edge polity / trade jurisdiction

**V1 status: required.**

LW-W sits near R4 where Stone Hills, Low Rivers, and Longwood interact.

It handles some combination of:

- timber and charcoal movement;
- trade;
- local roads;
- forest access;
- migrant labor;
- settlement pressure.

### Why it must be politically distinct enough to matter

The current logging / road conflicts need a local actor capable of saying yes, no, only under conditions, that concession is invalid, or that road crosses our rights.

LW-W may be a town-centered polity, petty domain, or alliance.

---

## LW-I — interior forest polity / alliance

**V1 status: required.**

LW-I represents a less trade-oriented interior jurisdiction around the G2 / deeper forest system.

It needs enough coherence to manage some combination of:

- local land and use rights;
- ecological response;
- water;
- specialist / warden authority;
- defense;
- relations with neighboring communities.

It should not be a mystical isolationist state.

Its importance is that Longwood interior politics has an actor not defined entirely by outside extraction.

---

## LW-S — southern forest-margin polity

**V1 status: required.**

LW-S sits on or near the R5 transition toward the Old Cities.

It creates a political interface among forest goods, southern market traffic, migrants, water / agriculture transitions, and road access.

### Why this third actor is useful

With only LW-W and LW-I, Longwood's political map would be defined mostly by western extraction versus interior resistance.

LW-S creates an independent southern axis with different incentives.

That is consistent with the rule that regions should not collapse into one conflict or personality.

---

## Longwood compacts

V1 does not create a regional council.

Instead:

- R4 issues can involve LW-W;
- G2 ecological / watershed issues can involve LW-I plus Low Rivers interests;
- R5 trade issues can involve LW-S and Old Cities actors;
- a major fire, disease, or invasion can temporarily coordinate all three.

That is enough to make decentralized politics operational.

---

# 9. Old Cities — minimum political geography

Old Cities requires the clearest plurality.

V1 uses **three city-states** as the minimum useful set.

Three is important because two can cooperate while a third fears that cooperation becoming hegemony, and leagues can form and break without reducing every conflict to one binary rivalry.

V1 does not claim the final setting has only three.

---

## OC-U — upstream / inland city-state

**V1 status: required.**

OC-U sits in the upper or middle S1 water system.

It controls or strongly influences:

- headworks;
- upstream diversions;
- inland estates and client territory;
- routes toward R5 and Longwood.

It should not control all S1 water by right.

Its leverage is precisely what makes shared-water politics consequential.

---

## OC-D — downstream / lower-basin city-state

**V1 status: required.**

OC-D depends materially on flow through the shared S1 system.

It controls:

- downstream agricultural territory;
- dense irrigated settlement;
- major markets;
- possibly a coastal or near-coastal outlet.

OC-D and OC-U are the natural first pair for the existing shared-irrigation test.

---

## OC-C — separate coastal rival

**V1 status: required.**

OC-C is not dependent on the exact same S1 arrangement to the same degree.

It should possess:

- coastal trade;
- access to M2;
- merchant and civic power;
- independent diplomacy.

### Why it exists

OC-C gives the current city-state coordination crisis a real third-party observer.

For example:

~~~
OC-U + OC-D coordinate over water / defense
→ OC-C worries a temporary arrangement is becoming a dominant bloc
~~~

The fear can be sincere even if OC-U and OC-D are not planning unification.

That matches the current-events model.

---

# 10. Port — P

## P — Port city-polity

**V1 status: established special jurisdiction; exact constitution open.**

Port remains politically separate from surrounding mainland jurisdictions.

It has:

- local civic government;
- harbor authority;
- courts and arbitration;
- watch / local security;
- revenue needs;
- treaty-constrained external status.

V1 does not assign Port to LR-A, an Old Cities state, High Roads, North Coast, or Stone Hills.

That separation is essential.

---

# 11. The Underpass — political overlay

The Underpass should not be one underground kingdom.

The physical V1 already has:

- U1 western branch;
- U2 northern branch;
- U3 forest branch.

Political V1 therefore assumes at least **three distinct control environments**.

## U-W

Western branch junction / enclave associated with Stone Hills and Western Gate traffic.

## U-N

Northern branch jurisdiction associated with North Shoulder movement.

## U-F

Forest-side branch / enclave system associated with Longwood routes.

These need not be sovereign polities.

They can be enclave councils, toll holders, local strongmen, guild-linked authorities, settlement alliances, or mixed legal zones.

The requirement is simply:

> **No single authority should automatically control all three.**

That preserves the Underpass as a bypass network rather than a hidden empire.

# 13. What V1 deliberately does not add

V1 does not yet create:

- a second full Stone Hills kingdom;
- a second large Low Rivers state;
- a High Roads federation;
- a Longwood national council;
- an Old Cities federal government;
- a North Coast national navy with universal authority;
- a protector state surrounding Port;
- a unified Underpass government;
- independent microstates at every bridge and pass.

Any of those can still emerge if later tests require them.

They are not currently needed.

# 14. Border logic

The political borders should not be drawn as clean cultural outlines.

V1 expects several types.

## Administrative borders

Where two territorial polities meet.

Examples may include SH-A versus neighboring eastern authorities, OC-U versus OC-D, and OC-D versus OC-C where they touch.

## Fuzzy frontier zones

Where authority weakens across forest, mountain, grazing, or low-density settlement.

Likely around Longwood, High Roads, and Stone Hills shoulders.

## Functional overlap zones

Where two authorities coexist over different issues.

Examples:

- G1 water body across LR-A local jurisdictions;
- North Shoulder route compact across NC-R / HR-N;
- S1 irrigation compact across OC-U / OC-D;
- fishery rights across NC-M / NC-I maritime space.

## Contact zones

Cultural mixing can cross all of the above.

The political map should therefore never be used as a cultural map.

# 15. Current-event stress test

## A. Stone Hills labor unrest

SH-A wants production stability.

SH-M contains owners, guilds, workers, lenders, and civic authorities.

This produces a real political conflict without requiring the King versus all miners.

**PASS.**

## B. Unusual Stone Hills metal movement

Metal can leave SH-M through SH-A official routes, Western Gate, HR-W / HR-C, other markets, secondary western ports, private contracts, or U-W.

SH-A can investigate without possessing perfect economic visibility.

**PASS.**

## C. North Coast piracy / convoy politics

NC-M can push for convoy, patrol, and harbor rules.

NC-I can resist or negotiate.

NC-R / HR-N offers an expensive overland substitute.

Port reacts to maritime risk.

**PASS.**

## D. Low Rivers abundance crisis

LR-A can attempt reserve purchases, export policy, and tax relief.

LR-M's storage / transfer capacity determines how much those policies matter.

Local estates, cooperatives, and creditors still shape outcomes.

**PASS.**

## E. High Roads caravan attacks

Attacks can affect HR-N corridor, HR-W corridor, and HR-C switching routes differently.

Coordination therefore requires actual political bargaining rather than a fictional High Roads ministry.

**PASS.**

## F. Longwood logging conflict

An outside or western interest can negotiate with LW-W.

LW-I may dispute ecological effects, local use-right holders may challenge the agreement, and LW-S can take a different political position.

Longwood therefore has internal politics rather than one regional response.

**PASS.**

## G. Old Cities drought / coordination

S1 drought pressures OC-U and OC-D.

Their cooperation alarms OC-C.

That directly supports water politics, bloc anxiety, genuine coordination, and genuine suspicion.

**STRONG PASS.**

## H. Port pressure

Port sits among several independent major actors:

- NC-M;
- SH-A;
- LR-A;
- HR interests;
- OC city-states.

No one outside polity can claim that Port is naturally its own regional city.

Neutrality therefore remains strategically intelligible.

**STRONG PASS.**

# 16. Convergence stress test

The Convergence needs enough real actors that six regional representatives is visibly wrong.

V1 supplies that immediately.

Potential participants or bound actors can include some combination of:

- SH-A;
- NC-M;
- NC-I if sufficiently sovereign;
- LR-A;
- HR-N / HR-W / HR-C individually or through issue-specific delegations;
- LW-W / LW-I / LW-S or larger alliances depending on period;
- OC-U;
- OC-D;
- OC-C;
- Port;
- route and water parties to attached agreements.

The exact historical signatories remain open.

## Important result

V1 shows that the Convergence can plausibly have major territorial rulers, city-states, local route powers, special jurisdictions, and collective delegations at the same settlement.

It no longer needs to pretend six culturally labeled kings signed one document.

**PASS.**

# 17. Competitive-sphere stress test

The competitive political / cultural spheres should cut across this map.

They should not become one geographic half of the world versus the other.

Instead:

- one Old Cities city can lean differently from another;
- one High Roads polity can favor different partners from another;
- merchant, guild, and faith networks can split internally;
- NC-I can disagree with NC-M;
- SH-M economic interests can differ from SH-A strategic policy;
- Port remains outside formal capture while residents and institutions lean in different directions.

V1 therefore supports networked competition rather than two colored empires.

**PASS.**

# 18. Does V1 create enough political friction?

Yes.

Without adding another state, V1 already creates recurring disputes over:

- Stone Hills ruler versus industrial city;
- mainland North Coast versus island authority;
- harbor and fishery rights;
- NC-R versus HR-N route obligations;
- SH-A versus HR-W Western Gate terms;
- LR-A versus LR-M commercial interests;
- LR-A local jurisdictions versus G1 institutions;
- HR-N / HR-W / HR-C corridor priorities;
- LW-W / LW-I / LW-S different external relationships;
- OC-U / OC-D water;
- OC-U / OC-D cooperation versus OC-C suspicion;
- every major actor versus Port neutrality;
- surface authorities versus Underpass jurisdictions.

The setting does not need more countries merely to create politics.

# 19. Where new polities would be justified later

A future actor should be added only if a concrete pressure cannot be represented by V1.

## Stone Hills

Add another sovereign western polity if SH-A becomes implausibly large, a western port needs independent diplomacy, or metal / food politics require interstate western rivalry.

## North Coast

Split NC-M if convoy politics require multiple mainland states, clan / harbor autonomy cannot survive inside one larger polity, or Port history requires a distinct maritime rival.

## Low Rivers

Add LR-B if G1 crosses a major internal interstate boundary, abundance policy differs across neighboring agrarian states, or upstream / downstream politics need state-level conflict.

## High Roads

Add another polity if a major route lacks a local political owner, HR-C becomes too central, or a herding / rural authority needs independent political standing.

## Longwood

Add another polity when a major watershed, road, city, or historical alliance requires one.

## Old Cities

Add additional city-states when a concrete port, water system, league, client territory, or rivalry needs an actor.

Old Cities is the region where future polity growth is most naturally expected.

# 20. Naming gate

Do not name SH-A, NC-M, LR-A, HR-N, and the other IDs yet.

Before naming a polity or city, determine:

1. language / naming history;
2. older local name versus official name;
3. what physical or political feature created the place;
4. whether the current name predates or postdates the Convergence;
5. whether outsiders use a different name;
6. whether a dynasty, guild, or conqueror renamed it;
7. whether the name belongs to the city, polity, dynasty, region, river, or route.

Do not create another set of fantasy compound names merely to replace the IDs.

Legacy polity names remain seed material only.

---

# 21. Border-drawing gate

V1 is ready for a political overlay but not final borders.

Before drawing a line, determine what the line represents:

- tax jurisdiction;
- military control;
- land title;
- court jurisdiction;
- treaty-recognized claim;
- route authority;
- water access;
- seasonal use.

One line should not silently represent all of those.

A final map may need:

- solid political borders;
- fuzzy frontiers;
- route jurisdictions;
- shared-water areas;
- maritime-use zones.

That is more faithful to the actual world than one layer of colored countries.

---

# 22. Strong V1 conclusions

The political geography can now be made concrete without returning to six-region state design.

Strongest results:

1. **Stone Hills can be tested with one major territorial polity** plus strong civic / industrial jurisdictions.
2. **Low Rivers can be tested with one major G1 basin polity** plus strong town, estate, village, market, and watershed institutions.
3. **North Coast needs at least one major mainland authority plus a genuinely distinct island jurisdiction** for its maritime politics to become concrete.
4. **High Roads requires multiple local political actors**, with HR-N / HR-W / HR-C as the minimum V1 arrangement.
5. **Longwood requires multiple local polities or alliances**, with at least western, interior, and southern political interfaces in V1.
6. **Old Cities needs at least three city-state actors** for shared-water cooperation, rivalry, and bloc anxiety to work without becoming binary.
7. **Port remains a separate special city-polity.**
8. **Underpass political control remains fragmented.**
9. Functional jurisdictions cross territorial borders throughout the map.
10. The resulting world is already politically dense enough that more states should be added only when a concrete system demands them.
