# Prose test

The first thing Wurdren learned when they reached the Longwood corridor was that the road worked.

He had expected this to be less inconvenient.

Carts moved through the lower section steadily.

Pack animals passed in both directions.

A repair crew was replacing a broken edge where runoff had eaten part of the roadbed.

Two roadside stalls sold:

- food;
- lamp oil;
- cord;
- bad tea.

A lodging yard was full.

Nobody Wurdren could see wanted the road gone.

The Longwood delegate noticed him looking.

"Disappointed?"

"No."

"You look disappointed."

"I had prepared myself for a simpler argument."

"You came to Longwood."

"That sounds like blame."

"It is information."

They continued uphill.

The forest closed nearer around the route than it had lower down.

Not untouched forest.

Wurdren could see:

- cut stools;
- younger regrowth;
- stacked fuelwood;
- marked trees;
- side paths;
- small cleared plots.

The place looked used.

That was different from looking empty.

A route worker stood beside a newly rebuilt drainage crossing and waved to the route-side representative traveling with them.

"You're late."

"We went to Port."

"That's what I said."

The worker looked at the Longwood delegate.

Then at Wurdren.

"Who's this?"

"Witness."

"To what?"

Wurdren answered:

"Apparently several things."

The worker considered him.

"Useful."

"No one has said that convincingly yet."

They left the carts near a cluster of houses and work sheds where the road widened around a water point.

People were waiting.

Too many people.

Wurdren looked at the delegate.

"You said meeting."

"This is a meeting."

"I imagined chairs."

"You imagined Port."

That was fair.

The delegate's arrival caused three different reactions.

One older man embraced them.

A woman near the water trough said:

"You should have stopped the work in Port."

A younger trader called from a doorway:

"Did they agree to widen it?"

All three spoke before the delegate had removed their travel pack.

Wurdren stepped aside.

He had learned enough to know when the neutral hand was mostly decorative.

The route representative did not receive a warmer welcome.

A local road custodian came forward and took the Port packet.

The custodian did not open it immediately.

"Lower work continued."

The delegate nodded.

"Fine."

The woman by the trough said:

"Fine?"

"The lower work is inside the existing road right."

"You don't know what they moved."

The road custodian answered:

"I do."

That started another argument.

Wurdren found a wall.

He leaned against it.

The delegate saw him.

"Comfortable?"

"Learning."

"Good."

The meeting eventually moved into a store building because rain began.

There were chairs.

Wurdren felt vindicated.

The Port copies were placed on a long table.

So were:

- a local road ledger;
- a cutting-right register;
- two hand-drawn site sketches;
- one document nobody could read easily because damp had taken half the ink.

The route representative looked at the collection.

"This is why we use copies."

The local woman who had wanted the work stopped answered:

"This is why we remember who was there."

The route representative wisely did not continue.

The Longwood delegate began with the Port result.

"Port certified that the commercial copy did not include the schedule governing new cutting outside the marked road bounds."

The younger trader said:

"So they can widen inside the bounds."

"Yes."

"Good."

The woman by the trough said:

"No."

The delegate looked at her.

"No what?"

"No widening."

"That is not what the local right says."

"I know what it says."

"Then say what you mean."

She crossed her arms.

"I mean every time we agree to one improvement, the road becomes the reason for the next one."

The younger trader laughed.

"The road is why you sold three loads of resin last month."

"And the carts tore the lower path."

"The road custodian repaired it."

"With whose labor?"

"Paid labor."

"Paid by whom?"

The argument widened.

Wurdren watched the Longwood delegate fail to control it.

That was useful.

In Port the delegate had looked like the person who knew what Longwood meant.

Here they were:

- one negotiator;
- with one mandate;
- among people who had known them too long to be impressed.

Wurdren leaned toward them.

"You represent all of this?"

The delegate looked at him.

"No."

"You let me think you did."

"I told you I was a delegate."

"To me that meant—"

"That was your mistake."

There it was again.

Wurdren nodded.

"All right."

The delegate looked almost disappointed that he had not argued.

The road custodian opened the local ledger.

"Can we do the actual sections?"

That helped.

There were two.

The first narrow section ran between:

- the road edge;
- a steep bank;
- a managed cutting strip used by several households.

The proposed widening reached:

- four marked trees;
- low brush;
- part of a footpath.

The second section was worse.

The proposed turn space sat beside lower wet ground used seasonally for:

- gathering;
- animal watering;
- access to another path.

The drainage improvement would send more water toward it.

The route representative pointed at the first sketch.

"We can compensate the cutting."

The older man who had embraced the delegate said:

"I want the road widened."

The woman by the trough looked at him.

"Of course you do."

"My sons haul on it."

"My daughters gather there."

"That is not the same ground."

"At the second turn it is."

"Then argue the second turn."

Wurdren looked at the sketches.

This was progress of a kind.

A different local speaker tapped the first section.

"Who holds the cutting claim?"

The road custodian listed three households and one community-held strip.

"All represented?"

"Two households."

"Third?"

"Winter pasture."

"How far?"

"Back tomorrow."

The route representative leaned back.

"We lose another day."

The younger trader said:

"You gain a road people will not block."

The route representative looked at them.

"You think people will block it?"

The trader shrugged.

"I think people do stupid things when they believe nobody asked."

Wurdren liked the trader.

The woman by the trough did not.

The meeting broke for food before anybody had formally called a break.

Wurdren ate something wrapped in leaves that he could not identify and did not ask about.

The delegate sat beside him.

"You expected one side."

"Yes."

"Still?"

"No."

"Good."

Wurdren chewed.

"What do you want?"

The delegate looked at the table.

"The widening."

He stopped chewing.

They continued.

"Not this version."

"You want the road widened."

"The route matters. The market matters. The people hauling resin matter. The people gathering beside the turn matter."

"That sounds inconvenient."

"It is government."

Wurdren looked at them.

"You say that as if it explains anything."

"It explains why I went to Port."

Fair.

After the meal, they walked the disputed sections.

This changed the argument again.

The first section was narrower than the route map made it look.

The four marked trees were not enormous.

Two were already damaged near the road edge.

The cutting strip behind them was actively managed.

One household representative said:

"Cut those four, pay the claim, move the footpath uphill."

Another said:

"Not until the third holder returns."

The route representative asked:

"What would they say?"

"Probably yes."

"Then—"

"Probably is not yes."

Wurdren smiled.

The route representative noticed.

"Do not."

They continued to the second section.

Here the road bent beside wet ground.

The proposed turn space had been marked with stakes.

Wurdren stood inside the staked area.

The woman from the trough pointed at him.

"You're in the gathering ground."

He stepped out.

"That specific?"

"That specific."

The route representative said:

"It is mud and reeds."

The woman looked at them.

"And?"

The delegate said:

"Do not."

The route representative stopped.

That was growth.

A local guide walked the edge of the road.

"The upper turn."

The route representative closed their eyes.

"No."

Wurdren looked between them.

The guide pointed upslope.

"We showed your surveyors that line last season."

The route surveyor traveling with the representative said:

"And we rejected it last season."

"Why?" Wurdren asked.

The surveyor answered:

"Steeper approach. Retaining work. Slower loaded carts. More repair after heavy rain."

"Possible?"

"Of course it's possible."

The route representative looked at Wurdren.

"Everything is possible if somebody else is paying."

The woman from the trough said:

"Now you understand us."

The route representative ignored her.

"The lower turn is flatter, faster, and cheaper."

The local guide said:

"And uses ground you did not buy."

"We did not buy the road either. We have a concession."

"For the road."

The argument threatened to return.

Wurdren said:

"So the upper turn wasn't rejected because it wouldn't work."

The route representative looked at him.

"It was rejected because the lower turn is better."

"For carts."

"For the route."

The Longwood delegate said:

"And worse for the right beside it."

Nobody answered.

That was not a solution.

It was, however, finally the same argument.

By evening they had not settled it.

They had reduced it.

The first section now depended on:

- one absent cutting-right holder;
- compensation;
- footpath relocation.

The second depended on:

- whether the cheaper lower turn justified the rights / drainage cost;
- whether the more expensive upper turn was worth avoiding that conflict;
- who would pay the difference.

The road itself was no longer the argument.

That mattered.

Wurdren sat outside the lodging yard after dark.

The Longwood delegate joined him.

"Still learning?"

"Unfortunately."

"You did very little today."

"I noticed."

"Good."

Wurdren looked at them.

They were not smiling.

"Tomorrow," the delegate said, "the third cutting holder returns."

"And then?"

"Then everyone becomes difficult in new ways."

Wurdren leaned back against the wall.

He had been expecting danger.

Instead he had received:

- a ledger;
- wet ground;
- four trees;
- an expensive turn.

Somehow it felt more real.

That made him uneasy.

# V2 assessment

## Alternate-route realism

**STRONG PASS.**

The upper turn is:

- known;
- feasible;
- materially worse in several ways.

The conflict becomes:

> who bears the cost of the cheaper design?

not:

> why did nobody see the easy answer?

## Wurdren role

**PASS.**

He does not discover the solution.

He notices only that:

> possible

and:

> preferred / cheap

are different claims.

## Sequence 10 setup

The compromise now has real ingredients:

### First section
- likely agreement;
- pending third holder;
- compensation;
- footpath.

### Second section
- lower route:
  - cheaper / flatter;
  - rights + drainage conflict;
- upper route:
  - more expensive;
  - steeper / more maintenance;
  - avoids the most contested wet ground.

Sequence 10 can now require:

> **someone to absorb a real cost.**

That is necessary for the counter-domino to feel earned.

## Recommendation

Use V2 as the current Sequence 9 baseline.

Proceed to Sequence 10:

> **A counter-domino works.**
