# 1. Transitional evidence must already exist in the rule

V1 risks making:

> station receipt = valid transition evidence

look like something the inspector improvises because Wurdren noticed it.

Correct by putting on the posted notice:

> travelers already dispatched before the change may prove prior departure through a recognized dated route / toll / station record.

The carrier:

- has valid evidence;
- does not realize it satisfies the new requirement.

Wurdren helps interpret the notice.

The inspector still decides.

That is stronger.

---

# 2. Reduce the slogan callback

V1 reuses:

> "A path is not permission."

It is accurate, but slightly too perfect in this scene.

Better:

- Wurdren explains the Longwood issue in ordinary language;
- no polished phrase.

The phrase belongs to Longwood.

It should not immediately become Wurdren's portable theme line.

---

# 3. Roadhead seller should blame the decision, not instantly identify Wurdren as author

The seller can learn that he:

- came from the corridor;
- was present at the settlement.

But:

> "this is your fault"

is too direct for someone who does not know his role.

Better:

> "You were there when they chose it?"

Then:

- Wurdren says yes;
- she explains the cost;
- he says he still thinks the other ground mattered.

This produces responsibility without melodrama.

---

# 4. Length

The sequence is long but justified because it performs three jobs:

1. causal afterlife;

3. Wurdren moral development.

Do not add more.

If later trimming is needed:

- shorten waiting transitions;
- preserve the carrier problem;
- preserve the seller exchange;
- preserve guarded-departure usefulness.
