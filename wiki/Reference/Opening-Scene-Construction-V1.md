# 1. Local decisions now justified

These are **working story defaults** for the opening.

They may be revised if scene drafting exposes a contradiction.

## Wurdren's guild-work model

Do **not** restore the old RPG-style universal Adventurers' Guild.

Working model:

> **Wurdren takes contract work routed through ordinary guild / professional networks: escort, recovery, protection, delivery, witness, and dispute-support work where merchants, carriers, guild branches, or local authorities need a trusted independent hand.**

He may belong to or be recognized by a security / guide / recovery association, but exact institutional membership remains open.

This preserves the owner's "guild work" concept while fitting current guild canon.

## Opening cargo

Working default:

> **one riverboat-scale commercial delivery of bagged Low Rivers milling grain.**

Do not lock the grain species yet.

Why this passes:

- Low Rivers is in abundance / price-pressure conditions;
- G1 river traffic plausibly carries bulk grain into Port;
- Three Moons increases food demand;
- the commodity is important but ordinary;
- it does not become a MacGuffin.

## Opening transaction problem

The shipment was contracted for delivery into a **recognized warehouse arrangement** using trusted:

- weights / measures;
- storage records;
- seals / witnesses;
- buyer / guarantor terms.

At arrival:

- festival crowding has consumed the expected storage capacity;
- the contracted warehouse cannot receive the grain on the expected terms;
- substitute storage exists, but the buyer / guarantor cannot simply treat an unverified substitute as equivalent;
- payment / final settlement therefore stalls.

This is a problem of:

> **capacity + records + trust**

rather than physical food scarcity.

## Cross-guild reform payoff

A newer Port cross-guild / shared clerical arrangement can verify that another warehouse's:

- measures;
- records;
- credentials

are reciprocally recognized.

That solves the **professional / procedural** problem.

It does not solve the remaining **credit / guarantee** problem.

This cleanly demonstrates that Villain-associated institutional reform genuinely works.

## Council-linked intervention

A powerful commercial / guarantor network then backs the transfer strongly enough for the alternate warehouse / buyer to proceed.

The help is real.

The author-side Council link remains hidden from Wurdren / reader at this stage.

### Cost

The same limited commercial capacity means another smaller transaction receives worse treatment:

- delayed extension;
- more expensive storage;
- lower priority;
- or refusal of a marginal guarantee.

Exact victim / transaction can remain scene-local.

The opening should let the records worker see both outcomes.

That creates:

> **help + hidden triage**

without requiring an evil act.

## Longwood dispute class

Working default:

> **a route / access concession is being interpreted by one party as permission to widen / improve passage through a forest corridor, while affected Longwood users argue that passage rights do not include unrestricted cutting / clearing or override other use rights.**

This follows established Longwood canon:

- passage;
- cutting;
- hunting / gathering;
- residence;
- water;
- restricted use

can be separate rights.

The Port disagreement concerns mismatched copies / summaries / annexes.

Port can clarify the paperwork.

It cannot remotely settle the local balance of rights.

## Outward Stone Hills handoff

Working default:

> **Port needs replacement iron fittings / chain / pins for cargo-handling or quay-repair equipment, and an ordinary procurement request is delayed because the relevant Stone Hills capacity is increasingly tied to priority contracts.**

Do not lock the exact machine or metal grade until the Stone Hills scene is built.

This is enough to move causality outward.

# 2. Opening packaging — working default

The first six causal sequences currently fit best as roughly **four chapter-sized units**.

This is not locked.

~~~text
Chapter-sized unit A
Arrival + delivery rejection

Chapter-sized unit B
Records + cross-guild solution + hidden commercial rescue

Chapter-sized unit C
Port ordinary life + Wurdren's old relationship + Longwood dispute begins

Chapter-sized unit D
Longwood record comparison + Three Moons information convergence + outward repair-order handoff
~~~

Reason:

- the delivery problem deserves enough room to make bureaucracy feel lived rather than summarized;
- the Port relationship needs emotional space;
- the Longwood dispute should not arrive in the same scene as the grain problem;
- Three Moons should broaden the world only after the reader has one stable human / institutional foothold.

# 3. Scene 1 — Port from the wrong end of an adventure

## POV

Wurdren.

## Immediate objective

Finish the escort contract.

He wants:

- delivery acknowledged;
- his portion signed / witnessed;
- payment;
- a room;
- food;
- then a look at the Port job boards / guild contacts for something more interesting.

## Internal condition

Wurdren has deliberately returned to the road because age has made his old fear harder to ignore:

> **there may not be enough time left for his life to become important.**

Do not state this as a thesis paragraph.

Show it through what he notices and dismisses.

Possible behavior:

- he mentally counts the job as "another delivery";
- he catches himself looking at festival notices / famous names / prestigious contracts;
- he imagines the next job will be the real one;
- he is mildly embarrassed that his return to adventure has involved guarding sacks of grain.

## Physical entry

Use Port's established transport logic.

The shipment reaches the **river-quay / transfer side** of Port.

The reader can encounter:

- river craft;
- warehouse belt;
- carts waiting to transfer cargo;
- local ferry / bridge traffic;
- labor crews;
- people asking for pilots / berths / storage;
- festival traffic already overwhelming ordinary patterns.

No citywide geography lecture.

## Breath details

Use 2–3 only.

Possible classes:

- lodging prices have doubled and locals complain visitors still expect gratitude;
- a lane / quay access is blocked for festival setup;
- workers are mocking a temporary sign / rule;
- someone says the city has been "almost ready" for Three Moons for six months.

At least one detail should never matter again.

## Scene conflict

The shipment reaches the expected warehouse.

The warehouse will **not accept it under the contracted arrangement**.

Not because grain is unwanted.

Because:

- expected space is gone;
- overflow terms have changed;
- the buyer / guarantor requires recognized storage;
- the warehouse cannot give the exact receipt / custody status the contract expects.

## Wurdren's first temptation

His escort obligation may technically be close to complete.

He could reasonably say:

> I got you here. The warehouse problem is yours.

He does not.

Not because he recognizes the story's theme.

Because:

- he gave his word about delivery;
- the carrier / merchant plainly cannot absorb the delay;
- leaving now feels cheap.

This is the first instance of:

> **his practiced character being better than the story he tells himself about what counts as important.**

## Exit

The carrier / merchant needs the right Port office / clerk before the day's records close.

Wurdren follows.

# 4. Scene 2 — the person behind the form

## POV

Wurdren.

## Immediate objective

Get someone with authority to recognize the delivery.

## New recurring role

**Port records / contract worker.**

Do not finalize name yet.

### Scene-level characterization

They are:

- overworked;
- precise;
- irritated by people treating record work as meaningless obstruction;
- good at distinguishing:
  - bad paperwork;
  - wrong office;
  - actual legal / commercial problem.

They do not care about Wurdren's quest for significance.

They care that:

> if they certify the wrong thing, somebody else inherits the liability.

### Independent life seed

Give one off-plot concern.

Examples:

- promised to meet a sibling after shift;
- temporary festival assignment is eating into ordinary work;
- resents a supervisor taking credit for clearing the backlog.

Do not pay this off unless later useful.

## Bureaucratic friction

The problem should take at least two steps.

Example:

1. original warehouse receipt / reservation is valid;
2. substitute warehouse can physically take the grain;
3. but substitute warehouse's records are not automatically covered by the buyer's existing guarantee.

The clerk refuses the easy but false solution:

> "just write the other warehouse in."

That establishes competence.

## Wurdren function

He is useful in a nonheroic way.

Possible actions:

- remembers the exact name of the warehouse person who spoke to them;
- goes back for a missing copy / seal;
- notices that two people are using the same word to mean different kinds of acceptance;
- keeps the merchant from exploding at the clerk.

His "adventuring skill" is partly:

> getting tired people through practical conflict.

## Character pressure

Wurdren can be internally impatient.

This is paperwork.

This is exactly the sort of thing he thinks is **not** the important life.

Yet the person in front of him may lose a season's margin over it.

## Discovery

The clerk identifies a new cross-guild / reciprocal-recognition route for the substitute warehouse.

That requires another desk / representative / shared service.

## Exit

There is now a procedural solution.

But the guarantor / buyer still has to accept it.

# 5. Scene 3 — a system works

## POV

Wurdren, with optional brief clerk continuation.

## Immediate objective

Get the grain accepted before delay becomes ruinous.

## Villain-associated reform payoff

The new Port coordination network does something concrete.

It can verify:

- warehouse standing;
- measures;
- recognized clerk / seal;
- reciprocal credential.

This should feel impressively mundane.

Somebody may truthfully say:

> before this arrangement, they would have spent days finding people willing to vouch for one another.

Another person can respond that:

> now every profession thinks it needs a permanent Port desk.

No ideological labels.

## Council-linked commercial payoff

The remaining credit / guarantee issue resolves unusually fast.

A major commercial house / guarantor accepts enough risk to let the substitute arrangement proceed.

No one calls it Council action.

Possible visible mechanism:

- a runner returns with endorsement;
- a known guarantor mark is added;
- the alternate warehouse accepts custody;
- buyer can release partial / full settlement.

## The cost appears nearby

Do not reveal it in omniscient narration.

Let the records worker encounter another person whose smaller transaction now receives worse terms.

For example:

- a local miller / small merchant loses a cheap overflow slot;
- a marginal borrower cannot get an extension that day;
- another cargo has to wait outside covered storage.

The institution's implicit logic may be understandable:

> staple food during festival strain is safer / more urgent than the marginal claim.

The loser does not have to agree.

## Wurdren's emotional result

He is relieved.

He gets paid.

The carrier is relieved.

This should feel like a **real ending** to the first problem.

Then let Wurdren diminish it in his own mind.

Something like:

> good. Grain delivered. Hardly the sort of thing a man leaves home to be remembered for.

Do not make the prose preach against him.

The reader should be allowed to notice the irony first.

## Evidence seed

The records worker files / copies the guarantee adjustment.

Later significance:

- repeated guarantor;
- repeated procedural language;
- repeated cross-sector backing.

Current significance:

> the account balances.

## Exit

Wurdren is finally free to look for the adventure he thinks he came to Port to find.

# 6. Scene 4 — the person who stopped looking

## POV

Wurdren.

## Immediate objective

Food / bed / reconnect with an old Port acquaintance before looking for work.

## Working recurring role

Use the **Port neighborhood / labor contact** as someone Wurdren already knows.

Strongest current candidate:

> **a former road / guild-work acquaintance who chose years ago to stop traveling and built an ordinary Port life.**

They now work in or around:

- warehouse labor;
- quay crews;
- ferry / roadhead operations;
- repair logistics.

Exact occupation remains open.

### Why this is stronger than a new stranger

They can remember the younger Wurdren.

They can also serve as a living counterexample to his assumption that:

> stopping the adventure means giving up on significance.

They should **not** understand themselves as a philosophical lesson.

## Conversation pressure

Let the old acquaintance ask why Wurdren is back on the road.

Wurdren can answer with a partial truth:

- bored;
- restless;
- still able;
- wanted to see the world again.

The deeper fear remains harder to say.

## Show Port's cost through them

Their complaints can include:

- temporary workers filling rooms;
- relatives sleeping in kitchens;
- food / lodging prices;
- long shifts;
- public cleanup;
- repairs delayed because festival contracts take priority;
- outsiders talking about Port as if nobody lives there.

They can still love Three Moons.

That contradiction matters.

## Wurdren thematic pressure

The acquaintance's life should contain things Wurdren cannot dismiss as failure:

- people depending on them;
- skill;
- local reputation;
- family / friends / apprentices;
- routines they chose.

Wurdren may respect all of it and still think:

> that is good for them; it is not what I meant for myself.

This prevents the arc from resolving too early.

## Villain / Port politics seed

Through ordinary talk, establish that the Villain's reform network has:

- made some guild / contract work easier;
- helped keep expertise in Port;
- also made people worry that big guilds / offices are becoming too powerful.

The acquaintance can support stronger Port capacity while distrusting elite centralizers.

No manifesto.

## Exit

Wurdren intends to check the hiring / contract boards.

Instead, an acquaintance / clerk / guild contact asks him to help with a dispute where:

> people are close to turning a paperwork disagreement into something worse.

This leads to Longwood.

# 7. Scene 5 — passage is not cutting

## POV

Wurdren.

## Immediate objective

Initially modest:

> keep two parties from making a public / physical scene long enough to determine what the records actually say.

## Parties

Do not make this "Longwood versus outsiders."

Use at least three interests.

Working structure:

### Longwood rights-holder / representative

Argues:

- passage may be allowed;
- widening / cutting / extraction is a different right;
- the copy being used in Port omits or flattens that distinction.

### Route / infrastructure / merchant-side representative

Argues:

- they possess a valid concession / agreement;
- the route is economically / practically important;
- maintenance or widening is necessary for the promised passage to mean anything.

### Clerk / record authority

Concerned with:

- which version is recognized;
- whether an annex / limitation was copied;
- what Port can certify without pretending to decide Longwood local law.

Each side needs a real point.

## Wurdren function

His contribution is primarily **attention + de-escalation**.

Possible actions:

- gets one furious party to state exactly what they believe the document grants;
- notices the other party is disputing a different verb / right;
- asks whether there is another copy;
- retrieves / identifies the annex or older reference;
- prevents supporters from turning the disagreement into a public humiliation contest.

He is not the best lawyer in Port.

## Documentary result

The records show enough to establish:

> **the concession / agreement cannot automatically be read as unlimited authority to clear / cut merely because passage exists.**

But they do **not** establish the final local solution.

That requires:

- affected communities;
- actual route conditions;
- local rights;
- compensation / maintenance questions.

## Wurdren's mixed motive

When asked to accompany the returning party / records or assist in the local discussion, he has at least two motives:

### Good motive

He helped open the problem and gave his word / sees a real risk.

### Self-regarding motive

This finally feels more like:

> **an adventure that might matter.**

Do not erase the second motive.

It is part of his arc.

## Exit

He agrees to continue after the festival / current Port obligation.

The first counter-domino is now planted but not solved.

# 8. Scene 6 — everyone has a piece

## POV

Wurdren.

Optional very short perspective handoffs only if necessary.

## Immediate objective

Wrap up Port obligations before leaving:

- collect / return papers;
- arrange departure;
- find supplies;
- perhaps check one guild contact.

## Festival function

Three Moons has reached peak information density.

The scene should feel broader than previous scenes but still local.

Wurdren does not stand in one room while six delegates explain the continent.

Use movement through Port.

## Fragment budget

Use **three or four** major fragments in the scene, not all possible ones.

Strongest combination:

### North Coast

A sailor / navigator complains about:

- convoy timing;
- who pays;
- who commands escorts.

### Low Rivers

A merchant is offering grain cheaply despite festival demand because:

- storage;
- debt;
- buyer timing

are the actual problem.

This echoes the opening without explaining the whole Low Rivers crisis.

### High Roads

A carrier complains that:

- safe-route capacity;
- animals;
- escorts

are being booked unusually early / tightly.

### Old Cities OR Stone Hills

Choose one in dialogue.

The other becomes the physical exit handoff.

## Stone Hills handoff — preferred physical exit

Wurdren's Port acquaintance or another practical contact has a repair / procurement problem.

A needed batch of:

- iron fittings;
- chain;
- pins;
- related cargo-handling / quay-repair components

will not arrive when expected.

Reason given:

> supplier / capacity committed elsewhere under priority terms.

Nobody yet knows what that means politically.

To the Port worker it means:

> the hoist / quay / repair stays half-functional during the busiest week in seven years.

That is enough.

## Wurdren's final opening choice

He has access to a more glamorous-sounding contract / rumor / opportunity in Port.

He nevertheless keeps the Longwood commitment.

Do not make him consciously say:

> small obligations are the meaning of life.

He may tell himself:

- he already promised;
- the other job will still be there;
- Longwood sounds interesting enough.

But structurally this is the first time the story lets him choose:

> **a specific responsibility over an abstract possibility of importance.**

He does not yet know that is what he chose.

## Opening exit image / feeling

He leaves or prepares to leave Port with:

- one new relationship with the records worker;
- one renewed old Port relationship;
- one unresolved Longwood obligation;
- a handful of apparently unrelated complaints in his head.

The reader should feel:

> **the world is busy everywhere.**

Not:

> **the conspiracy has begun.**

# 9. Opening information budget

Do not explain these beyond what the scene requires.

## Reader may understand by end of opening

- Port is a major transfer / information city.
- Three Moons is extraordinary but not invented for the plot.
- Low Rivers can suffer from abundance / storage / credit problems.
- guild coordination in Port is becoming more useful and more controversial.
- a powerful guarantor network can solve problems rapidly.
- Longwood rights can overlap in ways a simple ownership model cannot resolve.
- wider transport / materials systems are under pressure.
- Wurdren wants his life to become something larger.

## Reader should **not** yet understand

- the Economic Council as an institution;
- the Villain's covert escalation architecture;
- how the separate pressures will braid;
- which commercial records will matter later;
- that the Longwood compromise will frustrate the Villain;
- that ordinary security responses will later become war capability.

# 10. Wurdren behavior ledger for the opening

Do not explain his philosophy.

Show a contradiction between what he **believes counts** and what he **actually does**.

| Situation | What Wurdren thinks counts as meaningful | What Wurdren actually does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| grain delivery | "just another escort" | stays after the narrow escort obligation is nearly complete |
| records office | bureaucracy / delay | treats the clerk's knowledge seriously enough to solve the problem |
| system rescue | not adventure | helps a real person avoid major loss |
| old Port acquaintance | ordinary settled life | clearly respects the life while excluding it from his own idea of success |
| Longwood argument | finally something interesting | succeeds mostly through listening / clarification rather than force |
| departure | glamorous possibility remains available | keeps a specific promise instead |

This is the opening form of the complete-person arc:

> **his character is already pointing somewhere his self-story has not caught up to yet.**

# 11. Character promotion decisions

## Promote to recurring role — records worker

**YES.**

Reason:

- opening causal role;
- later evidence role;
- independent professional motive;
- does not duplicate another function.

Still needed before final naming:

- Port origin / language community;
- age / generation;
- whether Port-born or migrant;
- exact legal / guild employment status.

## Promote to recurring role — old Port acquaintance / labor contact

**YES, working default.**

Reason:

- anchors Wurdren socially;
- gives Port ordinary-life perspective;
- later settlement needs a non-elite relationship;
- provides a natural complete-person mirror without inventing a philosopher character.

Still needed:

- exact shared history with Wurdren;
- current occupation;
- household / off-camera relationships;
- naming community.

## Promote to recurring role — Longwood participant

**YES.**

Reason:

- opening dispute;
- local compromise;
- late trust / precedent payoff.

Still needed:

- which Longwood political concentration / community;
- exact right they hold / represent;
- why they personally came to Port;
- naming community.

## Opening grain carrier / merchant

**NOT YET.**

Keep as limited role unless later Low Rivers scenes require the same person's continuing choices.

Do not promote everyone Wurdren helps.

# 13. Draft test

The opening passes if a reader can finish it interested in:

- Wurdren;
- Port;
- the Longwood obligation;

without yet needing to know that a continental war plot exists.

On reread, the same material should contain recognizable seeds for:

- Council evidence;
- Villain legitimacy;
- Port reform;
- security conversion;
- Wurdren's complete-person arc.

The first read must work without the second-read knowledge.
