# 1. Main salvage result

The strongest old political idea is:

> **The six broad cultural regions should not map one-to-one onto six states.**

Volume 02 repeatedly tries to populate the world with:

- major kingdoms;
- city-states;
- principalities;
- marches;
- leagues;
- confederations;
- guild-run enclaves;
- protectorates;
- semi-autonomous counties;
- local councils;
- route fortresses;
- small maritime polities;
- warlord domains.

That basic instinct survives very well.

The newer [Kingdoms and Politics](../Politics/Kingdoms-and-Politics.md) owner now handles it more cleanly by separating cultural regions from political sovereignty.

# 2. Political forms worth preserving

## Buffer march

Useful mechanism:

- a frontier territory exists because a larger polity needs someone to administer a difficult border;
- its ruler / council gains autonomy because central enforcement is expensive;
- neighboring powers may both expect loyalty;
- local people develop mixed legal and cultural practice.

Old seed:

- **Moorcrest Marches**
- **Foxglove March**

Names remain provisional.

## Pass principality / route polity

Useful mechanism:

- a small polity survives because it controls a pass, bridge, ravine, ferry or caravan junction;
- larger neighbors may tolerate its independence because direct annexation could destabilize traffic;
- tolls, escort obligations and neutral arbitration can matter more than land area.

Old seeds:

- **Cliffhold Principality**
- **Cliffhaven**
- **Thunderstair**
- **Redwater Ravine**

The named warlords and theatrical fortress details are not retained automatically.

## Guild-run or merchant-run city-state

Useful mechanism:

- a city can become politically autonomous because the people financing / operating its productive base also administer law, contracts and defense;
- legitimacy may come from a charter, guild assembly, merchant houses or negotiated recognition.

Old seeds:

- **Blackbellow Consortium**
- **Cliffhaven Commonwealth**
- **Rashira Oasis League**

The "corporate monarchy" framing is too modern and should be avoided.

## Confederation

Useful mechanism:

- several local rulers / clans / towns cooperate for defense, trade or shared infrastructure while preserving substantial internal autonomy.

Old examples include:

- baronial confederation;
- clan confederation;
- city-state league;
- oasis league;
- maritime alliance.

The mechanism is strong.

The old assumption that an entire cultural region should use one confederation model is not.

## Protectorate / dependency

Useful mechanism:

- a smaller polity retains internal institutions while relying on a larger power for defense, treaty access, route security or finance;
- sovereignty can be negotiated rather than absolute.

Old seed:

- **Isle of Marabel** as a semi-autonomous maritime dependency.

The exact island and relationship remain provisional.

## Rotating federation leadership

Volume 02 proposes a federation in which member cities rotate a first chair / representative.

Useful mechanism:

- one office represents the federation externally;
- constituent cities retain sovereignty;
- leadership rotates or is selected among members;
- the spokesperson is not a monarch over the member states.

This is especially compatible with the current **Old Cities** model but should not be assumed universal there.

## Hereditary rule with institutional confirmation

One old succession model uses:

```text
dynastic heir
+ confirmation by an assembly / major institutional body
→ legitimate succession
```

This is worth preserving as a constitutional pattern.

The validating body could be:

- landholders;
- guilds;
- city delegates;
- clans;
- religious authorities;
- military elites;
- mixed estates.

It should arise from a specific polity's history.

## Elective / clan-confirmed leadership

Another old model uses:

- leading families;
- local chiefs;
- council approval;
- selection from a limited group of plausible candidates.

Useful mechanism:

**inheritance and election can coexist.**

A candidate can need both lineage and political confirmation.

The old storm / wind ritual framing is decorative and not necessary.

# 3. Succession should belong to polities, not regions

Volume 02 often assigns one succession style to an entire region.

Do not keep that.

A better rule:

> succession is produced by the history of a specific state, dynasty, charter, guild, clan coalition or city.

Within one cultural region there can be:

- hereditary monarchy;
- elective monarchy;
- oligarchic council;
- rotating chair;
- guild government;
- town assembly;
- clan council;
- appointed governor;
- protectorate administrator;
- military usurper;
- mixed constitutional system.

That diversity is more compatible with current world rules and with the user's anti-hard-border cultural model.

---

# 4. Minor polities matter because they create friction

Volume 02 is strongest when it asks what happens between the major powers.

Minor polities can:

- raise tolls;
- refuse passage;
- host rival guilds;
- provide alternate markets;
- become neutral meeting grounds;
- shelter dissidents;
- recognize marriages or contracts neighboring states reject;
- serve as buffer territories;
- create alternate credit / trade networks;
- switch allegiance;
- exploit rivalry between larger states.

This is exactly the kind of "middle worldbuilding" the current project values.

They should not exist only as quest locations.

They should alter the system around them.

# 5. Border administration worth preserving

Volume 02 contains several useful contact-zone governance ideas.

Preserve as mechanisms:

- joint local councils;
- overlapping jurisdiction;
- guild delegates in local government;
- elders / landholders sharing authority;
- joint patrols;
- relaxed small-goods tariffs;
- special recognition of cross-border marriages;
- water-sharing arrangements;
- locally negotiated exceptions to larger-state law.

These are now better owned by [Law and Jurisdiction](../Law-and-Jurisdiction.md) and [Border Towns](../Places/Border-Towns.md).

Exact old border-town examples remain provisional.

# 6. Education and knowledge transmission

Volume 02 repeatedly assumes education is institutionally tied to work.

Useful mechanisms include:

- apprenticeship as a major educational route;
- guild-sponsored technical teaching;
- advanced professional schools;
- religious teaching;
- scholarly / legal instruction;
- merchant / route knowledge;
- elite tutoring;
- local access differences.

The old drafts often over-specialize entire regions:

> "Ironcrest education is technical"  
> "Highridge education is scholarly"

Do not keep that as regional destiny.

A better retained rule is:

> **different institutions provide different kinds of education, and access follows class, profession, faith, location and wealth.**

This material should feed a dedicated future education / knowledge-transmission owner.

# 7. Local government and administration

Volume 02 suggests a wide range of local governing bodies:

- clan councils;
- farmers' assemblies;
- guild councils;
- merchant councils;
- city-state forums;
- local nobles;
- religious estates;
- wardens;
- governors;
- town councils.

The useful lesson is not the exact list.

It is that **local political authority should be institutional and visible**.

Ordinary residents should know:

- who collects local tax;
- who resolves a property dispute;
- who maintains a road;
- who organizes a watch;
- who can requisition labor;
- who speaks to a larger polity;
- who certifies a market;
- who controls water.

This aligns directly with the newer polity-function model.

# 9. Old ruler seed bank

Legacy names such as:

- Gareth Blackbarrow;
- Ingrid Stormvale;
- Roland Fairweather;
- Nalia Skylance;
- Elden Mossglade;
- Aurelia Mirabelle

remain provenance only.

Their associated constitutional mechanisms can survive independently of the characters.

The problem with the old ruler set is that each ruler was designed to embody a whole region's supposed personality.

That conflicts with current political and cultural design.

A future ruler should be:

- a product of one polity;
- constrained by institutions;
- opposed internally;
- culturally situated without representing every person in the region.

# 10. Warlords and rogue domains

Volume 02 contains many warlords.

Most named versions are generic fantasy antagonists.

The useful structural question is:

> **What allows a local strongman to collect revenue and survive?**

Possible answers supported by the old material:

- controlling a bridge / pass;
- possessing a fortress;
- commanding mercenaries;
- filling a security vacuum;
- offering protection;
- controlling a mine;
- exploiting disputed sovereignty;
- receiving covert support from a larger rival.

A "warlord" should therefore be treated as a temporary political-economic arrangement, not a fantasy job class.

---

# 11. Things that should remain legacy

Do not restore:

- one monarch for each cultural region;
- rulers designed as personifications of regional stereotypes;
- railways / advanced industrial machinery inconsistent with current technology;
- direct Mediterranean / Scandinavian / druidic regional templates;
- magical forests deciding every succession;
- "savage tribes" language;
- one festival / cuisine / costume per polity as template filler;
- RPG quest framing;
- universal "adventurers";
- named kingdoms whose only purpose is to populate a map;
- every border polity having a fantasy-compound name.

# 14. Polity test

Before promoting any old or new polity, answer:

1. What territory / population does it actually govern?
2. Why is it politically separate?
3. Who collects taxes?
4. Who enforces law?
5. Who settles local disputes?
6. Who maintains roads, water, walls or harbor works?
7. Who selects the ruler / council?
8. What makes succession legitimate?
9. Which institutions can block the ruler?
10. What larger power does it depend on?
11. What smaller communities resist it?
12. Which guilds / faiths / families cross its borders?
13. What would happen if it disappeared?
14. What does it add to the world system besides another name on the map?

If the answer is only "it adds political variety," it is not developed enough.
