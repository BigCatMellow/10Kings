The purpose of this page is to prevent a recurring failure mode:

> **every useful modern rule gets retroactively placed inside one giant Convergence charter.**

The current world supports a much more layered settlement.

---

# 1. Short model

A strong working model is:

**Layer 1 — core settlement guarantees**  
Peace, claims, protected exchange, route security, arbitration, Port access / neutrality, local autonomy.

**Layer 2 — attached agreements**  
Specific route, water, border, resource, security, or Port arrangements among the actors that actually need them.

**Layer 3 — later interoperability standards**  
Commercial practices that spread because repeated cross-border exchange rewards compatibility.

**Layer 4 — precedent and interpretation**  
Arbitration outcomes, recognized practices, and accumulated interpretations that clarify the settlement over time.

**Layer 5 — local law and institutions**  
The large majority of ordinary governance: property, labor, taxation, crime, family law, guild privilege, local infrastructure, and internal political structure.

This produces:

core settlement  
→ attached agreements  
→ repeated cross-border use  
→ standards and precedent  
→ local implementation

rather than:

one treaty  
→ every modern institution

---

# 2. Layer 1 — core settlement guarantees

The current [Convergence](../History/The-Convergence.md) supports six core outcomes strongly.

## A. Recognized claims / non-aggression

The settlement makes recognized territorial and political claims harder to overturn by force.

It does **not** require every boundary to be surveyed precisely.

The important change is:

> taking recognized territory by force becomes more politically costly and less legitimate.

## B. Protected exchange / passage

Some categories of legitimate cross-border movement receive predictable protection.

This can include:

- major trade;
- recognized routes;
- maritime access;
- caravans;
- agreed toll / customs expectations.

Exact protected categories remain open.

## C. Shared route-security obligations

Participating authorities accept some responsibility for threats that undermine agreed circulation.

Strong examples include:

- piracy;
- banditry;
- attacks on major caravans;
- deliberate obstruction of recognized routes.

This does not create one common army.

## D. Cross-border arbitration

The settlement creates or recognizes ways to handle disputes without immediate violence.

Likely subjects include:

- borders;
- passage;
- trade;
- tolls;
- cross-border contracts;
- maritime access;
- treaty obligations.

Exact forums remain open.

## E. Port neutrality / access

Port's special status is formalized or strengthened so that:

- major powers retain usable access;
- no major rival can easily monopolize it;
- local neutral institutions remain meaningful.

Exact guarantees remain open.

## F. Continued local autonomy

The settlement explicitly does **not** replace internal government.

Local / political authorities retain major control over:

- taxation;
- property;
- internal law;
- political offices;
- most criminal law;
- most family law;
- local infrastructure;
- ordinary economic regulation.

This is not an omission.

It is part of the post-Convergence political design.

---

# 3. Layer 2 — attached agreements

Many important cross-border rules should not be universal Convergence guarantees.

They can belong to narrower agreements among affected parties.

Possible subjects include:

- one river crossing;
- one pass;
- one irrigation system;
- one border grazing zone;
- one ferry;
- one harbor access arrangement;
- one disputed forest;
- one resource exchange;
- one route-security compact;
- one recognition agreement between guilds.

These may be:

- negotiated during the Convergence;
- added shortly afterward;
- created much later under Convergence principles.

The exact historical timing can vary.

---

# 4. Why attached agreements matter

The political map is not six equivalent states.

Therefore a universal clause may be the wrong tool for a local problem.

Example:

A pass used primarily by one North Coast authority and one High Roads authority does not require every Old Cities polity to regulate its pack animals.

A route-specific agreement can be recognized by the wider order without becoming universal law.

This lets the Convergence scale to a politically plural world.

---

# 5. Layer 3 — later interoperability standards

Repeated cross-border activity rewards compatibility.

Over generations, merchants, guilds, courts, roadheads, and Port institutions can develop shared standards for:

- weights / measures;
- cargo marks;
- warehouse records;
- recognized seals;
- date conversion;
- currency conversion;
- contract clauses;
- guide credentials;
- pilot reputation;
- insurance-like guarantees;
- professional certification.

These standards do **not** need to be founding treaty clauses.

A standard may become widespread because:

- traders prefer it;
- courts recognize it;
- lenders require it;
- guild branches reciprocate;
- Port uses it;
- High Roads brokers normalize it.

This is institutional evolution after the settlement.

---

# 6. Standardization is not uniformity

A shared standard can exist alongside local practice.

Examples:

- local farmers use one measure while export warehouses convert to another;
- households use one calendar while contracts use dual dates;
- a guild credential is recognized abroad only for some work;
- Port cargo marks are widely understood while local labels remain different.

Therefore:

> interoperability means systems can translate each other, not that they become identical.

---

# 7. Layer 4 — precedent and interpretation

A settlement package cannot anticipate every later dispute.

Repeated cases create precedent.

Examples:

- whether temporary route closure violates protected passage;
- whether a flood changes a recognized river boundary;
- whether a new toll is "reasonable";
- whether one polity may inspect foreign cargo twice;
- whether a guild sanction must be recognized abroad;
- what counts as discriminatory denial of Port access.

Different forums may treat precedent differently.

No universal supreme court is required.

The useful mechanism is:

> repeated decisions make some interpretations more predictable.

---

# 8. Precedent can become quasi-law without central legislation

If merchants, rulers, guilds, and courts repeatedly behave as though one interpretation is settled, violating it becomes costly.

That can create a practical rule even when:

- no central legislature enacted it;
- no universal court controls it;
- the original treaty is vague.

This fits the world's network-enforcement model.

---

# 9. Layer 5 — local law remains most law

The Convergence should not answer routine internal questions.

Usually local / political law governs:

- inheritance;
- marriage;
- land tenure;
- tenancy;
- criminal procedure;
- ordinary theft;
- apprenticeship;
- market licenses;
- internal taxes;
- local water allocation;
- building rules;
- succession;
- policing;
- municipal government.

Cross-border institutions may matter when those local rules interact with outsiders.

But the local rule does not become a Convergence rule merely because the dispute crosses a border.

---

# 10. The trigger question

A useful design test is:

> **What makes this issue cross-border enough to invoke Convergence-era obligations?**

Possible triggers:

- recognized territory;
- protected passage;
- agreed route access;
- Port access;
- cross-border contract;
- treaty-recognized arbitration;
- a shared infrastructure agreement;
- conduct that materially disrupts several participating systems.

If none applies, the issue should probably remain local.

---

# 11. Bound actors and beneficiaries are not the same thing

Exact signatories remain open.

But structurally, distinguish:

## Bound authority

An authority that has accepted an obligation through:

- signature;
- accession;
- recognized succession;
- attached agreement;
- later accepted practice.

## Protected user

A merchant, traveler, guild, ship, caravan, or resident who benefits from rights created by those obligations.

A merchant does not need to be a treaty signatory to benefit from protected passage.

This avoids imagining that every social institution personally signed the settlement.

---

# 12. Representation can be messy

Because the world contains:

- monarchies;
- city-states;
- clan authorities;
- leagues;
- route powers;
- local governments;
- special jurisdictions;

Convergence representation does not need one representative per cultural region.

A political unit may be represented through:

- ruler;
- city envoy;
- league delegation;
- recognized clan authority;
- treaty agent;
- special route or Port representative.

Exact forms remain open.

The core rule is:

> the settlement recognizes actors capable of making the relevant obligations meaningful.

---

# 13. Enforcement ladder

Not every violation should immediately become war.

A useful escalation ladder is:

1. complaint / notice;
2. local remedy;
3. negotiation;
4. commercial or guild pressure;
5. arbitration;
6. reciprocal restriction;
7. coalition pressure;
8. suspension of a specific privilege;
9. limited coordinated enforcement;
10. open conflict if the settlement fails.

Exact procedure is not universal.

The principle is:

> enforcement is graduated because the system exists to make violent escalation expensive and avoidable.

---

# 14. Violation severity should depend on system impact

A local crime is not automatically a treaty crisis.

Compare:

## Local theft

A warehouse employee steals one crate.

Usually local criminal / commercial law.

## Repeated official seizure

A ruler's agents systematically seize protected foreign caravans.

Potential Convergence issue because protected passage is being undermined.

## Private bandits

Local law first.

If authorities tolerate or sponsor attacks on a major protected route, the issue can escalate into route-security obligations.

## Harbor labor strike

Usually Port internal law / labor politics.

If an outside power uses the strike as justification to seize Port infrastructure, neutrality obligations become relevant.

# 16. North Shoulder test

The [North Shoulder Paired Roadheads](North-Shoulder-Paired-Roadheads-Test.md) make Layer 2 especially important.

Likely attached / later route agreement subjects:

- route-status exchange;
- shared rescue expectations;
- recognized guide credentials;
- treatment of stranded cargo;
- emergency closure;
- bridge / shelter maintenance;
- custody transfer.

These need not be universal treaty provisions.

Wider Convergence obligations matter because the corridor supports protected exchange.

**Pass.**

The route can have its own institutional history inside the larger settlement.

# 17. Port test

The [Port Estuary and Harbor Geometry](Port-Estuary-Harbor-Geometry-Test.md) shows the clearest distinction.

## Convergence core

Strongly connected to:

- neutrality;
- access;
- protection from unilateral monopoly.

## Port local law

Likely local:

- dock rules;
- fire response;
- labor law;
- quarantine administration;
- local policing;
- market regulation;
- ordinary city courts.

## Later interoperability

Likely developed through practice:

- cargo forms;
- warehouse procedure;
- pilot recognition;
- common commercial measures;
- arbitration conventions.

**Pass.**

Port can be internationally constrained and internally self-governing at the same time.

---

# 18. Shared infrastructure does not automatically become treaty property

A bridge, canal, pass, or quay used internationally can remain:

- locally owned;
- privately operated;
- guild maintained;
- jointly funded;
- politically contested.

The Convergence may constrain how access is denied without owning the infrastructure.

That distinction matters.

Example:

> a polity may own a bridge but still face wider consequences if it closes the bridge in violation of protected passage.

Ownership and treaty obligation are separate layers.

---

# 19. Guild privilege should stay negotiated

Guilds may gain cross-border recognition through:

- attached agreements;
- reciprocity;
- local law;
- later practice.

Do not make all guild privilege a universal Convergence clause.

A guild can be:

- powerful in one city;
- tolerated in another;
- unrecognized elsewhere;
- partially recognized for certification but not discipline.

This preserves political variation.

---

# 20. Standard weights should remain a later-practice candidate

The legacy material repeatedly proposes standardized weights / measures.

The current world strongly needs **conversion and comparability**.

It does not yet need universal identical measures.

Best working model:

local measures  
+ trusted conversion  
+ some commercial reference standards  
→ interoperable trade

This is more plausible than assuming every village abandoned its measures after the Convergence.

---

# 21. Mutual defense should remain narrow

Current sources support coordinated security against threats to shared circulation.

Do not automatically expand that into:

- collective defense against every invasion;
- one alliance army;
- universal military obligation.

Working distinction:

## Strongly supported

- anti-piracy coordination;
- anti-bandit cooperation;
- route reopening;
- protection of recognized access.

## Still open

- broader military defense of a member polity;
- automatic aid in interstate war;
- standing common forces.

This keeps the Convergence from becoming a hidden federation.

---

# 22. Withdrawal and suspension

The exact procedure remains open.

But the layered model suggests a useful distinction.

A political actor might:

- violate one attached agreement;
- lose one privilege;
- face one arbitration ruling;
- suspend one route arrangement;

without automatically leaving the entire Convergence order.

Full withdrawal should be a much larger step.

This creates room for political crisis below the level of total system collapse.

---

# 23. Why this structure survives

The settlement survives because different actors value different layers.

A ruler may value:

- recognized claims.

A merchant may value:

- protected passage.

A guild may value:

- recognized credentials.

Port may value:

- neutrality.

A roadhead may value:

- route cooperation.

A local village may care very little about the Convergence until a border, market, or water dispute reaches it.

That uneven relevance makes the system more believable.

---

# 24. Writing the Sequel — accumulated legal order

After generations, people may no longer distinguish cleanly between:

- original treaty;
- later annex;
- famous arbitration;
- customary practice;
- local implementation.

A merchant may say:

> "the Convergence requires it"

when technically the rule comes from:

- a later Port precedent;
- a guild reciprocity agreement;
- a route compact;
- an old bilateral annex.

That kind of legal shorthand is useful world texture.

It also creates room for political actors to argue over history.

---

# 25. Contested memory

Different institutions may tell different stories about the same rule.

Examples:

- a ruler says a route privilege was temporary;
- merchants say it was guaranteed permanently;
- a town claims an annex never applied to it;
- a guild treats a later commercial standard as if it were ancient;
- Port invokes "Convergence neutrality" more broadly than some guarantors accept.

The legal order therefore produces historical interpretation, not just legal certainty.

---

# 26. Design rule

Before calling something "a Convergence rule," ask:

1. Is it necessary to the core peace / access settlement?
2. Is it specific to one route, border, river, or group?
3. Could it have emerged later through repeated commerce?
4. Is it actually an arbitration precedent?
5. Is it simply local law interacting with foreigners?
6. Who is bound?
7. Who benefits?
8. Who enforces it?
9. What happens if it is violated?
10. Does making it universal erase useful political variation?

If a narrower layer works, use the narrower layer.

---

# 27. Strong working conclusions

The post-Convergence order works best as:

> **a layered legal-political ecosystem rather than a constitution.**

Strongly supported:

1. the founding settlement has a narrow core around claims, protected exchange, route security, arbitration, Port, and autonomy;
2. route / resource / border specifics can live in attached agreements;
3. commercial compatibility can emerge later without treaty legislation;
4. arbitration precedent can create practical rules without a supreme court;
5. most ordinary law remains local;
6. representation follows actual political actors rather than six cultural regions;
7. enforcement can escalate gradually rather than jumping from violation to war;
8. a polity can violate one layer without instantly exiting the entire system;
9. people can later misremember newer practice as original Convergence law.
