# World Synthesis — E/I Capture

## Status

**Reference / emergence capture — not canon.**

This page applies the MAPS_L **Emergence & Improvement (E/I)** pattern to the current Two Sons world model.

The purpose is to preserve potentially useful cross-connections without silently promoting inference into setting fact.

Method:

```text
IMAGINE → CAPTURE → PROMOTE
```

Each candidate below names its sources, linking mechanism, new implication, why it may matter, and a smallest test or falsifier.

## Candidate 1 — Port is a pressure-transmission hub, not merely a trade hub

**Sources**

- [Port](../Places/Port.md)
- [Trade and Dependencies](../Economy/Trade-and-Dependencies.md)
- [Current Events](../Story/Current-Events.md)
- [Danzig to Port Parallel](Danzig-to-Port-Parallel.md)

**Connection**

Port combines shipping, storage, finance, arbitration, information, migration, and illicit trade.

**New implication**

A relatively small event in Port can propagate into more systems than a similarly sized event elsewhere. Port may therefore be the world's fastest **pressure-transmission point** even if it is not its largest city or strongest state.

**Why it matters**

This could explain why Council intervention, Villain manipulation, rumor, refugee movement, and ordinary commercial accidents all become disproportionately consequential there.

**Smallest test**

Take three unrelated current-event shocks and trace whether Port amplifies all three without forcing the connection. If it only works by invention, reject the synthesis.

**Disposition**

Incubating.

## Candidate 2 — Port and Highridge are complementary chokepoints

**Sources**

- [Port](../Places/Port.md)
- [Highridge Plateau](../Regions/Highridge-Plateau.md)
- [Geography and Connections](../Geography-and-Connections.md)
- [Trade and Dependencies](../Economy/Trade-and-Dependencies.md)

**Connection**

Port concentrates maritime exchange; Highridge concentrates important overland movement and brokerage.

**New implication**

The world may have two different kinds of strategic hub:

- Port: maritime, financial, legal, cosmopolitan;
- Highridge: overland, logistical, arbitral, route-oriented.

Pressure on one could shift traffic toward the other rather than simply stopping exchange.

**Why it matters**

That creates substitution, competition, and cascading route effects without requiring either place to be a monopoly.

**Smallest test**

Lock the broad map and test whether major trade flows have plausible Port-avoiding and Highridge-avoiding routes.

**Test result — 2026-09-22**

[Working Map Draft — Geometry V1](Working-Map-Draft.md) produces plausible Port-avoiding and Highridge-avoiding substitutions rather than making either hub an absolute monopoly. A Northwind maritime disruption can push selected traffic into Highridge; a major west-east surface closure can push selected traffic toward Port, secondary ports, and Underpass capacity.

This is encouraging but not enough to promote the candidate. The next discriminating test is scale, ordinary travel time, and route capacity.

**Disposition**

Incubating pending scale / travel-time testing.

## Candidate 3 — The Council behaves like a hidden coordination utility

**Sources**

- [Economic Council](../Politics/Economic-Council.md)
- [The Convergence](../History/The-Convergence.md)
- [Guilds](../Politics/Guilds.md)
- [The World as a System](../World-System.md)

**Connection**

The post-Convergence world is politically fragmented but economically interdependent. The Council coordinates food, materials, routes, infrastructure, finance, and information.

**New implication**

The Council may persist not only because its families are powerful, but because it occupies a real **coordination gap** that kingdoms and guilds have never replaced openly.

**Why it matters**

If the Council is exposed or broken, reform cannot simply mean "remove the conspiracy." Somebody must replace functions that were actually useful or the world could destabilize further.

**Smallest test**

For each Council domain, identify the public institutions that could perform the same coordination. If adequate public alternatives already exist everywhere, weaken or reject this synthesis.

**Disposition**

Strong candidate; not yet promoted beyond the existing Council concept.

## Candidate 4 — Trans-regional institutions may contain the post-Council alternative

**Sources**

- [Religions](../Politics/Religions.md)
- [Guilds](../Politics/Guilds.md)
- [Port](../Places/Port.md)
- [Economic Council](../Politics/Economic-Council.md)

**Connection**

Faiths, guilds, merchants, pilgrimage networks, Port institutions, and professional communities already coordinate people across borders.

**New implication**

The same world that made the Council possible may also contain decentralized institutions capable of replacing parts of its coordination function.

**Why it matters**

This could support an ending in which reform emerges from institutions already present rather than a newly invented world government.

**Smallest test**

Map one Council function—such as dispute resolution, emergency food movement, or route information—to existing non-Council institutions and see whether they can plausibly coordinate it at scale.

**Disposition**

Incubating.

## Candidate 5 — Official regional identity and lived border identity may conflict

**Sources**

- [World Rules](../World-Rules.md)
- [The Convergence](../History/The-Convergence.md)
- [Geography and Connections](../Geography-and-Connections.md)
- [Regional Social Dynamics](../Culture/Regional-Social-Dynamics.md)

**Connection**

The Convergence stabilizes borders and institutions while trade, migration, mixed families, and border settlements continue blending culture.

**New implication**

Some of the strongest modern "regional traditions" may be institutional standardizations imposed on populations whose actual culture is more mixed.

**Why it matters**

This creates non-villain political tension involving language, schooling, law, guild rules, military service, marriage, land claims, and identity.

**Smallest test**

Choose one established border town and compare its daily practices with the official narratives of both neighboring regions.

**Disposition**

Strong candidate for later border-town detail.

## Candidate 6 — Abundance and scarcity are structurally similar crises

**Sources**

- [Greenvale](../Regions/Greenvale.md)
- [Sunplains](../Regions/Sunplains.md)
- [Northwind](../Regions/Northwind.md)
- [Trade and Dependencies](../Economy/Trade-and-Dependencies.md)
- [Current Events](../Story/Current-Events.md)

**Connection**

Greenvale can be destabilized by excess production; Sunplains by drought anxiety; Northwind by fish decline.

**New implication**

The political problem is not simply "too little resource." It is a mismatch among production, storage, debt, transport, expectations, and ownership.

**Why it matters**

This makes the economic system coherent across very different crises and gives the Council believable reasons to intervene.

**Smallest test**

Trace price, debt, labor, and political effects for one surplus and one shortage using the same causal template.

**Disposition**

Supported at the pattern level; candidate for a future economics deep dive.

## Candidate 7 — The Underpass is a systemic bypass valve

**Sources**

- [The Underpass](../Places/The-Underpass.md)
- [Geography and Connections](../Geography-and-Connections.md)
- [Crime and the Underworld](../Politics/Crime-and-Underworld.md)
- [Current Events](../Story/Current-Events.md)

**Connection**

The Underpass creates functional adjacency and contains public, local, religious, criminal, unstable, and hidden routes.

**New implication**

When official surface routes become expensive, blocked, militarized, or politically restricted, pressure should redirect toward Underpass routes.

That can make the Underpass a recurring **bypass valve** for legitimate trade, smuggling, refugees, intelligence, and political influence.

**Why it matters**

This gives Underpass politics direct consequences for the surface world instead of leaving it as a separate underground setting.

**Smallest test**

Take one surface-route disruption and trace what realistically shifts underground, who gains, and what new bottleneck appears.

**Test result — 2026-09-22**

[Working Map Draft — Geometry V1](Working-Map-Draft.md) gives the Underpass limited alternate branches beside major surface routes. In the Western Gate closure test, some movement can shift underground without giving the Underpass enough capacity or reliability to replace the surface network.

This supports the bypass-valve mechanism at the provisional map level. It is not yet promoted because route capacity, travel time, collapse risk, and actual entrances remain unresolved.

**Disposition**

Incubating pending scale / capacity testing.

## Promotion rule

Nothing on this page becomes canon because it sounds useful.

Promote only when:

1. the relevant owner pages support it;
2. the connection survives a concrete world example;
3. it does not contradict a stronger current owner;
4. it changes understanding enough to justify preserving;
5. the promoted statement is moved into the correct owner page rather than duplicated here.

When promoted, leave a short dated disposition here pointing to the new owner.
