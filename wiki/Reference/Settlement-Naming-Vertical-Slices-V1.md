# 1. LR-M — G1 head-of-navigation market

## What is actually known

LR-M exists because:

- bulk river navigation becomes impractical farther upstream;
- cargo transfers between river and road / cart systems;
- storage, weighing, custody, labor, and finance cluster there;
- it sits inside LR-A but has strong local commercial authority.

This is enough to produce a **commercial exonym / nickname** even though the older local settlement history remains open.

## Common-language candidate

> **Last Quay**

### Why this works

For merchants and river crews approaching from downstream:

- it is the last major reliable bulk-river quay before cargo must change mode;
- the phrase is functional rather than prestigious;
- it can spread through trade even if locals use an older R1 name.

### What it is not

It is not yet:

- the official civic name;
- the R1 endonym;
- proof that there is literally only one quay;
- the LR-A administrative name.

It is a common-language commercial form.

## Writing-the-Sequel layer

Possible sequence:

~~~
older local settlement
→ navigation transfer grows
→ downstream crews call it the last quay
→ merchant records shorten / standardize the phrase
→ outsiders increasingly know the market by the commercial name
→ locals continue using older civic name
~~~

## Test result

**PASS as a provisional interregional / commercial name.**

**Local civic name remains open.**

# 2. NC-R / HR-N paired roadheads

## What is known

The two settlements exist because carts cannot reliably cross the high section end-to-end.

Each has:

- yards;
- storage;
- animal transfer;
- guides;
- lodging;
- route-status information.

They are paired economically but politically separate.

## Common caravan shorthand

V1 tests:

- **North Yard** — North Coast-side roadhead;
- **South Yard** — High Roads-side roadhead.

Collectively, traders may call them:

> **the Yards**

### Why this works

This is not intended to replace local names.

It is exactly the kind of boring occupational shorthand that can spread because:

- caravan workers need to distinguish the two transfer yards;
- the distinction is directionally stable on the principal pass;
- the names remain understandable across several languages when translated.

### Political consequence

Official / local speakers may dislike the shorthand because it reduces real towns to infrastructure.

A person from North Yard may say:

> That's the caravan name. The town has a name.

That is useful breath.

## Convergence layer

Treaty / route documents may refer to:

- the local legal settlement names;
- plus a translated parenthetical equivalent used by foreign merchants.

The common shorthand can therefore be famous without becoming legally primary.

## Test result

**PASS as caravan / interregional shorthand.**

**Both local settlement names remain open.**

# 3. SH-M — major Stone Hills industrial city

## What is known

SH-M is:

- a major industrial / mining civic jurisdiction;
- older than at least some of the current SH-A central structure;
- home to mines, workshops, guilds, workers, merchants, and civic institutions.

## What is not known

We still do **not** know what caused the original settlement to exist.

Possibilities include:

- a river crossing;
- a mine mouth;
- a defensible hill;
- a valley market;
- a spring;
- an older village;
- a family estate.

Without that information, a local place name cannot be grounded.

## Legacy test

Names such as:

- Blackbellow;
- Ironhold;
- Cindermarch

remain weak for SH-M because they start from industrial atmosphere rather than settlement history.

## Could "the Works" function as a nickname?

Possibly.

But even that should wait until we know whether:

- one famous works complex actually dominated the city;
- workers / outsiders would plausibly use the phrase.

Current evidence only establishes a large industrial city, not one singular works.

## Test result

**FAIL / NOT READY.**

Do not name SH-M yet.

### Required next evidence

Establish one site-history vertical slice:

1. original physical reason for settlement;
2. older pre-industrial population;
3. what mine / workshop expansion changed;
4. whether the original settlement name survived;
5. which district / commercial nickname appeared later.

Only then generate W1 forms.

# 4. HR-C — central High Roads city

## What is known

HR-C is:

- major switching center;
- arbitration center;
- multilingual meeting place;
- likely host of recurring multi-route gatherings.

## Naming danger

This is exactly the kind of place likely to receive a bad fantasy name meaning:

- summit;
- crossroads;
- grand market;
- high throne.

The old **Summithold** pattern should not return.

## Missing evidence

The city still needs a mundane site origin:

- well;
- spring;
- old fort;
- bridge;
- family market;
- older village;
- junction of two specific roads.

## Test result

**NOT READY for local name.**

A generic commercial descriptor such as "the Crossroads" would add little and risks becoming another region-level label.

# 5. OC-U — upstream Old Cities city-state

## What is known

OC-U is:

- an old sovereign city;
- tied to S1 upper / middle water system;
- connected to old headworks / irrigation;
- surrounded by estates / client territories.

## Naming potential

Old Cities is the strongest place for an inherited opaque endonym.

But opacity must be earned by:

- S1 lexical roots;
- older city history;
- sound change;
- civic conservatism.

## Legacy test

**Zalhar** remains unsupported.

The city should not receive a replacement opaque string until its specific founding-site history and S1 lexical source exist.

## Test result

**NOT READY for final endonym.**

---

# 6. Naming readiness rule recovered

The political / language profiles are necessary but not sufficient.

A name needs **three layers of evidence**:

1. historical / physical naming cause;
2. local naming profile;
3. later administrative / commercial history.

LR-M and the paired roadheads have enough evidence for translated commercial names because their functions are exceptionally clear.

SH-M, HR-C, and OC-U still lack specific site origin.

Therefore:

> **the next naming work should be settlement-history work, not more name generation.**

# 7. Provisional names promoted only at the correct layer

## Provisional commercial / traveler forms

- **Last Quay** — LR-M interregional / river-trade name.
- **North Yard** — NC-R caravan shorthand.
- **South Yard** — HR-N caravan shorthand.
- **the Yards** — collective caravan shorthand for the paired roadheads.

## Still open

- local civic names for all three;
- SH-M;
- HR-C;
- OC-U;
- polity names.

These terms should remain labeled as **provisional common-language forms**, not final local names.
