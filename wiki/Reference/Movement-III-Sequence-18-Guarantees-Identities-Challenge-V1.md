## Summary

**Sphere sorting works. V1 makes the overlap too intentionally packaged.**

The story needs:

> separate actors solving separate access problems and discovering they keep relying on the same partners.

It should not feel like:

> one merchant house sells OC-C a proto-alliance bundle.

---

# 1. Break the reassurance package apart

OC-C should pursue:

- food access through one Low Rivers / merchant relationship;
- staged credit through a guarantor;
- shipping access through maritime intermediaries.

These can be negotiated:

- separately;
- at different times;
- for different reasons.

The political effect appears only when officials / clerks notice:

> several contracts lead back toward the same cluster of houses / counterparties.

That is stronger.

---

# 2. Keep OC-C motive bounded

OC-C fears:

- overdependence;
- possible accumulation in OC-U / OC-D cooperation.

It does **not** yet think:

> we need a bloc.

The official should ask:

> "If access tightens, who will still sell / carry / finance us?"

That is enough.

---

# 3. Reduce the Stone Hills overlap

Keep one small sign:

- a Stone Hills merchant asks about a guarantor already appearing in OC-C / shipping records.

Do not imply a fully integrated food-shipping-metal network yet.

Sequence 18 is the beginning of overlap.

---

# 4. Trim the pronoun montage

The idea is good.

Use 2–3 examples through ordinary dialogue rather than a narrator inventory.

The reader should notice the language before being told it is changing identity.

---

# 5. Soften OC-C self-awareness

The diplomatic reply can remain:

> no defense obligation.

But the official should not already diagnose the whole strategic problem.

They simply discover that a commercial arrangement now requires diplomatic explanation.

That is enough.
