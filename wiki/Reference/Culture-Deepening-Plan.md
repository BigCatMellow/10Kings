## Status

**Reference / roadmap — not setting canon.**

This plan audits the current cultural owners against the preserved 2026 legacy material and defines a controlled way to deepen the setting without restoring old stereotypes, duplicating owners, or creating encyclopedic detail that does not change how the world behaves.

## Revision principle — improvement over preservation

The purpose of this pass is **not to preserve old worldbuilding for its own sake**.

Current and legacy material should be judged by:

- cultural fit;
- historical plausibility;
- internal consistency;
- usefulness in scenes;
- distinctiveness;
- connection to the rest of the world.

If an older name, dish, custom, polity, festival, language idea or aesthetic no longer fits, it may be revised or replaced.

Legacy material remains useful as provenance and an idea source, but **retention is not a success criterion**.

The desired question is:

> What version makes the world feel most coherent, specific and lived-in now?

not:

> How can we justify keeping what we already wrote?

## Executive finding

The current culture wiki is **methodologically stronger but materially thinner** than the old notes.

The reconciliation pass successfully corrected several older problems:

- regions are no longer single-culture gimmicks;
- real cultures are inspiration pools rather than templates;
- language affects habits of attention rather than intelligence or personality;
- names need etymological history rather than fantasy sound;
- food follows ecology, trade, class, season and preservation;
- architecture is accumulated adaptation rather than one regional visual style;
- stereotypes are explicitly beliefs held by characters rather than objective facts;
- border and diaspora cultures are allowed to become their own cultures.

However, that cleanup compressed away substantial lived-world detail.

The main task is therefore:

> **rebuild depth underneath the better rules.**

Do not begin by inventing more capitals, dishes, festivals or conlangs.

Begin by reconstructing the mechanisms that would produce them.

# 3. Deepening model

Use the following as an **audit lens**, not a requirement that every region receive one symmetric paragraph for every category.

For a community, district, institution or cultural group, ask:

1. **Material base** — climate, water, fuel, crops, animals, materials, routes.
2. **Historical strata** — who lived here before, migrations, wars, disasters, old rulers.
3. **Institutions** — polity, guilds, faiths, kin groups, schools, markets.
4. **Household life** — who lives together and who owes whom care.
5. **Work rhythm** — daily and seasonal schedule.
6. **Food system** — supply, preservation, cooking, class, ritual.
7. **Material culture** — clothing, tools, furniture, decoration, repair.
8. **Language / names** — home speech, public register, old names, loanwords.
9. **Ritual / belief** — faith plus nonreligious custom and superstition.
10. **Arts / leisure** — how people entertain themselves and remember.
11. **Status** — what wealth, respectability, disgrace and aspiration look like.
12. **Internal diversity** — class, neighborhood, generation, profession, sect.
13. **External contact** — neighbors, diaspora, trade, conquest, migration.
14. **Present change** — what younger people are doing differently.
15. **Scene visibility** — what a visitor actually notices without receiving a lecture.

The last point matters.

Culture that cannot appear naturally in a scene is often not yet useful enough to canonize.

# 4. MAPS_L-compatible workflow

## Stage A — Recover

For each culture domain:

1. identify the current owner;
2. inspect its legacy sources;
3. extract compatible mechanisms and examples;
4. mark conflicts with current World Rules;
5. preserve discarded legacy material rather than deleting it.

Output:

**source → current owner → candidate detail → status → test**

Do not promote during extraction.

## Stage B — Connect

Run Spiderweb-style checks.

Every cultural detail should connect to several existing systems where appropriate:

```text
ecology
  ↓
work / resources
  ↓
household practice
  ↓
food / clothing / architecture
  ↓
class / institutions
  ↓
trade + migration
  ↓
regional variation
  ↓
current politics / story
```

Example:

A North Coast preserved-food practice should ideally connect to:

- fishing season;
- salt access;
- storage technology;
- household labor;
- winter shipping;
- class;
- religious restrictions if relevant;
- Port trade;
- current fish-stock pressure.

If it connects only to “North Coast is cold,” it is too shallow.

## Stage C — E/I capture

Useful emergent connections go first into E/I or the culture plan as candidates.

Examples:

- a food preservation guild might become politically important during the current fish crisis;
- a Port fashion derived from worker clothing might be adopted by Ascendant elites;
- a historical place-name layer may reveal an old border claim;
- a diaspora festival may preserve a version of history the home region has abandoned.

Do not promote because a connection sounds clever.

## Stage D — Vertical-slice test

Before expanding an entire region, build **one ordinary community or household** deeply enough to test the system.

For each major region plus Port, eventually test one slice such as:

- one household;
- one neighborhood;
- one market street;
- one village;
- one guild branch;
- one festival day.

A valid slice should let us answer naturally:

- what they eat;
- what they wear;
- what language / register they use;
- where food and water come from;
- what they do for work;
- how status appears;
- what they do after work;
- what they argue about;
- what they call nearby places;
- which institutions they depend on;
- what has changed since their grandparents' generation.

If the slice requires inventing disconnected facts to fill boxes, the larger culture model is not ready.

## Stage E — Promote

Only promote when:

- owner pages support the detail;
- the detail survives the vertical slice;
- it has historical / material cause;
- internal variation exists;
- it does not turn a region into a personality;
- naming follows naming history;
- real-world inspiration has been transformed rather than copied.

# 5. Recommended sequence

## Phase 1 — Naming + language history

Goal:

Create enough historical language logic that future names stop being arbitrary.

Work:

- identify probable language families / contact zones without full conlangs;
- sketch historical naming strata for each region;
- define name-generation rules;
- classify existing names as:
  - compatible;
  - needs etymology;
  - provisional;
  - likely replacement;
- establish Port / border naming behavior;
- connect personal names to kinship and religion.

Deliverables:

- deeper [Naming](../Culture/Naming.md);
- deeper [Language and Thought](../Culture/Language-and-Thought.md);
- a naming audit of currently important places / polities;
- no mass renaming yet.

## Phase 2 — Daily life + material culture

Goal:

Move from “what the region is known for” to “what it feels like to live there.”

Work:

- add **Daily Life and Customs** owner;
- add **Clothing and Material Culture** owner;
- recover useful clothing / social-life material from Volume 04;
- distinguish class, profession, urban / rural and generation;
- build first vertical slices.

Deliverables:

- ordinary household / neighborhood examples;
- material supply chains;
- etiquette and life-stage framework.

## Phase 3 — Food deepening

Goal:

Turn broad culinary inventories into historical cuisines.

For each test community:

- staple ecology;
- fuel;
- cookware;
- preservation;
- food labor;
- markets;
- class;
- work meals;
- ritual meals;
- prestige ingredients;
- imported foods;
- one or two historical changes.

Then:

- reconcile selected legacy dishes;
- rename surviving dishes according to real naming history;
- create a few diaspora lineages;
- connect current economic crises to actual diets.

Do **not** build dozens of recipe cards first.

## Phase 4 — Architecture deepening

Goal:

Make settlements visibly historical and socially stratified.

Work:

- material supply;
- builders / guilds;
- land patterns;
- water / waste;
- fire;
- street evolution;
- rebuilding;
- worker / merchant / elite / religious building differences;
- one settlement morphology per tested community.

## Phase 5 — Arts, leisure and public culture

Goal:

Give people something to care about that is not government, guilds or survival.

Add the **Arts, Music and Leisure** owner.

Develop:

- songs / oral history;
- performance;
- games;
- sports;
- public competitions;
- visual motifs;
- youth culture;
- popular fashions;
- patronage;
- old-fashioned versus fashionable forms.

This is also where Port's ECW-like cultural role and the competitive spheres can become visible without making culture subordinate to politics.

## Phase 6 — Festivals and life-cycle rituals

Goal:

Replace generic festival labels with events that emerge from actual histories.

For each surviving festival:

- origin;
- historical layer;
- name etymology;
- local variants;
- labor / market effect;
- class participation;
- food;
- clothing;
- religious interpretations;
- dissent / refusal;
- diaspora version.

Some current provisional festivals should disappear rather than be deepened.

## Phase 7 — Cross-regional synthesis

Goal:

Make cultural exchange follow the world's actual networks.

Test:

- border towns;
- Port;
- High Roads routes;
- itinerant peoples;
- pilgrimage;
- guild branches;
- competitive spheres;
- refugee / labor migration.

Trace concrete things moving:

- one dish;
- one song;
- one garment;
- one word;
- one name;
- one game;
- one ritual.

See where each changes and where it stops.

## Phase 8 — Breath pass

Goal:

Convert encyclopedic culture into usable scene texture.

For each region / Port, keep a small bank of:

- obsolete slang;
- annoying current fashion;
- ordinary food price;
- local joke;
- old building nickname;
- minor etiquette mistake;
- cheap snack;
- children's game;
- household object;
- neighborhood rivalry;
- cultural argument with no plot payoff.

Do not canonize hundreds of these.

They are scene seeds.

# 7. What not to do

## Do not restore old regional personality templates

Avoid:

- Stone Hills people are direct because they forge;
- North Coast people are communal because survival;
- Low Rivers people are gentle because farms;
- High Roads people are logical because trade;
- Longwood people are spiritual because forest;
- Old Cities people are elegant because warm climate.

Those can exist as **stereotypes, local traditions, institutions or specific subcultures**, not universal psychology.

## Do not assign one Earth culture to one region

No:

- North Coast = Scandinavia;
- Old Cities = Middle East;
- Longwood = Indigenous forest people;
- Stone Hills = Appalachia / Wales.

Use multiple research pools to solve specific human problems.

## Do not invent names before etymology

A good-looking fantasy word is not enough.

Ask:

- who named it;
- in what language;
- what did it mean then;
- who mispronounced / translated / shortened it;
- whether the current political name differs from the local one.

## Do not deepen culture only through festivals

Most culture is ordinary Tuesday behavior.

## Do not make every cultural detail ancient

Some traditions should be:

- new;
- commercial;
- fashionable;
- politically manufactured;
- imported;
- recently revived;
- widely mocked.

## Do not make every difference regional

Class, profession, religion, neighborhood, age, migration history and polity may matter more than region in a given scene.

## Do not make every detail plot-relevant

Apply [Worldbuilding Breath](Worldbuilding-Breath.md).

A cheap lunch or irritating song can simply exist.

# 8. First implementation pass

The best first execution is **not** “deep dive all six regions.”

It is:

1. deepen Naming and Language enough to control future creation;
2. create Daily Life / Customs and Clothing / Material Culture owners;
3. choose **one ordinary test community per region plus Port**;
4. run food + clothing + names + household + architecture through those vertical slices;
5. use results to refine the method before scaling;
6. then expand region by region.

This keeps the process falsifiable.

If the method produces six tidy stereotype cultures, stop and revise it.

If it produces recognizable regions containing multiple classes, towns, generations and borrowed traditions, then scale it.

## Execution status — Phase 3

**Started 2026-09-23.**

Completed first food deepening pass:

- rebuilt [Food](../Culture/Food.md) around ecology + fuel + cookware + preservation + labor + trade + class + season + religion + migration + history;

- the Low Rivers abundance crisis works especially well when cheap food coexists with farm debt and asset loss;
- Port food should become genuinely Port-born through repeated local use rather than being described as “fusion”;
- legacy dish names built from forge / frost / forest / sun / caravan imagery remain reference only.

Still unresolved:

- exact staple species;
- exact fats / oils;
- preservation salt geography at scale;
- precise pottery / metal-cookware distribution;
- ownership of communal ovens, mills and smokehouses;
- religious dietary practices;
- final local food names.

## Cross-phase principle — contact creates culture

The culture pass should actively look for **third traditions** created by overlap.

Do not model cultural bleed only as:
- source culture A preserved;
- source culture B preserved;
- a few borrowed details between them.

Also test:

```text
A + B + local conditions + generations
→ C
```

where C becomes a stable local tradition.

This applies across:
- food;
- language;
- architecture;
- clothing;
- naming;
- music;
- festivals;
- household customs.

The strongest candidates are Port, border towns, the High Roads, river markets, mining settlements and migrant agricultural communities.

# 9. Success test

The culture pass is succeeding when two people can both be from the same region and still differ meaningfully because of:

- town;
- class;
- occupation;
- generation;
- religion;
- family;
- migration;
- sphere affiliation;
- guild;
- neighborhood.

At the same time, a reader should still be able to notice shared historical patterns connecting them.

The target is:

> **recognizable without being uniform.**

And the deepest test is:

> Can a cultural detail be explained by history, material conditions and social transmission rather than by “because this is the Stone Hills / North Coast / Low Rivers culture”?
