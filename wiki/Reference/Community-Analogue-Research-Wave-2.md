# 1. SH-1 — major industrial mine / civic district

## Comparator A — South Wales mining communities and workmen's institutes

### Sources

Museum Wales:

- https://museum.wales/articles/1019/Coal-Society---A-History-of-the-South-Wales-Mining-Valleys-1840-1980/
- https://museum.wales/articles/1194/Oakdale-Workmens-Institute/

### Source-derived mechanisms

Museum Wales documents South Wales mining communities in which:

- industrial settlements could become heavily dependent on one major extractive industry;
- organized miners built institutions extending beyond wage bargaining;
- workmen's institutes supported:
  - libraries;
  - reading rooms;
  - education;
  - lectures;
  - entertainment;
  - meeting space;
- workers themselves could finance these institutions through regular contributions;
- the same institutions could become centers for:
  - labor organization;
  - political discussion;
  - cultural life;
  - community support during hard periods.

The important mechanism is:

> **industrial labor can generate its own civic institutions rather than existing only inside employer-controlled space.**

### 10Kings application

SH-1 should contain more than:

- mine owner;
- mine;
- workers;
- tavern.

A mature industrial district can support worker-built / worker-funded institutions such as:

- reading / training halls;
- injury funds;
- burial funds;
- meeting rooms;
- technical libraries;
- mutual-aid kitchens;
- music / recreation societies.

These can overlap with:

- guilds;
- faith organizations;
- labor reform networks

without being identical to any one of them.

This gives Stone Hills labor politics **institutional depth** before the current crisis.

### Myth consequence

The Knocking Below can live inside these worker institutions through:

- accident records;
- songs;
- memorial books;
- oral testimony.

A technical library may preserve skeptical explanations beside memorial accounts.

That creates internal disagreement without separating "educated" from "superstitious."

### Do not borrow

- Welsh ethnicity;
- chapel culture;
- 19th/20th-century union structure;
- coal-mining industrial chronology;
- exact workmen's-institute architecture.

## Comparator B — Butte, Montana mining city

### Sources

US National Park Service:

- https://www.nps.gov/places/butte-anaconda-historic-district.htm
- https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/butte-montana-the-richest-hill-on-earth.htm

Smithsonian / oral-history context:

- https://folklife.si.edu/magazine/butte-montana-irish-mining-heritage

### Source-derived mechanisms

The Butte historical material supports a city shaped by:

- extremely intensive mining;
- major corporate / ownership concentration;
- dangerous industrial labor;
- labor organization as a counterweight to concentrated economic power;
- immigration producing many neighborhood, ethnic, religious, and mutual-aid institutions;
- mining work influencing:
  - housing;
  - civic politics;
  - public health;
  - social organizations;
  - neighborhood identity.

The useful comparative mechanism is:

> **one industrial economy can create many communities inside one city rather than one homogeneous "mining culture."**

### 10Kings application

SH-1 should contain internal distinctions such as:

- mine / shaft district;
- smelting / processing neighborhoods if applicable;
- skilled technical workers;
- haulage / transport workers;
- migrant labor neighborhoods;
- older local families;
- managerial / owner districts;
- guild halls;
- worker associations.

This means the current Stone Hills strategic-metal conflict can fracture the city in several directions:

- workers who need wages;
- workers who oppose unsafe priority production;
- skilled guild members defending professional autonomy;
- merchants dependent on mine wages;
- households harmed by contamination / dust / water use;
- owners arguing that contracts keep the district solvent.

### Myth consequence

The Ore-Spoiler becomes especially plausible here as:

- technical slang;
- worker warning;
- labor-memory symbol.

A younger technical specialist can dismiss the creature while still using its name for a known class of dangerous ore.

### Do not borrow

- specific immigrant ethnic identities;
- company-town history one-to-one;
- American union law;
- copper-mining technology unless local metallurgy supports it.

# 2. NC-3 — major compact harbor

## Comparator A — Bryggen / Bergen merchant harbor

### Source

UNESCO World Heritage Centre:

- https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/59/

### Source-derived mechanisms

UNESCO describes Bryggen as a historic harbor district tied to long-distance fish trade.

Important mechanisms include:

- merchant offices;
- warehouses;
- lodging / working spaces;
- concentrated commercial privileges;
- foreign merchant presence;
- a district whose built environment integrates:
  - storage;
  - office;
  - residence;
  - assembly;
  - service functions.

The historical Hanseatic office functioned as more than a collection of independent shops.

It created a durable merchant institution embedded in the harbor.

### 10Kings application

NC-3 should be more institutionally layered than NC-1.

Possible harbor actors:

- local vessel owners;
- wage / share crews;
- fish merchants;
- preserved-fish / oil / processing interests;
- warehouse owners;
- pilots;
- repair yards;
- seasonal fishers;
- foreign / outside merchant houses;
- civic harbor authority.

A merchant quarter can possess:

- its own lodging;
- warehouses;
- records;
- internal norms

without becoming politically sovereign.

That creates fertile tension between:

> commercially privileged district

and

> local civic authority.

### Myth consequence

Great Mouth stories arriving from outer islands become:

- standardized;
- commercialized;
- written down

in NC-3 before they ever reach Port.

That gives the legend **two stages of reinterpretation**:

~~~text
island oral tradition
→ major harbor commercial version
→ Port scholarly / popular version
~~~

### Do not borrow

- Hanseatic identity;
- German merchant enclave structure one-to-one;
- exact timber architecture;
- medieval Bergen political law.

## Comparator B — harbor governance emerging from private facilities

### Source

Bergen City Archives:

- https://www.bergenbyarkiv.no/bergenbyleksikon/arkiv/1421841

### Source-derived mechanisms

Historical harbor development can begin with:

- privately controlled quays / storehouses;
- owners responsible for portions of maintenance / security;

then create pressure for:

- broader harbor coordination;
- public harbor administration;
- harbor masters / commissions

because the harbor functions as one system even when property is fragmented.

### 10Kings application

This maps strongly onto NC-M's political architecture.

NC-3 can have an old historical sequence:

~~~text
private / household / merchant quay rights
→ recurring shared navigation / repair / congestion problems
→ harbor-wide office
→ office gains authority over only the functions the shared harbor requires
~~~

This helps explain why North Coast politics already knows how to build **functional authority without erasing older rights**.

That later matters for:

- convoy governance;
- Port debates;
- Convergence institutions.

### Do not borrow

- Bergen office names;
- exact municipal history;
- modern harbor bureaucracy.

# 3. OC-1 / OC-2 — upstream / downstream water cultures

The strongest useful comparisons are institutional rather than aesthetic.

---

## Comparator A — Omani aflaj

### Sources

UNESCO World Heritage Centre:

- https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1207/

UNESCO water knowledge:

- https://www.unesco.org/en/virtual-science-museum/voices-water/oman

### Source-derived mechanisms

UNESCO describes **aflaj** irrigation systems as:

- gravity-fed water distribution;
- shared by communities;
- dependent on maintenance and inherited technical knowledge;
- organized through water shares / timed allocation;
- sustaining settlement patterns around access to water.

The broader institutional lesson is:

> **water allocation becomes a social system because timing, maintenance, and legitimacy must be coordinated repeatedly.**

### 10Kings application

Old Cities water culture should not reduce to:

- ruler opens gate;
- peasants receive water.

OC-U and OC-D can contain people whose civic identity is partly shaped by:

- water-share records;
- gate / channel schedules;
- maintenance obligations;
- inherited technical knowledge;
- participation in water institutions.

### Upstream / downstream difference

Same type of institution can feel different politically.

OC-U participants may emphasize:

- lawful control;
- headworks responsibility;
- maintenance cost;
- technical necessity.

OC-D participants may emphasize:

- predictability;
- transparency;
- downstream protection;
- proof that upstream actors did not take more than agreed.

This gives the shared-water institution **different local political cultures without inventing two completely different systems.**

### Myth consequence

The Charter Beast can be associated with:

- share markers;
- water records;
- old boundary stones.

But OC-U may frame it as:

> guardian of proper allocation.

OC-D may frame it as:

> witness against stolen flow.

### Do not borrow

- aflaj terminology;
- Omani tribal / religious institutions;
- exact time-share formulas;
- architecture / clothing.

## Comparator B — acequia common-property institutions

### Source

New Mexico State University:

- https://aces.nmsu.edu/pubs/_circulars/CR649/

### Source-derived mechanisms

Acequia associations demonstrate a system in which:

- multiple users share a diversion / ditch;
- users owe:
  - dues;
  - communal maintenance labor;
- local officers coordinate:
  - allocation;
  - maintenance;
  - disputes;
- water institutions possess a recognized legal / social identity separate from ordinary household ownership.

The key mechanism is:

> **rights come with labor and governance obligations.**

### 10Kings application

This works exceptionally well with Old Cities shared water.

A water-right holder might owe:

- ditch clearing;
- gate repair;
- inspection labor;
- emergency contribution.

That prevents water rights from feeling like abstract paperwork.

### Cultural consequence

Annual / seasonal maintenance can become:

- work event;
- civic ritual;
- neighborhood gathering;
- arena for conflict over free-riders / powerful estates.

The same institution can therefore generate:

- politics;
- food / work custom;
- songs;
- grudges;
- marriage / neighborhood contact.

### Do not borrow

- Spanish terminology;
- New Mexican legal status;
- Catholic / colonial history;
- exact officer titles.

## Comparator C — Valencia Water Tribunal

### Sources

UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage:

- https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/irrigators-tribunals-of-the-spanish-mediterranean-coast-the-council-of-wise-men-of-the-plain-of-murcia-and-the-water-tribunal-of-the-plain-of-valencia-00171

Tribunal de les Aigües:

- https://tribunaldelasaguas.org/en/

### Source-derived mechanisms

The irrigation tribunals demonstrate how water disputes can be handled through:

- specialized community institutions;
- representatives tied to irrigation communities;
- public / visible procedure;
- oral / relatively direct adjudication;
- legitimacy deriving partly from:
  - continuity;
  - practical expertise;
  - users recognizing the institution.

### 10Kings application

Old Cities water specialists should not be only engineers.

There can be respected figures whose authority comes from:

- precedent;
- measurement;
- water practice;
- repeated public dispute resolution.

This gives the current Old Cities drought braid actual social carriers.

### Important distinction

The shared tribunal / water compact is **not a regional government**.

It is powerful precisely because its competence is narrow.

That reinforces the existing Old Cities design.

### Myth consequence

A Charter Beast symbol on tribunal / record material could become:

- solemn legal emblem in one city;
- living canal folklore in another.

### Do not borrow

- exact tribunal ceremony;
- Valencia officer titles;
- Spanish regional law;
- irrigation vocabulary.

# 7. LW-1 — western Longwood compact community

## Comparator A — Petén community forest concessions

### Sources

FAO:

- https://www.fao.org/4/y5189e/y5189e11.htm
- https://www.fao.org/4/xii/0327-c2.htm

CIFOR / comparative research:

- https://www.cifor-icraf.org/knowledge/publication/1209/

### Source-derived mechanisms

Research on community forest concessions in Guatemala's Petén region supports several useful mechanisms:

- communities can receive defined rights to manage / use forest resources;
- commercial use can be governed through:
  - management plans;
  - extraction limits;
  - monitoring;
- community enterprises can engage external markets;
- earlier uncontrolled / externally driven extraction can create pressure for more locally governed systems;
- outcomes depend on:
  - governance;
  - market access;
  - community organization;
  - ecological conditions.

The useful lesson is:

> **commercial forest use and local stewardship are not inherently opposites.**

### 10Kings application

LW-W should not divide neatly into:

- "locals protecting forest"
versus
- "outsiders cutting forest."

Local actors may support:

- timber;
- charcoal;
- road access;
- commercial forest products;

while fighting over:

- scale;
- who grants permission;
- whose rights are consulted;
- who keeps revenue;
- regeneration / maintenance obligations.

That makes Wurdren's Longwood dispute much stronger.

A local representative can oppose a particular widening / cutting claim without opposing trade or roads in principle.

### Myth consequence

The Forest Owner becomes politically usable by **multiple sides**.

A conservation faction may invoke it.

A local timber cooperative may also invoke it:

> the Forest Owner punishes people who take without obligation, not people who cut.

That is much more interesting than "forest spirit hates logging."

### Do not borrow

- Guatemalan ethnic identities;
- legal concession terminology;
- rainforest ecology one-to-one;
- modern NGO / development structure.

## Comparator B — ACOFOP / representative community organization

### Source

FAO / community forestry research:

- https://www.fao.org/4/y5189e/y5189e11.htm

### Source-derived mechanisms

Community forest organizations can move from:

- advocacy for access rights

toward:

- technical support;
- commercial coordination;
- representation.

This produces a familiar institutional tension:

> the organization created to represent local communities can itself become a durable center with expertise / influence.

### 10Kings application

LW-W's compact authority can plausibly have developed because:

- separate communities could not negotiate major roads / concessions independently;
- external actors preferred one counterpart;
- local communities needed shared technical / legal capacity.

But that creates internal arguments:

- does the compact speak for us?
- can it grant what belongs locally?
- has the representative body become too permanent?
- do migrant / newer settlements count equally?

This directly supports the already established political-order rule:

> central compact authority may approve a concession that still violates a local use right.

Now the **social origin** of that contradiction is clearer.

# 9. Cross-wave synthesis

Wave 2 adds four major insights.

## Industrial culture creates civic culture

Stone Hills mines should generate:

- worker institutions;
- learning;
- mutual aid;
- neighborhood identity;

not merely conflict at the workplace.

## Large harbors create institutional layering

North Coast major harbors should contain:

- local civic power;
- merchants;
- warehouses;
- seasonal labor;
- long-settled diaspora / outside commercial communities.

## Shared water does not create identical politics

OC-U and OC-D can share infrastructure and law while producing opposite political instincts from:

- upstream responsibility;
- downstream vulnerability.

## Longwood market integration does not erase stewardship

LW-W can be:

- commercially engaged;
- institutionally sophisticated;
- protective of layered local rights.

That is important for avoiding romanticized forest isolation.
