# 1. Stonehearth / Stoneheart seed

Useful core:

- mountain settlement using geothermal warmth;
- steep site requiring paths, railings and terraced building;
- workshops integrated with housing;
- communal warm-water spaces.

Keep:

- geothermal heat as a possible local physical advantage;
- cliff / slope adaptation;
- communal bathing / gathering if local culture supports it.

Do not assume:

- glowing magical pools;
- every resident is a smith;
- the settlement must belong to Stone Hills;
- the old name survives.

---

# 2. Ironhold seed

Useful core:

- mining settlement whose mine entrance, processing, workshops and guild administration physically organize the city;
- guild hall as a political / economic center;
- worker movement and ore transport shape streets.

Keep:

- extractive industry physically shaping urban morphology;
- guild governance as one possible polity form;
- housing / market / workshop tensions around one dominant employer or industry.

Reject:

- city identity reduced entirely to weapons;
- industrial-scale technology beyond current baseline.

---

# 3. Winterhaven seed

Useful core:

- isolated cold-weather settlement;
- walls / watch systems because outside aid is slow;
- local political authority strengthened by geographic isolation;
- hunters / trappers / supply storage matter.

Keep:

- isolation changing governance;
- winter storage;
- watch / rescue / shelter institutions.

Reject:

- generic "ruthless ice lord" as default political character;
- monster-defense assumptions without current magic/ecology support.

---

# 4. Galecrest seed

Useful core:

- cliff-top settlement with docks far below;
- vertical circulation between harbor and homes;
- rope, stairs, ramps and hoists;
- storm-resistant shutters / materials;
- fishing and repair work integrated into public space.

This is a strong North Coast **or any cliff-harbor** settlement mechanism.

The architecture comes from site constraints, not cultural essence.

---

# 5. Rivercross seed

Useful core:

- settlement spread across river islands;
- bridges, boats and stilts as ordinary circulation;
- water-level changes alter access;
- docks and crossings create neighborhood differences;
- maintenance of bridges becomes political.

This is one of the strongest seeds in Volume 03.

It could fit Low Rivers, Longwood waterways, a border delta, or another river system entirely.

The old water-spirit decoration is optional local culture, not structural fact.

---

# 6. Drogan's / Dragon's Pass seed

Useful core:

- settlement exists because a narrow pass concentrates traffic;
- market, lodging, repair and escort services dominate;
- bridge / ravine infrastructure matters;
- pass closure directly changes the local economy.

This is a strong High Roads / Spine mechanism but should not be assumed to be the region's capital or primary city.

Use with:

- [Movement and Information](../Movement-and-Information.md)
- [Trade and Dependencies](../Economy/Trade-and-Dependencies.md)

---

# 7. Ebonmist seed

Useful core:

- wetland / swamp settlement on stilts and boardwalks;
- herbs and wetland products;
- boats / raised paths;
- fog and water make navigation local knowledge;
- settlement pattern follows dry ground and water depth.

Keep:

- wetland urbanism;
- specialist plant knowledge;
- boardwalk maintenance;
- difficult outside access.

Hold / reject:

- glowing spirit orbs as confirmed fact;
- ubiquitous alchemy;
- "mysterious hooded swamp people" as a cultural identity.

---

# 8. Sunsong seed

Useful core:

- oasis settlement dependent on controlled water;
- shade infrastructure;
- water storage / distribution;
- market traffic around scarce reliable water;
- fabric / awnings as climate response.

Keep:

- aqueduct / underground-channel possibility if local engineering supports it;
- water governance;
- market and pilgrimage importance of an oasis.

Do not establish:

- divine origin of the aqueduct;
- one sun-deity culture for the whole settlement;
- generic desert exotica.

---

# 9. City of Masks seed

The original full-city concept is too totalizing:

> everyone wears ornate masks all the time and identity is organized around concealment.

That is not strong enough to promote literally.

Possible salvage:

- one religious order uses masks;
- one civic festival requires anonymity;
- one court / guild uses masks to separate office from person;
- one market custom permits masked bargaining;
- one neighborhood is famous for mask craft;
- masks are used during mourning or initiation.

The **mechanism of temporary / institutional anonymity** is more interesting than a city where everyone is permanently masked.

---

# 10. Settlement design lesson

The strongest Volume 03 cities work when:

`site constraint -> infrastructure -> work -> movement pattern -> institution -> neighborhood form -> local culture`

Examples:

`river islands -> bridges / boats -> crossing economy -> bridge authority -> neighborhoods by island`

`mountain pass -> concentrated traffic -> lodging / repair / toll work -> route institutions -> market settlement`

That is the part worth carrying forward.

---

# 11. Naming rule

All names on this page are **legacy seed names only**.

Before promotion, test:

- local language history;
- endonym / exonym;
- actual geography;
- whether the name predates the current culture;
- whether the English compound is too transparently thematic.

No settlement should keep a legacy name because we are used to it.
