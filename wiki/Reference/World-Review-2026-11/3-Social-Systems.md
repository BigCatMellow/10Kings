# Cluster 3 — Social systems: review

1. No page contradicts the magic rule head-on, but Folk-Belief's "Do not make every folk belief false" needs a clause saying only the practical content can be true. Daily-Life's "Summithold" pulls in a legacy capital that canon never established.
2. Stale since D24 and D25: Medicine's open list leaves out the Apothic Circle. Daily-Life and Folk-Belief leave out the faiths' new rites and the stories' taboos. World-System §12 still lists language families and nomads as open. Culture-and-Influence still calls the Ridge-Roamers a "favored concept".
3. Duplicates: Culture-and-Influence repeats every region's inspiration list, and four pages restate "mixed places make their own culture". World-System §11 restates World Rules §12.
4. Promote: Social-Fabric's core rule and identity stack, World-System's shortest model plus chokepoints, Daily-Life's "ancient traditions may be post-Convergence standardizations", and Folk-Belief's practical-origin pattern.
5. Best insights: three faiths share one write-and-burn rite, a dead smith's tools are claimed three ways, quarantine collides with hospitality, the Apothic list collides with forest medicine, and Wurdren works as a social chokepoint.

## Insights

1. **Three faiths share one rite.** The working-life sections give the Radiant Path the renewal flame ("write their fears or failures and burn them"), the Dual Flame "write down their wrongs and burn the page", and the Silent Whisper "writing a thought down and destroying it". This is the sameness the legacy model produces. Folk-Belief §9 offers a better reading than cutting two of them: writing-and-burning is an older folk practice that crosses borders, and three faiths absorbed it, each claiming it as its own. That turns a duplicate into evidence of shared folk religion beneath the faiths (World Rules §13), and it gives mixed-faith households (Social-Fabric) a rite they already share. It also needs literacy and something to write on, so it is probably marked by class. The cost of paper is UNKNOWN, since Records-Literacy doesn't settle it. Change: Folk-Belief records it as an example of a cross-faith practice, and Religions notes the shared origin (Q1).

2. **A dead smith's tools are claimed three ways.** Daily-Life asks "Who inherits tools, land, debt…" and "what happens to tools / clothes / names / debts?" The Forge answers that a worn-out tool is "broken and melted down". Ironcrest values "reputation over generations". Social-Fabric's example person is a "debtor to an Ironcrest lender". When a Forge smith dies, the faith says melt, the heir says keep, and the creditor says sell. All three are legitimate under layered jurisdiction (Law-and-Jurisdiction). This is a ready world-story engine that adds no new lore. Daily-Life §11 should name it as the example of "faith + material + class + law".

3. **Quarantine collides with hospitality.** Medicine wants "quarantine of ships / caravans". The Infinite Compass owns "hospitality; travelers; road shrines", and Daily-Life says "In route societies… refusing lodging can be a serious act". Compass pilgrim networks "move information… faster than many governments expect", and they move disease the same way. World-System's information loop then means a rumour of plague closes a pass faster than plague would. That is a Villain move that "survives ordinary scrutiny" (World Rules §10): everyone who closes the road is being prudent. To settle: who may quarantine at a Highridge pass or on a pilgrim road. Port has rules; nobody else does (Q3).

4. **The Apothic list collides with forest medicine.** The Apothic Circle's "list of approved remedies" is a credential. Deepwood supplies "medicinal goods" and its healers "command prestige". Ridge-Roamers sell remedies on circuit, and Weaver keepers use herbs. Deepwood's pressures include "fungal disease affecting food or trade species". Run World Rules §12 on it: a blight on a medicinal species leads to a shortage of approved remedies, which leads to unlicensed itinerant remedies, which leads to fraud accusations (Magic's fraud-exposers), which puts pressure on Ridge-Roamers, who are already "fully trusted by almost no one". Medicine should state the list exists and whether forest and itinerant healers sit inside the Circle (Q2). Whether the Mushrooms blight reaches medicinal fungi stays UNKNOWN, as Decisions records for cellar fungi.

5. **Wurdren works as a social chokepoint.** World-System §10 casts Wurdren as passive: "lived consequence… sees what a 'necessary adjustment' means". Social-Fabric supplies the mechanism that World Rules §11 and D18 need: "A person can be a chokepoint. Someone trusted by two rival movements may matter more than a formal diplomat." Social-Fabric also says the Council finds "informal and overlapping networks… harder to model". Put together: Wurdren's systemic power is accumulated trust across groups, which is the one chokepoint the Council can't price. That is "mattering to one person, then more" (D18) seen from the system's side. Change: one sentence in World-System §10 linking Social-Fabric. It is consistent with D21, because nobody in a story has to see it.

6. **The Cellar Song shows how a tradition hardens.** Greenvale calls the Blight "whether exactly seven years or later mythologized". The Cellar Song has "seven verses, one for each year of the Blight, one sung at each door". In The Eighth Verse a village is asked to add a verse "for the year the cellar rats came". So the song's form may be what fixed "seven", and the story shows a tradition being edited in the present. That is Daily-Life §14 and Contested-Memory working together. Don't settle the Blight's length. Link the three pages so later writers don't treat "seven" as settled history.

7. **The identity stack lacks an outsiders' name.** Nomads: "Ridge-Roamers… is what outsiders call them. Among themselves they go by their clans". RSD's Port saying, "Every ship lands at Port, none stay", erases the Port-born people that Social-Fabric and Daily-Life insist exist. The stack has no row for what other people call you, and that is where stereotypes live. Add an "Exonym / reputation" row linking RSD and Naming. It also implies that Port-born people resent that saying, a story-ready friction drawn from existing text.

8. **One guild rule manufactures cross-membership.** Masoncraft apprentices "must work in three regions before joining". Social-Fabric says skills often fail to travel: "a guild credential is only recognized locally". So the masons are the structural exception. They are the most cross-regional workforce by rule and the carriers of mixed building methods that World Rules §3 and §17 call for, and "a small carved sign on every project" turns reputation into a record. Social-Fabric's "Cross-membership is normal" could use this as its institutional example in place of the Audience movement. The journeying mason is a natural POV for a world story.

9. **Folk lore can be right for the wrong reason.** Kirsa's "smoke lies down, boats stay up" is plausibly real weather lore, and the First Catch rule has a practical origin. Together with C1, this gives taboo stories a resolution that keeps the Magic rule intact: the custom was right, its stated cause stays a belief. Folk-Belief §14 should say this outright. Folk lore can be fully trustworthy as practice while staying silent on metaphysics.
