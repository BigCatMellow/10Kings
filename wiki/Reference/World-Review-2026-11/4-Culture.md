# Cluster 4 (culture): review report

1. Hardest conflict: the stories treat the Three Moon Festival as yearly ("Again next year", fifty years at one stall), but Three-Moons-Festival.md says it comes every seven years. The two pages don't even agree on the name (Three Moon / Three Moons).
2. Language-Families-and-Contact.md mostly defers to the author's family model. Three of its lines go beyond that model ("several unrelated languages" per region, Underpass "enclave languages", "one language per old region" called obsolete), and about 70% of the page is generic sociolinguistics.
3. The festival calendar, recipe names and Food-Diaspora quietly bring back legacy region themes (Ironcrest = fire, Deepwood = hush) and part of the rejected one-faith-per-kingdom map. Example: Forge Reawakening's relighting is the Eternal Forge's own rite.
4. Stale or orphaned items: old region names ("North Country", "High Country"), unadopted legacy polities ("Cliffhaven", "Summithold"), dead page references, and five pages with no status label.
5. Best new insight: the only family that spans two regions (Northic and Ironet) has no recorded geographic connection between them. The Trade Tongue vocabulary split (Ironet contracts, Heartland greetings) builds a power gap into how people speak.

## Insights

1. **The Northern family has no geography.** Language-and-Thought puts Northic (Northwind) and Ironet (Ironcrest) in one family, with neighbors who "half-understand each other". Geography-and-Connections lists eleven region pairings, but Ironcrest↔Northwind isn't one of them. No food, architecture, border-town, recipe or festival page shows any Ironcrest–Northwind tie. The only cross-region family therefore has no recorded contact. The world needs a mechanism: a coastal or sea route from the western uplands, an old migration, or a Highridge that once spoke Northern before Heartland speech spread (which would also fit plateau speech being "the most mixed member of its family"). Whichever is chosen should leave visible traces: kin ties, shared words for ore and fish, a shared festival logic. Also note that both Northern grammars mark proof (Northwind evidentials of who saw it; Ironcrest inspected/guaranteed). That reads like an inherited family trait. The Heartland family currently has no shared trait at all.

2. **Trade Tongue builds a power gap into speech.** "Words for money, shipping and contracts are heavily borrowed from Ironet; greetings and food words often come from Heartland Tongue." Greenvale grammar distinguishes "personal property from land… held in stewardship". The Ironet-derived contract vocabulary has no recorded equivalent. So a Greenvale family can greet a land agent fluently in Trade Tongue, but a land sale written in Trade Tongue may be unable to say "held for the family". Both translations can be defensible (Language-Families §23). This is a ready-made mechanism for the One Square / Three Pots land-agent thread, and it involves no villainy: the grammar does the work. A second effect is that Northic speakers half-hear Ironet contract words, so they get a small fluency advantage and a risk of false friends. Worth recording as a provisional rule on Language-and-Thought.

3. **The festival calendar brings back the rejected faith-per-kingdom map.** Religions.md left out the legacy faith-to-kingdom assignment "because the rule below forbids it". Yet:
   - Ironcrest's Forge Reawakening has "ceremonial relighting of fires", which is the Eternal Forge's own rite ("every fire in a town is put out and relit together"). The legacy assigned the Eternal Forge to the "Kingdom of Blaze", and Recovered-Cultural-Research records a Blaze "Forgefire festival", which surfaces as Food-Diaspora's "Forgefire Roast".
   - All four of Ironcrest's festivals are fire-themed, and so are all eight Ironcrest recipe names. That breaks World Rules §1.
   - Three or four of Deepwood's festivals are named for quiet (Silent Thaw, Gathering Quiet, Deep Silence, plus Canopy Vigil's "quiet observation"), alongside "Whispering Leaf Wraps" and "seasonality and restraint". This is the "forest hush" logic Language-Families itself calls obsolete.

   The fix uses existing rules: regional festivals are civic or guild events. Each faith keeps its own rite on its own date wherever its followers live, and reads the civic festival its own way. That produces story pressure, such as a Forge household in Sunplains relighting alone, or a Radiant family's reading of Forge Reawakening. The quiet-named festivals could be treated as outsiders' names (Language-Families §25 exonyms). The Blank Square already shows Gathering Quiet as a busy stitching-and-witness festival.

4. **Three Moon: cadence, name and sky.** Beyond C1, the name has no settled spelling (stories: "Three Moon"; page: "Three Moons"). Calendar-and-Timekeeping lists "moon system" as open, so a festival name could quietly settle the astronomy. Craft.md has already answered the name question in-world: "Nobody in Port knows why it's called Three Moon, and that's enough." That ruling deserves a place on the festival page in place of the "Still open: why it is called" bullet. It also means the moon count stays open.

5. **The two PR #4 proposals answer each other.** Audience Movements' first promotion-gate question is what gathering infrastructure made the original audience possible. The non-magical answer is a recurring mass gathering where the same performers come back: Three Moon in Port, plus the traveling-performer circuits Arts §10 describes. A seven-year cadence would make "who was really there" disputes generational. A yearly cadence would make them neighborly. Either way, Audience Movements gets a medium without the rejected broadcast device, and the Villain's "observational window" (Three-Moons §6) gets a population he can't model (Audience §Council).

6. **Deepwood is better written as a culture of witness than of silence.** Deepwood evidentiality ("tracks, signs, direct sight, inherited knowledge") is the most story-productive grammar in the cluster. The Blank Square uses it exactly: one square per thing a witness "saw with their own eyes", and a runner who "only carries". This counters the hush stereotype in insight 3 and should be the page's model example.

7. **Nobody owns armed force.** Weapons-and-Elite-Troops assigns one elite archetype per region: Ironcrest heavy infantry, Northwind marines, Deepwood rangers, Highridge scouts. That is the strategy-game faction-unit pattern. Kingdoms-and-Politics, by contrast, says a region can contain kingdoms, confederations and city-states. Meanwhile:
   - D24 brings in a fighters' guild that "trains militias" and ranks members by real service;
   - Port's charter limits kingdom troops;
   - Seven Hill is a fortress emptied by negotiated passage;
   - the Council works to stop Ironcrest "converting economic strength into unchecked military power".

   The page has no magic problem ("No magical 'silent bows'", "Not by… supernatural competence"). Its problem is that it has no politics. The elite-direction lines should say who recruits, who pays and who is forbidden. See Q4.

8. **Faith food rules are cited but don't exist.** Regions/Sunplains says "Wine is… forbidden or uncommon in others depending on faith". Food.md lists "religious dietary rules" as plot mechanics, and Port has "religiously specific kitchens". None of the nine faiths' Working life entries in Religions.md has a dietary rule. Fasts of silence, offerings and shared feasts are the closest. Either a local school gets a food rule (Religions allows "local schools"), or these claims should be softened to "custom". See Q5.

9. **The Latinate gods' names imply a liturgical register.** Nine faiths cross three mutually unintelligible families and share gods (Montara, Omnia, Concordia). Shared Latinate names fit a shared liturgical or scholarly register (Language-Families §18: "A religious language can be prestigious, archaic, partially understood"). Settling that would also answer Religions.md's open question of whether the gods' names follow Naming. See Q3.

10. **Mushrooms are a cross-story thread with no food owner.** Deepwood's fungal disruption hits the most mushroom-dependent cuisine (Deepwood recipes 1, 2 and 4) and Ironcrest's "mushrooms" base. Three stories already use it: the Three Pots caravan cook, the Heavy Scale cook's complaint, and The Mushrooms From Nowhere. Food.md has no "what becomes scarce" note (World Rules §12). One short section would keep the prices consistent across stories.

## Questions only the author can answer

1. **Is Three Moon yearly or every seven years, and is it "Three Moon" or "Three Moons"?** Lean: yearly and "Three Moon", as the stories already use it. Drop the seven-year cadence, and keep "nobody knows why it's called that".
2. **How did Northic and Ironet end up in one family?** Lean: leave the history open, but record that a lasting contact route existed: by sea along the western coast, or through a once-Northern Highridge. Then let one border or kin page show it.
3. **Are the gods' Latinate names a shared liturgical register, used alongside local names in each family?** Lean: yes, provisionally.
4. **After the Convergence, who fields elite troops: crowns and cities, towns hiring the fighters' guild, or both?** Lean: both. Polities keep small household and garrison forces, and the fighters' guild supplies most specialists for hire. Weapons gets rewritten to "recruited where, paid by whom".
5. **Should any faith school carry a food or drink rule, such as the Sunplains wine restriction?** Lean: yes, as a school-level rule in one or two faiths. Otherwise change "depending on faith" to "depending on custom".
