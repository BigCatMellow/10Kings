# Cluster 1 review: information systems

1. All four pages are consistent with World Rules and Magic, but none has caught up with D3, D5, D24 or the 2026-09-29 language ruling: seals, notary guilds, guild examinations, spy guilds, Port paper and stationers now exist, and the pages still list them as open or "if".
2. About 45-55% of the 11,300 words is generic inventory padding. The worst is Couriers: 43 sections, most of them one-line bullet lists.
3. Eighteen concepts are explained on two to five pages each. Ownership proposal below: Movement owns propagation, Couriers owns deliberate carriage, Records owns documents, literacy skill and archives, Education owns how skill is acquired. Couriers and Records must keep the section anchors the stories cite.
4. One live contradiction needs the author: The Eighth Verse's plan says "printing doesn't exist", but the Three Pots draft hands Jory "a single printed sheet". No owner page settles printing.
5. Best insights: the stories have built a culture of **non-written, witness-anchored records** that these pages don't model. Records **manufacture false signals** as well as preserve truth. Information failure is the device that keeps D21 working. Trade Tongue strips evidential markers, which gives Port's "amplifier" role a mechanism.

## Insights

1. **The setting's real answer to limited literacy is public, physical, witness-anchored records, and Records doesn't model them.** Evidence: the Weaver year-cloth, "one square for each thing a witness saw with their own eyes" (The Blank Square); every apprentice's name "cut into a steel pillar" (Ironheart); "a small carved sign on every project naming who oversaw it" (Masoncraft); ballads "as archives of people, crimes, disasters" (Nomads); a protest verse sung "so nobody forgets it was done to us" (The Eighth Verse); a name cut into a crate lid as a market mark (Mushrooms); sayings "carved in wood or stone" (Weaver). Change: add a Records section, "Records that aren't writing". These records can be read by people who can't read, and they are authenticated by being public and witnessed rather than by a seal. They resist forgery, but they can be erased, left unsung or refused a square. That gives stories a second evidence system beside the ledger.

2. **Records make false signals as well as keeping truth.** Movement §3F and §9 treat archives as slow, durable truth. The stories keep showing the opposite. In Three Pots, the inspector writes fines "to somebody who couldn't read the form … somebody upstairs adds up the column and says, look how many smugglers". In The Ladder Rules the footers are in no ledger. In The Blank Square, "the numbers don't agree from one ledger to the next", and guests offer to fill a witness gap from hearsay. Change: add a rule to Movement §8 or §9. When a rumor is written into a register it starts to count as verified, and an administrative count can produce its own rumor. The Villain's "leak real documents mixed with misleading conclusions" (Guilds) is this mechanism used on purpose.

3. **Paper provenance is canon now, and it has to be calibrated against D3 and D21.** Ismet, "a records man, recognizes the watermark as Port's and thinks nothing more of it". So specialists can read a stationer's mark. If they could routinely trace a mark to a buyer, someone in-world would connect the letters, which D3 forbids. Change, on Records: a watermark names a stationer or a city at most. Port stationers sell to anyone with money, so fine paper points to Port and to wealth, not to a hand. That protects C6 and The Same Paper (D25) and gives Records its missing "materials" entry.

4. **Information failure is what keeps D21 working, and these pages should say so.** Movement's Example C ("separate records from another region provide corroboration") is the very mechanism D21 bars from the stories. The stories already block it with ordinary breakdowns: a runner who can't read carries a witness book that ends up unreadable, a letter lies "folded small, and unanswered", rumors "never include a letter, a buyer, paper or a phrase". Change: add a note to Movement §15. In Sunday Morning stories, records cross regions only as bundles that nobody reads whole, and the delays and losses described in Couriers §34 are the tools. Example C belongs to the main story, not to the world stories.

5. **Trade Tongue gives Port's "amplifier" role a mechanism.** Northwind and Deepwood grammar mark the source of what you know (seen, heard from someone trusted, rumored). Highridge trade registers "distinguish one's own claim from a reported claim". Trade Tongue is a "simplified contact language" spoken by dock workers and clerks. A Northic "heard it" can come out of a Trade Tongue handoff sounding like "it is". Change: add one line to Movement §10. Rumor gains certainty when it passes through Trade Tongue hubs, and Highridge's careful registers are where it can lose that certainty again. This is a provisional design direction, not grammar canon.

6. **Education should be rebuilt from what D24 already brought in.** The Masoncraft rule that apprentices "must work in three regions before joining" is a knowledge-transmission engine, and it means credentials (§17) cross borders by design. The Illuminated Circle's mentorship "ends when the student argues against the teacher"; its scholars "go on teaching journeys to found schools". The Silent Whisper passes teachings on "only when someone is ready". The Harvestkeepers keep a shared almanac that is "revised as conditions change". Change: replace about half of Education's generic lists with these provisional specifics and their links.

7. **The Council splits the carrier from the content.** House Veynor (Routes, Port) holds the ships and caravans that carry mail. House Rhaelen (Lore, Deepwood) holds "communication", "scribes" and "market reports". Message houses (Couriers §14) sit exactly where those two seats overlap. Change: add a line to Economic-Council §6. This is a built-in friction between the two houses, in keeping with World Rules §8. It is out of story under D21.

8. **The Quill of Saint Othriel suggests a signing custom.** A relic "said to keep written agreements honest" implies contracts signed in its presence somewhere, where people watch the ink. That is authentication by belief, and it stays deniable ("bad ink"). It also creates a story pressure: a rival could supply bad ink to make a signer look like a liar. It meets Magic's "never load-bearing" rule, because the scene works if the relic does nothing. Change: one line in Records §8, provisional, linking Magic.

9. **The Overpath changes the hub model.** Movement has only two aggregation points, Port (sea) and Highridge (overland). The Overpath runs along the range, "which the pass roads cross" (D24), so every crossing is a junction where news can pass from one pass to the next without going through either hub. The legacy also calls the Underpass "the safer, faster, busier way through". Movement and Couriers frame it only as a covert bypass ("not a magic shortcut"), but The Underpass page has "public and maintained" sections and caravan lodging. Change: in Movement §5 and §12, treat routine Underpass carriage and Overpath news along the range as ordinary everyday life (D22), not only smuggling.

## Questions only the author can answer

1. **Printing:** does any exist? My lean: no movable type anywhere. Port offices keep hand-ruled blank forms, and Three Pots' "printed sheet" becomes "a blank form". Records #28 then states this, and The Eighth Verse's `VERIFIED` gets a source.
2. **Consolidation:** keep four compressed pages, or merge Couriers into Movement? My lean: keep four with strict owners, cut about 50% overall, and keep every heading a story cites.
3. **Records that aren't writing** (Insight 1): add them as a provisional Records section? My lean: yes.
4. **Watermarks:** can they show more than a stationer or a city? My lean: no, to protect D3 and D21.
5. **Crossing the Spine:** is the Underpass the routine fast route for messages crossing the range, with the Overpath slower and running along it? My lean: yes where branches are maintained, as the legacy says. Exact routes stay open.
