# Cluster 2 review: law, money and time

1. Two contradictions: Calendar calls Ledger Closing "not a fixed named holiday" while Festivals and the Goat File make it a named Highridge day; Three Moon is annual (Festivals table, Three Pots draft) but "once every seven years" on its own page.
2. D24's provisional guild and faith material (notary guilds, a measures consortium, default blacklisting, the Cyclic Order's calendar wheel, Radiant "weekly worship") has made several "open" lines on all three system pages stale.
3. The money page supports the Council's dependency power only in outline. It lacks the three mechanisms that turn lending into leverage: short renewable credit, conditions on renewal, and settlement that concentrates in a few places. The Goat File already shows all three.
4. The law page implies Convergence arbitration has no bailiff of its own. Its awards hold because credit and market access punish refusal, so the Council really is the "hidden mechanism keeping the Convergence alive", and that gives the Villain his ideal target.
5. The three provisional pages are roughly half generic lists and could be cut by about 50%. Their core rules are consistent and load-bearing enough to promote to working canon at pattern level, the label Trade already uses.

## Insights

1. **Convergence arbitration has no bailiff, and the Council fills that role.** Law §8 separates authority from enforcement. §9 says a foreign ruling matters when "merchants fear loss of credit or access". Nothing on the Convergence page enforces an award. Taken together, treaty awards are honored because credit, insurance and market access punish anyone who refuses. Those are the Council's levers (World-Rules §9). That turns the Convergence page's line, the Council "came to believe it was the hidden mechanism keeping the Convergence alive", from self-flattery into a literal description of one function. It also gives the Villain his best target: a fair award, refused by a ruler with popular backing, that only visible Council credit pressure could enforce. That is exactly the "moves the Council cannot openly stop without exposing" it (Economic-Council). This is D21-safe, because a story only ever shows "the lenders in Port".

2. **Leverage needs renewal, and the money page never says so.** Money §18 treats maturity generically. Lending gives power over a borrower only when the credit is short, has to be renewed, and comes with conditions: "They'll renew the town's credit this year only if our books are clean" (Goat File draft). The Tree is the controlled counterexample. An uncallable standing pledge gave the lender no power over the town, only a consent right over the asset. Add to Money: *short, renewable, conditioned credit is leverage; long or uncallable credit is not.* This is the missing mechanical link between Money §9–13 and the Council's "choice architecture".

3. **The continent's settlement season is late autumn.** Ledger Closing (Highridge, late autumn), Last Sail ("end-of-season settling of boats and debts", Notes/Rules.md) and Harvest Home's "public accounting of surplus" all cluster just before winter closes routes (Trade: "Autumn: harvest, storage, bulk trade and storm risk collide"). Highridge's credit year runs from Pass Opening, when the first caravan weighs in (Heavy Scale), to Ledger Closing. That is a provisional answer to Calendar's open "Highridge commercial calendar": a pass-season year. If renewals cluster in autumn, Council leverage and panic risk peak then every year, which gives the domino map a seasonal rhythm.

4. **The Council and the Villain pull the same lever.** Council tightening is deniable only because it looks like ordinary risk pricing: "No single action proves conspiracy." The Villain attacks through rumor, and Money §37 already shows rumor making lenders tighten "before the physical route actually fails". So the Council can't publicly tell its own tightening apart from a panic the Villain set off. The one thing it can do to stop a panic is lend against the risk signal (Port: "Council-linked finance attempts to prevent a credit panic"; Money §42: "which merchant failures are rescued"), and that is the one act that exposes it. Money §41 should say: *the Council is not a central bank in form; in a panic it does the one central-bank job that reveals it.*

5. **The Treasury seat sits away from both finance hubs.** Money and Trade put finance at Port and Highridge. Yet Luthain (Treasury) is an Ironcrest house, Veynor (Port) holds Routes, Torradon (Highridge) holds Works, and Drathain (Sunplains) holds mines and ore. If coin is metal (open, Money §51), coin metal passes through the Materials seat while exchange sits with Treasury. That would be a built-in Luthain–Drathain fault line the Council page doesn't list. Ironcrest's own page offers an origin: "practical literacy such as contracts, weights, measures, and records", and before the Convergence "towns changed allegiance through marriage or debt". There, debt moved sovereignty, which is a reason for rulers to fear credit. Luthain's actual origin is UNKNOWN; see Q4.

6. **Nobody owns classification, and that is a hidden power.** Law ("Violation severity and escalation") says a problem "should be classified before escalation": private, cross-border, systematic, or a direct challenge to a core guarantee. No institution does the classifying, and §11 adds that "the parties disagree about which forum has authority". Whoever succeeds in calling a border incident "Convergence-level" makes treaty retaliation legitimate. This is the gap a Villain plot will hit first. Lean: there is no standing classifier. The first forum to accept a case claims it and rivals contest it, which is forum shopping at treaty level.

7. **The Highridge trade register is the interoperability mechanism.** Calendar §6 and Money §46 look for a treaty-level way to convert dates and units. The wiki already has one: a language register with "standardized numbers, measures, dates, and contract vocabulary" (Language-and-Thought), kept by Highridge-trained clerks and weighers. That predicts where things fail: at the register's edges. The Heavy Scale's Northwind net-stone stamped "one stone" is a household substitution made outside the register. The Goat File's verbatim testimony is the register working inside it. Rule: *inside the register units are unambiguous; failures happen where it hands off to households and to non-register speakers.*

8. **Faiths keep the dates, which gives them weight as evidence.** The Heavy Scale's ledger is "dated by the shrine log". The Cyclic Order keeps "a great wheel for the calendar" and a sacred almanac. When a dispute turns on "since when", a shrine's record may be the only answer, so faiths hold real evidentiary weight without any church courts. That is consistent with Law §17, and it is a quieter channel of religious influence than mediation.

9. **No festival marks the Convergence.** Calendar lists "treaty anniversaries", and Contested-Memory predicts the settlement is remembered unevenly, yet the Festivals table has nothing for it. The nearest is the Harmonious Path's "festivals of harmony that mark … a treaty". The absence is either deliberate (the settlement is remembered as paperwork, not myth) or a gap. Either way, a Convergence day kept in Highridge and ignored in Deepwood would be ready-made story texture.

## Questions only the author can answer

1. **Is Ledger Closing Highridge's own named day, or a wider settlement season?** Lean: Highridge's named festival. Port and Northwind close their books under their own names (Last Sail), and the Calendar page says so.
2. **Is Three Moon annual or once every seven years?** Lean: annual, as the Festivals table and the reviewed Three Pots draft have it. Record the seven-year claim in the Source Register as a legacy proposal, and keep the name unexplained ("Nobody was entirely sure why it was called Three Moon"), so it commits the world to no astronomy.
3. **Is there a standing Convergence arbitration body?** Lean: no. Agreed forums sit ad hoc in Highridge halls and Port courts, with no permanent treaty court. That keeps enforcement in the credit networks (Insight 1).
4. **Where does House Luthain's financial power come from, given that its home is Ironcrest rather than a finance hub?** Lean: lending backed by metal and by Ironcrest's skill with contracts and weights. That sets up a Luthain–Drathain fault over coin metal. Keep it off the page in the stories (D21).
5. **Are the "lenders in Port" in the Goat File independent houses that clear through Luthain-linked correspondents, or Council houses themselves?** Lean: independent houses that clear through Luthain correspondents. Decide it off the page; in the stories it stays "lenders in Port".
