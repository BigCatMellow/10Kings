# Cluster 5 review: core canon

1. The cluster breaks no settled rule (magic, gods, faiths, Council). Its faults are staleness since D18/D24/D25 and heavy duplication: region pages repeat Architecture, Food, Pre-Convergence and Current Events.
2. Open Questions: 2 of 32 questions are STALE (the nomads' name, and whether they are one people), 15 are partly answered and 15 are still open. Its "Settled" list leaves out D24 and D25, and D24 opened new questions it doesn't carry.
3. Status: 16 pages have no `## Status` and 3 use non-standard labels. Proposals follow. Current Events and Villain's Dominoes should be promoted, because 14 drafted stories and the Domino Map rest on them.
4. Cross-region: the region pages, Geography and Trade never contradict each other. But region pages rarely name neighbours, and Greenvale states a Highridge border that Geography only calls "Likely". The strong adjacencies form one land chain, so the three "continents" must meet by land.
5. Best insights: Northwind is the only region with no Council house. There is no Labor seat, yet labor is the Villain's main lever. The ship's-lantern clue already points at Port. D24 makes Highridge's shift from toll warfare to negotiated passage the history's first fixed anchor.

## (c) Cross-region consistency

No contradictions. Ironcrest's edges "grade toward Greenvale, Highridge, and parts of Deepwood". That matches Geography's I–G strong, I–D used and I–H corridor. Northwind's "inland routes toward Highridge" matches Icestep. Sunplains, Deepwood and Highridge name no neighbours at all. The only overstatement is C2.

Trade agrees with the region pages:
- Ironcrest imports food and timber ("imported Greenvale staples", "forest/fuel rights").
- Northwind imports grain and metal ("imported Greenvale flour", "imported iron").
- Highridge markets import goods ("dried fruit, spices…").

Trade exports that no region page carries: Greenvale textiles, Deepwood dyes, Northwind furs.

Unstated consequences:
- Northwind imports grain, metal and medicines, but borders none of their suppliers (Greenvale, Ironcrest, Deepwood). All of it must come through Highridge (Icestep) or by sea through Port.
- Northwind is the only exporter of salt listed, and both Greenvale and Deepwood import it.

## Insights

1. **Northwind is the only region with no Council house.** The houses are Marielle (Greenvale), Drathain (Sunplains), Veynor (Port), Torradon (Highridge), Luthain (Ironcrest) and Rhaelen (Deepwood). The maritime Routes seat is held by a Port house, though Northwind has the "shipbuilding, sailors, ports" leverage (Trade). Northwind is sensitive to "outsiders buying control of harbors". One remembered tradition has Northwind controlling Port until "coalition pressure produced withdrawal and neutral status" (Contested-Memory). Taken together, that is a ready grievance: a region shut out of the system that runs its seas. It also explains why the Villain's Northwind move works ("make defensive actions look like aggression"). This changes Q1, and gives the Maris domino a motive beyond what is happening now.

2. **The Council has no Labor seat, and labor is the Villain's best lever.** Economic-Council: "The current model has no Labor seat". Drathain's labor organizer, Dorian, kept his place, but his job became Materials. Meanwhile Guilds has the Villain "encourage strikes at strategically chosen moments", Current Events opens with Ironcrest labor unrest, and Walkout Pies and Coalglen already use strikes. This is a structural blind spot: no house reads strikes as its own domain, so they are where the Council misreads first (Main-Conflict step 8, "Council misreads the adaptation"). It's worth one sentence on the Council page.

3. **The three continents must meet by land.** Every "strongly established" adjacency is a land border: N–H (Icestep pass), H–D, D–I, I–G, G–S. They link all six regions in one chain, and Northwind "occupies much of the Northern Continent". So either the continents join at the Spine/Highridge, or "continent" means something looser. This narrows the open question "Spine ↔ continental separation". It also explains why Highridge is a crossroads at all. Settle the junction before any map work.

4. **Ironet and Northic share a family with no shared border.** Language-and-Thought puts Ironcrest and Northwind in the Northern family. Geography gives them no adjacency, and the Heartland plateau speech lies between them through Icestep. The region pages' grammar fits a shared inheritance: Ironcrest's "inspected; guaranteed" and Northwind's "strong evidential markers" look like one evidential system split into craft proof and sea witness. Pre-Convergence names no migrations at all, though World-Rules §4 requires them. Story pressure: an Ironcrest smith and a Northwind captain half-understand each other at Icestep, and Highridge folk don't. The history page needs one migration or one wedge (plateau speech spreading) to explain the gap.

5. **The ship's-lantern clue already points at Port.** The C6 letters come "through the same Port stationer". D25 makes them a pattern that readers will notice in Season 2. The import's Villain "comes from Port" (Source Register §12, declined). The Villain's homeland is due before Season 3 (Long Road), and whatever is chosen must explain why his hand uses Port paper. The cheap answer works for any homeland: Port is everyone's stationer, so it's deniable. Record that so Season 2 doesn't accidentally imply he is from Port.

6. **D24 gives the history its first anchor.** Seven Hill was "emptied when Highridge's toll warfare gave way to negotiated passage". It has been a town "for generations". The Convergence chose Highridge for its "existing arbitration traditions". So negotiated passage is probably older than the Convergence, and is the prototype of its "Route rules". This orders the "Ridge Pass Toll Wars" (Pre-Convergence) before the Convergence, and possibly makes the "Highridge Consolidation War" the losing alternative. It answers Seven Hill's open "before it, or as part of it" (Q3).

7. **Greenvale distrusts exactly what its own house does.** The Seven-year Blight left "suspicion of concentrated control over seed and grain". House Marielle, from Greenvale, controls "seed networks… emergency reserves". The Eighth Verse's circles already call the buying-up of farms "coordinated theft". If Marielle's role surfaced in Greenvale, it would be read through the Blight memory as betrayal from inside, not foreign meddling. That is a strong Season-4 revelation beat. The Harvestkeepers' shared almanac and seed exchanges are the counter-institution already in place.

8. **The faith networks are a hazard under D21.** Four faiths' "current schemes" all approach the pattern. The Illuminated are "close to proving systematic manipulation". The Dual Flame "may recognize that separate crises share a source". Compass pilgrims carry information, and the Weaver documents damage. D21 says "nobody in a story sees the pattern". So the rule for Sunday Morning: the faiths collect, but never conclude, on the page. The novel can let them conclude later. Also, Rhaelen controls "commercial records; market reports", the very records the Illuminated are comparing. That gives a concrete future collision: who keeps the archive?

9. **The Deepwood logging fight is fed by Ironcrest's furnaces.** Ironcrest imports "timber/fuel"; people fear "weapon stockpiling", and stockpiling means charcoal. Deepwood faces logging "beyond accepted limits". Drathain's power "spans Ironcrest mines, Deepwood timber/fuel disputes". The Naruin domino leaks "Council-backed expansion plans". Which seat's plans those are (Materials for fuel, or Works for roads) gives two different stories. Settle it on Villain's Dominoes.

## Questions only the author can answer

1. **Is Northwind's lack of a Council house deliberate?** My lean: yes. Record why (the Port Seizure memory) and treat it as a live grievance the Villain can draw on, without making it his homeland.
2. **Are the twin suns literal?** My lean: yes, and treated as ordinary (D22). They should touch only the calendar, faith idiom and sayings. Fix "the sun's path".
3. **Did Seven Hill's garrison leave before the Convergence?** My lean: before. Highridge's negotiated passage was the model for the Convergence's route rules.
4. **Should the Current Events and Villain's Dominoes event set become Working canon, with names Provisional?** My lean: yes. Fourteen drafted stories depend on it.
5. **Is Greenvale↔Highridge a direct border?** My lean: yes. The Heartland family and the Convergence's congress site both need it.
