## Earlier fictional concepts — historical notes only

### 2024-10 recovered summary
- **Burning Sands tribes:** Desert travel, shamans, storms, oasis knowledge and trading corridors.
- **Searing Plains peoples:** Semi-mobile livestock and riding cultures; movement along grassland animal cycles.
- **Crimson Horde:** Steppe warband organized around mounted warfare and conquest.
- **Grey Riders:** Grassland herders, scouts, traders and mercenaries, with horse-associated identity; possible resistance to centralized rule.
- **Salt Flats communities:** Mobile camps and access to salt sites.

These are brainstorm concepts, **not** settled populations or canon locations. Some portrayals are reductive and need revision if revisited. The old account connected mobility, the Convergence, economic coercion, tolls, and pressures to settle. Reconcile with [The Convergence](../History/The-Convergence.md) and [Nomads](../Culture/Nomads.md) before using.

## Useful comparative questions for future worldbuilding

1. **Economic necessity:** Which grazing, fishing, labor, craft, religious, or market circuit makes movement rational?
2. **Rights and routes:** Who controls access to pasture, water, winter quarters, boat landings, repairs and markets? What customary rights endure?
3. **Kinship and institutions:** What social units travel together? Who mediates disputes? Do some relatives remain settled?
4. **Knowledge and culture:** Which music, oral histories, maps, songs, marriages, prices and tools move along routes?
5. **Material life:** How much can a household carry? How are tents, boats, food and animals maintained?
6. **Power:** How do taxation, guilds, border changes, state projects, safety and displacement change mobility?
7. **Internal variation:** Different generations, livelihoods, faiths and households cannot be treated as one uniform people.
