**Structure passes. Voice separation needs bounded correction.**

The scene already succeeds at:

- making records / warehouse recognition understandable through conflict;
- keeping Wurdren useful without making him a legal expert;
- showing the cross-guild recognition system genuinely help;
- preserving the unresolved guarantor problem for Scene 3;

The main weakness is that:

> **Wurdren, the Grain Agent, and the Records Worker all use roughly the same dry conversational rhythm.**

That creates authorial voice leakage.

---

# 1. Required voice separation

## Wurdren

### Function

Social / deflective humor.

He uses humor to:

- reduce tension;
- cover embarrassment;
- keep another person moving;
- avoid saying what he actually wants.

### Typical rhythm

- observation;
- understatement;
- mild self-deprecation;
- teasing directed at people he already has some relationship with.

### He should not

- constantly win exchanges;
- sound procedurally exact;
- speak like a clerk.

---

## Grain Agent

### Function

Cost / schedule / exposure thinking.

When stressed, she becomes **more concrete**, not funnier.

She notices:

- fees;
- delay;
- storage;
- payment;
- next contract;
- who is waiting for money.

### Typical rhythm

- short practical question;
- immediate consequence;
- recalculation.

### She should not

exist mainly to set up Wurdren's jokes.

---

## Records Worker

### Function

Category precision.

Their voice should sound like someone whose job depends on refusing apparently harmless conflations.

They distinguish:

- refusal vs complaint;
- accepted vs certified;
- recognized vs guaranteed;
- warehouse obligation vs guarantor promise.

### Humor

Rare and mostly incidental.

If a line is funny, it should be because the Records Worker says the exact procedural truth at a moment when everyone else wants simplification.

### They should not

trade quips with Wurdren for sport.

---

# 2. Specific V1 pressure points

## "I believe you"

Current sequence:

> "They refused us."

> "I believe you."

This works logically but contributes to a bantering cadence.

Better clerk voice:

> "I need the refusal."

This immediately redirects:

- testimony;
- into recordable evidence.

---

## "You had several. I have reduced them."

Good conceptual line.

Risk:

- sounds like the same dry wit as Wurdren.

Keep the idea but flatten the performance:

> "You had more than two. The warehouse part is the one I can reduce."

or simply:

> "I can reduce the warehouse problem."

The character's appeal should be competence, not punchlines.

---

## Runner exchange

The warehouse runner and Wurdren can retain more conversational humor.

That helps separate the office from Wurdren.

Wurdren's agreement:

> "It's not yours. It's also not hers."

is strong.

It shows his social competence.

---

## Sister interruption

Current:

> "Tell her I support her decision."

Too polished / comic.

Better:

> "Tell her not to wait."

This reveals:

- outside life;
- fatigue;
- priority

without another punchline.

---

# 3. Grain Agent needs more economic identity

Add / preserve moments where she thinks in consequences:

- berth charge accumulating;
- another move costs money;
- warehouse delay risks the buyer;
- missing the next contract matters;
- a copied document is valuable because losing it costs time.

This makes her more than:

> stressed customer.

---

# 4. Wurdren's scene function remains correct

Do not make him:

- catch a forged record;
- understand guarantee law;
- impress the clerk with brilliance.

His useful acts are:

- staying;
- asking what concrete thing is missing;
- retrieving it;
- not blaming the wrong person;
- resisting the hiring-board temptation.

This is already aligned with his arc.
