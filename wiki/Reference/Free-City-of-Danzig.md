# Free City of Danzig

## Status

**Research reference — not setting canon.**

This page exists because the imported Port notes explicitly say that Port's internal dynamics were developed from a **Free City of Danzig parallel**. It records the historical model separately so future work can distinguish real-world research from Two Sons canon.

## Historical snapshot

The Free City of Danzig existed from **1920 to 1939** under the post-World War I settlement.

It was neither an ordinary independent country nor simply part of Poland or Germany.

The Treaty of Versailles established Danzig as a Free City with its constitution guaranteed by the **League of Nations** and a League High Commissioner resident in the city.

At the same time, Poland received important rights tied to the city's strategic and economic role:

- Danzig was placed within the Polish customs frontier, while retaining a free area in the port.
- Poland was guaranteed access to waterways, docks, basins, wharves, and other port infrastructure needed for Polish trade.
- Poland received important rights involving the Vistula, railways, and communications.
- Poland conducted the Free City's foreign relations and protected its citizens abroad.
- Disputes between Poland and Danzig could be brought before the League's High Commissioner.
- Port administration involved institutions in which both Danzig and Poland participated.

The result was a deliberately hybrid political arrangement: Danzig possessed its own citizenship, institutions, and internal political life, while major parts of its external position and economic system were constrained by treaties and by the interests of surrounding powers.

In 1939 Nazi Germany incorporated Danzig into Germany as the European war began. The former Free City was assigned to Poland after World War II.

## Why it is useful for Two Sons

The strongest useful parallels are structural rather than cosmetic:

- **Local identity without complete sovereignty.**
- **A strategically indispensable port that larger powers cannot ignore.**
- **Economic dependence mixed with political resentment.**
- **Outside powers needing access to the city while also limiting its freedom.**
- **Treaties and administrative boards becoming part of everyday political life.**
- **Neutral or semi-neutral status that exists because competing powers cannot safely let one rival control the port.**
- **A population whose sense of belonging may center on the city itself rather than the states competing around it.**

These ideas help explain why Port can matter enormously while still having limited ability to control the systems built around it.

## Guardrails

Danzig should be treated as a **research pool, not a template to copy whole**.

The historical German-Polish ethnic conflict, Nazi politics, exact constitutional structure, chronology, institutions, and territorial circumstances should not automatically be transferred into Two Sons.

Use the historical case to understand pressures and relationships, then build Port according to Two Sons geography, history, cultures, and institutions.
