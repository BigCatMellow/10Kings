## Summary

**The political conflict passes. The proposed settlement is too institutionally specific for current canon.**

What works:

- workers are internally divided;
- owners have a real economic defense;
- priority / secrecy becomes the central question;
- the parties get close enough to compromise that Sequence 15 can meaningfully damage it.

The correction:

> do not accidentally establish a permanent Stone Hills labor-review system from one scene.

---

# 1. Keep the agreement temporary and local

V1 risks canonizing:

- standing external review;
- formal ordinary-repair floors;
- fixed overtime refusal law;
- a defined vote procedure;
- a permanent emergency-review group.

Better:

> **one temporary negotiated package for these shops / this dispute.**

Possible elements:

- owners show aggregate allocation figures to a mutually accepted small review group;
- safety inspector gets hours / stoppage data;
- a limited number of ordinary repair slots are protected during the review period;
- overtime rules are temporarily tightened;
- parties reconvene after a short interval.

No claim that this is general Stone Hills law.

---

# 2. Do not define the worker decision procedure yet

Instead of:

> vote tomorrow

use:

- worker meetings next day;
- shop delegates / representatives return with acceptance / rejection;
- exact constitutional procedure remains local / unresolved.

This avoids canonizing union voting structures.

---

# 3. Reduce the term list

The six-point board is slightly too clean.

Use four broad negotiated categories:

1. safety / hours;
2. aggregate protected-share disclosure to accepted reviewers;
3. some protected room for urgent ordinary repair;
4. fixed short review period.

That is enough.

---

# 4. Preserve confidentiality

Keep customer names confidential for now.

That is crucial because Sequence 15's true disclosure should alter the dispute.

The near-compromise must rest on:

> people accepting bounded secrecy if the amount / duration can be independently checked.

Then a leaked / released true contract can make them question whether the bounded summary was materially misleading.

---

# 5. Preserve factions

Strongest voices to retain:

- worker representative;
- priority-line worker;
- small-shop worker;
- production supervisor / owner;
- safety inspector;
- guild contract clerk.

Reduce extra anonymous speakers where possible.
