# 8. Cross-source V2 conclusions

The dense legacy material consistently supports five world rules more strongly than the first reconciliation recorded.

## Institutions contain constituencies

Not just "factions" in the abstract.

Members differ because:

- scale;
- debt;
- geography;
- class;
- professional role;
- risk;
- local autonomy;

change what the same policy means to them.

## Solutions create new politics

A solved crisis creates:

- new winners;
- new dependencies;
- new routes;
- new precedents;
- resentment;
- legitimacy.

## Information travels through ordinary work

The strongest information networks are:

- guild correspondence;
- manifests;
- contracts;
- pilgrims;
- sailors;
- merchants;
- clerks;
- festivals;
- route workers.

This supports Port / Three Moons without requiring omniscient spies.

## Ordinary competence can stop history

Legacy repeatedly includes:

- guilds stabilizing prices;
- towns rejecting rumor;
- officials comparing records;
- mediators reaching compromise.

These are now explicit counter-dominoes.

## Physical consequences make exposition unnecessary

Cargo, documents, people and prices can carry the plot between places.

The reader can see the world connect before anyone explains the connection.
