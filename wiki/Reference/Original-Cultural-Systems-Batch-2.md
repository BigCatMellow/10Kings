# Original Two Sons Institutions — Batch 2

## Status and test criteria

**PROVISIONAL DESIGN EXPERIMENTS ONLY — NOT CANON.** Eight new local, original institutional prototypes, developed from fictional problems. These are *not* adaptations of named sacred practices and do not rename historical cultures. The parallel [Obscure Social Systems Research](Obscure-Social-Systems-Batch-2.md) shows how many different mechanisms can address governance, risk, memory, apprenticeship and craft. The prototypes here start from **Two Sons constraints** and must stand alone without the external examples.

**Authority:** Before promotion confirm [World Rules](../World-Rules.md), setting geography, historical naming, and each linked wiki owner. No village, road, heraldic device, feast, law, ritual or office is established by this page.

**Hard test:** For each, find its material necessity, operating rule, costs, participants and dissenters, history, a scene where it fails, a contact pathway, and why it is not simply a new costume for a real tradition.

## 1. The Second Witness Market

**Micro-setting (unapproved):** A market ward at a river crossing, exact region unchosen.

**Recurring practical pressure:** Rumors of spoiled grain, theft and route closures spread faster than guild clerks can investigate, causing unnecessary price spikes.

**Invented institution:** People selling news about a road, crop or shipment receive no official seal. Instead, traders may pay into a common market fund to send two observers from different occupations; the findings are read publicly alongside any dissent. Reports expire after a fixed number of market days.

**Material and administrative costs:** Two wages, transport, clerical work, public reading, and the possibility of an inconclusive result.

**Participants / internal disagreement:** Small vendors benefit from credible reports, wholesalers resent delays, fishers know river conditions better than bankers, clerks fear untrained witnesses.

**Expressive culture that emerges *after* the institution:** An old market jingle repeats pairs of contradictory claims. Children learn to ask 'which witness saw the water?' rather than memorizing an official version.

**Historical cause:** Originates after a false bridge rumor caused hoarding; the first reform demanded two independently situated witnesses.

**Failure test and scene trigger:** A wealthy firm hires both observers through subsidiaries. The document is accurate but incomplete; residents discover 'independence' was not enforced.

**Generational contact / change:** Nearby fairs adopt only the expiry mark, not the public hearings. The method spreads unevenly.

**Why this is original, not a collage:** Not a copied oral trial, joking pact or divination: the practice follows **market verification cost and conflict of interest**.

**Owners to reconcile before any canonization:** [Movement](../Movement-and-Information.md), [Records](../Records-Literacy-and-Authentication.md), [Trade](../Economy/Trade-and-Dependencies.md).

## 2. The Mud-Day Debt

**Micro-setting (unapproved):** A flood-prone lowland village that trades with a road town.

**Recurring practical pressure:** Household walls and drainage berms require simultaneous repairs before heavy rain; piecemeal work sends water into neighbors' houses.

**Invented institution:** Before wet season, each property owes a quantified share of common ditch repair and trained earthwork. Workers lacking tools can contribute food, transport or childcare. A rotating inspection pair can order a dangerous breach filled immediately, but cannot change land rights.

**Material and administrative costs:** Clay, reeds, carts, tools, supervision, dispute records, time away from planting.

**Participants / internal disagreement:** Landlords try to pay their obligation and avoid being present; tenants do the actual work; skilled earthworkers demand authority to refuse unsafe shortcuts.

**Expressive culture that emerges *after* the institution:** Every wet season starts with residents comparing repair marks from the previous year. Jokes about 'dry promises' emerge from public failures, not a copied building festival.

**Historical cause:** A flood killed several livestock because one owner neglected the village's shared drain, prompting a narrowly defined obligation.

**Failure test and scene trigger:** A landlord sells to a trader who claims the repair obligation did not transfer with the land; the first rain threatens adjacent homes.

**Generational contact / change:** A migrant mason introduces a new reinforcement that works in one soil type but fails upstream in another.

**Why this is original, not a collage:** Not the Panamanian quincha meeting or a new holiday: its form derives from **runoff topology, distributed responsibility and tenant-landlord incentives**.

**Owners to reconcile before any canonization:** [Architecture](../Culture/Architecture.md), [Law](../Law-and-Jurisdiction.md), [Social Fabric](../Social-Fabric.md).

## 3. The Waiting-Shore Society

**Micro-setting (unapproved):** A coastal town with long seasonal fishing absences.

**Recurring practical pressure:** Injuries and missing boats leave households without income before officials or merchants can confirm what happened.

**Invented institution:** Crews and shore workers voluntarily contribute food, rope, repairs or small cash shares into a mutual-support store. Its rotating stewards may release emergency rations after a defined period of absence, without requiring declaration of death.

**Material and administrative costs:** Warehouse spoilage, guards, recordkeepers, replacement stocks, fraud investigation.

**Participants / internal disagreement:** Boat owners contribute more money but may receive fewer benefits; widowed households may have no surviving wage earner; an insurer-like merchant wants exclusive settlement rights.

**Expressive culture that emerges *after* the institution:** Empty seats are left at quarterly store audits—not as a mystical ritual, but so absent families can speak first. The custom later becomes debated as performative.

**Historical cause:** Created after a storm when formal inheritance rules blocked access to household provisions.

**Failure test and scene trigger:** A crew returns alive after the store has supported their households. A creditor demands repayment, and the society fears it cannot replenish stocks.

**Generational contact / change:** Itinerant workers adopt contribution certificates to use the system while staying temporarily; long-term members protest.

**Why this is original, not a collage:** Not a Barbadian Landship imitation: the organization's structure comes from **uncertain absence and delayed evidence**; no copied ranks, navy imagery or dances.

**Owners to reconcile before any canonization:** [Northwind](../Regions/Northwind.md), [Social Fabric](../Social-Fabric.md), [Law](../Law-and-Jurisdiction.md).

## 4. The Water-Step Apprenticeship

**Micro-setting (unapproved):** A steep inland valley with multiple small irrigation terraces.

**Recurring practical pressure:** Canals serve different heights and fail in different ways; a master inspector is too costly for every hamlet.

**Invented institution:** An apprentice earns credentials by tracing the entire path of one failed water delivery from intake to field, naming whose labor and rights intersect each break. Credentialing requires an upstream farmer, downstream household and independent mason to agree on factual findings—not on blame.

**Material and administrative costs:** Travel between terraces, inspection tools, days of missed farm labor, fair access to training.

**Participants / internal disagreement:** Water-right owners control access, maintainer families have practical knowledge, women and tenants may do labor yet lack formal certification.

**Expressive culture that emerges *after* the institution:** A valley proverb praises the apprentice who knows whose roof got wet before the canal looks dry. Regional outsiders misread it as a joke about bad plumbing.

**Historical cause:** A canal dispute exposed that the inspector knew construction but not the distribution agreements.

**Failure test and scene trigger:** A correctly repaired channel now floods a new migrant settlement that never entered the older allocation map.

**Generational contact / change:** A traveling engineer teaches measurement tools; the apprenticeship retains the rule that maps alone are insufficient.

**Why this is original, not a collage:** Not an aflaj/subak replica: it centers on **tracing social consequences of a specific hydraulic failure**.

**Owners to reconcile before any canonization:** [Food](../Culture/Food.md), [Education](../Education-and-Knowledge-Transmission.md), [Law](../Law-and-Jurisdiction.md).

## 5. The Price-of-Silence Hearing

**Micro-setting (unapproved):** A multi-language merchant quarter in Port or another major city.

**Recurring practical pressure:** Warehouse foremen know which walls, loads and cranes are unsafe but lose work if they publicly challenge wealthy operators.

**Invented institution:** A mixed labor-and-craft panel hears anonymous descriptions of danger, buys a limited inspection from a qualified expert and publishes a red/amber/clear mark with an expiry date. It cannot prosecute; it can refuse guild labor at listed sites.

**Material and administrative costs:** Inspection fees, panel time, confidentiality, exposure to retaliatory blacklists.

**Participants / internal disagreement:** Owners contest the panel's authority; poor laborers disagree about refusing urgently needed work; outside merchants use competing certifications.

**Expressive culture that emerges *after* the institution:** Workers say a dangerous warehouse 'has paid too much for quiet'. In taverns the phrase becomes part of a sarcastic game of guessing who financed a new facade.

**Historical cause:** Started when a collapsed storehouse injured paid workers and no official clerk had accepted their warnings.

**Failure test and scene trigger:** Two panels issue contradictory inspections; the Council-linked lender accepts the cheaper certificate.

**Generational contact / change:** Port interpreters adapt the warning marks for people who cannot read the local legal language; copycats in other cities omit confidentiality.

**Why this is original, not a collage:** Not a copied royal accountability trial or humor pact: it evolves from **labor retaliation and inspection incentives**.

**Owners to reconcile before any canonization:** [Guilds](../Politics/Guilds.md), [Law](../Law-and-Jurisdiction.md), [Records](../Records-Literacy-and-Authentication.md).

## 6. The Borrowed-Bottom Boat

**Micro-setting (unapproved):** A lake and canal community with seasonal shallow waters.

**Recurring practical pressure:** A single boat shape serves open water well but becomes stuck during low water or cannot carry certain loads.

**Invented institution:** Hull owners keep interchangeable, repairable shallow-water fittings shared through a registry. Boatbuilders certify which fittings and loads are safe; seasonal owners pool spare parts, but only in exchange for maintenance labor.

**Material and administrative costs:** Storage sheds, trained joiners, exact measurements, timber stock and the risk of mismatched parts.

**Participants / internal disagreement:** Boatbuilders favor standardized work; owners protect proprietary hull designs; small fishers cannot afford certification fees.

**Expressive culture that emerges *after* the institution:** Local speech distinguishes 'a fine boat' from 'a boat that knows its water'—a distinction first coined by a frustrated repair apprentice.

**Historical cause:** The registry begins after a flood cut off the normal timber route and replacement parts had to be shared.

**Failure test and scene trigger:** One shared fitting causes a hull failure. Owners dispute whether an incompetent joiner or outdated measurements were responsible.

**Generational contact / change:** A traveling boatmaker brings a faster hull profile, but the local registry accepts it only after seasonal trials.

**Why this is original, not a collage:** Not a moliceiro, clinker boat or batana under another name: the institution arises from **modularity, shallow-water variability and risk allocation**.

**Owners to reconcile before any canonization:** [Northwind](../Regions/Northwind.md), [Architecture](../Culture/Architecture.md), [Movement](../Movement-and-Information.md).

## 7. The Three Winters of a Charter

**Micro-setting (unapproved):** A former border market with overlapping pre-Convergence obligations.

**Recurring practical pressure:** Three adjacent polities once granted different privileges to the same market families; records survived unevenly through war.

**Invented institution:** Instead of declaring one definitive history, the municipal office displays three authenticated but contradictory charter excerpts with documented provenance. A current dispute must identify which jurisdiction and period govern the particular contract. Public speakers can challenge the application, but cannot rewrite the source.

**Material and administrative costs:** Scribes, archives, translation, authentication, guarding originals and slow hearings.

**Participants / internal disagreement:** Old families benefit from some interpretations; migrants lack inherited paperwork; former rulers' agents prefer their own legal reading.

**Expressive culture that emerges *after* the institution:** A common insult—'you have three winters for one debt'—comes from the length of the first case and changes meaning over time.

**Historical cause:** The method develops after a merchant forged a unified charter that never existed to claim exclusive toll rights.

**Failure test and scene trigger:** An old charter is genuine, but land changed hands during an unrecorded migration. The court must decide whether current claims outweigh historic title.

**Generational contact / change:** Itinerant storytellers produce competing ballads about the founding case; neither is accepted as legal proof, but both affect public opinion.

**Why this is original, not a collage:** Not a copied sacred charter or royal ritual: its identity follows **contradictory documents, a contested border and legal pluralism**.

**Owners to reconcile before any canonization:** [History](../History/Contested-Memory.md), [Law](../Law-and-Jurisdiction.md), [Records](../Records-Literacy-and-Authentication.md).

## 8. The Orchard Forecast Table

**Micro-setting (unapproved):** A farming district where several crops share one dangerous frost window.

**Recurring practical pressure:** Small growers have good local weather knowledge but no single household can afford to lose an early harvest entirely.

**Invented institution:** Growers meet at a practical communal table to record forecasts with confidence bands, then voluntarily distribute early harvest and firewood commitments across different fields. At season's end, they score which forecast methods worked, without punishing all wrong calls.

**Material and administrative costs:** Meetings, storage, transport, risk of collusion or free riding, lost opportunity from conservative estimates.

**Participants / internal disagreement:** Established orchard owners have data; tenant families have fine-grained observations but less bargaining power; merchants prefer one official date.

**Expressive culture that emerges *after* the institution:** A harvest story makes fun of the famously confident elder whose first forecast was wrong, while acknowledging that later corrections saved crops.

**Historical cause:** Created after a single year of identical planting dates caused simultaneous losses and emergency migration.

**Failure test and scene trigger:** A trader pays the best forecaster to withhold a warning; collective trust collapses despite technically accurate records.

**Generational contact / change:** Seasonal laborers carry methods into other watersheds; some adopt scoring without the pooled reserve.

**Why this is original, not a collage:** Not a magical calendar or seasonal rite: the institution develops from **forecast uncertainty and correlated agricultural risk**.

**Owners to reconcile before any canonization:** [Food](../Culture/Food.md), [Education](../Education-and-Knowledge-Transmission.md), [Trade](../Economy/Trade-and-Dependencies.md).

## Challenge review

These are **hypotheses, not approved new lore**. Strongest mechanisms to test against current geography and plot: Second Witness Market (rumors and verified information), Waiting-Shore Society (missing crew and aid), Three Winters of a Charter (contested memory and law). The weakest geography fit may be Borrowed-Bottom Boat until canal/lake hydrology, timber supply and shipbuilding technology are confirmed.

**Review questions:**
- Does an existing institution already perform this function? Then repair or distinguish rather than duplicate.
- Does the proposal require wealth, literacy, mobility or legal authority that the community cannot plausibly obtain?
- Is 'who pays' explicit, including excluded households?
- Is there a faction opposed to the custom for a comprehensible reason?
- What evidence would make the prototype invalid?
- Is there a human-scale scene and a third-order economic consequence?
- Is the expressive layer original to repeated local work and disagreement, not borrowed from research examples?

No named prototype should enter a canonical owner's factual prose without an explicit selection/reconciliation decision. Cross-reference [Original Culture Generation Method](Original-Culture-Generation-Method.md) and [Source Register](Source-Register.md).
