# Settlement Site-History Vertical Slices — V1

## Status

**Provisional site-history tests — not final settlement canon.**

These tests exist because [Settlement Naming Vertical Slices — V1](Settlement-Naming-Vertical-Slices-V1.md) found that SH-M, HR-C, and OC-U were not ready for local names.

They use:

- [Architecture](../Culture/Architecture.md)
- [Geography and Connections](../Geography-and-Connections.md)
- [Legacy Settlement Seed Bank](Legacy-Settlement-Seed-Bank.md)
- [Working Political Geography — V1](Working-Political-Geography-V1.md)

The target is narrow:

> **Establish the mundane physical reason each settlement existed before later political / economic importance accumulated.**

---

# 1. SH-M — river crossing before industrial city

## Existing constraints

Stone Hills supports:

- valleys cut by streams / rivers;
- old mining / metalworking centers;
- towns that can combine mine, farming, crossing, and repair functions;
- layered urban cores around mines or river crossings.

SH-M must eventually support:

- major mines;
- workshops;
- guilds;
- civic government;
- labor politics.

## Alternatives considered

### Mine-first settlement

A town founded directly at a mine mouth.

**Weakness:** makes the city too dependent on one industrial cause and encourages an industrial-theme name.

### Fortress-first settlement

A defensible hill / fort becomes city.

**Weakness:** current SH-M politics is more strongly economic / civic than military.

### Crossing-first settlement

A durable river crossing / lower-valley market predates large mining expansion.

Later ore routes and workshops accumulate around it.

**Best fit.**

---

## V1 site history

SH-M begins as a **crossing settlement at a constricted valley river**, where:

- ordinary local traffic can ford / bridge the stream;
- valley farming routes meet upland paths;
- water is reliable enough for settlement;
- nearby slopes contain later-exploited ore.

### Stage 1 — crossing settlement

Early functions:

- river crossing;
- small market;
- local farming / herding exchange;
- repair / lodging.

### Stage 2 — mining expansion

New or expanded mines in side valleys / slopes increase:

- ore traffic;
- charcoal demand;
- labor migration;
- repair work.

The old crossing becomes the natural transfer / market point.

### Stage 3 — workshop city

Waterpower where suitable, transport, labor, and existing markets support:

- furnaces;
- workshops;
- warehouses;
- guild halls.

Industrial districts expand uphill / along route corridors.

### Stage 4 — civic consolidation

The old crossing core remains:

- market;
- civic / legal center;
- bridge / river authority.

Industrial wealth does not erase the older town.

---

## Urban consequence

SH-M should physically contain at least two historical logics:

### Old crossing core

- bridge / ford approaches;
- market;
- older civic / religious buildings;
- dense mixed commerce.

### Later industrial districts

- mine roads;
- workshops;
- yards;
- warehouses;
- worker housing;
- guild property.

This helps explain why civic authority and industrial ownership remain distinct.

---

## Naming consequence

The oldest settlement name should most naturally derive from:

- crossing;
- river;
- nearby older lineage / farm;
- valley feature.

It should **not** need to mean:

- iron;
- forge;
- smoke;
- black;
- bellow.

A later industrial district / commercial nickname can carry craft meaning if history earns it.

**PASS.**

---

# 2. HR-C — spring before crossroads

## Existing constraints

High Roads settlements cluster around:

- water;
- route junctions;
- markets;
- caravan yards.

HR-C later becomes important for:

- route switching;
- arbitration;
- records;
- multilingual meetings.

The naming framework warns against giving it a grand crossroads / summit name merely because it later becomes important.

---

## Alternatives considered

### Junction-first

People settle simply because several roads meet.

**Weakness:** roads need a reason to converge at a useful stopping place.

### Fort-first

A fortress forces roads together.

**Weakness:** risks making later arbitration / neutrality an extension of military power.

### Water-first

A reliable spring / small spring-fed basin creates a natural stopping point.

Routes converge because travelers and animals need water.

**Best fit.**

---

## V1 site history

HR-C begins around a **reliable upland spring / spring-fed hollow** on a route across otherwise exposed country.

### Stage 1 — water stop

Travelers and herders use:

- spring;
- nearby pasture;
- sheltered ground.

A small permanent community maintains access / lodging.

### Stage 2 — route convergence

Several paths increasingly choose the same stop because:

- water is reliable;
- animals can recover;
- terrain offers onward choices.

A periodic market develops.

### Stage 3 — permanent market / town

Infrastructure accumulates:

- animal yards;
- storage;
- repair;
- lodging;
- cistern / water management;
- scales.

### Stage 4 — arbitration reputation

Because multiple routes and political communities already meet there:

- disputes are common;
- interpreters are available;
- records accumulate;
- neutral / reputable mediators gain work.

Arbitration is therefore an outcome of traffic, not the founding myth.

### Stage 5 — political prestige

Recurring multi-route meetings favor HR-C because:

- people already know how to get there;
- records / interpreters exist;
- it is politically important but not sovereign over the others.

---

## Naming consequence

The oldest local name should most naturally derive from:

- spring;
- hollow / basin;
- local household;
- nearby pasture / hill.

Later commercial forms may reference:

- market;
- meetings;
- crossroads.

The local name should **not** need to mean:

- summit;
- grand crossroads;
- capital;
- high throne.

**STRONG PASS.**

---

# 3. OC-U — terrace / perennial water before upstream power

## Existing constraints

OC-U:

- sits in upper / middle S1 system;
- controls or influences headworks / diversions;
- has old civic sovereignty;
- contains estates / client territory;
- participates in irrigation politics.

Old Cities architecture and settlement can grow around:

- springs;
- canals;
- gates;
- markets;
- defensible / flood-safe sites.

---

## Alternatives considered

### Headworks-first city

City founded specifically to control a canal gate.

**Weakness:** makes the city too obviously engineered around its later political role.

### Fort-first city

Old fort becomes civic center.

**Possible**, but current water history is more structurally important.

### Water-and-terrace-first

Early settlement occupies a **low safe terrace above normal flood reach beside a perennial source / river point suitable for later diversion**.

**Best fit.**

---

## V1 site history

OC-U begins as a settlement where:

- reliable water is accessible;
- a terrace / raised ground reduces flood risk;
- nearby soils support cultivation;
- routes can cross / follow the valley.

### Stage 1 — early permanent settlement

The site supports:

- household water;
- farming;
- local market;
- defensible / flood-safe dense settlement.

### Stage 2 — managed diversion

Growing population develops / expands:

- channels;
- gates;
- storage;
- maintenance institutions.

### Stage 3 — hinterland expansion

Water infrastructure increases:

- orchard / field value;
- estate growth;
- rural dependency on city-backed works.

### Stage 4 — city-state sovereignty

The city becomes politically durable because:

- it controls a dense civic core;
- its hinterland depends on infrastructure / markets;
- it can field force / collect tax.

### Stage 5 — cross-border inheritance

Political borders later divide a water system whose older infrastructure extends toward OC-D.

OC-U's upstream leverage is therefore inherited from geography / history, not designed by the modern state.

---

## Naming consequence

The oldest city name can plausibly derive from:

- spring / water source;
- terrace / raised ground;
- old founder / lineage;
- nearby pre-urban settlement.

A later civic title may emphasize:

- antiquity;
- sovereignty;
- headworks.

The root city name should not need to mean:

- upstream;
- sun;
- glory;
- irrigation capital.

**STRONG PASS.**

---

# 4. Cross-test result

The three sites now have distinct origin logics.

## SH-M

**crossing → mining / industrial accumulation**

## HR-C

**water stop → route convergence → arbitration**

## OC-U

**safe water settlement → irrigation expansion → civic sovereignty**

This is important because later political importance does not become retroactive destiny.

---

# 5. Naming readiness

These sites are now ready for **local lexical-name generation**.

The next naming test should generate small candidate sets from the established semantic source only.

## SH-M semantic source

Primary candidates should encode one of:

- crossing / ford;
- river;
- valley-place / old settlement.

## HR-C semantic source

Primary candidates should encode one of:

- spring;
- spring hollow / basin;
- older household at the water source.

## OC-U semantic source

Primary candidates should encode one of:

- perennial spring / water source;
- raised terrace;
- founder / older settlement at that site.

No candidate should be accepted merely because it sounds good.
