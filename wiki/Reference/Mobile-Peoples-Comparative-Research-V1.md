**External comparative research — not setting canon.**

Purpose:

> ground the first three 10Kings mobile circuits in specific real-world mechanisms without copying whole cultures.

Use with:

- [Nomads and Itinerant Peoples](../Culture/Nomads.md);

# 1. Research rule

For every source tradition distinguish:

- **source-derived mechanism**;
- **10Kings application**;
- **explicit non-borrow**.

No real-world people should become a one-to-one fantasy analogue.

# 2. Appalachian oral tradition — family memory, ballads, story continuity

## Sources

Library of Congress / American Folklife Center:

- https://guides.loc.gov/folktales-oral-storytelling/Appalachian-storytelling
- https://guides.loc.gov/folktales-oral-storytelling/jack-tales-magic-tales
- https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2025/03/caught-my-ear-ballads-and-childrens-songs-by-eunice-yeatts-mcalexander/

Smithsonian Center for Folklife and Cultural Heritage:

- https://folklife.si.edu/masters-of-tradition/virtuosic-women

## Source-derived mechanisms

The collections document:

- strong family transmission of ballads / songs;
- storytelling repertoires preserved across generations;
- migration of older British / Irish tale traditions into Appalachian communities and their local transformation;
- songs carrying:
  - memory;
  - work experience;
  - social criticism;
  - family history.

Jean Ritchie, for example, learned a very large song repertoire from her extended family.

The Library of Congress collections also preserve:

- belief stories;
- tall tales;
- ghost stories;
- riddles;
- music;
- local oral tradition.

## 10Kings application

Useful for the favored Appalachian-influenced mobile network:

- family / kin memory carried through song;
- work stories becoming historical memory;
- routes remembered through verses / stories;
- old events surviving without formal archives;
- individual families having different versions of the same ballad.

### Important limit

Appalachian communities are **not being used as a nomadic model**.

The useful source mechanisms are:

- oral history;
- mountain / route storytelling;
- family repertoire;
- work / class memory.

Mobility must come from other real mobile traditions and from 10Kings economics.

## Do not borrow

- Appalachian ethnicity;
- dialect caricature;
- poverty stereotypes;
- one-to-one music style;
- "hillbilly nomad" imagery.

# 3. Irish Traveller tinsmithing — portable repair economy

## Sources

Ireland's National Inventory of Intangible Cultural Heritage:

- https://nationalinventoryich.ccs.gov.ie/traveller-tinsmithing/

National Museum of Ireland:

- https://cms.museum.ie/en-IE/Collections-Research/Folklife-Collections/Folklife-Collections-List-%281%29/Trades-and-Crafts/The-tinsmith
- https://source.museum.ie/en-IE/Collections-Research/Collection/Traveller-Culture

## Source-derived mechanisms

Traveller tinsmithing historically used:

- small portable anvils / hammers / rivets;
- sheet tin;
- mobile production;
- repair of household / farm containers;
- travel to fairs and settlements;
- barter as well as cash;
- intergenerational transmission.

The National Museum notes that traveling tinsmiths:

- returned repeatedly to communities;
- made / repaired household objects;
- could repair clocks, umbrellas, ceramics, and metal wares;
- were valued not only for goods / repair but also for bringing **news**.

The craft's mobility worked because:

> the tools and workplace could travel with the practitioner.

## 10Kings application

Strong mechanisms for a repair circuit:

- compact toolkits;
- repeated customer stops;
- annual / semiannual return;
- repair economy stronger than new-goods economy in some places;
- information carried incidentally through trusted work relationships;
- household specialization by craft.

This can pair naturally with:

- Appalachian-derived oral memory;
- local 10Kings guild systems;
- seasonal fair / festival traffic.

## Do not borrow

- Irish Traveller ethnic identity;
- Traveller language;
- exact tinsmith craft as the sole livelihood;
- stereotypes historically imposed on Traveller communities;
- living ceremonial / family traditions.

# 4. Central European itinerant tinkers — repair, migration, multilingual skill

## Source

House of European History:

- https://historia.europa.eu/en/digital-offer/throwaway-history-modern-crisis/everyday-life

## Source-derived mechanisms

The museum describes itinerant tinkers from economically weaker areas of the Danube Monarchy who:

- traveled widely to repair goods;
- developed regional occupational specialization;
- migrated far beyond their home areas;
- used multilingual ability as an economic advantage;
- worked in a repair economy where household objects were expensive enough to justify repeated mending.

The same source notes the contradiction:

- the work was useful and in demand;
- the workers could still be socially marginalized / stereotyped.

## 10Kings application

Useful for:

- repeat service circuits;
- repair extending object life;
- multilingual mobile households;
- being welcomed economically but distrusted socially;
- fixed home communities plus long absences;
- craft identity changing when cheap replacement goods / guild centralization threaten the old niche.

## Do not borrow

- Slovak / Danubian ethnicity;
- 19th-century stereotype;
- historic legal regime.

# 5. Itinerant journeyman system — travel as professional formation

## Source

Germanisches Nationalmuseum:

- https://zunft-und-handwerk.gnm.de/en/apprentice-and-master

## Source-derived mechanisms

Selected European craft traditions required / encouraged trained craftspeople to travel after apprenticeship in order to:

- learn outside the master's household;
- encounter new techniques;
- gain experience;
- prove competence.

Travel could therefore be:

> **a stage of professional formation**

rather than a permanent ethnic lifestyle.

## 10Kings application

This should probably **not** become one of the three mobile peoples.

Instead it can intersect with them.

Guilds may have:

- traveling apprentices / journeymen;
- temporary work exchanges;
- professional host networks.

That creates people who are mobile without belonging to a mobile culture.

Important distinction.

# 6. Qashqai and Iranian transhumance — summer / winter quarters plus cultivation

## Source

Encyclopaedia Iranica:

- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/asayer-tribes/

## Source-derived mechanisms

Iranica describes transhumant groups, including Qashqai examples, moving between:

- **winter quarters**;
- **summer quarters**.

Movement could coexist with:

- farming at winter grounds;
- farming at summer grounds;
- seasonal crop schedules.

This is important because pastoral mobility does not imply:

> no agriculture / no fixed places.

## 10Kings application

Useful for a High Roads-linked pastoral circuit:

- two or more deeply important seasonal bases;
- crops / rights at one or both;
- movement shaped by pasture and temperature;
- people returning to the same places across generations.

This fits the current Nomads owner rule:

> mobility can create deep attachment to place.

## Do not borrow

- Qashqai tribal organization;
- clothing;
- language;
- Iranian political history;
- exact livestock mix.

# 7. Rabari — adaptive route choice and permanent villages

## Sources

Indira Gandhi National Centre for the Arts:

- https://ignca.gov.in/rabaris-the-pastoral-community/
- https://ignca.gov.in/rabari-a-pastoral-community-of-kutch/

Sahapedia:

- https://www.sahapedia.org/adapting-changing-landscapes-rabari-migration-strategies-across-generations
- https://www.sahapedia.org/the-rabaris-the-nomadic-pastoral-community-of-kutch

## Source-derived mechanisms

Research materials describe:

- permanent villages occupied for part of the year;
- different community members sometimes moving differently;
- seasonal herd movement;
- migration patterns changing with:
  - rainfall;
  - water;
  - pasture;
  - political access;
  - land tenure;
  - irrigation;
  - markets.

Sahapedia describes route decisions as active productive strategy, not automatic ancient wandering.

It also describes changing bargains with farmers as agricultural systems changed.

## 10Kings application

Strong mechanisms:

- part of household travels, part remains;
- route changes are politically negotiated;
- fixed settlement can coexist with mobile herd economy;
- mobility can be altered by irrigation / land-use development;
- seasonal access may depend on exchange with farmers.

This prevents the pastoral circuit from becoming a static map loop.

## Do not borrow

- Rabari identity;
- embroidery as decorative shorthand;
- origin myths;
- gender roles one-to-one;
- exact livestock / geography.

# 8. Fulani / Wodaabe and Sahelian pastoralism — mobility as flexible system

## Sources

FAO Pastoralist Knowledge Hub:

- https://www.fao.org/pastoralist-knowledge-hub/pastoralist-networks/regional-networks/west-and-central-africa/en
- https://www.fao.org/pastoralist-knowledge-hub/news/detail/Securing-transboundary-mobility-for-sustainable-pastoralism/en

FAO livestock / pastoral studies:

- https://www.fao.org/4/x5539e/x5539e06.htm
- https://www.fao.org/4/t6260e/t6260e03.htm
- https://www.fao.org/4/y2647e/y2647e02.htm

## Source-derived mechanisms

FAO materials emphasize:

- mobility follows changing pasture / water / disease conditions;
- pastoral categories are flexible rather than cleanly fixed;
- transhumance can cross political borders;
- household camps can be more important than broad ethnic categories for actual movement;
- herds / households may split;
- movement links to:
  - markets;
  - crop residues;
  - water;
  - disease avoidance;
- access restrictions can create serious conflict;
- modern governance may use:
  - transhumance certificates;
  - pastoral codes;
  - cross-border committees.

## 10Kings application

This is particularly useful for legal design.

A pastoral circuit should have:

- negotiated access;
- some predictable crossings;
- alternate routes;
- household / herd splitting;
- political conflicts when corridors narrow;
- relations with settled farming communities;
- mobility treated as an economic system rather than cultural eccentricity.

## Do not borrow

- Fulani / Wodaabe identity;
- religious history;
- exact kin / clan system;
- Sahel ecology wholesale.

# 9. Banjara caravan transport — mobile freight as livelihood

## Sources

Geetika Gupta, "Modes of Transportation in Early Medieval Western India" (2025):

- https://doi.org/10.1177/23484489251321644

Additional comparative research:

- https://www.srmap.edu.in/news/colonial-impact-on-pastoral-nomads-and-caravan-traders-in-india/

## Source-derived mechanisms

The research describes historical Banjara groups as:

- itinerant cattle breeders;
- major inland goods carriers;
- organized in moving camps / caravans;
- transporting bulk goods between ecological / commercial regions;
- sometimes operating under hereditary or elected caravan leadership.

The key economic point is:

> mobility itself was freight infrastructure.

## 10Kings application

Excellent model for a **mobile freight people / network** distinct from merchants.

They need not own the goods.

Their expertise can be:

- animals;
- loading;
- route timing;
- camp organization;
- security;
- finding fodder / water;
- moving bulk commodities.

This gives High Roads / continent trade a human logistics layer.

## Do not borrow

- Banjara identity;
- language;
- caste / colonial history;
- clothing;
- religious traditions.

# 10. Persian / Central Asian caravan organization — temporary moving institutions

## Sources

Encyclopaedia Iranica:

- Caravan:
  - https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/caravan
- Commerce / road infrastructure:
  - https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/commerce-iv/

## Source-derived mechanisms

Historical caravan organization could involve:

- groups of pack animals managed in subunits;
- a caravan leader responsible for:
  - route;
  - guards;
  - provisions;
  - lodging;
- different travel timing depending on ecology;
- caravanserais / stopping infrastructure;
- roadside water / service provision;
- public and private support for route infrastructure.

## 10Kings application

Useful for distinguishing:

### caravan

A temporary moving organization.

from:

### caravan-service people

Households / specialists who repeatedly:

- lead;
- load;
- supply;
- repair;
- handle animals;
- broker lodging.

A merchant can hire into the system without culturally belonging to it.

That distinction should survive.

# 11. Research synthesis — three different mobility logics

The research strongly supports separating three mobile systems.

## Circuit A — portable service / repair

Mobility solves:

> many settlements need specialized work, but not enough to support a permanent specialist everywhere.

Research mechanisms:

- Traveller tinsmithing;
- itinerant tinkers;
- portable craft;
- repeated customers;
- news exchange;
- Appalachian family oral tradition.

## Circuit B — transhumant pastoralism

Mobility solves:

> animals / households need different ecological zones at different seasons.

Research mechanisms:

- Qashqai summer / winter bases;
- Rabari adaptive routes;
- Fulani / Wodaabe flexible transhumance;
- split households / herds;
- access negotiation.

## Circuit C — freight / caravan service

Mobility solves:

> bulk trade needs expert moving infrastructure between regions.

Research mechanisms:

- Banjara freight caravans;
- Persian / Central Asian caravan organization;
- High Roads route systems.

These are different economies.

They can:

- intermarry;
- overlap;
- share camps;
- exchange songs / stories;
- use the same fairs;

without becoming one people.

# 12. Culture / legend consequence

The three systems should carry folklore differently.

## Repair circuit

Best carrier of:

- Road Hound;
- songs;
- local ghost / work stories;
- stories collected from customers and changed by family repertoire.

## Pastoral circuit

Best carrier of:

- Weather Animal;
- Road Hound;
- pasture / water / seasonal creature lore.

## Freight circuit

Best carrier of:

- Night Road;
- route omens;
- Port / North Coast / High Roads story exchange;
- distorted stories from far-apart markets.

This gives myth transmission a material basis.
