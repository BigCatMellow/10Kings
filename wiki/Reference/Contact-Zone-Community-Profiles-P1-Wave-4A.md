# 1. CZ-1 — Stone Hills ↔ Low Rivers foothill labor-market community

## Formation

The strongest working history:

~~~text
older foothill farming / market settlements
→ nearby mine / quarry / workshop demand grows
→ households add seasonal wage / haulage / repair work
→ pre-Convergence rulers contest the corridor
→ Convergence stabilizes a political boundary across an older labor / market zone
→ cross-border household economy persists
→ legal / tax / guild workarounds become local institutions
~~~

The modern border is therefore **real politically** but not culturally primordial.

## Livelihood

Households may combine:

- grain / orchard / livestock work;
- mine / quarry seasonal labor;
- haulage;
- repair;
- market trade;
- food provisioning;
- workshop subcontracting.

The key cultural fact is:

> one household can depend on both agricultural and industrial calendars.

## Household rhythm

A household might contain:

- one person doing farm work most of the year;
- another taking seasonal mine / transport work;
- older relative managing animals / household accounts;
- young person apprenticed in a cross-border craft town;
- relative boarding near a mine during peak work.

This creates regular partial separation without making the household nomadic.

## Institutions that are specifically contact-zone

Possible locally evolved needs:

- wage / debt witnesses recognized on both sides;
- market brokers who understand both measurement systems / contract styles;
- remittance carriers;
- seasonal lodging tied to mine labor;
- mixed guild / informal repair recognition;
- marriage / inheritance specialists for property divided by the political border.

Do not canonize all as formal offices.

Some can simply be trusted professions / families.

## Food

This is an ideal place for culturally blended work food.

Not:

> Stone Hills stew + Low Rivers bread.

Instead:

- Low Rivers grain / vegetables adapted into portable mine / road meals;
- Stone Hills preservation / hearty work methods applied to Low Rivers ingredients;
- food sold specifically to seasonal laborers.

After generations, locals may not think of the food as mixed.

It is simply foothill food.

## Language / speech

Likely:

- bidialectal households;
- occupational vocabulary crossing the border;
- contract / measure terms borrowed unevenly;
- children understanding one side's speech better than grandparents did.

Political officials may call one form foreign while local speakers do not.

## Internal divisions

- full-time farmer vs wage labor household;
- guild-recognized skilled worker vs casual seasonal worker;
- people with land on one side / work on the other;
- people benefiting from clearer border law vs people burdened by tax / licensing;
- old local families vs newer mine migrants.

## Mythic oicotype

**Knocking Below** changes here.

Mine district version:

> underground warning.

Foothill version may become:

> warning from ground people have opened / worked too deeply.

Possible applications beyond mine shafts:

- well collapse;
- old quarry;
- subsidence;
- abandoned working beneath pasture.

A farming household can preserve the story without identifying culturally as Stone Hills.

## Current-event vulnerability

Stone Hills strategic demand raises:

- seasonal wages;
- food demand;
- rent / boarding;
- labor competition.

Low Rivers abundance collapse simultaneously lowers:

- farm income.

Therefore the same household may experience:

> cheap grain + better mine wage + worse farm solvency.

This is an excellent cross-system crisis lens.

## P1 result

**STRONG PASS.**

This community is already behaving like a third culture.

# 2. CZ-2 — Longwood ↔ High Roads forest-pass community

## Formation

A pass / road skirts or crosses forested highland where several systems overlap:

- Longwood use rights;
- High Roads passage;
- pastoral access;
- route maintenance;
- guide work;
- timber / firewood;
- bridge / road materials.

The settlement grows because people repeatedly need help translating among those systems.

## Livelihood

Possible mix:

- guide work;
- pack-animal support;
- lodging;
- forest products;
- small cultivation;
- seasonal grazing;
- bridge / road repair;
- timber / fuel supply;
- trade.

The same household can hold:

- passage-related work;
- one gathering right;
- one grazing agreement;
- one market stall.

## Institutions that are specifically contact-zone

Strong local mechanisms:

### route custodians

People responsible for:

- one bridge;
- pass segment;
- detour;
- winter marker system.

Their authority is about **the route**, not surrounding forest ownership.

### local-right witnesses

People who know:

- which community can cut where;
- which travelers may gather fuel;
- where grazing is allowed.

### guide / interpreter households

Understand:

- High Roads contracts;
- Longwood permissions;
- weather / terrain.

This is more than language translation.

It is **jurisdiction translation**.

## Household rhythm

Different seasons emphasize:

- route traffic;
- pasture;
- forest work;
- winter maintenance.

A household may host:

- travelers;
- seasonal herders;
- related Longwood kin.

Crowding / hospitality are ordinary economic questions.

## Food

This zone should produce food that suits:

- travelers;
- cold / wet route conditions;
- forest ingredients;
- High Roads dairy / grain;
- imported salt / market goods.

Some portable traveler foods may originate locally because the forest-pass environment rewards them.

## Internal divisions

- route merchants vs local-right holders;
- pastoral users vs road wideners;
- guides wanting maintained routes vs forest users opposing clearing;
- locals whose income depends on travelers vs people burdened by them;
- people identifying more with Longwood institutions vs High Roads markets.

## Mythic oicotype

This is a prime **hybrid legend zone**.

Possible local tradition combines:

- Forest Owner:
  - wrong use / wrong path;
- Road Hound:
  - guidance / threshold;
- Weather Animal:
  - route danger / weather.

Do **not** collapse all three objectively.

The local culture may instead tell a story in which:

> a dark animal appears at the fork people should not take when the forest / weather has made the route unsafe.

Outsiders debate whether this is:

- Hound;
- Weather Animal;
- Forest Owner in animal form.

Locals may consider the distinction pointless.

That is exactly the desired texture.

## Current-event vulnerability

Road expansion / security pressure can force a choice among:

- wider cleared corridor;
- local cutting rights;
- grazing;
- forest drainage / fire risk;
- military / convoy access.

A solution that is good High Roads logistics can be bad Longwood land use.

## P1 result

**VERY STRONG PASS.**

This may be one of the best contact-zone examples in the setting.

# 3. CZ-3 — Low Rivers ↔ Old Cities water-market corridor

## Formation

The strongest model is a corridor of:

- irrigated farms;
- canal settlements;
- market gardens;
- road / river markets

near the Low Rivers / Old Cities political-cultural transition.

Some water / labor / market relationships predate the current border.

## Livelihood

Possible:

- intensive vegetables / fruit;
- grain;
- canal maintenance;
- market transport;
- seed / nursery work;
- seasonal labor;
- food processing.

The zone is more market-connected than a typical isolated irrigation village.

## Household structure

Households may contain:

- land / water rights recognized under one polity;
- work / market relationships in another;
- relatives across border;
- migrant canal worker who settled permanently;
- children speaking a mixed local form.

This creates real legal complexity.

## Contact-zone institutions

### cross-recognized water witnesses

Not necessarily officials.

People whose testimony is trusted about:

- old shares;
- maintenance;
- flow history.

### canal / market brokers

Translate among:

- Low Rivers cooperative practices;
- Old Cities formal water records;
- market contracts.

### mixed maintenance crews

A canal segment may physically require workers from both jurisdictions.

That produces repeated contact independent of politics.

## Food

This is one of the strongest places for culinary bleed.

Potential mechanism:

~~~text
Low Rivers fresh / abundant produce
+
Old Cities oils / drying / seasoning / market techniques
+
canal-worker / market labor schedule
→ local dishes neither side considers imported anymore
~~~

This directly matches the user's earlier rule:

> salted fish from one culture + fresh vegetables from another can become a new local dish.

## Language / naming

Water terminology may be especially hybrid because people need precision.

A local speaker may use:

- one language's term for legal share;
- another's for physical gate;
- a third borrowed merchant term for measured volume.

Outsiders may find the speech sloppy.

Locals may find it precise.

## Internal divisions

- older water-right households vs new market growers;
- Low Rivers cooperative affiliates vs Old Cities-style record formalists;
- upstream local users vs downstream local users;
- households with cross-border land vs purely local households;
- migrants who maintain old food / marriage practices vs second-generation locals.

## Mythic oicotype

Strongest hybrid so far:

Low Rivers Marsh Lure:
- light follows lost / shifted water.

Old Cities Charter Beast:
- remembers rightful flow / boundary.

Contact-zone story:

> **a moving light appears where water has been diverted from the course / share it remembers.**

Possible interpretations:

- ghostly creature;
- methane / reflection;
- warning of unauthorized gate change;
- old children's story;
- legal metaphor.

This oicotype could later acquire its own name.

## Current-event vulnerability

Old Cities drought pressure + Low Rivers abundance creates a tempting policy:

- export more Low Rivers produce south;
- redirect / intensify water;
- expand market gardening.

That may enrich some households while:

- raising local water conflict;
- changing crops;
- increasing labor migration;
- making the border corridor politically sensitive.

## P1 result

**VERY STRONG PASS.**

# 4. Third-culture test

## CZ-1

Could it exist without calling itself Stone Hills or Low Rivers?

**YES.**

Its defining structure is:

> farm + mine labor + border law.

## CZ-2

**YES.**

Its defining structure is:

> route custodianship layered across forest / pasture rights.

## CZ-3

**YES.**

Its defining structure is:

> cross-border water / market corridor.

All three now pass the rule:

> **contact produces a third thing.**

# 5. Shared contact-zone traits that should NOT become stereotypes

Several mechanisms recur:

- bilingual / multi-register speech;
- cross-border kin;
- legal translation;
- mixed food;
- institutional ambiguity.

Do not conclude:

> all border people are cosmopolitan / tolerant / flexible.

Contact zones can also produce:

- resentment;
- exclusion;
- hard local identity;
- suspicion of both centers;
- opportunistic switching;
- intense arguments over who is a "real local."

Cultural mixture does not imply social harmony.
