# 1. Borderland mechanism — older ties can predate modern borders

## Comparative case — Maramureș / Banat borderlands

### Source

European Review of History:

- https://doi.org/10.1080/13507486.2020.1747403

### Source-derived mechanisms

The research describes borderland regions containing:

- multiethnic populations;
- different class / occupational groups;
- overlapping religious / kin networks;
- economic ties that did not map neatly onto later state boundaries.

When new borders were imposed, they could:

- cut existing social ties;
- redirect trade;
- create new centers / elites;
- turn ordinary cross-regional relationships into "cross-border" ones after the fact.

### 10Kings application

This is extremely useful for the post-Convergence world.

A contact community can plausibly say:

> **the border is newer than our market / marriage road / pasture agreement / workshop relationship.**

Therefore cultural mixture need not begin after the Convergence.

The Convergence may instead have **formalized a political line across an older social landscape**.

This is a strong general rule for:

- CZ-1;
- CZ-2;
- CZ-3.

### Do not borrow

- ethnic composition;
- Habsburg / post-WWI politics;
- military-border institutions;
- exact religions / languages.

# 2. CZ-1 — Stone Hills ↔ Low Rivers foothill

## Comparator A — Upper Silesian mining culture

### Sources

Upper Silesian State Museum:

- https://oberschlesisches-landesmuseum.de/en/startseite-oslm-english/museum/collection/

Coal Mining Museum, Zabrze:

- https://muzeumgornictwa.pl/en/

### Source-derived mechanisms

The museum sources document a region where:

- mining transformed daily life;
- mining heritage coexisted with wider local cultural traditions;
- households / communities cannot be understood from workplace alone;
- musical, family, gendered, religious, and neighborhood memories grew around industrial work.

The broader borderland history of Upper Silesia also makes it useful as a reminder that:

> industrial culture and rural / regional identity can coexist in a politically contested borderland.

### 10Kings application

CZ-1 should not look like:

> Stone Hills miners moved into Low Rivers farms.

A stronger system:

- old foothill farms;
- small mines / workshops;
- seasonal mine labor;
- Low Rivers produce / market systems;
- Stone Hills craft / guild systems;
- families with members in both economies.

One household can be:

- farmer in one season;
- mine / haulage worker in another;
- repair household year-round.

This makes "mining" and "farming" household strategies rather than ethnic identities.

---

## Comparator B — border-making cuts economic systems

Using the Maramureș / Banat comparative mechanism:

CZ-1 may have existed as a foothill market / labor zone **before** the current SH-A / LR-A boundary stabilized.

The Convergence could have:

- made customs / law clearer;
- protected routes;

while also creating new questions:

- Which court hears a debt?
- Which polity taxes seasonal mine wages?
- Does a farm inheritance cross the border?
- Is a guild credential valid on the other side?

### 10Kings consequence

The community can develop specialists in:

- bilingual / bidialectal contracts;
- cross-border marriage;
- wage remittance;
- produce / tool exchange.

### Cultural outputs

Strong candidates:

- Low Rivers foods adapted for mine / road work;
- Stone Hills repair traditions applied to agricultural tools;
- hybrid work songs / vocabulary;
- household calendars built around both harvest and mine demand.

### Myth consequence

**Knocking Below** can enter a farming population through seasonal mine work.

But farmers may reinterpret it as:

- warning about ground / wells / old workings

rather than a mine-creature story.

### Research result

CZ-1 has a strong comparative mechanism:

> **mixed household economy + border formalization over older labor / market ties.**

# 3. CZ-2 — Longwood ↔ High Roads forest-pass community

## Comparator A — Madriu-Perafita-Claror Valley

### Source

UNESCO World Heritage Centre:

- https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1160

### Source-derived mechanisms

The valley contains / historically contained overlapping systems of:

- high pasture;
- wooded lower valley;
- summer settlements;
- terrace cultivation;
- communal land management;
- tracks / communication routes;
- ironworking evidence.

The key mechanism:

> **mountain pasture, forest use, agriculture, route movement and extraction can occupy the same landscape without belonging to one social institution.**

### 10Kings application

CZ-2 should sit where:

- High Roads route / pasture systems;
- Longwood cutting / gathering / local-right systems

overlap.

A pass community may therefore contain disputes over:

- road clearing;
- grazing;
- timber;
- bridge material;
- firewood;
- seasonal camps;
- guide access.

No single "forest people vs road people" divide.

The same household might use:

- High Roads market;
- Longwood timber right;
- seasonal grazing;
- guide work.

## Comparator B — Qhapaq Ñan local road custodianship

### Source

UNESCO World Heritage Centre:

- https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1459

### Source-derived mechanisms

The Qhapaq Ñan research documents:

- a long-distance road network crossing highly varied ecology;
- towns / villages / rural areas linked by route infrastructure;
- local communities continuing to safeguard road segments and associated cultural traditions.

Useful mechanism:

> **a continental route can be locally maintained / interpreted.**

### 10Kings application

CZ-2 can have families / communities who are neither:

- central High Roads authority

nor

- generic Longwood wardens,

but whose local status comes from knowing / maintaining:

- one pass;
- one bridge;
- one forest-road segment;
- one seasonal detour.

This gives **guide / route custodianship** a believable local basis.

### Jurisdictional consequence

A route compact may recognize:

- passage / maintenance rights.

Longwood communities may separately control:

- cutting / gathering / nearby water.

Therefore:

> **authority over the road does not imply authority over the forest beside it.**

This is a contact-zone version of Longwood's strongest rule.

### Myth consequence

This zone is ideal for hybridization among:

- Forest Owner;
- Road Hound;
- Weather Animal.

Example mechanism:

- Longwood story says something turns violators around;
- High Roads travelers say a dark hound refuses the wrong fork;
- local contact-zone version gradually treats them as one warning tradition.

Do not decide yet whether they are "really" the same creature.

### Research result

CZ-2 has a strong mechanism:

> **route custodianship layered over forest / pasture rights.**

# 4. CZ-3 — Low Rivers ↔ Old Cities canal / migrant community

## Comparator A — Huerta de Valencia irrigation landscape

### Sources

Valencia cultural heritage / Water Court:

- https://cultural.valencia.es/en/patrimonio-cultural/water-court-unesco-world-heritage-site/

Universitat Politècnica de València:

- https://riunet.upv.es/handle/10251/233001

### Source-derived mechanisms

The Huerta material supports:

- irrigation communities tied to actual water systems;
- dispersed farms / settlement linked by:
  - canals;
  - roads;
  - crops;
  - local institutions;
- specialized irrigation dispute / representative structures.

The key contact-zone mechanism:

> **an irrigation landscape can connect people across settlement forms more strongly than a simple urban/rural divide.**

### 10Kings application

CZ-3 could lie where:

- Low Rivers agricultural / migrant traditions;
- Old Cities canal institutions

meet along a practical water / labor system.

People may move seasonally or permanently for:

- canal work;
- market gardening;
- construction;
- harvest;
- maintenance.

This can create mixed neighborhoods / villages where:

- Low Rivers household / food practices;
- Old Cities water law / urban market practice

combine into something local.

---

## Comparator B — Al-Ahsa oasis as agricultural + commercial landscape

### Source

UNESCO World Heritage Centre:

- https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1563

### Source-derived mechanisms

Al-Ahsa demonstrates a large human-created water landscape connecting:

- springs / wells;
- canals;
- gardens;
- villages;
- urban markets;
- agriculture;
- trade.

Its usefulness here is the systems relationship:

> **water, settlement, agriculture and commerce form one evolving landscape.**

### 10Kings application

CZ-3 should not be a neat border village where two cuisines mix.

It can be a **water-market corridor** where:

- produce moves toward Old Cities markets;
- Low Rivers labor / seed / storage practices move south;
- Old Cities legal / canal expertise moves north.

Repeated practical cooperation can produce:

- shared maintenance days;
- mixed contract forms;
- multilingual / multi-register water vocabulary;
- marriages / households spanning political jurisdictions.

---

## Border-formalization consequence

Using the general borderland mechanism:

The modern LR-A / Old Cities political boundary may be newer than:

- some canals;
- family land;
- road / market relationships.

This creates recurring questions:

- which polity recognizes a water share?
- can a household inherit fields across the boundary?
- which court recognizes a canal-maintenance obligation?
- do drought restrictions travel across the border?

### Myth consequence

This is the strongest **Marsh Lure ↔ Charter Beast hybrid zone**.

Possible evolution:

Low Rivers:
- light follows forgotten water.

Old Cities:
- creature remembers diverted / stolen water.

Contact-zone version:
- **a moving light marks where water was taken from its rightful course.**

That is a true oicotype:

- not copied whole from either source tradition;
- generated by local water politics.

### Research result

CZ-3 has a strong mechanism:

> **shared irrigation / labor / market systems crossing a newer political-cultural boundary.**

# 5. Cross-contact-zone findings

## A. Borders can create cultures by interrupting older ones

A contact zone is not always:

~~~text
culture A
+
culture B
→ mixture
~~~

It may be:

~~~text
older social / ecological system
→ political border imposed / recognized
→ old ties become cross-border
→ people invent new legal / cultural workarounds
→ distinct border culture
~~~

This is particularly appropriate in a post-Convergence setting.

## B. Contact zones need their own institutions

Strong candidate institutions include:

- cross-border market brokers;
- interpreters;
- dual-recognized witnesses;
- route / water specialists;
- seasonal labor hosts;
- marriage / inheritance intermediaries.

Do not invent all of these everywhere.

## C. Hybrid myth should follow hybrid life

The best cross-zone creature variants emerge from actual overlap:

- CZ-1:
  - mine warning enters farm / foothill life;
- CZ-2:
  - forest boundary + road guidance + weather;
- CZ-3:
  - marsh light + legal water memory.

This is much stronger than adding a new "border monster."

## D. Border identity can become primary

After generations, a person may describe themselves first by:

- valley;
- market town;
- pass;
- canal district

rather than by one regional cultural label.

That does not erase wider affiliations.
