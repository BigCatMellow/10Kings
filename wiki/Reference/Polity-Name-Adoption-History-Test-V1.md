# 1. Daroa → LR-A

## Starting facts

Daroa is the provisional G1 hydronym.

The river:

- predates LR-A;
- crosses much of LR-A's basin;
- rises toward High Roads / uplands;
- reaches Port's special jurisdiction;
- therefore is not wholly controlled by LR-A.

LR-A's political consolidation nevertheless follows much of the central Daroa basin.

---

## Possible naming paths

### A. Seat-derived state name

Still possible, but no central seat is yet developed strongly enough to justify it.

### B. Dynasty-derived state name

Possible, but ruler / succession remains intentionally open.

### C. Basin-derived political name

A broad geographic label based on the Daroa becomes increasingly administrative.

This requires no new institution.

**Strongest fit.**

---

## V1 adoption sequence

~~~
Daroa — old river name
→ Daroa basin / Daroa lowlands — geographic speech
→ tax / route / court records use Daroa as a practical district reference
→ consolidation brings much of that basin under one territorial authority
→ outsiders increasingly use Daroa as shorthand for the polity
→ state and river share a name without implying identical boundaries
~~~

This is historically ordinary.

A river-derived state name does not logically claim every mile of the river.

Context distinguishes:

- the Daroa — river;
- Daroa — polity / territory.

---

## Political friction

The shared name can still create rhetoric.

Port or High Roads speakers may mock LR-A claims that sound like:

> Daroa policy

when the subject concerns water outside LR-A's jurisdiction.

The state can be river-named without owning the river legally.

That tension is useful rather than disqualifying.

---

## Result

> **RALEN — PASS AS WORKING COMMON-LANGUAGE NAME FOR LR-A.**

Status remains provisional.

Exact local formal state style, ruler title, and capital remain open.

# 2. Auvor → NC-M

## Starting facts

Auvor is the provisional principal mainland political harbor.

NC-M formed through recurring coordination among:

- harbors;
- territorial authorities;
- clans / kin networks;
- shipowners;
- defense / convoy institutions.

A simple seat-named kingdom would understate that history.

---

## Possible naming paths

### A. State simply called Auvor

Possible but risks implying the harbor absorbed all others through ordinary territorial centralization.

### B. Old mainland geographic name

Possible, but no such older label is established.

### C. Compact name based on meeting place

Repeated multi-harbor agreements become associated with Auvor because major negotiations / records occur there.

The political order later becomes more state-like while retaining the historical compact label.

**Strongest fit.**

---

## V1 adoption sequence

~~~
Auvor — old sheltered harbor
→ recurring defense / rescue meetings held at Auvor
→ "Auvor agreement / compact" becomes foreign and legal shorthand
→ later maritime coordination becomes more durable
→ mainland territorial consolidation grows around the compact institutions
→ outsiders use the inherited compact name for the polity
→ internal jurisdictions continue to remember that the name originally described an agreement, not ownership by the city
~~~

This is exactly the kind of institutional fossil Writing the Sequel favors.

---

## Working common-language form

> **the Auvor Compact**

This is a **translated/common-language political label**, not necessarily the local constitutional title.

Ordinary speech can shorten it to:

> **Auvor**

depending on context.

### Why retaining "Compact" is useful

It encodes political history.

Even if the modern polity is stronger than the original compact:

- islanders;
- harbor autonomists;
- centralizers

can disagree over what the word still means.

Centralists may treat it as an inherited name.

Autonomists may treat it as constitutional evidence.

---

## Result

> **KELAN COMPACT — STRONG PASS AS WORKING COMMON-LANGUAGE NAME FOR NC-M.**

Auvor remains the central city.

The Compact remains provisional and does not decide monarchy / elective / mixed executive structure.

---

# 3. Varel → SH-A

## Starting facts

Varel is the provisional ruling seat.

SH-A is a composite territorial polity built by:

- inheritance;
- marriage;
- protection;
- debt;
- negotiated incorporation;
- limited warfare.

Older jurisdictions retain identities and privileges.

A polity name that implies all territory was originally one unit would conflict with that history.

---

## Possible naming paths

### A. New territorial country name

Requires an invented old territory we do not need.

### B. Dynasty name

Plausible, but ruling house remains open.

### C. Court / seat metonymy

Foreigners and central administrators increasingly refer to the composite authority by the seat from which orders are issued.

**Strongest current fit.**

---

## V1 adoption sequence

~~~
Varel — older valley-junction seat
→ one ruling jurisdiction centered there expands
→ incorporated towns / districts keep local names
→ diplomats say "the court at Varel"
→ records shorten this to "Varel" when referring to central policy / authority
→ outsiders increasingly use Varel for the polity
→ some internal jurisdictions still distinguish "Varel" from their own territorial identity
~~~

This makes the political name a **metonym**, not proof of cultural uniformity.

---

## Working common-language form

> **Varel**

Possible usages:

- Varel envoy;
- Varel levy;
- Varel court;
- Varel forces.

But a person from Kavel need not say:

> I am Varel

as their primary local identity.

They may mean:

> Varel is the state / court we belong to.

This supports layered identity.

---

## Formal-name caution

The actual formal polity style may later include:

- ruler title;
- house;
- legal territorial formula.

Do not invent it yet.

---

## Result

> **VAREL — PASS AS WORKING COMMON-LANGUAGE POLITICAL METONYM FOR SH-A.**

Varel remains both seat name and foreign/common political shorthand.

That is intentional.

---

# 4. Three different adoption paths

The resulting political names now encode different state histories.

| V1 actor | Working common name | Adoption mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SH-A | **Varel** | ruling-seat metonym for a composite state |
| NC-M | **the Auvor Compact** / Auvor | old meeting / compact label retained by a maritime state |
| LR-A | **Daroa** | river / basin geographic name becomes political shorthand |

This is a strong anti-symmetry result.

The names do not merely sound different.

They become state names for different historical reasons.

---

# 5. Political consequences of the naming history

## Varel

Internal localists can object to centralizing language:

> Varel is the court, not the whole west.

That statement can be politically meaningful.

## Auvor Compact

Autonomists can cite the word **Compact** as evidence that the center is fundamentally an agreement among jurisdictions.

Centralizers can call that anachronistic.

## Daroa

The government can derive legitimacy from basin coordination while critics point out:

- Port;
- upstream users;
- neighboring water institutions

also belong to the river system.

The name naturally produces arguments about the difference between geographic identity and sovereignty.

All names on this page remain **provisional working names**.

- surrounding-name tests do not expose repetitive / implausible morphology;
