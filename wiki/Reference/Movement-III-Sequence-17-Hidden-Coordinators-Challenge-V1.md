---

# 1. Keep the intervention multi-domain, but make results mixed

The Council's distinct advantage is precisely:

> finance + storage + routes + information can be coordinated together.

Do not reduce that to one single lever.

But V2 should show:

- one released storage block quickly rebooked by a large buyer;
- staged credit mostly helps buyers with clean records / recognizable counterparties;
- smaller / stranger buyers remain excluded;
- transport windows ease one bottleneck but create preferred access elsewhere;
- public water data weakens extreme rumor in some markets and is dismissed as political reassurance in others.

The Council genuinely reduces the feedback loop.

It does **not** normalize the whole system.

---

# 2. Add one blind spot

The Council should discover after acting that:

> at least one Old Cities buyer was purchasing early because of a separate political guarantee / domestic instruction not captured in ordinary market logic.

This can seed Sequence 18.

They thought:

> drought anxiety + storage fear.

Part of the behavior was also:

> emerging guarantee identity.

That keeps them from being the author.

---

# 3. Make legitimacy discomfort less thesis-like

V1's Finance representative is close to articulating the full legitimacy critique.

Better:

- a house clerk asks:
  > "Who approved taking the loss?"
- Finance answers:
  > "We did."
- the clerk means:
  > which customer / principal?
- Finance realizes:
  > the private meeting itself became authorization.

Keep the discomfort concrete.

Do not give them the final philosophical diagnosis yet.
