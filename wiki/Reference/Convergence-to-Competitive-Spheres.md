# Convergence to Competitive Spheres

## Status

**Reference / historical synthesis — provisional.**

This page asks a specific question:

**How could the history of the Convergence plausibly produce the present WWF/WCW-style rivalry?**

It does not promote the legacy polity names below into current canon. They are evidence and candidate mappings to be reconciled with the live world.

## Working conclusion

The strongest current model is **not** east versus west and not two individual kingdoms.

It is a rivalry between two historical-political ecosystems:

1. **The Charter / Convergence establishment** — the older recognized centers and institutions whose legitimacy, borders and influence were stabilized by the Convergence.
2. **The Ascendant / revisionist network** — cities, guild powers, secondary states, patrons and ambitious rulers whose real wealth and influence grew under the post-Convergence order faster than their formal status did.
3. **Port** — outside the binary: the ECW/Danzig third space where both ecosystems trade, recruit, imitate, compete and encounter new people and ideas.

The names **Charter Sphere** and **Ascendant Sphere** are descriptive placeholders, not final in-world names.

This model is not good side versus bad side.

Both contain people who benefit from stability, people protecting privilege, reformers, opportunists, workers, patrons and internal rivals.

## The key historical irony

The Convergence may have created **both** competitors.

It stabilized:

- borders;
- recognized governments;
- trade routes;
- resource agreements;
- arbitration;
- Port neutrality;
- guild access across domains.

That gave older recognized centers a durable institutional advantage.

But the same peace also allowed:

- secondary cities to grow;
- guilds to expand across borders;
- merchant fortunes to compound;
- specialists to move farther;
- city-states to compete for talent and prestige;
- local powers to become richer than their old constitutional position implied.

So the later rivalry can arise from a **status gap**:

```text
Convergence freezes a workable political settlement
→ peace and trade create new wealth
→ some secondary actors grow faster than the old settlement
→ economic importance no longer matches formal prestige
→ challengers coordinate across borders
→ established powers defend the system that recognizes them
→ recruitment, patronage and prestige become substitutes for open war
```

This is a stronger historical cause than simply deciding that one half of the map dislikes the other.

## What pre-Convergence history tells us

The old sources repeatedly show **temporary coalitions around concrete interests**, not permanent civilizational camps.

Examples preserved in the legacy material include:

- North Coast clans obtaining Stone Hills metal support during maritime conflict;
- Old Cities orchard powers financing Low Rivers resistance to an Stone Hills expansion;
- Stone Hills and Old Cities interests backing different High Roads pass-lords;
- Old Cities patrons changing forging partners when circumstances changed;
- merchant and guild interests pressuring rulers toward stability;
- famine, routes, ore, fishing access and tolls changing alliances.

The important inheritance is therefore a political habit:

**actors already know how to cooperate with distant partners against a nearby rival when their interests overlap.**

The modern spheres can grow from that behavior without requiring ancient permanent alliances.

## What the Convergence changes

The Convergence transforms the competitive environment.

Before it, power could be expressed through:

- raids;
- temporary occupation;
- toll seizures;
- private armies;
- crop or canal sabotage;
- shifting border claims.

After it, those tools become more expensive politically.

Competition increasingly moves into:

- recruitment;
- guild charters;
- patronage;
- trade privileges;
- court appointments;
- infrastructure;
- festivals;
- scholarship;
- public works;
- immigration incentives;
- commercial standards;
- prestige.

This is exactly the environment needed for the WWF/WCW analogy to work.

The rivalry becomes a contest over **who can attract the people and institutions that define the future**.

## Sphere A hypothesis — the Charter establishment

### WWF structural role

This sphere best matches the **established competitor under pressure**.

Its power comes from accumulated legitimacy:

- old capitals;
- recognized borders;
- treaty institutions;
- established guild relationships;
- inherited diplomatic procedures;
- control of important public systems;
- the assumption that serious people traditionally pass through its institutions.

Its weakness is that established status can turn into complacency.

When important people leave for the rival sphere, the loss is embarrassing precisely because this side assumes it should be the natural center.

### Strongest legacy candidates

These are candidate relationships, not promoted canon.

| Actor / institution | Historical signal | Working fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Summithold / central High Roads institutions** | High Roads proposes or hosts the Convergence; arbitration, routes and consensus become central to the new order | **Strong Charter core candidate** |
| **Verdanthearth central authority** | large organized agricultural state; friendly toward Summithold and Aurorashore; food stability is central to the settlement | **Strong Charter partner candidate** |
| **Aurorashore / North Coast Clanhold leadership** | recognized maritime participant; old source gives friendly ties with Verdanthearth; Convergence regularizes maritime access | **Charter partner, but internally loose** |
| **Stone Hills Sovereignty** | one of the largest established powers and a major resource pillar of the settlement | **Institutional beneficiary, but likely internally contested / possible hinge** |

Stone Hills should not be forced wholesale into one side yet.

Its crown, major guilds, industrial towns and Blackbellow already have enough conflicting interests that **different Stone Hills actors may align differently**.

### WWF-style pressure cycle

```text
old Charter institutions hold prestige
→ Ascendant patrons offer better terms to prominent people
→ visible defections make the old centers look stale
→ Charter leaders initially rely on established names and procedures
→ financial / political pressure grows
→ younger local people receive opportunities they previously would not
→ some new figures become more important than the defectors
→ the establishment changes because competition forces it to
```

That is the useful WWF mechanism.

## Sphere B hypothesis — the Ascendant network

### WCW structural role

This sphere is not necessarily anti-Convergence.

Its strongest grievance is more specific:

**the Convergence stabilized yesterday's hierarchy, while the world created by the Convergence has produced new centers of wealth and influence.**

Its members can argue that they obey the rules, generate prosperity and employ important people but remain subordinate to capitals and institutions whose status was fixed generations earlier.

That gives them a coherent reason to coordinate.

### Strongest legacy candidates

| Actor / institution | Historical signal | Working fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cliffhaven** | explicitly attempts to undercut Summithold's toll dominance | **Very strong Ascendant candidate** |
| **Blackbellow Consortium** | wealthy guild-run city-state; higher wages attract workers; tense with Stone Hills crown | **Very strong Ascendant candidate** |
| **ambitious Old Cities city-states / patron networks** | long tradition of city competition, merchant wealth, prestige contests and external patronage | **Strong source of money, spectacle and recruitment** |
| **Dunespire / Zalhar-type independent Old Cities powers** | suspicion of central Solanterra authority and guarded independence | **Possible Ascendant members** |
| **Whitepeak-type North Coast elite** | explicitly torn between outside industrial ties and inherited clan politics | **Excellent swing / divided-alignment example** |
| **smaller High Roads, Low Rivers and North Coast powers** | several legacy polities resent regional capitals, tolls, tribute or centralization | **Peripheral or issue-specific recruits** |

The exact named polities remain legacy candidates until reconciled.

### Why this can become WCW-sized

At first glance this sphere looks like a collection of secondary powers.

That may be its origin rather than its mature state.

Its combined resources can be formidable:

- Blackbellow-style industrial money;
- Old Cities patron and merchant wealth;
- secondary ports;
- ambitious courts;
- guild networks;
- famous defectors from Charter institutions;
- specialists developed in Port;
- cities frustrated by old toll and treaty arrangements.

A later major-state patron or defection could transform a loose challenge into a peer competitor without requiring that the sphere begin as one giant kingdom.

### WCW-style pressure cycle

```text
secondary powers become rich under Convergence peace
→ they coordinate to bypass old centers
→ they recruit famous Charter figures
→ each defection proves the new network is "where the future is"
→ wealthy patrons spend heavily on prestige
→ imported stars gain political leverage
→ homegrown members resent being blocked
→ internal patrons and famous incumbents become hard to control
→ success creates bureaucracy and faction lock-in
```

That is the useful WCW mechanism.

## Port — the third space

Port remains outside both.

Its combined structural role is:

- **Danzig:** strategically necessary, politically constrained, externally contested;
- **ECW:** culturally independent, experimental, intensely local, influential beyond formal size.

Under the sphere model, Port becomes:

- neutral meeting ground;
- recruitment market;
- talent nursery;
- cultural laboratory;
- intelligence market;
- site of proxy patronage;
- place where defectors can disappear and re-emerge;
- place where both spheres encounter things neither created.

Port does not need to support either sphere consistently.

Different Port neighborhoods, guilds, patrons and institutions can lean in different directions.

## The most useful present-day fault line

The rivalry may therefore be less:

> Which kingdom is stronger?

and more:

> **Does legitimate power come from the institutions that built and preserved the Convergence, or from the people and institutions that have become important since it was signed?**

That question can appear everywhere without becoming an explicit ideology.

A guild master might support the Ascendant sphere because old charters restrict expansion.

A farmer may support Charter institutions because standardized grain contracts protect the village.

A young engineer may leave a Charter capital because Blackbellow offers a laboratory and twice the pay.

A noble may publicly defend the Charter while privately financing Ascendant projects.

A Old Cities patron may bankroll an Ascendant festival while relying on Charter arbitration for international contracts.

This is a rivalry of **status, access and future direction**, not two ethnic camps.

## Actors that should probably resist clean alignment

### Longwood

Current and legacy material make Longwood suspicious of outside extraction and centralization.

That does not automatically make it a Charter or Ascendant member.

Different communities could cooperate with whichever side offers stronger ecological guarantees.

Longwood is more useful as a place where both spheres discover that their normal incentives do not map cleanly onto local priorities.

### Tunnels

The Tunnels can remain transactional and internally divided.

Control of a route may matter more than sphere loyalty.

### Religions

Major faiths are trans-regional.

Treating them as sphere-owned would weaken the social model.

Specific orders, bishops, schools or local temples may lean one way without the faith as a whole doing so.

### Port

Port remains deliberately outside the binary.

## Present-day pressures that naturally intensify the rivalry

Current events already supply mechanisms:

- **Stone Hills labor unrest:** Blackbellow-style competitors can recruit workers, engineers or organizers.
- **Low Rivers abundance crisis:** rival finance and storage networks can offer competing rescues.
- **North Coast convoy politics:** Charter maritime rules can collide with independent harbor interests.
- **High Roads caravan attacks:** Summithold and Cliffhaven-style route systems can compete over diverted traffic.
- **Old Cities coordination:** attempts at city-state cooperation are already interpreted as possible bloc formation.
- **Port merchant conflict:** both spheres have incentives to influence finance, insurance, warehouses and prominent Port figures.

The Villain therefore does not need to invent the rivalry.

He only needs to make each sphere read ordinary crises as evidence that the other is gaining ground.

## A possible historical sequence

This chronology is a **working synthesis**, not canon chronology.

### Phase 1 — fragmented coalition politics

Pre-Convergence powers cooperate temporarily around ore, food, fisheries, passes and local wars.

### Phase 2 — exhaustion

Repeated conflict fails to create durable gains.

Guilds, merchants and ordinary populations push for predictable routes and borders.

### Phase 3 — Convergence

The major centers achieve recognized status.

Trade and guild barriers fall.

Port neutrality strengthens.

### Phase 4 — unexpected winners

Peace disproportionately benefits some actors that were not dominant when the settlement was negotiated:

- secondary trade cities;
- guild-run industrial centers;
- merchant families;
- ports;
- specialist schools;
- cultural patrons.

### Phase 5 — status conflict

Those actors demand privileges, representation and access proportionate to their actual importance.

Established powers resist surrendering advantages that they see as part of the settlement that keeps the world stable.

### Phase 6 — sphere formation

Individual disputes begin linking together.

A fight over High Roads tolls, an Stone Hills labor market and a Old Cities patronage dispute stop looking separate.

Participants increasingly understand them as part of the same competitive ecosystem.

### Phase 7 — present rivalry

By the opening story, people recognize two broad spheres even though nobody can draw a clean border around either.

## Candidate genesis event

The legacy history contains a provisional **Merchant Guild War** in the post-Convergence period.

That is worth investigating as a possible historical hinge.

It could become the moment when rulers first realized that cross-border economic actors were capable of forming a political alignment independent of regional governments.

Do **not** promote the old event unchanged yet.

The useful question is whether a better-developed historical episode can explain:

1. why several challenger cities and guilds learned to coordinate;
2. why the established capitals began treating that coordination as a systemic threat;
3. why Port remained useful to both;
4. why the rivalry became cultural and prestigious rather than simply another war.

## Current provisional alignment model

```text
                CHARTER / ESTABLISHMENT SPHERE
          recognized capitals + treaty institutions
                /          |          \
      established guilds  courts   route systems
                    \       |       /
                     \      |      /
                       PORT
                 ECW + DANZIG
             culture / trade / talent
                     /      |      \
                    /       |       \
         wealthy challengers  free cities  patrons
                 ASCENDANT SPHERE
```

Crucially, lines cross the diagram.

A Charter kingdom can contain an Ascendant city.

An Ascendant patron can depend on Charter courts.

A guild can split.

A family can have members on both sides.

A whole polity can drift.

## What is strong enough to keep

**Strong structural inference**

- the rivalry should descend from post-Convergence political-economic development;
- it should be cross-regional;
- established centers versus post-settlement ascendants is a strong WWF/WCW translation;
- Port remains outside both;
- internal divided alignment is essential.

**Still provisional**

- final sphere names;
- exact members;
- whether Stone Hills's crown ultimately leans Charter, Ascendant or becomes the major contested hinge;
- which Old Cities cities lead the Ascendant network;
- whether the Merchant Guild War becomes the genesis event;
- when ordinary people begin using a shared label for each sphere.

## Next discriminating test

Take six legacy actors:

- Summithold;
- Verdanthearth;
- Aurorashore;
- Cliffhaven;
- Blackbellow;
- one major Old Cities city-state.

For each, reconstruct:

1. what it gained from the Convergence;
2. what constraint it inherited;
3. how its wealth changed afterward;
4. who it competes with for people and prestige;
5. what kind of defection would humiliate it;
6. what would make it switch or split spheres.

If the same Charter-versus-Ascendant pattern survives all six without forcing the evidence, the model is strong enough to promote further.
