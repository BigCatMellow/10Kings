# Places of Interest

## Status

**Provisional.** Proposed 2026-11-09 at bt 7274's request ([D35](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)): "I think the world is missing interesting places. Like Oak Island in the real world. Or the pyramids, wonders, and places of interest. I remember making things up and ther was the 'one tree forest'. A forest of interconnected trees where reverent people who worshipped the forest lived. I feel like it needs more places of interest to make it feel more whole. Maybe a holy site or two, great big statues to forgotten kings, carvings, etc. Stuff to really make it feel lived."

This page owns the world's landmarks, wonders, holy sites, monuments and mysteries: places people travel to, argue about, live beside and stop noticing. Each entry is provisional until bt 7274 keeps it, cuts it or changes it.

Then: "I feel like you could do even more, looking at the world, the lore, the people, the map could host almost as many as we want" ([D36](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)). So the page grew from twenty to 67, arranged by where they are, each one drawn from something the region, a faith, a guild, a festival or the old history already has.
- **The One Tree** is his.
- **A few entries** are recovered from the legacy notes ([Source Register](../Reference/Source-Register.md)).
- **The rest** are proposals.

Region, faith and history pages keep their own facts and link here. Where each place is, by name, is on the [Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md).

**How every entry is built,** following [World Rules](../World-Rules.md):
- **Remembered differently.** Every place has more than one story told about it ([Contested Memory](../History/Contested-Memory.md)), and the page doesn't say which is right.
- **Lived beside.** Somebody keeps it, farms around it, sells things near it, or has stopped seeing it.
- **Deniable.** Where people say a place does something strange, it stays something people say ([Folk Belief](../Culture/Folk-Belief-Taboo-and-Superstition.md), [Magic](../Magic.md)).
- **From what exists.** Where an entry leans on something the wiki leaves open (scripts, the empire's name, the creation myth), it says so and doesn't settle it.

## Northwind

### The Tide Road

*The Northwind coast.*

A causeway of stones runs a mile out across the sand to a rock island with a house of the [Silent Whisper](../Politics/Religions.md#6-the-silent-whisper) on it: archives, a garden, a bell. Twice a day the sea covers the road. The tide times are cut into a post at the landward end, and the island's keepers ring the bell when it's time to go.

- **What it's for.** The Silent Whisper keeps records there that it won't keep anywhere a horse can reach.
- **How it's remembered.** The road was built by the keepers, or by the sea, or by a king who wanted to walk to his prayers. Everybody on the coast knows somebody who misread the post.

### The Ice Halls

*the cliffs of Sealfjord, on the Northwind coast ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#northwind)).*

Sea caves on a cold stretch of coast freeze in deep winter into halls of blue ice. Fishers' families take their children in once, by lamplight, on a still day.

- **How it's remembered.** Some say they're where the cold lives in summer. Couples who marry during [Aurora Nights](../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#northwind) sometimes go there afterwards, which their grandparents say is showing off.

### The Rescue Houses

*The Northwind cliffs.*

A chain of lifeboat sheds runs along the worst of the coast, each with a boat, a bell and a door covered in names: everyone that shed's boat has brought back. Any clan's boat goes out for any boat, which is the one rule every clan keeps ([Northwind](../Regions/Northwind.md#historical-identity): "shared rescue").

- **How it's lived with.** Children learn to row in the rescue boats. A family that's had a name cut on a door brings bread to that shed every year on the day.
- **How it's remembered.** The oldest shed claims the oldest name, and three others say they've got an older one.

### The Burned Strand

*The Northwind coast.*

A long beach where the ribs of old boats stick out of the sand, black with fire. Northwind songs say raiders from the south burned a fishing fleet here; Sunplains stories say the boats were northern raiders' and were burned to stop them ([the Salted Coast disputes](../History/Contested-Memory.md#the-salted-coast-disputes)).

- **How it's lived with.** Nobody takes the wood. Lovers who argue are told to walk it end to end before they decide anything.

### The Moot Isle

*the Holm, at the end of the Long Skerries off the Northwind coast ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#northwind)).*

A bare island where no one may live, with a ring of stone seats facing the sea. Clans meet there to settle fishing banks and winter anchorages, which have always mattered more than land on shore. Whoever calls the moot has to bring the food for everyone.

- **How it's remembered.** The ring is older than any clan that sits in it, each clan says, except their own.

### The Seafarer's Beacon

*Cape Wending, a Northwind headland ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#northwind)). Recovered from the [legacy notes](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/01_Foundations_World_Myth_History.md): "a towering lighthouse guiding ships safely to port".*

A fire tower on a headland, kept by one family for so long that the family's name and the tower's are the same word. They burn whatever the coast sends them, and the coast sends them a good deal.

### Icefang Fjord

*Northwind. Recovered from the [legacy notes](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/01_Foundations_World_Myth_History.md): "a perilous but lucrative fishing ground".*

A fjord whose mouth is a row of black rocks like teeth. The fish inside are the best on the coast, and the way in has to be learned from someone who knows it. Fathers teach it to one child.

## Ironcrest

### The Burning Hill

*Smoulder Knowe, Ironcrest ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#ironcrest)).*

A coal seam caught fire under a hill a hundred years ago and has never gone out. Snow doesn't lie on it. Steam comes out of cracks in the turf, and the village that stood on top moved down the valley.

- **Who stayed.** A few households, who grow winter greens on the warm slope and sell them in the town in midwinter.
- **Who keeps it.** The [Eternal Forge](../Politics/Religions.md#4-the-eternal-forge), for whom creation and destruction are linked, keeps a shrine at the foot.
- **How it's remembered.** A judgment, a warning, or bad luck and a careless lamp.

### The Wall of Names

*An Ironcrest foundry town.*

A long wall of dressed stone with the names of every worker the town's furnaces and mines have killed, cut one under another for two hundred years. At [Ember Remembrance](../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#ironcrest) the fires are banked, work stops, and families stand in front of their names.

- **How it's lived with.** Apprentices are taken to read it on their first day. The masons who cut the names are paid by the guild, and they refuse to be paid for a child's.

### The Black Field

*Ironcrest. From the region's own memory, and the legacy notes' "Great Foundry Fires" ([backlog 5](../Reference/README.md#legacy-reconciliation-backlog)).*

A wide meadow on a valley floor where nothing has been built since a great burning in the old wars over resources, which Ironcrest remembers "as both achievement and warning" ([Ironcrest](../Regions/Ironcrest.md#historical-identity)). The grass is ordinary now. Nobody builds there, and nobody can quite say who decided that.

- **Open:** what burned, and when. The legacy fires are unpromoted.

### The First Wheel

*the gorge of Cinder Brook, Ironcrest ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#ironcrest)).*

The oldest water wheel in the region still turns in a gorge, driving a hammer that no longer hits anything. The guild keeps it running so apprentices can be shown what everything else came from. It's been rebuilt so many times that not a board of the first wheel is left, and that's part of what they're shown.

### The Thousand Steps

*the Ironbacks, an Ironcrest ridge ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#ironcrest)).*

A stair cut into the rock up to an old mine mouth, climbed by miners every shift for a century. The mine is closed. People still climb it, and every one of them counts the steps on the way up, and nobody agrees on the number.

### The Star Iron

*An Ironcrest village square.*

A lump of iron the size of a calf sits in a village square. It fell from the sky, the village says, and the smiths touch it before a great work. The [Eternal Forge](../Politics/Religions.md#4-the-eternal-forge) calls it a gift. The guild once offered to buy it, and the village is still telling that story.

### The Blue Pit

*Ironcrest.*

An old open pit that filled with water when the workings closed. The water is a blue no lake has, from what's in the rock. Nobody swims in it, and everybody has a cousin who did.

## Greenvale

### The Straight Road

*Across Greenvale.*

Forty miles of fitted stone road run dead straight across hills, fields and one river, and stop in a meadow. Farmers use it as a cart road and graze sheep on its verges.

- **How it's remembered.** The empire built it toward something that was never finished. Or it was finished, and that something is gone. Or the road "knows where it's going". The [Ridge-Roamers](../Culture/Nomads.md#the-ridge-roamers) use it every year and have a song about the meadow.

### The Drowned Bell

*the Millburn's valley, Greenvale ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#greenvale)).*

A village lies under a millpond, flooded when the dam was raised in someone's great-grandmother's time. On still days, people say, you can hear its bell. In drought years, the roof of its shrine comes up out of the water, and people row out to touch it.

- **How it's remembered.** The village was paid to move, or it wasn't. Some say the bell rings for a drowning, and others that it only rings for weddings.

### The Grand Orchard

*Greenvale. Recovered from the [legacy notes](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/01_Foundations_World_Myth_History.md): "a massive grove symbolizing abundance and unity".*

The oldest orchard in Greenvale. It's said that every orchard in the region was grafted, scion by scion, from its trees, and half the farms in Greenvale can tell you which of its trees theirs came from. Like the One Tree, it's many trees from one beginning, though here it was people who did the joining.

### The Count Stones

*the far side of the Chalk Downs, on the Greenvale–Deepwood border ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#greenvale)).*

A ring of standing stones in a field. On the longest and shortest days, the shadows of two of them meet a third. Farmers plough around them; nobody remembers who set them up.

- **Who keeps it.** The [Cyclic Order](../Politics/Religions.md#1-the-cyclic-order), for whom everything moves in cycles, keeps a small house by the ring. Its keepers say their calendar is checked against the stones ([Calendar](../Calendar-and-Timekeeping.md)).
- **How it's remembered.** The Greenvale side says the stones were set by farmers. The Deepwood side says they were set before farmers. Both sides hold their midsummer there, on different days.

### The Seed House

*Barley Cross, a Greenvale market town ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#greenvale)). After the legacy notes' "Seed Vault".*

A cool stone house where every farming family in the district keeps a handful of its own seed in a jar with its name on, so that whatever the year does, nobody starts from nothing. At [Harvest Home](../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#greenvale) the jars are opened and seed is traded across the table.

- **How it's remembered.** It was built after the [Seven-year Blight](../Regions/Greenvale.md), most say. Its keepers say it was built before, and the Blight is why it was kept.

### Cellar Hill

*Greenvale.*

A village dug into a south-facing hillside: every house has its root cellar going back into the hill, and some cellars join up. On [Root Cellar Night](../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#greenvale) the doors are left open and people walk the hill from cellar to cellar, tasting.

### The Floating Fields

*the Wetlands of Saltings, Greenvale ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#greenvale)).*

Gardens built on rafts of reed and mud in a wide marsh, tethered with poles and poled about by the families who farm them. In a dry year they settle onto the bottom and become ordinary gardens, which the families find embarrassing.

### The Hiring Oak

*Four Lane Ends, a Greenvale crossroads ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#greenvale)).*

A great oak where seasonal workers stand to be hired at harvest. Its bark is cut with marks: a tally for every year a family came back. Some of the oldest marks have grown so high you need a ladder.

### The Ship on the Hill

*Ship Hill, toward the Highridge foothills ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#greenvale)).*

A seagoing ship, keel and ribs and most of its planking, sits on a hilltop forty miles from the sea. Nobody knows how it got there. A family keeps sheep in its hold and a shrine in its bow.

- **How it's remembered.** A flood; a king's whim; a ship hauled overland in some war; or the hill used to be the shore. Children are told it's waiting for the sea to come back for it.

## Highridge Plateau

### The Salt Mirror

*the Whitepan, on the high Highridge plateau ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#highridge-plateau)).*

A salt flat so wide you can't see across it. After the rains, it holds an inch of water and becomes a mirror, so that walking across it is walking on the sky. Caravans cross it by the stars when it's dry, and they don't cross it at all when it's wet.

- **Who keeps it.** The [Infinite Compass](../Politics/Religions.md#9-the-infinite-compass), for whom life is a journey, holds it holy. Walking the mirror once, in the wet season, from one cairn to the other, is a thing the faithful try to do in their lives.
- **Who lives by it.** The salt-cutters, who work it dry and think the pilgrims are in the way.
- **How it's remembered.** A sea that dried up; a god's spilled salt; or a flat, depending on who's cutting.

### The Head in the Square

*Kingshead, a Highridge market town ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#highridge-plateau)).*

In the middle of a market square lies a stone head as tall as a house, on its side. It's the missing Sitting King's.

- **How it got there.** After the war, one story says, it was carried up from the gorge as a trophy. Another says it was bought. A third says it rolled.
- **How it's lived with.** Traders meet "at the nose". Children slide off the brow, and pigeons have the ears. Every few years Port asks for it back, politely, and the town says it will think about it.
- **Open:** which town.

### The Golden Caravanserai

*The Highridge caravan corridors. Recovered from the [legacy notes](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/01_Foundations_World_Myth_History.md): "a legendary inn that has hosted travelers from across the world".*

A caravanserai that has never closed, through wars, winters and the Convergence. The walls of its long room are covered with travelers' names, marks and debts, and the keepers will paint over anything except a debt.

### The Debate Steps

*A Highridge city.*

A great semicircle of stone steps where the [Midsummer Debates](../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#highridge) are held. Two hollows worn into the bottom step mark where speakers have stood for generations, and a third, smaller one is where the timekeeper stands.

- **How it's lived with.** The rest of the year it's a market and a place to eat lunch. Children practice arguing on it, loudly.

### The Ledger Vault

*Tallybridge, a Highridge trading town ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#highridge-plateau)).*

A house of the [market and records guilds](../Politics/Guilds.md#market-and-records-guilds) that holds the oldest account books on the plateau. At [Ledger Closing](../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#highridge) families come to see an ancestor's debt, and sometimes to pay it, out of pride.

### The Stepwell

*Deepstep, a dry Highridge plateau town ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#highridge-plateau)).*

A well dug as a deep square funnel of stone steps, flight after flight, down to water that's always cool. In summer the town eats its supper on the lower steps. Every caravan that waters there leaves a mark on the top flight.

### The Whistling Notch

*A pass on the Highridge plateau.*

A cleft in a ridge where the wind never stops and the rocks whistle. Caravans tie a strip of cloth to the cairn as they go through, for a safe road, and the cairn is more cloth than stone.

### The Milestones

*the Milestone Way, the old Highridge caravan corridor ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#highridge-plateau)).*

Old carved milestones give distances to everywhere, including towns nobody can find any more. Caravan children learn their numbers from them. A few point to places that only exist in songs.

### The Rocking Stone

*A Highridge hillside.*

A boulder the size of a house balanced on a ledge, so finely that a child can rock it with one hand, and a crowd can't push it over. The [Dual Flame](../Politics/Religions.md#8-the-dual-flame), which teaches balancing what builds against what breaks, brings its young people here to try.

### The Congress Hall

*Lowmeet, a Highridge plateau town ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#highridge-plateau)).*

A long hall in a plateau town, said to be where the Convergence was negotiated. It's been a granary, a barracks and a school since. Now it holds hay, and the family who own it will show you the table it was signed on for a copper. Everyone in town knows the table is a newer one.

- **Open:** where the congress actually sat; this is one town's claim.

## Deepwood

### The One Tree

*Deepwood, beyond the Cedar Reaches ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#deepwood)). bt 7274's: "a forest of interconnected trees where reverent people who worshipped the forest lived."*

From the edge it looks like any old forest. It's one tree. Every trunk for miles comes up from the same root, and when one falls another rises from the root beside it. The people who live inside belong to the [Path of the Great Weaver](../Politics/Religions.md#2-path-of-the-great-weaver), for whom everything is a thread in one living weave; here the weave has a body.

- **How they live:**
  - Nobody cuts a living trunk.
  - Nobody lights a fire under the canopy, so every meal is cooked at the forest's edge and carried in.
  - Paths are walked barefoot, so as not to bruise the root.
  - The dead are buried among the roots, which they call being given back.
  - Visitors are welcome to eat at the edge, and asked to leave their axes there.
- **How it's remembered.** To the faithful, it's one being, the forest spirit Arborealus with a body. To timber men, it's a stand that suckers from its roots, like some aspens do. To other Deepwood towns, the people inside are odd, and the towns will still defend them to an outsider. Some say the forest drops a branch on people who lie under it.
- **What it touches.** Deepwood's [sacred or restricted areas](../Regions/Deepwood.md#geography); the [Ages of Silence](../Regions/Deepwood.md#historical-identity), "partly historical and partly religious"; some at the One Tree tell them as the price of cutting a sacred grove.
- **Open:** its name in Deepwood speech; what its people call themselves.

### The Quiet Villages

*the Hush, deep in Deepwood ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#deepwood)).*

Villages left during the [Ages of Silence](../Regions/Deepwood.md#historical-identity), grown over with moss and trees until only the shapes of doorways show. Most Deepwood people won't go in. Some go once a year, during Deep Silence, and say nothing while they're there.

- **How it's remembered.** A sickness, a famine, a war, or the forest's answer to a cut grove: the Ages of Silence are "partly historical and partly religious", and the villages don't say which.

### The Canopy Road

*the Alderfens, Deepwood's flood country ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#deepwood)).*

Rope bridges and platforms in the high branches let people cross the forest when the rivers are up. During [Canopy Vigil](../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#deepwood) people sit on the platforms and watch, and don't work.

### The Black Pool

*a backwater of the Brownwater, Deepwood ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#deepwood)).*

A still forest pool so dark with leaf-water that it doesn't seem to reflect anything. People drop a leaf in to see it float, to be sure it's water. The [Silent Whisper](../Politics/Religions.md#6-the-silent-whisper) walks there at night without lanterns.

### The Telling Tree

*A Deepwood village.*

A great tree under which the village's history is told aloud once a year, from the beginning, by whoever knows it best. The [Path of the Great Weaver](../Politics/Religions.md#2-path-of-the-great-weaver) keeps oral history; here it's kept by turns, and arguments about what happened next are part of the telling.

## Sunplains

### The Long Arches

*the Arch Valley, Sunplains ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#sunplains)).*

An aqueduct of tall stone arches crosses a valley and still carries water to the orchards on the far side.

- **Who keeps it.** Each arch is kept by a family, who patch it, argue about it, and hand it down. A water guild settles the arguments, more or less ([Sunplains water law](../Regions/Sunplains.md#geography)).
- **How it's remembered.** Two city-states each say their ancestors built it. The orchard owners say it doesn't matter who built it as long as it doesn't leak.

### The Stepped Tombs

*the Dry Edge of the Sunplains ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#sunplains)).*

Stepped stone tombs, five of them, rise out of the scrub where the irrigated country gives out. They were robbed so long ago that the robbers' tunnels have names. Nobody claims them: not the nearest city-state, which would have to maintain them, and not the faiths, which don't know whose dead they are.

- **How they're lived with.** A goat market is held in the shade of the biggest on market days. In summer, families picnic on the steps at dusk, when the stone gives back the day's heat.
- **How they're remembered.** Older than the empire, or the empire's, or a city-state's that lost its name; any of the three is said with total confidence.

### The Dig at Harrow Isle

*Harrow Isle, the plainest of the Sapphire Isles off the Sunplains coast ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#sunplains)).*

Two hundred years ago, a boy found a dip in the ground under an old oak, and a pulley block hanging from a branch. Under the dip there was a shaft. Every ten feet down there's a platform of oak logs. At the ninetieth foot, the sea comes in.

- **Who's dug it.** Every generation since, somebody has formed a company, sold shares, dug deeper and gone broke. The island has an inn for the diggers, a pump-makers' shop and a graveyard with three digging companies' founders in it.
- **What's down there.** Treasure, the empire's last treasury, a joke, or nothing. Whatever it is, it has paid for the inn.
- **How it's lived with.** The islanders are certain there's nothing down there, and they would never sell their shares.

### The Patrons' Wall

*A Sunplains city square.*

A wall of carved names: every patron who has paid for a fountain, a school, a road or a feast. At the [Festival of Patrons](../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#sunplains) houses compete to get a name on it, and the city's stonecutters compete over whose letters are deepest.

### The Lantern Stair

*A Sunplains hill city.*

A long public stair climbs the city's hill, with a niche for a lamp beside every step, and each niche belongs to a family. At [Solstice Lanterns](../Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#sunplains) every lamp is lit, and the stair can be seen from the sea.

### The Groaning Wheels

*Twelve Wheels, a Sunplains river city ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#sunplains)).*

Enormous wooden waterwheels lift river water into the city's channels, and groan as they turn, day and night. People born in the city can't sleep anywhere quiet.

### The Boundary Fountain

*Three Spouts, where three Sunplains city-states meet ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#sunplains)).*

A fountain with three spouts, one for each city, on the exact spot where their lands meet. Each city keeps its own spout, and argues about the others'. [Water law](../Regions/Sunplains.md#geography) is taught here to apprentices of all three.

### The Reed Towns

*the Thousand Channels, the Sallow's delta ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#sunplains)).*

Towns on stilts above a delta's channels, where people go to market by boat and children swim before they walk.

### The Argument House

*A Sunplains city. After the legacy notes' "Scholar's Library".*

A library of the [Illuminated Circle](../Politics/Religions.md#5-the-illuminated-circle) where any question may be asked aloud in the reading room, and any reader may answer it. It's the loudest library in the world, and very proud of it.

### The Dawn Hill

*A hill above a Sunplains–Highridge road.*

A bare hill where the [Radiant Path](../Politics/Religions.md#3-the-radiant-path), whose moral life is a struggle toward courage, keeps a vigil fire. The faithful sit up there through the longest night of their lives, by their own choosing, and walk down at dawn.

## Port

Port's past as the last ruling empire's city ([D30](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)) leaves the most ruins here and on the road down to it; see also [Pre-Convergence](../History/Pre-Convergence.md#the-last-ruling-empire-d30).

### The Old Light

*Lamp Point, on the north side of the Throat, Port's harbor mouth ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#port)).*

The empire's lighthouse stands at the harbor mouth, fallen to half its height. Port's watch still keeps a lamp burning on the stump, because ships expect one there.

- **How it's lived with.** Lovers go up it, and so do gulls. The harbor runners race to the top on festival days.
- **How it's remembered.** Port folk say it's the oldest thing in the city. Northwind families say their great-grandparents' trade paid to keep it lit ([the Port Seizure](../History/Contested-Memory.md#the-port-seizure)).

### The Sitting Kings

*the Kings' Gate, the gorge of the Long Water on the road down to Port ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#the-great-features)).*

Where the river cuts through the last hills before the coast, colossal seated kings are carved out of the gorge walls, facing the water. Their faces have worn smooth. Their hands rest on their knees. One is missing his head. Barges pass between their feet.

- **Whose they are.** Nobody agrees.
  - The likeliest answer, given [Port's past](Port.md#the-last-empires-city-d28-d30), is the kings of the last ruling empire, whose great city Port was. Nobody on the river says so with any confidence.
  - Port people call them "ours".
  - Greenvale bargemen call them "the Old Men".
- **How they're lived with.** Each new barge captain leaves a coin in the lap of one of the kings. Which one depends on which family taught you. Swifts nest in the folds of their robes, and children dare each other to climb.
- **How many.** The bargemen argue about it, because a fallen one may be in the river. *For bt 7274:* if the world's title should mean something, there could be ten ([question 1](#for-bt-7274)).

### Stationers' Row

*Port.*

A street of paper sellers, each house's mark carved over its door. Every record in the world passes through a shop like these sooner or later ([Records](../Records-Literacy-and-Authentication.md#paper-and-provenance)). The stationers drink together and say nothing to each other about their customers.

### The Stair of Flags

*the Enclave Quarter, Port ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#port)).*

A long stair climbs through the [foreign enclaves](Port.md#neutrality), and every landing has a different flag, a different kitchen smell and a different idea of what's polite. Port children run it as a dare: up to the top, saying good morning in every language on the way.

### The Floating Market

*the Inner Pool, Port's inner harbor ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#port)).*

Boats tie up gunwale to gunwale at dawn and sell to each other and to the quays until the tide turns. By noon it's gone.

### The Seizure Warehouse

*the Old Bond, Port ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#port)).*

An old warehouse that families on both sides remember as the place where the [Port Seizure](../History/Contested-Memory.md#the-port-seizure) began or ended. Who was seized, and by whom, depends on whose grandmother is telling it ([seed](../Sunday-Morning/Stories/Story-Seeds.md#the-seizure-warehouse)).

## The Spine, the Overpath and the Underpass

### The Mother's Cradle

*the Cradle, in the central Spine, at the top of the Pilgrims' Way ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#the-spine-the-overpath-and-the-underpass)). Recovered from the [legacy notes](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/02_Regions_Kingdoms.md).*

A natural amphitheater high among the central peaks, where a whisper at the bottom carries to the top. Pilgrims on the [Spine's pilgrimage routes](The-Spine.md) climb to it to say something out loud once.

- **How it's remembered.** The legacy notes say it's where "the Mother" last touched the land. That belongs to a creation myth that's still [open](../Open-Questions.md#faiths), so here it's one belief among several.
- **Open:** which faith, if any, keeps it. *Lean:* the [Silent Whisper](../Politics/Religions.md#6-the-silent-whisper), whose pilgrims walk "to stones where the acoustics carry the faintest sound". The legacy notes' **Celestium Spire**, a peak "used for celestial observations and rituals", could stand near it.

### The Speaking Cliff

*above the road through Orrimath, a pass on the Highridge side of the Spine ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#the-spine-the-overpath-and-the-underpass)).*

A cliff face above a pass road is carved with one long proclamation, three times over, in three languages. One is so old that only scholars can read it. One is [Old Spine speech](../Language-Families-and-Contact.md), extinct except in place names. The third is a Heartland tongue, worn but readable. The three versions don't quite agree.

- **Who studies it.** Copyists from the [Illuminated Circle](../Politics/Religions.md#5-the-illuminated-circle) and the [Silent Whisper](../Politics/Religions.md#6-the-silent-whisper) come with ladders and argue about the disagreements. A family of rope-men in the pass village has lowered copyists down the face for generations, and has opinions about all their translations.
- **What it says.** That depends on which version you trust. One reading is a boundary, one is a peace, and one is an apology.
- **Open:** scripts are [open](../Language-Families-and-Contact.md#21-scripts-remain-open), so the page says three languages, not three scripts.

### The Pass Springs

*just below the Hollin Gap, a high pass on the Spine ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#the-spine-the-overpath-and-the-underpass)).*

Hot springs where travelers soak before the descent. By custom nobody brings a weapon into the water and nobody talks business in it, so enemies have been known to sit in the same pool.

### The Storm Bell

*the Bellsaddle, the worst saddle on the Overpath ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#the-spine-the-overpath-and-the-underpass)).*

A tiny shrine in the worst saddle on the [Overpath](The-Spine.md#the-overpath), with a bell that whoever's sheltering rings every few minutes through a storm, so travelers lost in the cloud can find it.

### Eagle's Summit and Shimmerfall Ravine

*The Overpath.* Already on [The Spine](The-Spine.md#the-overpath), from the legacy notes:
- **Eagle's Summit**, "a high-altitude resting place".
- **Shimmerfall Ravine**, a valley of cascading waterfalls.

### Seven Hill

*The Highridge side of the Spine.* Already on [Seven Hill](Seven-Hill.md): an old pass fortress, emptied when toll warfare gave way to negotiated passage.

### The Carvers' Gallery

*the Long Bore, in the Underpass ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#the-spine-the-overpath-and-the-underpass)).*

A long stretch of [Underpass](The-Underpass.md) tunnel is carved from floor to ceiling by travelers: names, boats, a goat, prayers, a whole wedding party in one panel. The custom is that you carve once in your life, on your first crossing, and never again.

- **Who keeps it.** A keeper's hut at the gallery's mouth keeps a lamp, sells chisels, and will tell you who carved the goat.
- **How it's remembered.** Every family that crosses has a carving it can show you, or says it does.

### The Rockfall Stones

*The Underpass. After the legacy notes' "Rockfall Memorial Days" ([backlog 1](../Reference/README.md#legacy-reconciliation-backlog)).*

A side cavern where a stone is set up for every collapse the tunnels have had, each with the names of those it took. Once a year the Underpass remembers them, and the stones are washed.

### The Lantern Fair Cavern

*the Lantern Hall, in the Underpass ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#the-spine-the-overpath-and-the-underpass)). After the legacy notes' "Lanternlight trade fair" ([backlog 1](../Reference/README.md#legacy-reconciliation-backlog)).*

A cavern so big a whole fair fits in it. Once a year, traders from every tunnel branch set up stalls by lamplight, and for a few days it's the brightest place underground.

## Between the kingdoms

### The Treaty Stones

*along the Treaty Line ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#between-the-kingdoms)).*

Boundary stones set along the lines the [Convergence](../History/The-Convergence.md) recognized, each cut with a clause or two. Farmers know theirs by heart. Some have been moved by floods, or by farmers ([seed](../Sunday-Morning/Stories/Story-Seeds.md#the-treaty-stone)).

### The Reconciliation Bridge

*between Upper Brayle and Nether Brayle ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#between-the-kingdoms)).*

A narrow bridge between two towns that fought each other for longer than anyone remembers why. Feuds are ended by meeting in the middle and sharing bread, as the [Harmonious Path](../Politics/Religions.md#7-the-harmonious-path) teaches. The middle plank has been replaced more than any other.

### The Letter Tree

*a crossroads on the Carriers' Road ([Gazetteer](Gazetteer.md#the-great-features)).*

A hollow tree where people leave letters for travelers who might pass, and for the dead. The [carriers](../Couriers-Posts-and-Message-Networks.md) check it as they go by, take the ones for the living, and leave the others.

### The Fiddlers' Hollow

*A sheltered valley on the Ridge-Roamers' circuit.*

The meadow where the [Ridge-Roamers](../Culture/Nomads.md#the-ridge-roamers) winter. Every clan has its place in it, and the songs made there each winter go out with them in spring.

## Using them in stories

A place of interest is a setting, not a plot. A Sunday story can happen beside one, and the place does what any setting does: it gives people something to argue about, a custom to keep, and a view. Most of these places would suit a [World story](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Rules.md#world-stories) or an [exposition story](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Rules.md#exposition-stories). A few could carry a thread:
- the Old Light and the Sitting Kings sit on the road to Port;
- the Speaking Cliff is a record read three ways.

Story seeds for them can be added to [Story Seeds](../Sunday-Morning/Stories/Story-Seeds.md) when one is wanted.
