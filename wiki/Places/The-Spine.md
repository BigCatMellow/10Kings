# The Spine

## Status

**Working canon.**

The Spine is the central mountain system around which much of the known world's geography and transport is organized.

## Physical role

The range is:
- high enough to redirect weather;
- difficult enough to concentrate travel into passes;
- geologically active enough for earthquakes and collapses to matter;
- old and complex enough to contain caves, faults, mineral belts, valleys, and isolated communities.

The Spine should not be one continuous impassable wall. It contains:
- high peaks;
- lower saddles;
- inhabited valleys;
- pilgrimage routes;
- mining districts;
- seasonal passes;
- old fortifications;
- hidden routes.

## Human role

The range creates political choke points.

A pass can matter more than a hundred miles of open border.

This explains:
- Highridge's importance;
- Underpass development;
- ancient toll states;
- strategic fortresses;
- migration corridors;
- bandit economies;
- religious pilgrimage traditions.

## The Overpath

**Provisional.** bt 7274 named it on 2026-11-04: "the Overpath (the trail that goes the length of the Spine over the mountains". That's all that's settled. Where the Underpass runs through the range, the Overpath runs over it.

It sits alongside what this page already lists, without being settled as any of it: the range's pilgrimage routes, seasonal passes and migration corridors, and the [Infinite Compass](../Politics/Religions.md#9-the-infinite-compass)'s road shrines and hospitality for travelers.

### What the legacy notes say

The legacy notes call it **the Overpass**, not the Overpath ([Volume 01](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/01_Foundations_World_Myth_History.md), [Volume 02](../../legacy-notes/2026-09-22/consolidated-package/02_Regions_Kingdoms.md#src-world-the-spine)). None of this is settled; it's evidence for bt 7274 to rule on:

- "A treacherous and winding trail that weaves through the mountains, used by those who cannot—or choose not to—navigate The Underpass."
- It "caters to adventurers, pilgrims, and caravans carrying goods unsuitable for the confined Underpass", with avalanches, predators, bad weather and bandits; the Underpass is the safer, faster, busier way through.
- "Seasonal nomads traverse both the Underpass and the Overpass, acting as guides and transporters of goods."
- Named landmarks: Eagle's Summit, "a high-altitude resting place", and Shimmerfall Ravine.
- The old domino and current-events notes use it often: tolls, a checkpoint, repairs, the Warriors' Guild guarding caravans on it, and a rivalry between Overpass and Underpass communities.

The legacy describes a way *through* the range. bt 7274's 2026-11-04 statement describes a trail along its whole length. His later word is the one recorded above.

Still open:

- the name: Overpath (bt 7274, 2026-11-04) or Overpass (legacy);
- who walks it, and why: pilgrims, herders, couriers, people who walk it for its own sake;
- whether it is one made trail or a chain of older paths that came to be walked as one;
- whether anyone keeps it (huts, markers, a guild, a faith, nobody), and whether its route rules are the [Convergence](../History/The-Convergence.md#route-rules)'s or older;
- how long it takes, which stretches close in winter, and where it meets the passes.

Stories that want it are on [Story Seeds](../Sunday-Morning/Stories/Story-Seeds.md#the-spine-above-and-below).

## Geologic history

Earlier notes propose that the three major landmasses emerged from ancient tectonic rifting and collision, with The Spine formed through long mountain-building processes.

Treat exact geology as provisional until the map is finalized.

## Current events

Recent earthquakes or rock shifts have:
- damaged routes;
- revealed old ruins or sealed spaces;
- created arguments over newly accessible resources;
- changed Underpass conditions;
- attracted explorers, guilds, religious groups, criminals, and Council attention.

## Story function

The Spine is where geography becomes politics.

It should generate hard choices about which route remains open, who pays to maintain it, who controls access, and what happens when nature ignores human borders.
