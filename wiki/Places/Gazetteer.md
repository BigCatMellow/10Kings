# Gazetteer

## Status

**Provisional names; relative positions only.** Proposed 2026-11-09 at bt 7274's request ([D37](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)): "I think the more named things we have, the more consistent we can be with the world, especially in the stories, it gives things even more connectivity, gives, paths, shortcuts, forests, plains, hills, etc."

This page owns the world's **named geography**: rivers, coasts, islands, forests, plains, hills, valleys, passes, and the roads, paths, shortcuts, fords and ferries between them. It also gathers the towns and villages the stories have already named, so a story can say "down the Ember to Coalglen" and the next story can agree.

**What a name here does and doesn't do:**
- **It fixes a name and a relationship:** what flows into what, which road runs past which town, what lies upstream or across the hill.
- **It never fixes a coordinate, a border or a distance** beyond what's written. [Geography](../Geography-and-Connections.md) owns the macro-geography, and the exact map stays [open](../Open-Questions.md#map). Where an entry leans on an open question (where Port sits, whether Greenvale borders Highridge), it says so.
- **It follows [Naming](../Culture/Naming.md)**: each region names things its own way, border places often have two names, and the oldest names in the Spine are Old Spine speech nobody can translate ([Language and Thought](../Culture/Language-and-Thought.md)).
- **It isn't a cast list.** When a story uses a name from here, the story's plan registers it in the [Registry](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Registry.md#names) as usual. Story-placed names are marked *(story)*.
- **It links landmarks to places.** The wonders, holy sites and mysteries are on [Places of Interest](Places-of-Interest.md). This page says where they are.

The waters between the continents, named 2026-11-09 for the [map draft](../../atlas/map/README.md) ([D38](../Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions)). The legacy map draws them; nothing named them yet. All five are provisional, and each leans on the map draft's reading of the continents.

| Name | What it is | Where, relatively | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Narrows** | the strait | between the Northern and Eastern Continents, from the Inner Sea out to the open sea | the Inner Sea's only way out to the ocean, so every ship bound for Port passes through it, the sea road from Frostbay Way among them *(lean)* |
| **The Inner Sea** | an enclosed sea | between the Northern Continent, the Spine and the Eastern Continent | Port's bay *(lean)*: Port stands on its southern shore, and it opens to the ocean only through the Narrows; Highridge holds its northern shore *(lean)* |
| **The North Gut** | a long sound | between the Northern and Western Continents, north of the Spine | the Long Skerries and the Holm are in it |
| **Footwater** | a lake | at the Spine's southern foot, between the Western and Eastern Continents | between Greenvale and Deepwood *(lean)*; the Long Channel drains it to the sea |
| **The Long Channel** | a narrow channel | from Footwater along the Western Continent's southern shore to the sea past the Eastern Continent's tip | Sunplains cities on both shores *(lean)* |
