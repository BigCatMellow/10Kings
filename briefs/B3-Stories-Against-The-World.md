# B3. The stories against the world

**Goal.** Read the 16 drafts as written, and list every place where a story and the current canon disagree. Do the same where a story relies on something canon has since moved, renamed or ruled differently.

**Why.** The drafts were written before the map (D38–D47, D53), the blended peoples (D43–D44), the physical world (D48–D51) and the three moons (D52). bt 7274 has already caught one clash: "Again next year" against a seven-year Three Moon. There will be others. Season 1's text is fixed, so where a story and a lower page disagree, the story usually wins. Only bt 7274 can change a story.

**Done means.** One report with every clash, sorted into two lists:
- **canon should bend**, the reviser's to fix;
- **story might bend**, bt 7274's to decide, each with a lean.

Each clash quotes both sides.

## Fixed: don't re-argue

- **The story text.**
- **bt 7274's quoted rulings** in [Decisions](../wiki/Sunday-Morning/Notes/Decisions.md#the-authors-decisions).
- **The open questions** in [STATE](STATE.md#waiting-on-bt-7274). "Again next year" is already known: list it once, and don't analyze it further.

## Read, in this order (about 70,000 words)

1. `AGENTS.md`, `briefs/README.md` and `briefs/STATE.md`.
2. `wiki/Sunday-Morning/Notes/Rules.md`, only **The world-tie rule**, **Canon discipline** and **Setting rules that still apply**. These say how stories may and may not bend canon.
3. `wiki/Sunday-Morning/Notes/Collection.md`, only **Reading order** and **Cross-story promise ledger**.
4. `wiki/Sunday-Morning/Notes/Registry.md`, only **Names**, which lists who and what each story names.
5. The B2 reports for slices A and D, from `wiki/Reference/Reviews/`, if they exist. They are your index of canon claims; use them instead of re-reading those pages.
6. **The 16 drafts in `wiki/Sunday-Morning/Drafts/`, in the Collection's reading order,** with Mending Day and The Same Paper last.
   - Read each one straight through, as a reader would, before checking anything.
   - Then grep canon for each place, festival, custom, distance and season the draft uses.
   - Open the draft's plan page in `Stories/` only when you need the story's intent to judge a clash.

## What to check, per draft

- **Places.** Is each named place where canon and the map put it? Does it have the character canon gives it? Does it belong to the people D43 would put there?
- **Distance and time.** Does each journey and each "a day", "a week" or "by morning" hold at the lean scale of 1.8 miles to the map pixel? If it doesn't, say whether the map should move (it usually should) or the lean scale is wrong.
- **Season and sky.**
  - Does the weather fit the season and region on [Sky, Seasons and Sea](../wiki/Sky-Seasons-and-Sea.md)? Read only the sections you need.
  - Does the festival fall when [Festivals](../wiki/Culture/Festivals-and-Seasonal-Life.md#seasonal-calendar) says?
  - Does anything assume one sun or one moon?
- **Law and rule.** Does any dispute over land, tax or jurisdiction follow D44 (the land's law follows its holder, personal law the person)?
- **Cross-story promises.** Is each promise in the Collection's ledger kept by the drafts as written?
- **Status.** Does the story establish something that canon still marks open, which canon should now record? This is the *promotion* list in Rules.

## Output

`wiki/Reference/Reviews/B3-Stories-Against-The-World-2026-11.md`, in the [report shape](README.md#report-shape). Make the findings two tables, **canon should bend** and **story might bend**, then a third list for **promotions**. For each "story might bend" row, give the smallest change that would fix it and a lean. Never write replacement prose longer than the line it replaces. Who writes what is set by [Craft](../wiki/Sunday-Morning/Notes/Craft.md#ai-and-the-author-who-writes-what).

## Stop when

The report is written. Don't edit any draft or any canon page.
