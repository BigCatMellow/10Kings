## Gaps this part exposes

Things the wiki doesn't settle yet. Listed for the author to decide; nothing here was filled in.

- **No dish is tied to a named festival.** Four recipes mention unnamed "festival versions" (Braided Honey and Grain Loaf, Aurora Berry Preserve, Honey-Drizzled Nut Pastries, Blacksmith's / Forged Harvest Stew), and the festivals page names no dishes. Every dish card's Festival row says "none stated". The only festival links drawn are two inferred ones for Greenvale (Root Cellar Night, Harvest Home). The pairing of Forged Harvest Stew with Forge Reawakening exists only in the Sunday Morning stories, which are not canon. Name echoes (Aurora Berry Preserve and Aurora Nights; Harvest Stew and Harvest Home) are not links either.
- **Port has no dishes.** Food lists six kinds of Port kitchen (dock-worker, ship, merchant, immigrant neighborhood, street and religious), and the diaspora page gives Port a cooking bias (borrowing, ship food, imported condiments), but there is no Port recipe book and no Port staples, techniques, meals, celebrations or drinks on record. Lean Vigil "can mark the anxiety of winter shipping and food supply" but names no food.
- **Deepwood–Ironcrest border food has no dishes.** The Ironcrest page says its border food "blends strongly with Greenvale and Deepwood", and Darkroot Gulch sits on that border, but Border Fusions has no Deepwood–Ironcrest section.
- **Three border towns have no border dishes.** Twilighthollow (Deepwood / Highridge), Frostbay Way (Northwind / Port) and Darkroot Gulch (Deepwood / Ironcrest) have no matching section in Border Fusions.
- **Four fusion pairings have no named town, and their borders aren't settled.** Ironcrest–Highridge (likely a border or a short mountain corridor), Greenvale–Deepwood (a working border), Deepwood–Sunplains (likely) and Highridge–Sunplains (unsettled: a border, a corridor, or mediated through Greenvale or Deepwood) have dishes but no border town on record. Border Fusions says that where adjacency changes, those dishes should be treated as trade-route or diaspora dishes. Greenvale–Highridge has a likely border but no dishes.
- **Border-town links are inferred.** Border Fusions doesn't say which town eats which dish; the seven dishes linked to Stonefield Forge, Harveston Vale and Icestep Summit are matched by border pair only.
- **The diaspora model has no dishes of its own on the wiki.** Food Diaspora and Adaptation gives the three-stage model and two illustrative paths (Guardian Stew, Forgefire Roast), and says the host-region versions shouldn't be treated as automatically canonical dishes. Forgefire Roast has no recipe in the Ironcrest book. Diaspora dishes worked out in earlier sessions (such as Forager's Crock, Wildwood Turnover and Listening / Caravan Broth) aren't on any wiki page, so they aren't on the board.
- **Most food imports are unsourced.** Only three are named as imports (Greenvale grain to Ironcrest; Greenvale flour and Sunplains oils or fruit possibly reaching Northwind's wealthier ports), plus food moving both ways between Ironcrest and Greenvale. Border dishes name a few origins in passing (plateau spice in Icefire Brined Fish; Sunplains oil, figs and dried fruit in Sunlit Orchard Grain, Forest-Orchard Medley, Harvest Orchard Salad and Highridge Orchard Pilaf), but Highridge's markets, Sunplains' "spices from trade" and Deepwood's "trade grain and salt" don't say where the food comes from.
- **Deepwood has no class or variation note** comparable to the other regions', and Port and border cuisine lack most cuisine facts.
- **Food as plot mechanics is stated but not mapped.** Food says shortages, grain prices, fish seasons and road closures should be usable as plot; those links belong to Parts 4 and 7.

**Northwind cuisine**

- *Staples:* Fish and shellfish; sea greens; roots; hardy grains; dairy in pastoral zones; berries; preserved meat. ([source](../../wiki/Culture/Food.md#northwind))
- *Techniques:* Drying, smoking, salting, fermentation, pickling, cold storage. The provisional recipe book adds cold and warm smoking, drying in wind or protected racks, root-cellar and ice-cellar storage, all-day hearth pots, and ovens heated for both food and household warmth. ([source](../../wiki/Culture/Food.md#northwind))
- *Everyday meals:* Breakfast: porridge with smoked fish; rye or barley cakes; sour milk. Lunch: dried fish, broth, flatbread, pickles. Dinner: fish soups; shellfish pots; root dishes; dairy and grain where available. ([source](../../wiki/Culture/Food.md#northwind))
- *Celebrations:* Shared catches; rich stews; imported flour and sugar used for special pastries. ([source](../../wiki/Culture/Food.md#northwind))
- *Drinks:* Grain ales, berry ferments, honey drinks, imported spirits. (The region page: alcohol "can include" these.) ([source](../../wiki/Culture/Food.md#northwind))
- *Cooking bias:* Preservation first; brine, smoke, fermentation, cold-storage traditions, berries and sea or coastal ingredients. ([source](../../wiki/Culture/Food-Diaspora-and-Adaptation.md#regional-cooking-biases))
- *Class and variation:* Should show both scarcity management and trade; foods may include imported Greenvale flour and Sunplains oils or fruit in wealthier ports. ([source](../../wiki/Regions/Northwind.md#food))
- *Avoid:* Making it only fish: inland herding, dairy, grain, berries and preserved meat should stay visible. ([source](../../wiki/Culture/Recipes/Northwind.md#status))
- *Also on:* [Northwind](../../wiki/Culture/Recipes/Northwind.md), [Northwind](../../wiki/Regions/Northwind.md#food)

**Salt-Fish and Barley Porridge**

- *Why it exists:* Preserved fish stretches a grain breakfast through winter.
- *Key ingredients:* Barley or oats; water or light fish broth; soaked salt fish; leek or onion; root vegetable; dill-like herb; rendered animal fat or butter.
- *Method:* Salt fish is soaked and rinsed. Grain simmers until nearly soft, roots and alliums go in, and the fish is added near the end so it stays in flakes, with a little fat stirred through.

**Hearthfire Fish and Root Bake**

- *Why it exists:* The evening meal uses the same masonry hearth that warms the house.
- *Key ingredients:* Whole cod-like fish, haddock-like fish or char; thin-sliced turnip, parsnip or potato-like roots; coarse grain crumbs; sea salt; kelp; dill-like herbs; tart berries or imported sour fruit.
- *Method:* The oven is fired earlier for bread and heat. Roots go into a clay dish with fat and a little broth, the fish rests on top under crumbs and herbs, and it cooks in falling heat rather than fierce flame.
- *Serving:* Tart berry mash is spooned over at the table.

**Frostbound Fish Stew**

- *Why it exists:* A communal pot turns small fish, kelp and roots into a substantial supper.
- *Key ingredients:* Fresh and/or smoked fish; fish stock; kelp; roots; onion; barley; seal fat, butter or other local cooking fat; juniper-like herb.
- *Method:* Roots and barley begin first, kelp and smoked fish flavor the broth, and fresh fish goes in only near the end. The pot stays hot at the central hearth and is replenished during the day.
- *Note:* Correction to early brainstorming: chilling cooked stew in snow can preserve it but doesn't "gelatinize" fish broth. Thick versions come from barley, collagen-rich fish parts or reduction.

**Frosted Mead Cakes**

- *Why it exists:* A durable oat cake that turns scarce sweetness into travel food.
- *Key ingredients:* Coarse oats; honey; small beer or mead; butter or rendered fat; salt.
- *Method:* A stiff oat batter is cooked on a griddle or baking stone and brushed with honey while hot. In winter they cool rapidly outdoors, setting the glaze.
- *Serving:* They travel well with cheese, smoked fish or berry paste.

**Snow-Dried Fish Cakes**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*
- *Key ingredients:* Soaked salt fish; mashed roots; oat or barley meal; egg when available; onion; herbs; cooking fat.
- *Method:* Fish is flaked into root mash, bound with grain meal, formed into patties, chilled and fried on a hearth griddle.
- *Serving:* Coastal households serve them with fermented berry relish or pickled kelp.

**Kelp Crisps**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*
- *Key ingredients:* Clean kelp; a little salt.
- *Method:* Rinsed, lightly salted, dried on racks and briefly toasted near the hearth.
- *Serving:* A snack, soup garnish and travel seasoning.

**Pickled Winter Roots**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*
- *Key ingredients:* Turnip, beet-like roots, carrot-like roots and onion; salt brine.
- *Method:* Packed in salt brine and allowed to sour.
- *Variations:* Better-off households add berry vinegar, honey or imported spice.

**Aurora Berry Preserve**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*
- *Key ingredients:* Berries; honey or malt syrup; festival jars may include mead.
- *Method:* Berries are simmered with honey or malt syrup until thick.
- *Variations:* In lean households the preserve stays sharply sour; festival jars use more sweetener and may include mead.

**Wind Dumplings**

**Plateau Noodle Soup**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*
- *Key ingredients:* Hand-cut or pulled noodles; broth; dried meat; greens; onion; root vegetables.

- *Variations:* Market versions add trade spices and fresh herbs that remote villages don't have.

**Caravan Skewers**

- *Why it exists:* The point is speed and portability, not giant feast portions.
- *Key ingredients:* Small pieces of meat; sour dairy or vinegar; spice; salt.
- *Method:* Marinated, then quickly cooked over a brazier.

**Spice Rice / Grain Pilaf**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*
- *Key ingredients:* Imported rice or another long-grain cereal where affordable, otherwise local wheat berries, millet or barley; fat; broth; dried fruit; nuts.
- *Method:* The grain is toasted in fat, simmered in broth, and dried fruit and nuts are folded in.
- *Variations:* Where rice or another long-grain cereal isn't affordable, the same technique uses local wheat berries, millet or barley.

**Lentil Fritters**

- *Why it exists:* Common market food because the batter can be mixed in advance.
- *Key ingredients:* Soaked pulses; onion; herbs; spice.

**Mushroom-Simmered Broth**

**Guardian Stew**

- *Why it exists:* The name fits ceremonial or communal versions; ordinary households make simpler descendants.
- *Key ingredients:* Venison, boar or domestic meat; mushrooms; roots; onion; forest herbs; stock; a little honey or fermented fruit paste.
- *Method:* Meat is lightly browned in a clay pot if fat is available, roots and stock go in, and the pot cooks for hours in low embers or a banked earth hearth. Mushrooms go in later, and fermented fruit or honey balances the broth.

**Miner's Gruel**

- *Why it exists:* Cheap grain, scraps and leftovers become a hot meal that can be kept going with little attention.
- *Key ingredients:* Cracked barley, oats or another hardy grain; water, thin broth or whey; onion or leek; a little smoked cheese, cured pork, beans or yesterday's meat if available; salt and a pepper-like local pungency; optional malt or dark fruit syrup in households that favor sweet-salty breakfasts.
- *Method:* Grain is soaked when fuel is scarce, then simmered in a heavy pot near a steady work fire, with scraps in early and cheese or cured meat near the end, thinned through the morning. Workers eat from bowls or carry a thick portion wrapped in bread.
- *Variations:* Poorer versions are grain and onion; better-off crews add dairy, meat and pickles.

**Smoked Ember Stew**

- *Why it exists:* Tough preserved meat becomes tender during a workday without needing a dedicated cook.
- *Key ingredients:* Salted or smoked goat, mutton, beef-like livestock or game; onion and garlic-like alliums; roots; dried mushrooms; hardy grain or beans; broth, small beer or water; highland aromatics; vinegar or sour ale to finish.
- *Method:* Meat is browned in rendered fat in an iron cauldron, the browned residue is loosened with liquid, mushrooms and grain go in, and the pot sits on low residual heat for several hours. Roots go in later so they stay whole, and sour vinegar or ale cuts the fat before serving.
- *Real-world inspiration:* Working-class barley stews, goulash-like braises and northern European meat pots.

**Ashen Stone Loaf**

- *Why it exists:* A durable sour loaf suited to upland grain, irregular ovens and work travel.
- *Key ingredients:* Rye-like and barley flour; sourdough starter; water; salt; malted or toasted grain; lard, tallow or butter when available.
- *Method:* A stiff dough ferments in a warm corner. Small households cook flat rounds on a heated stone or iron plate; larger loaves bake in a lidded pot among cooling embers, dark and dry enough to keep several days. Stale bread thickens soup, is fried, or is soaked in broth.
- *Note:* "Ashen" describes color and cooking environment; edible ash or coal is not required.

**Coal-Roasted Boar with Root Mash**

- *Why it exists:* A feast dish using the region's love of intense browning without pretending industrial fuel is a seasoning.
- *Key ingredients:* Boar, goat or pork-like shoulder; salt; rock thyme; crushed aromatic seed; onions; potatoes, turnips, rutabaga or parsnips; rendered fat; dark ale or broth.
- *Method:* Meat is salted the day before and roasts slowly in a covered iron pan above or beside clean charcoal or hardwood embers, never buried in raw coal; the lid comes off near the end so the outside darkens. Roots are cooked separately and mashed with fat and roasting juices.
- *Serving:* Large pieces are carved onto bread or root mash at communal tables.

**Smoked Ash-Marrow Broth**

- *Why it exists:* Bones, trimmings and stale bread become valuable calories.
- *Key ingredients:* Roasted marrow or joint bones; charred onion; dried mushroom; thyme or sage; water; salt; stale bread or grain.
- *Method:* Bones are roasted by the hearth, then barely simmered for much of the day with water, onion and mushrooms. Laboring households don't skim all the fat, and bread crumbs or cooked grain turn it into a meal.

**Forge-Fired Sausages**

- *Why it exists:* Sausage turns scraps into portable, preservable protein.
- *Key ingredients:* Chopped goat, pork-like livestock or game; fat; salt; dried allium; rock thyme; pepper-like pungent seed; cleaned casing.
- *Method:* Meat and fat are chopped rather than finely ground in poorer districts, seasoned, and poached or gently heated first. Some are eaten fresh; others hang in a smokehouse. Work stalls split smoked sausages and sear them on iron plates.

**Smoked Meat and Root Pie**

- *Why it exists:* A portable meal for shifts, travel and winter storage.
- *Key ingredients:* Coarse grain pastry; smoked meat; cooked roots; onion; thick broth or gravy; hard cheese if available.
- *Method:* Leftover stew meat and roots are bound with reduced broth, shaped into thick hand pies and baked in a communal oven or lidded iron pan. The crust is meant to be sturdy, not delicate.

**Ember Nuts**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*
- *Key ingredients:* Nuts; salt; toasted grain dust; a little rendered fat.
- *Method:* Roasted in a heavy pan near the hearth and tossed with salt, toasted grain dust and fat.
- *Variations:* Imported spice makes this ordinary street food noticeably more expensive.

**Forgefire Roast**

- *What survives:* The idea of the food: it should be deeply browned and served after collective labor.
- *What changes:* The Sunplains host version could use a different meat, oil, acid and herb set.
- *Caution:* Illustrative, on a page that is a working design method, not a fixed menu of canon dishes.

**Greenvale cuisine**

- *Staples:* Extremely broad: wheat, barley and other grains; beans; peas; vegetables; dairy; eggs; poultry; pork, goat or sheep depending on district; orchard fruit; herbs. ([source](../../wiki/Culture/Food.md#greenvale))
- *Techniques:* From the provisional recipe book: Communal stone or brick ovens; farmhouse hearth stewing; fruitwood roasting; dairy culturing; pickling and preserving; cellar storage; grain fermentation; leftovers used across several meals. ([source](../../wiki/Culture/Recipes/Greenvale.md#method-profile))
- *Everyday meals:* Breakfast: bread, cheese, eggs; porridge; fruit preserves. Lunch: soups; bean dishes; filled breads; cold farm meals. Dinner: grain or dumplings; vegetables; meat where affordable; sauces of herbs, dairy, fruit or vinegar. Snacks: hand pies; toasted grains and nuts; fresh fruit; cheese. ([source](../../wiki/Culture/Food.md#greenvale))
- *Celebrations:* Enormous seasonal variety; communal baked goods; preserved foods opened from previous harvests; first-fruit and first-grain rites. The region page adds: celebrations center on abundance, storage, first harvests, seed exchange, and survival after bad years. ([source](../../wiki/Culture/Food.md#greenvale))
- *Drinks:* Cider, ale, fruit wine, grain spirits. (The region page: "plausible".) ([source](../../wiki/Culture/Food.md#greenvale))
- *Cooking bias:* Abundance and sharing; dairy, orchard fruit, grains, garden herbs, baked and communal foods. ([source](../../wiki/Culture/Food-Diaspora-and-Adaptation.md#regional-cooking-biases))
- *Class and variation:* Class and season matter greatly. ([source](../../wiki/Regions/Greenvale.md#food))
- *Avoid:* Treating abundance as permanent plenty; it means diversity. ([source](../../wiki/Culture/Recipes/Greenvale.md#status))

**Fresh Morning Bread and Milk**

- *Why it exists:* Bread ties the workday to the shared oven schedule.
- *Key ingredients:* Wheat-like flour, often blended with barley; starter or yeast; water or milk; salt; butter or nut oil.
- *Method:* Dough is mixed before dawn or the night before, carried to a shared oven, and baked after the fire is raked down.
- *Serving:* Eaten warm with milk, curds, butter or preserves. Day-old bread becomes soup thickener or field food.

**Braided Honey and Grain Loaf**

- *Why it exists:* Braiding makes a familiar loaf ceremonial without requiring luxury ingredients.
- *Key ingredients:* Wheat and barley flour; warm milk; honey; starter or yeast; butter; salt.
- *Method:* A soft enriched dough is kneaded, risen, braided from three ropes, brushed with milk or butter and baked during a communal oven cycle.
- *Variations:* Festival versions add nuts, dried fruit or seed.

**Harvest Stew**

- *Why it exists:* The recipe changes with whatever the village has in quantity.
- *Key ingredients:* Beans or barley; onion; carrots, squash, greens, roots; chicken, goat or pork-like meat when affordable; garden herbs; broth; vinegar, cider or cultured dairy for acidity.
- *Method:* Meat, if used, is browned. Beans or grain and sturdy vegetables simmer first, delicate vegetables and greens go in later, and fresh herbs finish the pot.
- *Variations:* Communal versions accept contributions from multiple households.

**Orchard-Fire Roasted Fowl**

- *Why it exists:* A celebratory roast that makes direct use of orchard pruning wood and cider.
- *Key ingredients:* Duck, goose, chicken or game bird; salt; sage, thyme or fennel-like herbs; cider; honey; apples, pears or plums.
- *Method:* Orchard wood burns down to clean coals. The salted bird turns slowly over indirect heat, basted with cider, with honey added late so it doesn't burn, while fruit roasts in a pan beneath and catches the drippings.

**Farmer's Midday Mash**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*
- *Key ingredients:* Cracked barley; milk or water; butter; hazelnuts or walnuts; thyme or sage; salt.
- *Method:* Barley cooks slowly until soft, milk and butter enrich it, and nuts and herbs are stirred in.
- *Serving:* Carried to the fields in covered crocks or eaten at midday tables.

**Orchard Hand Pies**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*
- *Key ingredients:* Thick pastry; fillings such as apple and soft cheese, pear and onion, beans and herbs, chicken and leek, or plum and honey.
- *Method:* Filled, sealed and baked during the communal oven's falling heat.
- *Variations:* Plum and honey for sweet versions.

**Roasted Grain Clusters**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*
- *Key ingredients:* Leftover cooked grain or toasted cereal; honey; nuts; seeds.
- *Method:* Mixed, pressed into clusters and dried in a cooling oven.
- *Serving:* A common field snack.

**Herbed Farm Cheese**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*
- *Key ingredients:* Fresh curds; salt; meadow herbs.
- *Method:* Curds are drained, salted and mixed with meadow herbs.
- *Variations:* Some are eaten soft; others are pressed and aged for winter.

**Orchard-Pear Clay-Pot Braise**

- *Key ingredients:* Cooked pulses; oil; garlic-like allium; salt; sour fruit or vinegar.

- *Serving:* Eaten with flatbread and raw or grilled vegetables.

**Orchard Pilaf**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*

**Blacksmith's / Forged Harvest Stew**

- *Why it exists:* Greenvale produce regularly feeds upland work towns; Ironcrest smoke and browning alter the farm stew.
- *Key ingredients:* Smoked goat, pork-like meat or boar; barley; onion; carrots, parsnips, turnips; apple or pear; thyme; broth; cider vinegar; optional nuts.
- *Method:* Meat is browned hard in an iron pot and onion charred at the hearth's edge; barley and broth simmer with the meat, Greenvale roots and orchard fruit go in later, and vinegar finishes the pot.
- *Variations:* Nuts appear mainly in better-off or festival versions.

**Iron-Grain Herbed Bread**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*
- *Key ingredients:* Ironcrest's darker flours and toasted grain; Greenvale herbs.
- *Method:* Greenvale bakers adapt their softer communal loaves to Ironcrest's darker flours, toasted grain and lidded-pot baking. Ironcrest households adopt Greenvale herbs and longer fermentation.

**Forge and Plateau Kebabs**

- *Why it exists:* Highridge caravan cooks bring sour marinades and layered spice to Ironcrest's fast iron-plate and charcoal cooking.
- *Key ingredients:* Goat, boar or other upland meat; sour dairy, vinegar or tart fruit; cumin/coriander-like seed; dried pungent spice; onion; salt; flatbread.
- *Method:* Small pieces marinate in sour liquid and spice, are skewered tightly and grilled over a clean charcoal brazier on Ironcrest metal skewers with Highridge seasoning.
- *Serving:* Wrapped in plateau-style flatbread with Ironcrest pickles.

**Smoked Iron-Grain Flatbread**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*
- *Key ingredients:* Highridge-style flatbread; Ironcrest smoked meat; onions; curd cheese; a restrained amount of imported spice.
- *Method:* The flatbread is topped and finished on a hot stone or iron plate.

**Icefire Brined Fish**

- *Why it exists:* Northwind fish preservation meets Highridge caravan seasoning and drying.
- *Key ingredients:* Firm fish; salt brine; kelp; juniper-like herb; crushed plateau spices; rendered fat or butter.
- *Method:* Fish is brined cold, rinsed and air-dried until tacky, pressed with a thin layer of crushed spice, then cold- or warm-smoked depending on storage needs.
- *Serving:* Highridge traders often brush it with spiced butter when reheating.

**Icy Caravan Fish Pot**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*
- *Key ingredients:* Soaked salt fish; a Highridge-style aromatic legume or dairy sauce.
- *Method:* The salt fish simmers briefly in the sauce, added late so it doesn't fall apart.
- *Serving:* Eaten with flatbread or grain.
- *Note:* It doesn't require modern coconut milk.

**Frost and Spice Fish Cakes**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*
- *Key ingredients:* Salt fish; mashed root; onion; barley meal; imported plateau spice.
- *Method:* Shaped into cakes, seasoned with plateau spice and shallow-fried.
- *Serving:* Sour dairy or fermented berry relish accompanies them.

**Shadow Hearth Porridge**

- *Why it exists:* Greenvale grain becomes the stable carbohydrate; Deepwood contributes mushrooms, nuts and low-ember technique.
- *Key ingredients:* Cracked wheat or barley; mushroom broth; roasted root; chestnut or hazelnut; thyme; optional milk or honey.
- *Method:* Grain begins in a clay pot, ember-roasted root is mashed into the liquid, mushrooms and nuts go in, and the pot cooks slowly beside the fire.
- *Variations:* Greenvale households may finish it with milk; Deepwood households more often use nut paste or broth.

**Wild Orchard Tartlets**

- *Key ingredients:* Greenvale pastry; Deepwood mushrooms, herbs and nuts; a little apple or pear.
- *Method:* The pastry encloses the filling.

**Sunlit Orchard Grain**

**Forest Blossom Tart**

- *Why it exists:* Sunplains pastry technique and orchard sweetness absorb Deepwood nuts, forest fruit and earthy aromatics.
- *Key ingredients:* Thin or short pastry; fig, pear or plum; chestnut or hazelnut; mild forest herb; honey; cinnamon-like spicebark.
- *Method:* Fruit is chopped with toasted nuts and herbs, baked in a shallow pastry in a Sunplains oven or under a clay baking cover, and brushed with honey afterward.
- *Variations:* Border versions may include a small savory mushroom component.

**Forest-Orchard Medley**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*

**Forest-Citrus Creamed Grain**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*

- *Method:* The grain is slowly fed hot broth, mushrooms are added during cooking, and bright orchard acid goes in at the finish.
- *Real-world inspiration:* Risotto-like technique, without requiring rice to be universal.

**Plateau Orchard Clay Pot**

- *Why it exists:* Highridge spice layering meets Sunplains steam-trapping clay braise.
- *Key ingredients:* Goat or lamb; onion; dried apricot or raisin-like fruit; pear or quince; cumin-like seed; cinnamon-like bark; broth; oil or butter.
- *Method:* Meat is browned, spices toast in fat, fruit and broth are layered in a covered clay pot, and it cooks slowly until tender.
- *Serving:* Plateau flatbread or grain accompanies it.

**Highridge Orchard Pilaf**

- *Why it exists:* *Not in the wiki yet.*
- *Key ingredients:* Long grain, millet, wheat berries or barley; fat; broth; spice; dried Sunplains fruit; nuts.
- *Method:* The grain is toasted in fat, simmered with broth and spice, then finished with dried Sunplains fruit and nuts.
